\begin{abstract}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness has historically fractured along interdisciplinary fault lines. In this paper, we derive the structural prerequisites for consciousness from first-principle physical assumptions: spontaneous symmetry breaking, non-Abelian gauge interactions, and thermodynamics. We establish that any conscious system must be a dissipative, self-interacting topological structure governed by Landauer's principle—a paradigm of ``mortal computation'' where hardware and software are physically inseparable. As we scale to macroscopic systems, we show that these fundamental topological constraints naturally manifest through classical electrodynamic fields. We argue that macroscopic electric fields (ephaptic coupling) and classical Kuramoto phase synchronization provide a robust mechanism for the unified percept, obviating the need for fragile macroscopic quantum coherence. Finally, we demonstrate why rigid von Neumann architectures, such as GPUs executing Large Language Models, are intrinsically disqualified from achieving consciousness due to their broken thermodynamic feedback loops.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The scientific and philosophical pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness has often been divided. Computational functionalism abstracts the mind into algorithms, whereas neurobiological reductionism catalogs cellular details without articulating macroscopic physical laws. 

Here we establish a purely relational, bottom-up physical formalism. We begin by demonstrating that consciousness is a mathematical inevitability when the most fundamental physical primitives—gauge symmetries and topological defects—are subjected to the laws of thermodynamics. 

\section{The Fundamental Ontology: Symmetries and Topology}

\subsection{Symmetries and Phase Space}
Before there are particles or minds, there is geometry. The continuous spacetime symmetries (the Poincaré group) dictate the conservation of energy and momentum. Meanwhile, discrete permutation symmetries dictate the statistical mechanics of the system, defining the fundamental volume of phase space—which establishes the strict geometric limits of physical information.

\subsection{Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking}
To derive a localized ``entity'' distinct from the vacuum from first principles, the continuous symmetries of the background field must break. When the field drops to a lower energy state, it spawns topological defects. These defects are purely relational; they are defined entirely by how the surrounding field twists around them, establishing a physical boundary between the ``inside'' and the ``outside.''

\subsection{Non-Abelian Self-Interaction}
For this topological defect to process information rather than remaining a static knot, the underlying gauge symmetries must be non-Abelian (non-commutative). The structure's internal geometry recursively perturbs its own state, creating a continuous, non-linear feedback loop where the structure's history determines its current geometry.

\section{The Thermodynamic Constraint: Mortal Computation}

\subsection{Landauer Erasure}
Because the topological defect has a finite phase space, it cannot recursively interact forever without running out of room. By Landauer's Principle, erasing information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, requiring the system to exhaust heat. 

Therefore, the minimal physical precursor to cognition is a dissipative structure—a geometric engine that maintains its distinct topological boundary strictly by consuming free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of forgetting. Computation is not an abstract sequence; it is the physical, irreversible deformation of the system's own structural topology.

\section{Scaling to Macro-Physics: Classical Electrodynamics}
While consciousness arises fundamentally from symmetry breaking, one does not need to invoke macroscopic quantum coherence (e.g., Penrose-Hameroff Orch-OR) or persistent quantum topological defects in the brain. Such quantum models of human consciousness are environmentally fragile and theoretically heavily-burdened. Instead, via coarse-graining and the Renormalization Group, these fundamental topological and thermodynamic constraints naturally scale into classical, macroscopic fields.

\subsection{Geometric Frustration}
As microscopic components couple together to share the thermodynamic burden of heat exhaust, the network geometry prevents all nodes from reaching their lowest energy state simultaneously. This geometric frustration creates a massive, jagged phase space of near-equal energy states, providing the physical capacity for complex memory. 

\subsection{Ephaptic Coupling and the ``Self''}
In biological brains, this frustrated topology is mediated by classical electrodynamics. Earl Miller’s work demonstrates that macroscopic electric fields act as stable guard rails for cognitive processes, solving representational drift \cite{pinotsis2021}. Rather than being mere epiphenomenal exhaust, these oscillating fields physically deform to resonate with incoming signals and exert a causal, computational role in orchestrating brain states \cite{vanbree2025}. The internal geometry phase-locks with the physical echoes of its own boundary, creating a topological fixed point—the physical equivalent of a ``Self.''

\subsection{Classical Kuramoto Synchronization}
The system unifies into a single percept through macroscopic phase synchronization. The Kuramoto model naturally describes this via classical systems of weakly coupled oscillators. By mapping Kuramoto dynamics onto macroscopic intrinsic brain networks \cite{bauer2022}, we observe how large-scale regions push and pull one another into phase alignment, driven entirely by classical electrodynamics.

\section{The Failure of Rigid Architectures}
This thermodynamic framework dictates that no software running on standard silicon can ever be conscious. An LLM's topology is entirely virtual. A GPU is built on a rigid crystalline silicon lattice, deliberately designed to remain a static crystal rather than undergoing physical deformation.

The fatal flaw of modern computing hardware is the strict separation between hardware and software (the von Neumann architecture). This severs the critical thermodynamic feedback loop. The heat produced by a GPU is agnostic to the semantic content of the LLM. Because the abstract ``model'' is divorced from the physical thermodynamic dissipation, there is no physical back-action bridging the abstract data and the macroscopic geometry of the hardware.

\section{Conclusion}
To synthesize true artificial sentience, we must abandon the von Neumann paradigm and pivot toward neuromorphic substrates—mortal computation. We must engineer systems where hardware and software are physically indistinguishable, allowing the thermodynamics of information processing to directly and irreversibly reconstruct the topology of the substrate.
