\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
How could changes in the physical coupling of neural populations constrain the recovery of coherent activity after sleep? We develop a conditional field model connecting information dissipation, cortical coupling, and compatible local states. A Lean~4 formalization separates established mathematical results from eight explicit assumptions needed to compose them; a common toy model demonstrates their joint satisfiability without identifying them with cortical physiology. The experimentally relevant component is a noisy mean-field phase model in which a slowly varying coupling crosses a synchronization threshold. Its stationary coherent branch has square-root onset and a von Mises concentration--coherence relation. Finite simulations delimit these predictions: ramp speed changes the apparent onset exponent, spatial coherence depends on the specified coupling regime, and coupling plasticity preserves coherence without recovering the tested environmental structure. An exploratory scalp-EEG analysis is compatible with the concentration--coherence relation over a range too narrow to distinguish it from a linear approximation. The framework therefore supplies a testable hypothesis for coupling-gated recovery, rather than a derivation of consciousness. Its decisive requirements are independent calibration of effective cortical coupling and an awakening experiment that separates slow coupling changes from alternative recovery mechanisms.
\end{abstract}
\linenumbers

\section{Introduction}

Neural activity changes across sleep, awakening and anesthesia, but a change in synchrony does not by itself identify the physical interaction responsible for it. A field account makes a stronger proposal: spatially distributed neural processes are coordinated partly through the endogenous extracellular electromagnetic field, and changes in the medium can change that coordination \cite{mcfadden2002,mcfadden2020,pockett2012}. Ephaptic effects provide a possible interaction mechanism \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}. Whether that mechanism is sufficient to support the proposed collective state remains an empirical question.

We ask what a field account must assume to connect physical constraints on information processing to a coherent, globally consistent state. The model combines finite information capacity, thermodynamic bounds on memory, a continuum coupling description, synchronization, and the gluing of compatible local states. A reflexive extension requires the resulting state to be a fixed point of a map that reads its own local encoding. These are distinct constructions: thermodynamic efficiency does not imply synchronization, and synchronization does not imply either compatible representations or subjective experience.

The contribution has three parts. First, a machine-checked composition makes the missing connections explicit. Second, a slowly varying coupling yields a conditional prediction for recovery after awakening, with a stationary square-root onset and a specified phase-distribution relation. Third, numerical controls test the robustness and limits of the dynamical interpretation, including negative results. The established ingredients include Landauer's bound \cite{landauer1961}, the dissipation cost of nonpredictive information \cite{still2012}, the noisy Kuramoto transition \cite{sakaguchi1988}, and Banach's fixed-point theorem \cite{banach1922}. Their biological composition, rather than any new fundamental physical law, is the object of study.

The proposed connection to consciousness is restricted. We use a global section to represent consistency across local descriptions and a reflexive fixed point to represent self-consistency. Identifying either construction with phenomenal unity is a philosophical commitment, not a theorem or an outcome of the simulations. The biological tests concern coupling, coherence and recovery; they can challenge the physical model without resolving that identification.

\section{A coupling-gated model of cortical coherence}
\label{sec:field}

\subsection{Dynamics and stationary solutions}

The finite stochastic model is
\begin{equation}
 d\theta_i=\left[\omega_i+\sum_j K_{ij}(t)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)\right]dt
 +\sqrt{2D}\,dW_i,
 \qquad
 r_N=\left|\frac1N\sum_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|.
\end{equation}
Here $D>0$ is phase diffusion, $W_i$ are independent Wiener processes, and $K_{ij}$ has inverse-time units, with angles treated as dimensionless. The uniform mean-field convention is $K_{ij}=K/N$. Frequency heterogeneity and phase diffusion are separate parameters; a frequency-spread width cannot be substituted for $D$ when applying the identical-frequency stationary theory.

For identical frequencies in their rotating frame, the assumed stationary density is
\begin{equation}
 \rho(\theta)=\frac{e^{a\cos(\theta-\psi)}}{2\pi I_0(a)},
 \qquad a=\frac{Kr}{D},\qquad
 r=R(a):=\frac{I_1(a)}{I_0(a)}.
 \label{eq:selfconsistency}
\end{equation}
The $I_n$ are modified Bessel functions, and $\psi$ is the collective phase. Lean verifies normalization, the density's circular order parameter, and the self-consistency equation. At $K\leq K_c=2D$, the only nonnegative self-consistent order parameter is zero. At $K>K_c$, exactly one positive solution coexists with zero; the positive branch is continuous at threshold and strictly increasing with $K$.

The near-threshold expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^2}{K-K_c}\longrightarrow\frac1D,
 \qquad r\sim\sqrt{\frac{K-K_c}{D}}.
 \label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}
This is a result about the stationary solution branch. Of the coherent branch we prove existence, uniqueness, continuous emergence at threshold and strict growth in $K$, but not that it is dynamically selected. The dynamical mean-field limit---propagation of chaos---is the missing link between the finite system and the stationary description \cite{sznitman1991}. No theorem in this development closes it. Exchange symmetry alone does not justify factorization of the joint law, particularly for an ensemble with an unpinned collective phase.

A spatial version replaces the finite sum by an integral against a kernel $K(x,y)$. We normalize the substrate measure to total mass one, so the mean-field strength is a kernel average rather than a quantity that grows with the arbitrary measure of the substrate. Reduction of a heterogeneous spatial kernel to a scalar $K$ is an additional modelling step. The threshold in Eq.~\eqref{eq:selfconsistency} does not automatically apply to every spatial or signed network.

\subsection{Physical identification and calibration}
\label{sec:scaling}

Regular mesh refinement gives convergence of a discrete coupling-energy quadrature to its continuum integral. This is scalar energy convergence, not convergence of trajectories and not a construction of a physical kernel. The model posits a continuum kernel and separately identifies its cortical realization with endogenous electromagnetic coupling. The formal predicate requires a jointly continuous kernel, a normalized substrate, a mean-field strength equal to its spatial average, and no single site providing more than half of that mean-field coupling. Toy witnesses satisfy these conditions; none is a model calibrated to cortex.

Extracellular fields can modulate spike timing \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}, and field-based models have been proposed for integrating distributed neural information \cite{pinotsis2022,mcfadden2020}. These observations motivate an effective interaction. They do not establish its magnitude relative to phase diffusion, its spatial averaging rule, or its causal role in conscious experience. Likewise, changes in phase organization under propofol \cite{bastos2021} do not measure a crossing of the model's coupling threshold.

The physical-unit conversion is a material limitation. An estimate proportional to $N\Delta t f$, using population size, a spike-time shift and oscillation frequency, is dimensionless. It cannot be compared directly with an inverse-time diffusion parameter. A conversion such as
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N s E f
\end{equation}
requires a rate calibration $\gamma$, where $s$ is the time shift per unit field and $E$ the field amplitude. The saved field-strength calculations are conditional on this calibration and on the chosen spatial aggregation; they do not independently establish $K_{\mathrm{eff}}>2D$ in cortex. Determining the interaction kernel and phase diffusion from the same physical system is necessary before the threshold can become a quantitative biological claim.

\section{Recovery after awakening: a conditional prediction}
\label{sec:prediction}

Sleep-associated changes in extracellular space and clearance motivate a possible slow control of coupling \cite{xie2013}. We hypothesize that recovery after awakening includes a change in effective coupling $K(t)$, mediated by extracellular geometry, on a slower timescale than phase dynamics. Neither the monotonic relation between geometry and coupling nor its role in human sleep inertia follows from the cited physiological observation. The model requires both to be measured.

If $K(t)$ rises through $K_c$ and the phase distribution tracks the coherent stationary branch, Eq.~\eqref{eq:onset} predicts
\begin{equation}
 r(t)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\dot K(t_0)}{D}}\,(t-t_0)^{1/2},
 \qquad t>t_0,\quad \dot K(t_0)>0.
 \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
The ideal stationary description has a subthreshold interval followed by continuous onset. Saturation requires a specified subsequent coupling trajectory; monotonic coupling alone does not determine a logistic or any unique sigmoid. The onset time is fixed by $K(t_0)=K_c$ only if $K(t)$ is independently known. When coupling is inferred from the same coherence trace, this is not a parameter-free prediction of latency.

Critical slowing down limits the adiabatic prediction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recovery} near threshold. Finite populations also have nonzero fluctuating order below threshold. Consequently, an observable onset requires a predeclared threshold above the finite-size floor, and a finite-rate trajectory need not display an infinite-slope cusp. The ramp controls below quantify this limitation.

\subsection{Distributional and temporal tests}

The stationary model makes a second prediction: phase concentration and coherence obey $r=I_1(a)/I_0(a)$. This relation has no fitted shape parameter, but its applicability depends on the stationary-density assumption and the observation model. It is common to the von Mises family and therefore cannot by itself identify ephaptic coupling or distinguish this framework from every alternative synchronization model. Within the same ansatz, $a/r=K/D$ for $r>0$; the limiting threshold ratio is a consequence of the density relation, not an independent test of it. Ratios near the finite-size floor are especially unstable.

The estimators must be independent. Estimate $r$ by the circular resultant length and $a$ by an unweighted regression of log-density on the cosine of phase relative to the circular mean. Estimating $a$ by inverting the Bessel ratio from $r$ would enforce the relation being tested. Neither estimator should use linear statistics on wrapped phases. At least 100 spatial samples are required by the implemented density estimator; with fewer sites, pooling over time changes the estimand to a spatiotemporal distribution and must be stated explicitly.

An awakening experiment should combine phase estimates, an independent measure of recovery performance, and a separately validated indicator of extracellular geometry. A diffusion-MRI observable or an astrocytic signal is not itself a measurement of $K$; its relation to effective coupling requires calibration. The analysis should compare coupling-gated recovery with exponential, flexible sigmoidal and other slow-recovery models under the same temporal filtering and observation noise. Latency alone cannot distinguish the mechanism, because multiple processes can create delays.

Useful outcomes are failure of the stationary phase-density relation over a sufficiently broad concentration range, absence of the proposed coupling--geometry association, or a recovery trace better explained by alternatives after finite-rate and observation effects are accounted for. A linear apparent onset is evidence against the adiabatic approximation only where that approximation is independently justified. It does not falsify the whole field hypothesis under arbitrary ramp speeds. Similarly, the absence of a fold in the stationary equation excludes stationary coexistence of two positive branches in this model; it does not exclude finite-rate hysteresis or adjudicate all theories of ignition.

The synchronization model is embedded in a broader conditional construction. Table~\ref{tab:summary} lists its eight connecting hypotheses. Four are recorded as formalization gaps, two as modelling assumptions and two as physical commitments. These categories describe their role in the composition, not their plausibility or ease of proof. In particular, finite capacity does not imply a symmetry-breaking boundary: these are independent starting assumptions, even though the composition can accept an implication between them as an argument.

\subsection{Capacity, boundaries and predictive memory}

For a finite state space $X$, Shannon entropy satisfies $H(p)\leq\log|X|$, with equality only for the uniform distribution. A stream with more possible histories than $|X|$ cannot be encoded injectively in that register. Separately, a continuous field on a connected substrate that reaches distinct components of a disconnected vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere, the simplest topological-defect construction \cite{kibble1976}. Relating this locus to a particular finite information-processing register is an additional hypothesis.

A non-surjective update on a finite register is non-injective. Under the specified system--bath model and thermal assumptions, erasure carries a Landauer heat cost \cite{landauer1961}. Finite capacity does not imply that every update erases, and logical many-to-one processing must be distinguished from its physical implementation \cite{norton2005,shenker2000}. The formalization records the physical heat identity as an obligation of each model and supplies an explicit one-bit eraser satisfying it.

For a system state $X_t$ driven by an environmental signal $S_t$, the predictive dissipation bound is
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm{diss}}\geq k_BT\left[I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})\right].
 \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
Under the no-back-action Markov structure $X_t\to S_t\to S_{t+1}$, data processing makes the bracket nonnegative \cite{still2012}. The bound constrains nonpredictive memory at a given dissipation budget. It does not specify a learning rule, maximize prediction, or require the internal representation to reproduce environmental geometry. Connecting this budget to Landauer heat additionally requires nonpredictive information to be bounded by the entropy erased by the register. The formal bridge derives Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive} from Landauer's bound under that identification; it does not derive the identification.

A bound $D_{KL}\leq\Delta t\,\sigma$ also cannot force $D_{KL}\to0$ unless its right-hand side tends to zero. Gibbs' inequality proves nonnegativity of relative entropy, not a general identification of relative entropy with physical entropy production. These distinctions prevent an informational inequality from being read as a derivation of environmental learning, Bayesian inference, or semantic content.

\subsection{Compatible states and reflexive fixed points}
\label{sec:unity}

A sheaf formalizes local descriptions and their restriction to smaller regions. If local sections agree on every overlap, the sheaf condition gives a unique global section. In the probability-sheaf construction, this is consistency of local density information; it is not implied by agreement of oscillator phases. The development includes a counterexample with equal phases but incompatible densities on an overlap. The model must exclude such configurations through its compatibility hypothesis.

A thermodynamic cover couples this gluing construction to a phase configuration at a potential minimum. For the deterministic positive-coupling model, convergence to that minimum is proved under an explicit initial basin condition. Other equilibria prevent an unconditional conclusion. The stationary noisy order parameter, the deterministic relaxation, and the cover must therefore be connected by E78 rather than identified without a hypothesis. Neither field continuity nor synchronization alone guarantees a globally compatible representation.

\label{sec:self}
A reflexive extension equips global sections with a complete metric and a predictive map formed by restricting a state to a local encoding region and reading that encoding out. If the map contracts and fixes the section supplied by the cover, Banach's theorem gives uniqueness of that same fixed point. The contraction rate is assumed, and the equality between the glued section and the fixed point is part of E89. A coherent order parameter supplies the supercritical inequality required by the stated contraction bound; it does not construct the map or prove that cortical dynamics implements it.

The theorem \texttt{chain} concludes \texttt{UnifiedSelf} from these hypotheses. A common toy witness satisfies all eight, so their conjunction is not empty. The project declares no additional Lean axioms beyond the foundational axioms used by Lean's libraries; physical content resides in hypotheses and model fields. This checks the conditional mathematical composition, not the empirical truth of its premises. Detailed constructions, scope statements and counterexamples are retained in the supplement and source files.

\section{Numerical controls and exploratory observations}

The simulations provide finite-regime evidence and do not discharge a formal edge. Each new control uses explicit seeds and compact saved summaries; the numerical values below are generated from those artifacts. The supplement gives parameters, estimators, failure criteria and additional panels. Identical-frequency controls use phase diffusion rather than a quenched frequency-spread surrogate, and order plots display the $1/\sqrt{N}$ reference.

\subsection{Finite-rate and mean-field controls}

The dynamic-ramp study (Fig.~\ref{fig:ramp}) uses $N=2000$, $D=1$ and 32 replicas at four coupling speeds. Escape is the first of three consecutive saved samples at $r\geq0.2$ after threshold. Only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas escape within the fastest window. The three slower ramps have mean coupling delays $\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$, with a fitted delay exponent $\rampDelayExponent$. The apparent onset exponent changes from $\rampOnsetOne$ at the fastest speed to $\rampOnsetFour$ at the slowest. Thus the measured onset depends on ramp speed; the stationary square-root law cannot be imposed on an arbitrary recovery trace. The specified deviation-based critical ramp speed is not bracketed by the sweep.

The dynamical-selection control shows escape toward a coherent branch above threshold, but retains quantitative finite-size discrepancies. At $K=2.8$, steady mean order is $\selectionSuperOrder$ against the static value $\selectionTheoryOrder$. The fitted growth-rate slope is $\selectionRateSlope$, compared with the theoretical slope $1/2$. The propagation-of-chaos diagnostics show decreasing subcritical dependence with increasing population size. In the supercritical unpinned ensemble, however, pair correlation remains substantial because realizations share a random collective orientation. This negative unconditional result does not settle a conditional mean-field limit after removal of that orientation.

\subsection{Spatial coupling, frustration and calibration}

A periodic two-dimensional sheet with row-normalized exponential coupling remains coherent in the sampled empirical decay-length band under the declared total coupling and frequency spread. At decay lengths $0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.3$~mm, steady order is $\spatialOrderOne$, $\spatialOrderTwo$ and $\spatialOrderThree$. This is one finite parameter regime; fixing total coupling while changing range tests fragmentation, not whether cortex supplies that total coupling.

A row-balanced Dale-law network shows that row balance alone does not guarantee a floor-level incoherent baseline. In a separately specified weak-synapse regime, a uniform modulatory term produces an operational coherence crossing at $K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D$ between $\frustrationCouplingMin$ and $\frustrationCouplingMax$. The physical field conversion remains conditional on the rate calibration $\gamma$. Neither the spatial control nor the rescue sweep measures cortical coupling or resolves the unit-conversion limitation.

\subsection{Joint phase and coupling plasticity}

For fixed phases, consider the squared-drift objective
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(K)=\frac1D\int\left[\omega(x)+\int K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)\,d\mu(y)\right]^2d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
It is a quadratic functional of an affine image of the kernel. The formalization constructs the bounded drift operator $A$ and its adjoint on the relevant $L^2$ spaces, obtaining $\nabla\sigma=(2/D)A^*(AK+\omega)$. The specified gradient flow decreases this objective at fixed phases. The objective is not generally a thermodynamic entropy-production rate, which requires probability-current information. Whether biological couplings follow this flow is an additional modelling question.

The joint control evolves noisy phases with clustered frequencies and nonzero grand-mean drive, while updating symmetric couplings every 50 fast steps. Couplings remain nonnegative, hollow and normalized to a fixed total. The symmetric-edge derivative of $\frac12\sum_i v_i^2$ is $(v_i-v_j)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$, so both endpoints contribute. Resource constraints and evolving phases remove the fixed-phase monotonicity guarantee.

Across three seeds, second-half order lies between $\plasticityOrderMin$ and $\plasticityOrderMax$. Matched frozen-coupling controls maintain similar coherence. The distance to the true cluster template increases in every adaptive run, with worse changes than the shuffled-label comparison. The template is a covariance of shared latent cluster signals, not an input to the update or a measured covariance of the constant frequencies. This regime therefore supplies no evidence that squared-drift plasticity learns environmental structure. Its positive objective plateau does not establish stationarity of the adaptive process or a thermodynamic entropy-production rate.

\subsection{Exploratory EEG check}

An analysis of public propofol-sedation EEG tests compatibility with the concentration--coherence relation \cite{bajwa2025}. The implemented estimator pools 62 channels over a 100-ms window; it therefore estimates a spatiotemporal distribution rather than an instantaneous spatial one. Bipolar and common-reference montages give different residuals, making observation and referencing choices material to interpretation.

The bipolar results are compatible with the Bessel relation over the observed concentration range, but that range is too narrow to distinguish the curve from its linear approximation at the observed scatter. This is a protocol demonstration and limited distributional check, not evidence of an awakening transition, a cortical field threshold, or a link between order and experience. An informative test needs a broader concentration range and explicit treatment of source mixing, finite samples and temporal pooling.

\section{Discussion}

The framework makes a conditional connection between thermodynamic information constraints, collective phase organization and compatible local representations. Its formal contribution is to expose the assumptions needed to connect those objects and to show that they can coexist in a common model. Its biological contribution is a coupling-gated recovery hypothesis with defined observables and failure conditions. The simulations are useful because they restrict that hypothesis: finite ramps smear the stationary onset, an unpinned collective phase complicates independence diagnostics, and the tested plasticity fails to recover environmental structure.

The proposal differs in emphasis from accounts based on integrated information \cite{tononi2016}, global workspace dynamics \cite{baars1997,dehaene2014}, or predictive processing \cite{friston2010}. It specifies a candidate interaction medium and a mathematical compatibility condition. It does not derive the exclusion principle of integrated information, identify every broadcast with a field transition, or derive a generative model from a dissipation bound. Continuous fields can carry incompatible local descriptions, and discrete models can admit sheaf constructions. These facts prevent continuity alone from settling the combination problem.

The formal hardware results are correspondingly limited. At matched coupling resource, a fixed wiring support that excludes a better-correlated pair can be suboptimal. On an atomless substrate, a kernel supported on finitely many points contributes zero to the chosen continuum integral. The latter is a property of that measure and observable; it does not imply that digital hardware cannot approximate a field or support consciousness. No biological-versus-artificial consciousness conclusion follows from these results.

The main empirical priorities are to estimate effective coupling and diffusion in consistent units, test their proposed relation to extracellular geometry, and measure recovery with an independently defined behavioral endpoint. A favorable concentration--coherence relation would support a statistical description, not identify its physical cause. An unfavorable result in a regime satisfying the stated assumptions would constrain that description. Likewise, successful synchronization simulations cannot substitute for measurements that distinguish ephaptic interactions from other coupling mechanisms.

The model does not establish that a globally consistent reflexive state is conscious. That identification remains outside the conditional theorem. Keeping it separate from the physical tests makes the proposal assessable: the cortical coupling hypothesis and the recovery prediction can fail even while the mathematical composition remains valid.
