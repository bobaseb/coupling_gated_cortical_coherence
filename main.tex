\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
Coordinated cortical activity need not describe a single experienced situation. We develop a conditional field account of coherence, compatibility and self-representation. Its demonstrated results establish what coordination leaves undetermined: increasing coupling, decreasing diffusion and a matched common drive can produce identical stationary phase distributions; equal phases permit incompatible local states; and a plasticity control preserves coherence while losing alignment with a specified environmental template. The conditional construction glues compatible spatial measures into one state and makes that state the unique fixed point of a contracting reconstruction map. Lean~4 checks the composition under eight connecting hypotheses and additional structures, with compatibility and accuracy at the glued state supplied as premises. We propose the glued state as a formal correlate of conscious unity and its accurate internal reconstruction as a correlate of a minimal self. The physical hypothesis is that changing extracellular geometry increases field-mediated coupling during awakening. A noisy mean-field phase model gives a square-root stationary onset, but finite ramps delay the response and the distributional equivalence requires measurements that distinguish recovery mechanisms. Testing the account further requires identifying neural content and self-encoding independently, then determining whether coordination maintains their agreement and whether these relations matter for unified experience.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

How could activity distributed across cortex support one experience? A field account proposes that endogenous extracellular electromagnetic fields partly coordinate spatially separated neural processes \cite{mcfadden2002,mcfadden2020,pockett2012}. Ephaptic effects provide a possible interaction mechanism \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}. Yet even successful coordination leaves a question: what makes the coordinated activity a description of one experienced situation, with a perspective on that situation?

We propose an account with three requirements. \emph{Coherence} concerns whether coupling sustains collective activity. \emph{Compatibility} concerns whether local descriptions agree where their domains overlap. \emph{Self-representation} concerns whether the resulting global state supports an accurate model of itself. The latter two express commitments about consciousness whose motivation and possible dissociations we develop below.

Consider the experience of reaching for a cup. A description of the cup's location and a description of the hand's relation to it can share information about where the cup is while contributing different contents to the episode. Synchronous activity in the two processes would leave any disagreement about that shared quantity unresolved. The formal counterexample isolates this problem using spatial mass profiles at a common phase that disagree on an overlap. We return to the cup example to ask what agreement would mean for perceptual content, and what an internal representation would have to add to place the perceiver in that situation. It is an illustration of the modelling task, not a neural model implemented here.

Awakening gives the physical requirement a concrete setting. We hypothesize that changing extracellular geometry slowly increases effective coupling through a synchronization threshold. Under stationary branch tracking, a noisy phase model fixes a square-root onset of coherence. The experiment faces two distinct obstacles. Finite ramp speeds delay the response, so a fitted onset exponent depends on the drive. Moreover, successive stationary phase distributions can be identical under increasing coupling, decreasing diffusion or a suitably matched common drive. Recovery must therefore be measured in a way that identifies dynamics and distinguishes causes, rather than inferred from the shape of a coherence trace alone.

A plasticity control tests another route to representation. Its squared-drift objective rewards equalizing drift, which in the chosen frequency-cluster model favors connections across the clusters that define the diagnostic template. Coherence survives while alignment with that template declines. Together with the overlap counterexample, this result establishes a limit on what the coordination model explains. Compatible local content needs its own mechanism and observable.

The contribution has three levels. The separation results establish limits on what coordination explains. The conditional construction gives a unique global state from specified compatible spatial measures and makes it the unique fixed point of a contracting map assumed to reconstruct that state. Lean~4 checks the composition, and toy witnesses establish joint satisfiability of its assumptions. The biological interpretation proposes that the glued state and its internal reconstruction capture conscious unity and a minimal self. This last step requires identifying meaningful neural descriptions and explaining how coordination maintains their agreement. Figure~\ref{fig:chain} locates the proposed connections, and Section~\ref{sec:chain} states the physical and thermodynamic assumptions.

\section{Coherence: the coupling model}
\label{sec:field}

\subsection{Dynamics and the stationary threshold}

The finite stochastic model is
\begin{equation}
 d\theta_i=\left[\omega_i+\sum_j K_{ij}(t)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)\right]dt
 +\sqrt{2D}\,dW_i,
 \qquad
 r_N=\left|\frac1N\sum_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|.
\end{equation}
Here $D>0$ is phase diffusion, $W_i$ are independent Wiener processes, and $K_{ij}$ has inverse-time units, with angles dimensionless. The uniform mean-field convention is $K_{ij}=K/N$. Frequency heterogeneity and phase diffusion are separate parameters.

For identical frequencies in their rotating frame, the stationary ansatz is
\begin{equation}
 \rho(\theta)=\frac{e^{a\cos(\theta-\psi)}}{2\pi I_0(a)},
 \qquad a=\frac{Kr}{D},\qquad
 r=R(a):=\frac{I_1(a)}{I_0(a)},
 \label{eq:selfconsistency}
\end{equation}
where $I_n$ are modified Bessel functions and $\psi$ is the collective phase. Lean verifies normalization, the circular order parameter and self-consistency for this density. At $K\leq K_c=2D$, the only nonnegative self-consistent order parameter is zero. At $K>K_c$, exactly one positive solution coexists with zero; it emerges continuously and increases strictly with $K$. Near threshold,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^2}{K-K_c}\longrightarrow\frac1D,
 \qquad r\sim\sqrt{\frac{K-K_c}{D}}.
 \label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}
These results characterize the stationary branch (Fig.~\ref{fig:bifurcation}). Connecting it to finite trajectories requires a dynamical mean-field limit and dynamical selection, neither proved here. Propagation of chaos concerns the first of these steps \cite{sznitman1991}; exchange symmetry alone does not establish independence, especially when realizations have an unpinned collective phase.

A spatial version replaces the finite sum by an integral against a kernel $K(x,y)$. The substrate measure has total mass one, making the mean-field strength a kernel average. Applying the scalar threshold to a heterogeneous or signed kernel requires a justified reduction to that mean-field description.

\subsection{Physical identification and calibration}
\label{sec:scaling}

The cortical hypothesis identifies part of the effective coupling with endogenous extracellular electromagnetic interactions. Fields can modulate spike timing \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}, and field models have been proposed for coordinating distributed neural information \cite{pinotsis2022,mcfadden2020}. The quantitative question is whether that interaction supplies sufficient coupling relative to diffusion. Changes in phase organization under propofol \cite{bastos2021} motivate studying this regime, but do not measure the coupling itself.

The physical field predicate requires a jointly continuous kernel, a normalized substrate, a mean-field strength equal to the spatial average, and no single site providing more than half that strength. Regular mesh refinement establishes convergence of a scalar coupling-energy quadrature; constructing the kernel and relating it to trajectories are separate modelling tasks. The formal witnesses supply toy instances of the predicate.

Calibration is essential because a spike-time shift is not a coupling rate. An estimate proportional to $N\Delta t f$, using population size, timing shift and oscillation frequency, is dimensionless. A conversion of the form
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N s E f
 \label{eq:keff}
\end{equation}
requires a rate $\gamma$, where $s$ is the timing shift per unit field and $E$ the field amplitude. This rate specifies how induced timing shifts persist and accumulate into phase drift. Supplemental Material explains why the other factors cannot determine it. The biological threshold test requires independent estimates of the interaction kernel, its aggregation, and phase diffusion in the same cortical regime.

\section{Testing coherence through recovery after awakening}
\label{sec:prediction}

The proposed control variable is extracellular geometry. Sleep-associated changes in extracellular space and clearance \cite{xie2013} motivate a coupling parameter that evolves more slowly than phase dynamics. The extracellular volume fraction is approximately $\alpha=0.2$ at diffusion tortuosity $\lambda\approx1.6$ \cite{sykova2008}; the cited sleep study reports a rise in $\alpha$ from roughly $0.14$ to $0.23$ \cite{xie2013}. Below $10$\,kHz cortical tissue conducts approximately resistively at $0.3$--$0.6$\,S\,m$^{-1}$ \cite{barbour2017}. In an effective-medium description at fixed transmembrane current density, a larger extracellular volume fraction raises conductivity and reduces field amplitude.

This gives the coupling-gated hypothesis a directional prediction: contraction of extracellular space on awakening raises $E$ and, with the other factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:keff} held fixed, raises effective coupling. The proposed geometry--coupling relation and its role in human recovery both require measurement. The effective-medium argument supplies a sign under stated conditions; calibration supplies the magnitude and the mapping to the scalar model.

If $K(t)$ rises through $K_c$ and the phase distribution tracks the coherent stationary branch, Eq.~\eqref{eq:onset} gives
\begin{equation}
 r(t)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\dot K(t_0)}{D}}\,(t-t_0)^{1/2},
 \qquad t>t_0,\quad \dot K(t_0)>0.
 \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
The crossing time satisfies $K(t_0)=K_c$. It is a prediction of latency only when coupling is measured independently of coherence. The subsequent shape and saturation depend on the later coupling trajectory. Critical slowing down restricts branch tracking near threshold, while finite populations have fluctuating order below it. A measurable onset therefore needs an explicit criterion above that fluctuation floor.

\subsection{What a recovery distribution can identify}
\label{sec:identifiability}

Equation~\eqref{eq:selfconsistency} gives a concentration--coherence relation with no fitted shape parameter. Testing it requires separate estimators: $r$ from circular resultant length and $a$ from density shape. Estimating $a$ by inverting $R$ from the measured $r$ makes agreement automatic. Even a valid agreement tests a statistical description shared by the von Mises family; it does not determine the coupling medium.

There is a stronger limitation. Successive equilibrated phase distributions cannot, by themselves, distinguish three different recovery mechanisms. Let $q(t)$ be a prescribed ratio crossing $2$, let $r(q)$ denote its stationary branch, and choose a fixed rate $D_0>0$. Increasing coupling with $K(t)=D_0q(t)$ and $D=D_0$ gives the same stationary density as fixed coupling $K_0$ with decreasing diffusion $D(t)=K_0/q(t)$. Both have concentration $a=q(t)r(q(t))$. Uncoupled oscillators under a common prescribed drift $-h(t)\sin(\theta-\psi)$, with $h(t)=D_0q(t)r(q(t))$ and $D=D_0$, have that same density. Their distributions coincide window by window, even though their physical causes differ. Supplemental Material supplies the construction and its numerical feasibility check.

The missing observable is the physical clock. Resolved phase increments contain rate information: in the ideal continuous-path model, quadratic variation accumulates at rate $2D$, while conditional increment means constrain drift. This can separate coupling and diffusion under the specified observation model. Distinguishing endogenous coupling from the matched common drive additionally requires a measured drive or a controlled perturbation, because their mean-field drifts can coincide. Identifying the coupling as electromagnetic then requires evidence specific to that interaction. This hierarchy determines the experiment: distribution, dynamics and physical mechanism are distinct targets.

\subsection{Finite ramps change the observable onset}
\label{sec:numerics}
\label{sec:ramp}

Finite simulations characterize how the stationary prediction can appear in a recovery measurement. The ramp study uses $N=2000$, $D=1$ and 32 replicas at four coupling speeds. Escape is the first of three consecutive saved samples at $r\geq0.2$ after threshold. The supplement gives parameters, estimators and further controls.

Faster ramps delay escape (Fig.~\ref{fig:ramp}). Only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas escape within the fastest window. The three slower ramps have mean coupling delays $\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$. Their fitted onset exponents run from $\rampOnsetTwo$ to $\rampOnsetFour$, compared with $\rampOnsetReferenceTwo$ to $\rampOnsetReferenceFour$ from the same estimator on the exact stationary branch at the same coupling grids. At the fastest speed the ensemble mean never reaches the declared fit window; the returned value $\rampOnsetOne$ is a local log-slope of a delayed trajectory.

This is delayed passage through a noisy bifurcation \cite{berglund2002}, with a direct consequence for the awakening test: the apparent exponent depends on the ramp and on the fitting window. A deviation from the square-root law constrains the adiabatic approximation only where branch tracking is independently justified. The sweep does not bracket the specified deviation-based critical ramp speed.

Additional controls test the approximations used by the physical model. Time-step refinement attributes most of the sampled discrepancy from the stationary order to integration bias. Population-size diagnostics show why a shared random collective orientation can preserve unconditional pair correlation in the coherent regime. Spatial and signed-network controls exhibit coherence at specified total coupling, with the refined signed-network crossing bracket $K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D\in[\frustrationBracketMin,\frustrationBracketMax]$ still containing $2$. These controls delimit finite regimes and numerical resolution; the supplement reports their full evidence. Calibrating the cortical coupling remains the experimental task.

\subsection{What the estimators produce on EEG}

Public propofol-sedation EEG \cite{bajwa2025} provides an exploratory exercise of the concentration and coherence estimators. The implemented analysis pools channels over 100-ms windows, estimating a spatiotemporal phase distribution. Bipolar and common-reference montages produce different residuals. The bipolar results are compatible with the Bessel relation, but the concentration range is too narrow to distinguish it from its linear approximation at the observed scatter.

An informative recording needs a broader concentration range, estimator calibration that accounts for dependence, and an observation model for source mixing, referencing and temporal pooling. The sleep-recovery hypothesis remains untested by these sedation recordings.

\subsection{A protocol for the awakening experiment}
\label{sec:protocol}

The analysis defines six requirements for the physical test.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Coupling and diffusion in the same units.} Estimate $K$ and $D$ from resolved phase dynamics in the same cortical regime, separating diffusion, frequency heterogeneity and observation noise. A measured drive or controlled perturbation is additionally needed to distinguish endogenous coupling from the matched common-drive alternative.
\item \textbf{Geometry calibrated to coupling.} Establish the relation between an extracellular-geometry observable and effective coupling, with uncertainty. A diffusion-MRI or astrocytic proxy for volume fraction requires its own validation; the conductivity argument supplies only the expected sign.
\item \textbf{Adequate phase measurements.} For the proposed spatial estimator, use at least 100 sites per estimate, $r$ from resultant length, and $a$ from unweighted regression of log density on the cosine of centered phase. This numerical minimum needs null calibration at the actual sample count, concentration range and dependence. Temporal pooling changes the estimand.
\item \textbf{An independent recovery endpoint.} Measure behavioral recovery separately from the phase trace.
\item \textbf{A predeclared onset criterion.} Fix a coherence threshold above the finite-size floor and account for finite ramp speed before interpreting an onset exponent.
\item \textbf{Matched model comparison.} Compare coupling-gated recovery with diffusion change, common drive and alternative slow-recovery models under the same observation and filtering assumptions. Stationary distributions alone cannot resolve the equivalence in Section~\ref{sec:identifiability}.
\end{enumerate}

Failure of the concentration--coherence relation over an informative range would constrain the phase-density model. Failure of the calibrated geometry--coupling association would constrain the proposed physical gate. A competing dynamical explanation that better predicts independently measured recovery would count against its role in awakening. These tests address the first requirement of the framework. Even a favorable outcome leaves the question of what the coordinated activity represents.

\section{Compatibility: from coordinated activity to a global description}
\label{sec:unity}

\subsection{Why common timing is insufficient}

Consider overlapping patches $U$ and $V$, each carrying a local description. Compatibility requires that restricting either description to $U\cap V$ gives the same object. Agreement concerns the shared domain; descriptions on distinct parts can remain different. For the finite spatial measures used here, equal overlap restrictions give a unique measure on the covered domain. This is the gluing property formalized by a sheaf.

In the cup example, suppose a visual description locates the cup on a table and a reaching description relates the hand to the cup. To compare them, a model must express their shared quantity, such as cup location, in a common coordinate system. Restriction would retain that quantity while dropping details peculiar to each description. Agreement on it permits the visual description to contribute surface appearance and the reaching description to contribute hand configuration without duplicating the whole situation. Disagreement after coordinate conversion would make the two estimates incompatible at that level of description, however closely timed their activity.

The formal counterexample makes the additional requirement visible. Every patch has the same oscillator phase, yet two local mass profiles assign different densities to a shared site. They cannot be restrictions of a single global measure. Phase order therefore cannot establish compatibility. Continuity of a physical field does not repair disagreement in the descriptions associated with it.

Agreement to within a tolerance determines less than a glued state, and more than nothing. Suppose the local profiles differ by at most $\varepsilon$ in the uniform distance at every shared site. Nonnegative weights that vanish off their own patch and sum to one at each site then select a global profile lying within $\varepsilon$ of every patch on that patch, and any two such selections differ by at most $\varepsilon$, with no dependence on how many patches cover a site; at $\varepsilon=0$ the selection is the unique glued state. What the tolerance costs is existence: for every positive $\varepsilon$ there are families at that tolerance admitting no global description at all, so selection returns a choice among states rather than an extension of the data. A readout that is $L$-Lipschitz in the state varies by at most $L\varepsilon$ across selections, which is the resolution condition an interpretation at scale $\delta$ would have to meet. The supplement states these theorems and a three-site witness that attains the bound. Selection supplies no process by which local descriptions come to agree, and no rule that picks the cover or the weights.

For a theory of experience, the attraction of gluing is this contribution of different local contents to one description. The cup example does not yet instantiate the theorem: receptive territories and content domains must be related to the spatial patches, and their descriptions must have the required extension property. In particular, a decoder assigning probability laws to perceptual contents needs a further extension model, because matching overlap marginals need not determine a unique global joint distribution \cite{abramsky2011}. The spatial-measure construction provides a precise case of gluing; a content model must justify its own existence and uniqueness claims.

\subsection{Coherent adaptation can lose environmental alignment}
\label{sec:plasticity}

An adaptive coupling rule might appear to supply that missing representational structure. The squared-drift control shows why the objective matters. Symmetric coupling conserves mean drift: pairwise sine terms cancel when summed over nodes. Minimizing the sum of squared drifts at that fixed mean therefore rewards equalizing drift across nodes. In the chosen frequency-cluster model, this favors coupling between unlike-frequency clusters, whereas the diagnostic template rewards coupling within like-frequency clusters. The objective and the scored structure pull in different directions.

For fixed phases, the continuum objective is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(K)=\frac1D\int\left[\omega(x)+\int K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)\,d\mu(y)\right]^2d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
The formalization constructs the bounded drift operator $A$ and its adjoint, obtains $\nabla\sigma=(2/D)A^*(AK+\omega)$, and proves descent for the specified gradient flow. This is a squared-drift functional; identifying thermodynamic entropy production would require probability-current information.

The finite control co-evolves noisy phases and symmetric, nonnegative couplings at fixed total resource. The frequency-cluster template is a diagnostic covariance of shared latent signals, not a training input. Across three seeds, second-half order remains between $\plasticityOrderMin$ and $\plasticityOrderMax$, while the within-cluster to between-cluster coupling ratio falls from $\plasticityRatioInitialMin$--$\plasticityRatioInitialMax$ to $\plasticityRatioMin$--$\plasticityRatioMax$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:resonance}). The supplement reports random-update, relabelled-gradient and frozen-kernel controls, including comparisons at overlapping deformation and coherence ranges and against partitions blind to frequency.

The objective reduction is transient: averaging over complete update intervals removes the apparent gain seen immediately after updates. A prespecified grid of $\plasticityStudyCaseCount$ rate--cadence combinations contains no case meeting the declared $5\%$ sustained-reduction criterion while retaining coherence and losing alignment. The finding is coherent adaptation away from the specified structure, with no demonstrated sustained cost reduction.

Kernel alignment and overlap compatibility are different observables. This control tests the narrower suggestion that coherent adaptation under this objective acquires the designated environmental structure. It fails for an intelligible reason: the objective rewards a different organization. A thermodynamically specified learning model can include data in its objective \cite{corberi2026}; explaining agreement about the cup would likewise require a learning rule connected to the relevant content.

\subsection{What must be specified and measured}

Compatibility enters the construction as a hypothesis about the local states. For deterministic positive coupling, convergence to a potential minimum is proved under an explicit initial basin condition; the proposed bridge E78 connects supercritical noisy coherence to relaxation of the specified cover. Neither step explains how initially inconsistent representations become consistent. That is the mechanism a biological account must add.

A neural test must first identify the domains and objects whose agreement is at issue. Two decoders should estimate a shared content on overlapping domains, with restriction maps specified before inspecting their agreement. Their discrepancy needs a null under decoding noise, and the procedure must distinguish deliberately constructed compatible and incompatible examples. A common prior can make separate decoders agree artificially; region-specific bias can make them disagree artificially. The supplement develops these requirements. An estimator built from phase agreement would miss the property the counterexample separates.

Choosing a cover is also part of choosing a candidate subject. Trivial descriptions can agree while carrying little content, and a suitably chosen partition can conceal disagreement. The scientifically useful choice must therefore be constrained independently by what local activity encodes and which shared quantities can be compared. The uniqueness theorem then answers a precise question about that choice: whether the supplied local descriptions determine one global state. It leaves the empirical selection of domains and descriptions to the model.

\section{Self-representation: a state that models itself}
\label{sec:self}

\subsection{The reflexive construction}

Compatibility yields a global state. Reflexivity adds a relation between that state and an encoding within it. Equip global sections with a complete metric. Under the additional restriction-resonance condition, the encoding of a section $s$ is its restriction to a region $U$, and the map takes the form
\begin{equation}
 F(s)=\operatorname{readout}(s|_U).
 \label{eq:selfmap}
\end{equation}
The same region and readout are used across the model's state space. If $F$ contracts and fixes the section $s_*$ supplied by the cover, Banach's theorem makes $s_*$ its unique fixed point \cite{banach1922}. The formal bridge E89 supplies the contraction law and $F(s_*)=s_*$ for the boundary's encoding--readout composite. Identifying that encoding with restriction is a separate resonance condition; E89 does not supply it. The supercritical order parameter provides an inequality used by the specified contraction bound; constructing the encoding and readout remains a separate obligation.

The equality ties the reconstruction to the state formed from the local descriptions. In the cup example, the candidate global state would include the represented scene and the perceiver's relation to it. A local encoding would have to retain enough information for the specified readout to recover that modelled state. An encoding of cup position alone would be insufficient if the readout could not recover the represented hand and viewpoint. The term \emph{predictive} names this reconstruction map; a temporal neural prediction requires a dynamical interpretation.

\subsection{Why connect these properties to unity and a minimal self?}
\label{sec:commitment}

We identify the section glued from compatible local states with the unity of a conscious episode, and its accurate internal reconstruction with a minimal self. Two features of the episode motivate this interpretation: different contents belong to one situation, and that situation is experienced from a perspective.

The unity proposal starts from a distinction between having several contents and having several experiences. Seeing the cup's surface and feeling the reaching hand contribute different contents to one situation. Compatibility expresses their agreement about what they share while preserving those differences. An episode can also contain uncertainty or conflicting judgments. A model might represent the cup's apparent position separately from a judged position and consistently describe the discrepancy. Deciding whether two estimates concern the same quantity or distinct aspects of experience is therefore part of defining the content model. A choice made only to ensure agreement would give the unity proposal no empirical force.

The motivation for a minimal self is that the cup is encountered from a location and in relation to a body that can reach it. The proposed internal representation concerns the perceiver's condition in that encounter: where the perceiver is situated, what is being represented and how the body relates to it. Such a representation could be implicit in the organization of activity; it need not describe a person verbally or carry an autobiographical narrative. Requiring it to reconstruct the same global state links the perspective to the episode whose contents it organizes.

The fixed-point condition requires accuracy about the very state containing the encoding. Reconstruction alone is insufficient for a perspective: a recording can be reconstructed without being used by a system to represent its own condition. The proposed interpretation therefore needs evidence that the encoding helps the system distinguish changes in itself from changes in its surroundings. Changing the encoded hand or viewpoint while holding the external scene fixed should alter the system's estimates of its relation to the cup as the readout predicts. This functional criterion goes beyond $F(s_*)=s_*$; whether the represented perspective is experienced remains a further question.

Accuracy concerns the variables represented by $s_*$, rather than every microscopic physical fact. For a fixed map $F$ with Lipschitz factor $q<1$ and fixed point $s_*$, approximate reconstruction has a quantitative bound:
\begin{equation}
d(s,F(s))\leq\varepsilon
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
d(s,s_*)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-q}.
\label{eq:approximate-self}
\end{equation}
At the stipulated rate $q=e^{-(K-2D)\tau/2}$, this radius grows as $2\varepsilon/((K-2D)\tau)$ near threshold, for fixed positive $\varepsilon$ and $\tau$. The bound weakens while the exact fixed point remains unique. The supplement gives explicit inequalities and composes reconstruction error with displacement of the state. A cortical realization must specify the represented variables, their metric and the required fidelity; changing readouts require a further dynamical model.

We propose reflexivity as necessary because even an unreflective episode has a perspective: the cup appears somewhere relative to the perceiver, whether or not the perceiver attends to themselves. Requiring internal self-encoding on that basis is the philosophical hypothesis. Gluing alone does not require it; the dissociations below test the two commitments separately.

\subsection{Which cover and which self-model?}

Both uniqueness results are conditional on their objects. Gluing gives one global extension of specified compatible local data; Banach gives one fixed point of a specified contracting map. These solve ambiguity within a chosen construction. They do not choose between different covers, different encodings or different candidate subjects. Integrated information theory makes exclusion an explicit part of its account \cite{tononi2016}; the present uniqueness results do not settle that selection problem.

The relevant cover and map must be tied to neural organization by evidence independent of the desired conclusion. Local descriptions should predict the contents attributed to their domains, and their restrictions should preserve meaning on shared domains. The encoding and readout should be specified before testing them, with their sensitivity to changes in the proposed state measured over a declared range.

A constant readout returning one predetermined state contracts and has a fixed point, even when it discards an encoding supplied by restriction. Such a construction fails the sensitivity requirement above. This is why selecting the map requires evidence about its functional role in addition to mathematical existence.

\subsection{Dissociations that would bear on the identification}

A test of the full commitment must measure its clauses separately: effective coupling relative to diffusion, overlap compatibility, and the accuracy of the proposed self-encoding, alongside an independent endpoint for experience. A case meeting all three physical and representational conditions with evidence against a unified conscious episode would challenge their sufficiency. Unified experience with demonstrably incompatible local descriptions would challenge the unity identification. Unified experience with compatibility but without the specified self-encoding would challenge the reflexive requirement.

Anesthetic emergence, dreaming and dissociative states are candidate settings because the framework asks whether coordination, unified content and self-representation can separate. Interpreting such evidence requires a declared relation between the experience endpoint and report; absence of report alone cannot define absence of an episode. The compatibility observable in the supplement is a specification, and the corresponding neural self-encoding has yet to be identified. These are the main obstacles to testing the interpretive commitment.

The proposal concerns which state is conscious. It supplies no explanation of why satisfying these relations should be accompanied by experience. Nor do its gluing and fixed-point conditions select an electromagnetic substrate: the cortical field commitment resides in the physical identification and calibration. Their multiple realizability leaves the status of other biological and artificial implementations open.

\section{Thermodynamic constraints and the conditional composition}
\label{sec:chain}

The broader composition constrains one proposed physical implementation through finite-register capacity $H(p)\leq\log|X|$, erasure heat under a specified system--bath model \cite{landauer1961,norton2005,shenker2000}, and a separate field-boundary result for disconnected vacuum components \cite{kibble1976}. The supplement develops these results and their witnesses. The phase threshold and representational argument stand independently of a derivation from finite capacity.

The constraint most directly relevant to representation concerns predictive memory. For a state $X_t$ driven by an environmental signal $S_t$,
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm{diss}}\geq k_BT\left[I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})\right].
 \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
Under the no-back-action Markov structure $X_t\to S_t\to S_{t+1}$, the bracket is nonnegative \cite{still2012}. The bound limits nonpredictive memory at a given dissipation budget; zero retained information satisfies it too. Relating it to Landauer heat requires that memory waste be bounded by erased entropy at the same thermal scale and heat budget. These distinct information constraints \cite{rosas2026} leave content and learning dynamics to be specified.

An acting agent changes its future inputs, so the passive assumption requires justification for the variables and interval considered. A feedback model specifies a policy $A_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid X_t)$, world dynamics $S_{t+1}\sim K(\cdot\mid S_t,A_t)$, an observation channel and a memory update. For finite states, the exact information balance is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})
 ={}& I(X_t;S_t\mid S_{t+1})\\
    &-I(X_t;S_{t+1}\mid S_t).
\end{split}
\label{eq:feedback-information}
\end{equation}
Both conditional terms are nonnegative. The last term vanishes for the passive model; with feedback, the signed difference can be negative because actions create correlations with the future. Its causal interpretation requires an environmental state sufficient to account for the relevant dynamics.

The finite thermodynamic extension instead bounds joint entropy reduction. For an elementary update that holds the controller fixed, strictly positive forward and reverse path probabilities give
$\Sigma=D_{KL}(P_F\|P_R)=\Delta H(X,S)+\langle Q\rangle/(k_BT)\ge0$
under local detailed balance, with $Q$ positive into the reservoir \cite{horowitz2014,ito2013}. This supplies the thermodynamic constraint for the active branch below. A proved two-substep extension composes actuation with a memory update initialized from its coordinate-swapped final law. It identifies the output with the perception--action channels and cancels the same intermediate entropy and expected energy. At a common thermal scale, the whole update's joint entropy decrease is bounded by its total heat budget divided by $k_BT$, without assuming stationarity. The supplement gives the balances and reciprocal two-bit witnesses with explicit interaction energy, heat and work. Action and observation alias the existing states; additional physical registers require further accounting.

An explicit objective also permits finite policy selection. For a supplied terminal reward and a finite set of deterministic policies, a nonempty set satisfying the complete update's heat budget has a reward maximizer. A two-bit lamp task has a unique feasible optimum that strictly improves on a feasible constant-action baseline; a stronger policy exceeds the budget. Reward, heat and work are evaluated on each policy's actual composed process, with common initial law, channels, energy and reservoir convention. The baseline meets the heat budget while missing the task target, so the objective supplies content that thermodynamics alone does not. This result compares fixed policies for one update.

A separate finite learning model specifies how a policy register adapts. Its bit selects between the two feasible lamp policies, and its energy decreases with their known expected reward. An autonomous thermal channel changes the register, carrying each final distribution into the next update. From the specified initial preparation, Lean proves strictly increasing expected task reward and geometric convergence to a stationary mixture. Transition heat obeys local detailed balance, and cumulative update heat equals the register's finite energy loss at zero external work. The mixture remains below the optimal policy; individual updates can lower reward, and a different initial preparation lowers expected reward. This is adaptation toward an encoded objective. Preparing the register and implementing its readout in an actuator require additional accounting. Task reward and task heat are prospective expectations under the prepared lamp law; the learning clock executes no task episodes and supplies no spatial coupling convergence or cortical identification.

A second learning model supplies no values at all. Which of two actions is rewarded is an environmental bit that no channel of the learner takes as an argument: the register's update is a function of the observed outcome alone, and its readout, which fixes the action executed, is a function of the register alone. The world returns success with probability $3/4$ for the rewarding action and $1/4$ for the other; success leaves the register where it is and failure redraws it. Acting, observing and updating compose into one channel on the joint law of parameter and register, so reward, heat and entropy are read from the same process. From a uniform prior over both bits, Lean gives every mass of that law at every horizon: each agreeing pair carries $3/8-\frac18(1/2)^n$, so the expected reward of the action actually executed is $3/4-\frac14(1/2)^n$, strictly increasing from $1/2$ and always short of certainty. A frozen register and an uninformative world, in which success has probability $1/2$ whatever is done, both hold $1/2$ at zero cost, so the improvement is bought by the observations rather than by the update rule. Cumulative heat is $(\log3/4)[1-(1/2)^N]$, and the register's two states are energetically degenerate, so that heat is external work. It is irreducibly so: a register energy that made the updates free would force the drive's heat to be equal at both parameter values, and this drive's is not. The learner acquires no preference between the actions, each of which it executes half the time at every horizon, but a correlation with the parameter. Preparing the prior, supplying the work, executing further task episodes and fabricating the readout are separate accounting, and no cortical identification follows.

\subsection{What the composition establishes}

A continuing finite agent sequences actuation, observation-driven learning and
reset on one joint law of an unknown task parameter, a register and a task
flag. The register drives the actuator; the observation reads the flag that
action changed; the reset prepares its next use. Each stage starts from the
preceding output. For a supplied initial work store $B$, a common energy $E$
and thermal scale $\theta$, the first law and entropy balance telescope to
\begin{equation}
 \theta[H(p_0)-H(p_N)]\leq Q_{<N}
 =W_{<N}+\langle E\rangle_0-\langle E\rangle_N
 \leq B+\langle E\rangle_0-\langle E\rangle_N,
 \label{eq:continuing-budget}
\end{equation}
provided expected cumulative work stays within $B$ at every prefix and each
stage satisfies positive support and local detailed balance. A bit witness
retains agreement with the unknown rewarding action above chance after every
completed cycle, while every cycle draws a positive minimum of work, including
reset. Blinding the observations leaves chance performance and makes the
learning stage free. Replacing reset by idle drift preserves some learning but
reduces the next cycle's performance. A finite store therefore supports only
a bounded prefix of this protocol. This is accounting in expectation:
preparation of the initial law, microscopic battery dynamics, fabrication and
a separate sensor-memory implementation are supplied or outside the model.
Heat is assigned to the composite learning channel through local detailed
balance; no pathwise battery guarantee or cortical identification follows.

Table~\ref{tab:summary} lists the connecting hypotheses of two branches. The theorem \texttt{chain} uses passive predictive memory; \texttt{chain\_active} uses a specified finite feedback process. Each accepts eight implications and shares the passage from coarse-graining to the phase model and its proposed representational role. In both, the finite register and the field boundary are independent starting objects.

For the active branch, the controller has the register's state type and the fourth node records
\begin{equation}
 \theta>0,\qquad \theta[H(p)-H(p')]\leq\langle Q\rangle\leq B,
 \label{eq:active-chain-bound}
\end{equation}
where $p,p'$ and $\langle Q\rangle$ belong to that same controlled substep. E34Active supplies positive path probabilities, local detailed balance at the register's thermal scale $\theta$, and the allocation $\langle Q\rangle\leq B$, with $B$ equal to the register's heat. Landauer's lower bound does not imply this upper-budget allocation or identify erasure with actuation. A resource model of one register supplies it instead: every operation is controlled by that register and exchanges heat with its reservoir at $\theta$, and the register's dissipated heat is the total its operations deliver there. An operation that does not increase the joint entropy then has a nonnegative share, by the left inequality of Eq.~\eqref{eq:active-chain-bound} applied to it, so an operation whose companions all compress has a share no larger than the register's dissipation. That accounting identity and that compressiveness are the model's physical inputs; the allocation follows from them. Composing the operations into one evolving law with its preparation and power source is a separate model, and the register--bath model at the end of this subsection shows that a microscopic bipartite dynamics supplies neither input. E45Active requires convergence of the specified coupling energies. One scalar constitutive model connects those energies to the named feedback step: writing $d=H(p)-H(p')$, it sets the spatial density to $f_A(x)=g\,d\,b(x)$, with supplied gain $g\geq0$ and profile $b$. Regular spatial refinement gives
\begin{equation}
 E_n(f_A)\longrightarrow L_A=g\,d\int_S b\,d\mu,
 \qquad
 L_A\leq g\,\frac{B}{\theta}\int_S b\,d\mu
 \label{eq:actuated-coupling}
\end{equation}
when $\int_S b\,d\mu\geq0$. The limit follows from mesh assumptions; the upper bound uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:active-chain-bound} for the same process. The constitutive law uses ensemble entropy reduction and supplies neither a microscopic actuator nor its work account. Changing the gain or profile at fixed process and heat changes the coupling energies.

A second, microscopic model supplies an actuator and a kernel. The hardware carries finitely many spatial response modes with fixed profiles $\phi_i$ and installation prices $u_i$. A configuration $z$ of the controller and its switches sets the mode occupancies $c_i(z)$, and those same occupancies determine both a reciprocal kernel on the product space and a stored installation energy,
\begin{equation}
 K_z(x,y)=\sum_i c_i(z)\,\phi_i(x)\,\phi_i(y),
 \qquad
 U(z)=\sum_i u_i\,c_i(z).
 \label{eq:microscopic-kernel}
\end{equation}
A transition $z\to z'$ changes $K$ only through the modes whose occupancy it changes, so a pair outside the response of those modes is untouched, and the transition's external work is $U(z')-U(z)+Q$. The kernel the executed step installs is the average of $K_z$ over the law that step actually leaves; its change is the mean of the pathwise changes, and the mean of the transition works splits into the change in stored energy and the channel's heat. A finite measurable partition of the substrate with one sample per cell reconstructs the installed kernel throughout each cell pair, and the cell-mass weighted finite energy equals the product-measure integral of that reconstruction exactly. On a compact substrate with continuous profiles, a sample error tending to zero gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{E}_n\longrightarrow\iint K\,d\mu\,d\mu .
 \label{eq:kernel-limit}
\end{equation}
The refinement index here is spatial and is independent of the number of executed control updates; convergence in one is not convergence in the other. The same hardware discharges the passive edge from a declared predictive system's own joint law, and two declared systems with different dissipated work give the identical edge, so predictive efficiency selects neither the hardware nor the partition. Mode profiles, prices, occupancy readout, reservoir convention and substrate are declared hardware data, which no heat bound supplies. Fabricating that hardware, powering it beyond one prepared update, and identifying the substrate or the modes with a physical field remain separate requirements.

A third finite model examines the register's own bath. One register bit and one bath bit carry each of the four deterministic maps of the register as a permutation of the pair, acting on a bath prepared in its ground state: an injective map moves the register alone, and an erasure swaps the register's bit into the bath. With bath levels $0$ and $2\log2$, degenerate register levels, a uniform register and $\theta=1$, the bath's mean energy gain along the executed paths is $0$ for each injective map and $\log2$ for each erasure, and those values inhabit Landauer's heat equation with the bath supports the gates actually reach. For an arbitrary finite protocol and an explicitly modelled bath energy $E_B$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k<N}\langle\Delta E_B\rangle_k=\langle E_B\rangle_N-\langle E_B\rangle_0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{k<N}\langle q_k\rangle=\langle E_B\rangle_N-\langle E_B\rangle_0
   +\sum_{k<N}\langle q_k-\Delta E_B\rangle,
 \label{eq:bath-ledger}
\end{equation}
where the first identity needs no positive support and supplies no entropy inequality, and the second is the correction a reported heat ledger carries when it omits part of that transfer. Three consequences fence what a map-indexed budget can mean. Executing the same erasing gate twice returns the $\log2$ and restores the initial law, so the two shares are $\log2$ and $-\log2$ about a total of zero, and a reusable eraser requires its bath prepared again. A second reversible gate holds the register fixed and excites its bath, performing the same register map as idling at $2\log2$ instead of $0$; the work supplied is $\log2$ for one erasure, $2\log2$ for that drive and zero for two erasures. And a swap is an involution, so the gates' own transition log-ratios vanish on every realized path while their bath gains $\log2$: a deterministic reversible gate is not a strictly positive reduced channel, and local detailed balance at any thermal scale assigns it none of the heat its bath receives. Positivity, local detailed balance and the ledger identity are inputs of the resource model rather than consequences of microscopic reversibility. Preparing the bath, supplying the work, and identifying this bath with the reservoir of the ledger's operations remain separate; the numerical values are those of the declared energy gap and preparation.

\section{Discussion}

The strongest results concern what a coherence measurement leaves unresolved. Different recovery mechanisms share stationary distributions; equal phases permit incompatible local descriptions; and the tested plasticity objective sustains coherence while moving away from its diagnostic structure. These findings make the coupling hypothesis more demanding to test and prevent a successful synchronization model from doing unexplained representational work.

The proposed account identifies a compatible, internally represented state with a conscious episode. Its main explanatory gap is the mechanism that would maintain those relations: compatibility and accuracy at the glued state enter the construction as premises. Their mathematical consequences leave open why cortical activity should acquire or preserve them.

The most informative next step for that connection is a test of whether coordination helps maintain independently measured content agreement. After validating separate decoders and their overlap restrictions, a controlled change in coupling or coordination could test whether agreement deteriorates in the predicted way, with controls for changes in sensory information and decoder quality. Such a result would bear directly on the proposed role of coherence. In parallel, the awakening hypothesis needs its own calibrated geometry--coupling relation and resolved phase dynamics to distinguish a coupling increase from diffusion change or common drive. A favorable recovery trace alone would leave the representational connection open.

Testing the self clause requires perturbing a candidate encoding and comparing the resulting changes in represented scene, body and viewpoint with the readout's predictions. Establishing that functional role would make dissociations from unified experience informative. The exact fixed-state idealization must also be assessed against the fidelity and time dependence of the biological process.

Five components of the account are not derived here, and the formalization marks each. Coarse-graining the discrete couplings converges a scalar energy and produces no kernel: the vertex-sited candidate contributes exactly zero continuum coupling energy on an atomless substrate, which is a no-go for that architecture rather than for kernels, since cell pairs of positive mass do pass to a kernel limit. The field conversion $K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N s E f$ carries its physical content in $\gamma$, a hypothesis about how long field-induced timing shifts persist; the oscillation frequency, a phase relaxation rate and the diffusion constant are candidates that need not agree, and the remaining factors cannot choose between them. Descent of the squared-drift objective is proved for the specified flow at fixed phases, whereas the claim that such adaptation acquires a designated environmental structure is not proved and fails in the bounded study reported above. The non-identifiability result concerns stationary distributions, so it limits what a coherence trace establishes rather than what any measurement can establish; separating the mechanisms requires resolving dynamics. The thermodynamic results take a prepared initial law as given and leave the supply of free energy to a separate model. These are boundaries of scope and derivation. Reading any of them as a positive finding about cortex, or as support for scale separation, maladaptive learning or a limit on observation in general, would overstate what has been checked.

Two of these results bear on formulations that read a generative model off a system's steady state, as the free-energy principle does \cite{friston2010}. Nothing here refutes that principle on its own terms: the squared-drift functional is not a variational free energy, it is not identified with entropy production, and no bridge between them is claimed or needed for any theorem above. What transfers is narrower. The stationary distributions of increasing coupling, decreasing diffusion and a matched common drive coincide, so a steady-state density does not by itself determine which mechanism produced it, and here the competing mechanisms are written down rather than supposed. Separately, local descriptions agreeing pairwise on their overlaps need not determine one global state, and at every positive tolerance a three-patch family admits no global section at all; a decomposition into local descriptions therefore does not by itself deliver one belief about one world. Both points instantiate identifiability concerns already raised about that principle \cite{aguilera2022,biehl2021,bruineberg2022}, in a model where the alternatives are explicit. Neither establishes that cortex fails to perform inference, and the plasticity control above bears on the objective it names and on no other.

Integrated-information, global-workspace and predictive-processing accounts organize unity, access and modelling differently \cite{tononi2016,baars1997,dehaene2014,friston2010}. The present proposal contributes a candidate interaction mechanism and explicit relations to test between local content and internal self-encoding. Its explanatory value will turn on whether those relations can be identified independently and shown to matter for experience. For the cup example, that means explaining how the system maintains one represented situation and how the perceiver's place within it contributes to the episode.
