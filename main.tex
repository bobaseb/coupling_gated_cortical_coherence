\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
\small
Collective phase order can coordinate distributed cortex, but does not establish shared content or self-representation. We distinguish coherence, compatibility and accurate reconstruction. Shared content requires independently identified quantities on overlapping regions; compatible local descriptions determine a unique global state. A contraction fixes that state uniquely if it reconstructs the state exactly; fidelity across other states separately requires discriminating codes. Counterexamples show why a phase resultant and fixed-point uniqueness cannot replace these measurements. Lean~4 verifies the conditional implications and a model jointly satisfies their eight connecting hypotheses. This establishes mathematical consistency, not cortical realization. A declared actuator's stored mode energy bounds mean coupling and yields a necessary condition for a coherent stationary branch; cortical mode prices remain unknown. We propose a held-out comparison of field-score and structural-connectivity onset models during anaesthetic emergence. A predictive advantage would support the specified spatial model; causal field identification requires intervention. Unity and a minimal self remain interpretations without an independent measure of experience.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Endogenous extracellular fields can affect spike timing and may coordinate
activity across cortical regions \cite{mcfadden2002,mcfadden2020,pockett2012,frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}.
Coordination raises a further question: when do those regions describe one
situation, and when does the resulting state represent its own place in it?

Consider reaching for a cup. Vision and movement can describe different
features while agreeing on the cup's location. Matching their timing leaves
agreement about that shared quantity untested. An internal description of the
perceiver's relation to the cup asks for still more. We call the three
requirements \emph{coherence} (collective phase order), \emph{compatibility}
(agreement of local descriptions on overlaps), and \emph{self-representation}
(accurate reconstruction from an internal encoding). The mathematical states
are spatial measures and fields; identifying them with perceptual content
requires independent decoding evidence.

The familiar noisy mean-field oscillator supplies a stationary coherence
threshold \cite{sakaguchi1988,strogatz1991}. We add a resource condition for a
declared actuator, bounds that connect phase order to content only through
specified encoders, and conditions under which compatible descriptions glue
and an accurate reconstruction fixes the glued state. A Lean~4 development
checks these implications and a joint witness satisfies their eight connecting
hypotheses in a specified model. It proves neither that cortex satisfies those
hypotheses nor that the glued state is experienced. The latter identification
motivates the framework and needs its own endpoint.

The empirical question is which measurements could support the proposed
connections. A phase resultant can miss a locally ordered winding field;
stationary phase distributions can leave the coupling mechanism unidentified;
and coherent adaptation can lose alignment with a designated content
template. Independent decoders must identify shared quantities before their
agreement can be tested. We therefore distinguish stationary branches from
finite observed onset, and compare prespecified field and connectivity onset
models on held-out emergence recordings. The comparison tests a specified
spatial prediction, not the presence of experience.

Sections~\ref{sec:unconditional}--\ref{sec:self} develop the three requirements;
Section~\ref{sec:gpu} applies their measurement obligations to a digital
candidate. Section~\ref{sec:chain} states the resource and composition premises,
and Sections~\ref{sec:observations}--\ref{sec:prediction} examine observables and
the cortical test. Table~\ref{tab:summary} lists the eight connecting
hypotheses; Supplemental Material gives their derivations and controls.

\section{Limits of phase order}
\label{sec:unconditional}

Several limits hold without the cortical hypothesis. Equal phases can coexist
with incompatible local descriptions; phase order constrains shared content
only through encoders related on overlaps. A winding field can have zero global
resultant while remaining locally ordered. A constant readout can have a unique
fixed point without reconstructing its input. A region that does not distinguish
relevant states cannot encode them, and a site cannot report a change outside
its causal past by a declared deadline. These limits reflect familiar phase
oscillator and information constraints \cite{acebron2005,ott2008,dorfler2014,breakspear2017}.
The sections below state each one beside the condition or measurement it
motivates.
\section{Coherence: physical coupling and its resource condition}
\label{sec:field}

\subsection{Dynamics and the stationary threshold}

The finite stochastic model is
\begin{equation}
 d\theta_i=\left[\omega_i+\sum_j K_{ij}(t)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)\right]dt
 +\sqrt{2D}\,dW_i,
 \qquad
 r_N=\left|\frac1N\sum_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|.
 \label{eq:kuramoto}
\end{equation}
Here $\theta_i$ is the phase of oscillator $i$, $\omega_i$ its natural frequency, $D>0$ is phase diffusion, $W_i$ are independent Wiener processes, and $K_{ij}$ has inverse-time units, with angles dimensionless. Uniform mean-field coupling is $K_{ij}=K/N$; frequency heterogeneity and diffusion are distinct parameters. The stationary calculation below uses identical frequencies and this scalar coupling. Applying it to a spatial kernel requires an aggregation rule and a check of the resulting approximation.

For identical frequencies in their rotating frame, the positive normalized stationary densities of the one-body Fokker--Planck equation are exactly
\begin{equation}
 \rho(\theta)=\frac{e^{a\cos(\theta-\psi)}}{2\pi I_0(a)},
 \qquad a=\frac{Kr}{D},\qquad
 r=R(a):=\frac{I_1(a)}{I_0(a)},
 \label{eq:selfconsistency}
\end{equation}
where $I_n$ are modified Bessel functions and $\psi$ is the collective phase. The formal derivation uses the absence of stationary current for a periodic gradient drift to obtain this density, then verifies normalization and self-consistency. At $K\leq K_c=2D$, only $r=0$ is self-consistent \cite{sakaguchi1988,strogatz1991}. Above threshold, exactly one positive solution coexists with zero and increases strictly with $K$. Its onset is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^2}{K-K_c}\longrightarrow\frac1D,
 \qquad r\sim\sqrt{\frac{K-K_c}{D}}.
 \label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:bifurcation} shows this stationary branch.

Linearization about the uniform density gives rate $K/2-D$ for the first harmonic and $-Dn^2$ for harmonics $n\geq2$. A growing mode therefore exists exactly above threshold. About the coherent density, the quadratic form is strictly dissipative transverse to collective rotation, whose rate is zero. These linear estimates concern the limiting equation. Relating them to finite stochastic trajectories requires a dynamical mean-field limit and branch selection; exchange symmetry alone does not establish the needed independence \cite{sznitman1991}.

\subsection{Installed energy bounds mean coupling}
\label{sec:installation}

A spatial model replaces the finite sum by integration against a kernel. One declared implementation carries response modes with continuous profiles $\phi_i$, installation prices $u_i$ and nonnegative occupancies $c_i(\Xi)$ in configuration $\Xi$:
\begin{equation}
 K_\Xi(x,y)=\sum_i c_i(\Xi)\phi_i(x)\phi_i(y),
 \qquad U(\Xi)=\sum_i u_i c_i(\Xi).
 \label{eq:microscopic-kernel}
\end{equation}
The same configuration sets the reciprocal kernel and stored energy. An executed transition changes the kernel through its changed occupancies and requires work $U(\Xi')-U(\Xi)+Q$, with $Q$ positive into the reservoir. The installed kernel is averaged over the resulting configuration law $p(\Xi)$. Cell-pair reconstruction on refining spatial partitions converges to its product-measure integral under the specified compactness and sampling assumptions; the refinement index is independent of the number of control updates.

Normalize the substrate measure $\mu$ to one. Write $m_i=\int\phi_i\,d\mu$ and declare $\kappa>0$ with $m_i^2\leq\kappa u_i$ for every mode. Factorizing each mode's integral gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 U_{\mathrm{inst}}&=\sum_\Xi p(\Xi)U(\Xi),\\
 K_{\mathrm{mf}}&=\iint K(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)
 =\sum_\Xi p(\Xi)\sum_i c_i(\Xi)m_i^2
 \leq\kappa U_{\mathrm{inst}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:installed-coupling-bound}
\end{equation}
If this mean coupling is the $K$ of the scalar model, $\kappa U_{\mathrm{inst}}\leq2D$ excludes a coherent stationary branch and any growing Fourier mode about the uniform density. Thus coherence on that branch requires $U_{\mathrm{inst}}>2D/\kappa$. The condition is necessary: energy above it need not produce sufficient coupling because the bound can be loose.

The theorem concerns stored energy in the declared actuator modes. The estimated cortical signalling expenditure and extracellular electrostatic energy in Supplemental Material measure different quantities; neither identifies the occupancies, mode prices or their relation to metabolic supply. Consequently the bound supplies a necessary condition for a declared actuator, but no cortical value of $\kappa$ or demonstration that cortex meets it. Applying the scalar threshold to heterogeneous or signed spatial dynamics also needs a justified reduction.

\section{Compatibility: from coordinated activity to a global description}
\label{sec:unity}

\subsection{Why common timing is insufficient}

For overlapping patches $U$ and $V$, compatibility means that restricting either local description to $U\cap V$ gives the same object. Descriptions can agree on shared quantities while contributing different information elsewhere. Compatible sections of the specified sheaf have a unique global extension. On a finite cover, local finite spatial measures agreeing as measures on every overlap also have a unique finite-measure extension whose sheaf image is the glued section. No mass-one normalization is imposed.

In the cup example, visual and reaching descriptions must express a shared quantity, such as cup location, in common coordinates. Restriction retains that quantity while dropping details particular to each description. Agreement permits surface appearance and hand configuration to contribute to one situation. The mathematical counterexample assigns the same phase to every patch while two local mass profiles disagree on a shared site. Those profiles have no common extension. Because they are chosen independently of phase, synchrony places no constraint on their disagreement.

This distinction limits the biological interpretation. Receptive territories and content domains must be related to spatial patches, and the descriptions must support the required extension. Matching overlap marginals of decoded probability laws need not determine a unique global joint distribution \cite{abramsky2011}.

The cover is itself a choice, and the construction constrains it in one way only: synchronized patches agree where they overlap. Where they do not overlap there is nothing to compare, so a cover by pairwise disjoint patches satisfies that obligation for \emph{every} assignment of local descriptions and phases and the gluing theorem still returns a unique global section, which is the tuple it was handed. Membership of the class then reports nothing about the system beyond the patches covering it. What a gluing claim asks of a cover is therefore a non-degeneracy the theorem does not assume: that distinct patches meet, and that what they share is a quantity fixed independently of the agreement it is being used to establish.

\subsection{When coherence does constrain content}
\label{sec:content-connection}

A declared relation between phase and content gives a positive result. Let contents take values in a real normed vector space $\mathcal{C}$. Patch $i$ reads an assigned oscillator through $e_{i,x}$ at site $x$, evaluated on the circle point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. Assume a uniform Lipschitz constant $L$ in chord distance and require $e_{i,x}=e_{j,x}$ wherever patches $i$ and $j$ overlap. Encoders may vary across sites; their equality on overlaps is a supplied relation between descriptions of the shared quantity. The order-parameter identity
$N^2r^2=\sum_{ij}\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)$ implies
$1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)\leq N^2(1-r^2)$ for each pair. Consequently every overlap discrepancy is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \delta_r=L\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^2}.
 \label{eq:main-content-coherence}
\end{equation}
Here discrepancy is the norm of the difference, and $r$ and $N$ are read on whichever population the bound is applied to. That choice is what decides whether the estimate says anything. A patch carries an order parameter of its own, and reading the bound there carries that patch's resultant and that patch's site count, so a large population observed through small patches is constrained by the small count. Read instead on the whole array, the factor $N$ makes the estimate weak, because it draws a pointwise guarantee from an average over $N^2$ pairs. At $r=1$ the observations agree exactly at either scale. A shared scalar encoder is a special case. Dropping the overlap relation permits disagreement even at perfect locking. A common nonexpansive linear projection preserves agreement, but agreement of a projection does not establish agreement of the full vector.

What Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-content-coherence} bounds is a chord, which never exceeds $2$, so the estimate constrains anything only where the right-hand side falls below that. At the sheet's own patch order, the bound is informative for at most \wavePatchInformativeSites{} sites; beyond that, the coherence-to-content link requires the bridge assumption (E78) of the cortical hypothesis. Supplemental Material provides the detailed derivation of this saturation, and states three conditional derivations that replace the uniform factor $N$ by a count of disagreeing pairs, a chain across a cover's shared sites, or a spectral gap at a frequency-locked configuration.

Mix retained fields with observations at gain $\eta\in[0,1]$:
$s_i\mapsto(1-\eta)s_i+\eta o_i$.
Content and observation discrepancies bounded by $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ give a next discrepancy at most $(1-\eta)\varepsilon+\eta\delta$. Repeated observations bounded by $\delta$ therefore give
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_n\leq(1-\eta)^n\varepsilon_0+\delta.
 \label{eq:main-content-retention}
\end{equation}
Thus agreeing observations preserve exact compatibility and, for $\eta>0$, reduce an initial mismatch. Approximate observations leave a nondecaying term in the error bound. Taking a uniform bound on Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-content-coherence} connects this update to the phase process.

\subsection{What approximate agreement determines}

Exact gluing requires exact restrictions. For nonnegative profiles on a finite substrate, suppose every overlap discrepancy is at most $\varepsilon$ in uniform distance. Weights that vanish outside their patches and sum to one at each site select a global profile by weighted averaging. The selection lies within $\varepsilon$ of each patch there, and any two such selections differ by at most $\varepsilon$, independently of cover multiplicity. An $L$-Lipschitz readout consequently varies by at most $L\varepsilon$ across selections.

At zero tolerance, the selection is the unique exact extension. At every positive tolerance there are families with no exact extension; selection then gives an approximation whose dependence on weights is bounded. Supplemental Material gives a three-site witness attaining the bound. The mass-profile construction uses the scalar case of the content dynamics and additionally requires nonnegativity of the initial profiles and observations. The general vector result supplies no identification with the spatial-measure sheaf.

These constructions concern declared objects: a specified cover, contents valued in a normed space, and encoders related on overlaps. They select neither the neural cover nor the decoder, and identifying their contents with perceptual content, or establishing that the identification bears on experience, is a further modelling task; Section~\ref{sec:observations} sets out what a measurement of it would have to establish.

\section{Self-representation: a state that models itself}
\label{sec:self}

\subsection{The reflexive construction}

Compatibility gives a global state. Reflexivity relates it to an encoding within that state. Equip global sections with a complete metric. If the additional restriction-resonance condition identifies encoding with restriction to a region $\mathcal{A}$, the reconstruction map is
\begin{equation}
 F(s)=\operatorname{readout}(s|_{\mathcal{A}}).
 \label{eq:selfmap}
\end{equation}
The region and readout are fixed across the state space. If $F$ contracts and fixes the section $s_*$ formed from the local descriptions, Banach's theorem makes $s_*$ its unique fixed point \cite{banach1922}. The formal bridge E89 supplies the contraction and $F(s_*)=s_*$; restriction resonance is separate. Supercritical coupling makes the stipulated contraction factor $\Lambda=e^{-(K-2D)\tau/2}$ less than one for a relaxation time $\tau>0$. Constructing a map with that factor remains a modelling obligation.

In the cup example, the global state includes the represented scene and the perceiver's relation to it. A local encoding must retain enough information for the readout to recover that modelled state; cup position alone would be insufficient if hand configuration and viewpoint could not be reconstructed.

For the same map with Lipschitz factor $\Lambda<1$, approximate reconstruction gives
\begin{equation}
 d(s,F(s))\leq\varepsilon
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d(s,s_*)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\Lambda}.
 \label{eq:approximate-self}
\end{equation}
At the stipulated rate this radius grows as $2\varepsilon/((K-2D)\tau)$ near threshold, for fixed positive $\varepsilon$ and $\tau$. Exact uniqueness survives while the error guarantee weakens.

\subsection{Reconstruction across a family of states}
\label{sec:reconstruction-family}

Sensitivity can be required quantitatively. Declare relevant macrostates, an encoder $\Phi$ and readout $R$, and require $d(s,R(\Phi(s)))\leq\varepsilon$ throughout that family. If two states have the same code, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(s)=\Phi(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \max\{d(s,R(\Phi(s))),d(t,R(\Phi(t)))\}\geq\tfrac12 d(s,t).
 \label{eq:reconstruction-separation}
\end{equation}
Thus $M$ relevant states pairwise more than $2\varepsilon$ apart require at least $M$ distinguishable codes; an alphabet of at most $2^b$ codes requires $M\leq2^b$. Applied to the reflexive boundary, $E$ is its local encoding and $R\circ E$ is the existing self-prediction map.

This is an additional accuracy criterion on a family, with no requirement for multiple exact fixed points. A blind encoder or constant readout fails it for separated states despite having a unique fixed point.

Where the encoding is read as the state's own restriction to a region, the criterion becomes a condition on the region. Restriction resonance requires the encoder and that restriction to identify the same pairs of states, so a boundary reconstructing two relevant states more than $2\varepsilon$ apart while its region reads them identically is not resonant. Read forwards, a resonant boundary that reconstructs a pairwise separated family restricts injectively on it. Which states a region reads identically is fixed by where the region sits, before an encoding is chosen, so the resolution the family demands is the region's and no readout supplies it. The implication runs one way: a region that does resolve the family is not thereby shown to carry a resonant encoding, and E89 supplies no such resolution.

Those two demands are jointly satisfiable with nothing folded anywhere. Take the region to be the whole substrate: restriction is then the identity, so the boundary is resonant, reconstructs every declarable family exactly, and restricts injectively on all of them. A properness condition on the region is therefore a separate requirement rather than a consequence of resonance and resolution, and what a fold into a local region is asked for. Its quantitative form replaces the alphabet by a metric. With an $L$-Lipschitz readout, a family reconstructed within $\varepsilon$ whose members lie pairwise more than $d_{\min}$ apart has $\delta$-separated codes whenever $\delta L\leq d_{\min}-2\varepsilon$, so the family is no larger than the $\delta$-packing number of what the region carries. That is the shape a continuous family of states needs, and at the whole substrate it says nothing, because the region's range is everything. The gain $L$ is declared and derived here for no physical readout, and at unbounded gain the bound says nothing.

\subsection{Agreement after a change takes communication}

Snapshot compatibility does not specify how quickly descriptions respond when a shared quantity changes. In a synchronous network whose updates read only the site's own state and declared neighbours' messages, two initial configurations agreeing on a site's causal past through $T$ rounds produce the same state there at round $T$. The deadline consequence is the one Section~\ref{sec:unconditional} states, and it follows from the reconstruction bound in Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction-family}: a report from that site is a readout of a code that the two interventions leave identical.

A three-site delay line witnesses failure after one round and exact reporting after two; cutting the path prevents delivery at every round. A report may still happen to be correct about one fixed world. The bound concerns responsiveness across interventions.

Throughout this section \emph{prediction} names a reconstruction map, and the relevant family of states, the metric, the tolerance, the communication graph and the deadline are all declared inputs, as are the represented variables and the fidelity counted as accurate. Reading the map as temporal neural prediction would require dynamics; reading the resulting code count as physical memory capacity would require a hardware model, which a state-dependent readout would have to extend.

\section{What the conditions constrain in a digital candidate}
\label{sec:gpu}

The results of Section~\ref{sec:unconditional} hold for any candidate, and for a large language model (LLM) running on graphics processing units (GPUs) four of them are checkable rather than merely stateable. Those four are where this framework bears on claims made for such systems. It does not exclude them, and no result here could; what it does is fix what a claim of unity or self-representation commits its maker to measuring.

\paragraph{A quantitative bound for a linear bottleneck.}
Let a finite spatial coupling operator $K$ have orthonormal eigenmodes
$e_i$ with $|\lambda_1|\geq\cdots\geq|\lambda_N|$. Every linear
approximation $A$ of rank at most $d$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i=1}^{N}\|Ke_i-Ae_i\|^2\geq\sum_{i=d+1}^{N}\lambda_i^2.
 \label{eq:spectral-bottleneck}
\end{equation}
Retaining the first $d$ modes attains equality. A linear encoder and readout
factoring through $d$ real coordinates therefore pay this error. Applying the
bound requires a spectrum for the target and a rank constraint on the actual
approximation. Query/key dimension alone supplies no such constraint on
softmax attention: even a two-position rank-one logit matrix can yield rank-two
attention. The result prices an explicitly linear bottleneck in a finite
representation of a spatial kernel; it gives no universal gap between
transformers and continuous fields. Supplemental Material derives the bound
and checks the softmax counterexample.

A fixed synthetic task makes the scope visible. Two local views share scene
and self coordinates; the target is the global state and a self-relative
coordinate. On the parity-held-out family, a rank-two linear decoder's
input-weighted predicted training-error floor is \gParityRankTwoFloor{}, and
its held-out error is \gParityRankTwoHeldout{}. Equal numerical values do
not make the training-law floor a held-out guarantee. On a separate
scene-family shift, the fitted full linear decoder has training error
\gSceneFullTrainError{} and held-out error \gSceneFullHeldoutError{}.
The task's declared target map reconstructs the noiseless held-out inputs
with error \gSceneDeclaredMapHeldout{}; the restricted training family leaves
one direction unidentified. A small causal attention decoder and a coupled
phase code also lose held-out accuracy on that shift under their stated CPU
fits; neither is governed by the linear rank floor. The measured codes,
overlap perturbations, self-state interventions and communication deadlines
are described in Supplemental Material. These task results rank no
architecture family and supply no hardware-energy or experiential claim.

\paragraph{Codes, not reports.} Reconstruction across a declared family requires the encoder to distinguish states more than $2\varepsilon$ apart when the target error is $\varepsilon$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:reconstruction-separation}). Verbal reports alone do not establish that condition. For a proposed self-model, declare the family and fidelity, identify the internal code under intervention, then test whether it separates the family. A token sequence is one possible code; an activation region is another and requires its own measurement.

The same interface has an information ceiling. For any finite joint law of
an internal state $X$ and a length-$k$ token sequence $Y$ over vocabulary $V$,
$I(X;Y)\leq H(Y)\leq k\log_2|V|$ bits. The proof uses the full joint law and
allows dependent emissions and zero-probability outcomes. A binary copy attains
one bit per emitted binary symbol; an independent output conveys zero.
This counts the declared output sequence, so a cache, timing signal or other
accessible state must be included separately when it forms part of the channel.

\paragraph{The region, not the readout.} If the code is a restriction of the candidate's state to a region, that region must distinguish the relevant family before a readout is chosen. In one forward pass of a decoder-only transformer, the causal mask restricts position $t$ to positions at or before $t$; an intervention outside that causal past cannot be reconstructed from its activation alone. Across passes, prior output adds a separate channel. The bounded-fan-in communication result of Section~\ref{sec:self} applies to a declared physical interconnect and deadline; a full-support attention graph does not satisfy its sparse-neighbourhood premise. Supplemental Material gives the graph bounds and their hardware scope.

\paragraph{Agreement about what.} Unity requires the candidate's local descriptions to agree on independently identified shared quantities (Section~\ref{sec:unity}). Common timing, a shared residual stream and fluent output supply none of that, for the reason Section~\ref{sec:unconditional} gives: coordination bounds content disagreement only through encoders already related on overlaps. Which quantities two parts of a model describe in common is a decoding question, carrying the failure modes of Section~\ref{sec:observations}. The cover is a choice, and choosing it to secure agreement empties the claim.

\paragraph{Physical scope.} Digital representations can satisfy compatibility and reconstruction. The coupling-gated route additionally requires a calibrated physical coupling, its noise regime and an independently identified relation from phase order to shared content. An attention matrix or a shared clock supplies none of these; the cortical proposal carries the same physical obligations. Finite-resolution capacity depends on code-space volume, resolution and readout gain, so continuity alone favours no substrate. Supplemental Material gives the capacity and hardware-support comparisons. Neither the installed-energy condition nor those comparisons exclude a digital realization.

The conditions impose measurements on a digital candidate but settle no question of machine experience. Supplemental Material places this comparison beside biological naturalism, substrate-dependent computation and current proposals about LLM organization.

\section{Thermodynamics and composition}
\label{sec:chain}

The phase and representation results concern coupling, content disagreement and reconstruction error. A finite change in phase order has a probability-current cost bound under the conditions below. Thermodynamic heat requires a separate physical identification of the current and reservoir. The installed-energy condition of Section~\ref{sec:installation} is necessary for its declared actuator; whether it restricts a physical candidate depends on measured mode prices, coupling reduction and diffusion. No composition edge derives coherence, compatibility or reconstruction from a heat bound. Finite-register capacity obeys $H(p)\leq\log|X|$; erasure heat depends on specified system--bath assumptions \cite{landauer1961,norton2005,shenker2000}. A separate boundary result concerns a field reaching disconnected vacuum components \cite{kibble1976}. Table~\ref{tab:summary} states the connecting assumptions.

\subsection{Changing order carries a probability-current cost}

For a smooth positive normalized periodic phase density $\rho$ satisfying
$\partial_t\rho=-\partial_\theta J$, with diffusion $D>0$, define
$\sigma_J=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}J^2/(D\rho)\,d\theta$ and
$r=|\int e^{i\theta}\rho\,d\theta|$. Differentiation under the integral
gives $\dot r^2\leq D\sigma_J(1-r^2)$ wherever $0<r<1$. A smooth path
staying in that interval therefore obeys
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^\tau\sigma_J\,dt\geq
 \frac{[\arcsin r(\tau)-\arcsin r(0)]^2}{D\tau}.
 \label{eq:order-change-cost}
\end{equation}
Endpoint order, duration, diffusion and the probability current determine
this floor. In an exploratory finite-volume onset calculation, a threshold
chosen after inspecting pilot runs gives current cost
\gOnsetCurrentCost{} versus a floor of \gOnsetCurrentFloor{}. These numbers
describe the discretized model, not thermal or metabolic power.

The current $J=v\rho-D\partial_\theta\rho$ also satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}J\,d\theta\right)^2\leq D\sigma_J.
 \label{eq:current-cost}
\end{equation}
A uniform density under constant drive $\omega$ attains
$\sigma_J=\omega^2/D$. The identical-frequency gradient model has positive
self-consistent stationary order above $K=2D$ with $J=0$ and
$\sigma_J=0$. Stationary order therefore has no positive current-cost floor.
Nor does the bound price an externally chosen $\dot K$: in the
identical-frequency zero-drive model, the uniform density remains an exact
zero-current solution under any coupling schedule.
A cortical power claim needs the physical current, reservoir and thermal
conversion to be identified. This current functional is distinct from the
substrate squared-drift objective used in the plasticity controls.

\subsection{Feedback changes the information constraint}

For passive memory $X_t$ driven by an environmental signal $S_t$, the predictive bound is
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm{diss}}\geq k_BT\left[I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})\right].
 \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
The Markov structure $X_t\to S_t\to S_{t+1}$ makes the bracket nonnegative \cite{still2012}. Zero retained information also satisfies it, so a task and a learning rule require additional specification. Relating this bound to Landauer heat requires a common thermal scale and budget, and memory waste bounded by erased entropy.

An acting agent changes the signal it will receive. Specify policy, world, observation and memory-update channels. For their finite laws,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})
 ={}&I(X_t;S_t\mid S_{t+1})\\
 &-I(X_t;S_{t+1}\mid S_t).
\end{split}
\label{eq:feedback-information}
\end{equation}
The second conditional term vanishes in the passive model. With feedback, the signed difference can be negative as action creates correlations with the future. A causal reading requires an environmental state sufficient to describe the relevant dynamics.

For a controlled substep, the active thermodynamic bound instead uses the joint entropy. Forward and reverse path laws give
$\Sigma=D_{KL}(P_F\|P_R)=\Delta H(X,S)+\langle Q\rangle/(k_BT)\geq0$
under local detailed balance, with thermal scale $k_BT>0$ and heat positive into the reservoir \cite{horowitz2014,ito2013}. Every forward path of positive probability must have a positive-probability reverse for this finite identity. A missing reverse gives infinite divergence. The supplement develops the support conditions and composes actuation with memory updates on their shared intermediate law.

\subsection{Learning and repeated operation need explicit resources}

The finite models in Supplemental Material separate policy selection, learning
from a phase-derived sensor and repeated operation. A binary closed-loop
witness lets a register install the phase energy landscape, lets the phase
evolve under a bath-supported channel, senses the new phase and updates that
same register. A fixed decoder trained on one family predicts the next phase
on a held-out family with accuracy \gFeedbackInformedAccuracy{} under an
informative sensor, versus \gFeedbackBlindAccuracy{} under a matched blind
sensor. Their marginal phase order is the same. The executed law also charges
installation, sensing, reset and preparation against a finite work store;
phase-flip bath heat is recorded separately. The symbol task and mode prices
are supplied toy inputs, so this does not identify a cortical mechanism or a
heat cost for the full sensing and reset protocol.

For a complete cycle with a common energy observable $\mathcal{E}$ and
thermal scale $k_BT$, positive forward and reverse path support, and local
detailed balance at each stage, the conditional joint-law budget is
\begin{equation}
 k_BT[H(p_0)-H(p_N)]\leq Q_{<N}
 =W_{<N}+\langle \mathcal{E}\rangle_0-\langle \mathcal{E}\rangle_N
 \leq B+\langle \mathcal{E}\rangle_0-\langle \mathcal{E}\rangle_N,
 \label{eq:continuing-budget}
\end{equation}
when expected cumulative work stays within the supplied allowance $B$ at every prefix. This bounds expectations. The numerical witness specifies a bath for the phase transition; its sensing, reset and preparation prices do not establish reverse protocols or heat for the complete cycle. Separate constructions track a store on each trajectory and specify when a mean allowance represents an available resource; an expected budget alone permits overdrawn paths.

For the declared process, a finite source with positive net cost funds finitely
many cycles. A common physical ledger for the sensing and reset channels, and
a cortical calibration of every mode price, remain separate obligations.

\subsection{What the composition establishes}

The passive and feedback composition theorems each accept eight connecting implications (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). In the active branch, a named substep at the register's thermal scale satisfies
\begin{equation}
 k_BT>0,\qquad k_BT[H(p)-H(p')]\leq\langle Q\rangle\leq B,
 \label{eq:active-chain-bound}
\end{equation}
with $B$ the register's heat. The upper allocation follows from a ledger whose operations deliver that total heat to one reservoir and whose other operations do not increase joint entropy. Those ledger assumptions are physical inputs. Neither Landauer's lower bound nor microscopic reversibility supplies them. Finite two-bit witnesses exhibit positive forward and reverse elementary paths, local detailed balance at one thermal scale, and interaction energies of order $k_BT$; these conditions alone do not quantify proximity to equilibrium. Cortex has not been shown to satisfy these premises. \section{What the observations establish}
\label{sec:observations}

The composition holds under its premises. Whether observation can establish those premises is a separate question, and the three requirements it connects concern different objects: phase dynamics, local descriptions and internal encodings. Their observables must retain these distinctions. The following controls show which mechanisms remain indistinguishable, which temporal approximations fail, which states one observable cannot separate, and what independent content measurements must establish. Each is a finite numerical regime on a declared model, with full parameters and controls in Supplemental Material, and none of them calibrates a cortical quantity.

\subsection{Stationary distributions leave the mechanism undetermined}
\label{sec:identifiability}

Equation~\eqref{eq:selfconsistency} gives a concentration--coherence relation with no fitted shape parameter. Estimate $r$ from circular resultant length and $a$ from density shape: inverting $R$ from the measured $r$ would make agreement automatic. Even independently estimated agreement identifies a statistical family shared by different mechanisms.

Let $q(t)$ cross $2$, let $r(q)$ be its stationary branch, and fix $D_0>0$. Increasing coupling $K(t)=D_0q(t)$ at diffusion $D_0$ and decreasing diffusion $D(t)=K_0/q(t)$ at coupling $K_0$ both give concentration $a=q(t)r(q(t))$. Uncoupled oscillators with prescribed drift $-h(t)\sin(\theta-\psi)$, where $h(t)=D_0q(t)r(q(t))$, and diffusion $D_0$ have that same density. Their stationary distributions coincide window by window despite different causes.

Resolved dynamics supply additional information. In the ideal model with continuous paths, quadratic variation accumulates at rate $2D$ and conditional increment means constrain drift. Separating endogenous coupling from a matched common drive additionally requires a measured drive or controlled perturbation; identifying electromagnetic coupling requires evidence specific to that interaction. Supplemental Material gives the equivalence construction and its numerical check.

\subsection{Finite ramps change the observable onset}
\label{sec:ramp}

The square-root law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:onset} describes stationary solutions. A changing coupling requires time for the phase distribution to approach those solutions. In a deterministic mean-field control that crosses the threshold symmetrically, none of the scanned rates meets the declared tracking tolerance: slowing the crossing does not remove the terminal residual under that initialization. A slow control variable therefore supplies no general guarantee of branch tracking at onset. Supplemental Material gives the residual, rate range and admissible rates away from threshold.

The finite-population ramp study uses $N=2000$, $D=1$ and 32 replicas at $\rampDelaySpeedCount$ coupling speeds from $v=\rampDelaySpeedFastest$ to $v=\rampDelaySpeedSlowest$. Escape is the first of three consecutive saved samples at $r\geq0.2$ after threshold. Faster ramps delay escape (Fig.~\ref{fig:ramp}); only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas escape within the fastest window. The $\rampDelayUncensoredCount$ uncensored legs supply the delay scaling below; the decade-spaced ones among them have mean coupling delays $\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$. Fitted onset exponents differ from those of the exact stationary branch evaluated on the same grids. At the fastest speed the ensemble mean never reaches the fit window, so its fitted slope does not estimate stationary onset.

Delayed passage through a noisy bifurcation \cite{berglund2002} makes the apparent exponent depend on ramp speed and fitting window. The specified deviation-based critical ramp speed is not bracketed by this sweep. With fluctuating coupling, the relevant stationary comparison also depends on rate: the mean coupling approximates order in the tested fast regime, while averaging stationary order over coupling works for the tested slow drives confined to one side of threshold. Slow crossing drives can satisfy neither approximation.

\paragraph{The prediction.} Over the $\rampDelayUncensoredCount$ uncensored legs a finite-range fit gives $\Delta K\propto v^{\rampDelayExponent}$. The interval from fitting those means is $[\rampDelayExponentLow,\rampDelayExponentHigh]$; resampling replicas independently within each speed gives a $95\%$ interval $[\rampBootstrapLow,\rampBootstrapHigh]$, with fraction $\rampBootstrapPAbove$ of $\rampBootstrapCount$ bootstrap fits at or above $1/2$. This fraction describes the resampling distribution under the fitted protocol; it is not a test of the ideal exponent. A deterministic mean-field run seeded at the fluctuation floor gives exponent $\quasistaticDelayExponent$ with the same estimator and escape criterion; tightening its criterion to $r\geq\quasistaticEscapeTight$ gives $\quasistaticEscapeTightExponent$. These controls show that the criterion affects the fitted exponent, but do not prove that it accounts for the entire finite-population shortfall. The larger-population low-threshold fit has substantial log residuals; matched-speed fits do not converge toward one-half across the tested sizes and criteria. Some trajectories also cross the absolute escape level before $K_c$, so the first postcritical crossing can yield a zero or grid-limited delay. This finite sweep establishes no common asymptotic exponent.

The ideal delayed-onset calculation motivates a square-root scaling hypothesis; its exponent is generic to suitable bifurcations and does not identify the coupling medium. A within-subject emergence design can vary the propofol taper and test how an operational onset changes with that manipulation, but interpreting the result as a coupling-rate law requires a measured or calibrated relation between taper and $\dot K$. The escape criterion must be set relative to each recording's pre-threshold fluctuation scale. Section~\ref{sec:identifiability} describes how $D$ could be estimated from resolved phase traces.

\subsection{A winding state leaves the order parameter ambiguous}
\label{sec:wave}

The global order parameter $r$ discards spatial arrangement. On a periodic
sheet, a phase field can wind once around the sheet and have $r=0$ while
neighbouring sites remain locked. At identical frequencies, every such winding
is stationary for an isotropic symmetric kernel: contributions at opposite
separations cancel. It violates the quarter-turn initial-spread premise of
the convergence result, which is why that result can still select the uniform
state.

In the spatial control, the winding persists below the interpolated decay
length \waveBoundary~mm and unwinds above it (Fig.~\ref{fig:winding}). At
0.1~mm, uniform and winding starts under the same coupling, diffusion and
kernel end at $r=\waveBandControlOrder$ and $r=\waveBandOrder$; patch-local order
is \waveBandLocalOrder{}. Under quenched frequency spread
\waveSpread~rad\,s$^{-1}$, the measured retention boundary is
\waveSpreadBoundary~mm, and the corresponding order comparison is
$\waveSpreadBandControlOrder$ against $\waveSpreadBandOrder$ over
\waveSeedCount{} frozen realizations. These are finite controls, not a cortical
wave identification or a derived transition.

rate destabilizes that winding. The criterion concerns linear rates, not
asymptotic stability, and the measured retention boundary need not equal its
limit. An amplitude extension adds an existence condition:
$\Gamma+\lambda>0$, where $\Gamma$ is linear gain and
$\lambda=\sum_d f_d(\cos\Delta\theta_d-1)$ comes from the same kernel.
For the nonnegative kernels used here, a broad kernel can remove a winding
from that model even though the
phase-only equations retain it as a stationary state.

Neither model turns these states into measured cortical spirals. The periodic

although its global $r=0$ form is uninformative. A nonzero ring winding number
$q$ also obstructs a global real-valued phase lift; it has no identified
content meaning here. Thus an onset criterion based only on $r$ can register a
change of basin as well as a change of coupling. The protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} needs a spatial phase observable.
\subsection{Coherent adaptation can lose environmental alignment}
\label{sec:plasticity}

An adaptive coupling rule might supply representational structure, but its objective must reward the relevant agreement. For symmetric coupling, pairwise sine terms cancel in the population mean drift. Minimizing squared drift at that fixed mean therefore rewards equalizing drift across nodes. In the frequency-cluster control, this favors coupling between unlike-frequency clusters, whereas the diagnostic environmental template rewards coupling within clusters.

The formalization proves descent of the squared-drift objective for a specified gradient flow at fixed phases. Its functional differs from thermodynamic entropy production: at a coherent stationary density the probability current vanishes while phase-averaged squared drift is positive. Supplemental Material gives the operator, its gradient and the conditions of the descent result.

The finite control co-evolves noisy phases and symmetric, nonnegative couplings at fixed total resource. Its template is a diagnostic covariance of latent signals, never a training input. Across three illustrative seeds, second-half order remains between $\plasticityOrderMin$ and $\plasticityOrderMax$, while the within-cluster to between-cluster coupling ratio falls from $\plasticityRatioInitialMin$--$\plasticityRatioInitialMax$ to $\plasticityRatioMin$--$\plasticityRatioMax$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:resonance}). Random-update, permuted-gradient and frozen-kernel controls are reported in Supplemental Material.

The immediate post-update objective reduction disappears when averaged over complete update intervals. A prespecified grid of $\plasticityStudyCaseCount$ rate--cadence combinations contains no case meeting the $5\%$ sustained-reduction criterion while retaining coherence and losing alignment. The finding is coherent adaptation away from the specified structure, with no demonstrated sustained cost reduction.

Kernel alignment and overlap compatibility are different observables. This control addresses acquisition of its designated template under its particular objective. Learning models can explicitly include data in an objective \cite{corberi2026}; an account of agreement about the cup must specify the content its learning rule is meant to preserve.

\subsection{Content agreement needs independent decoders}

A neural test needs independently identified domains, local descriptions and restriction maps. Two decoders should estimate a shared content on overlapping domains before their agreement is examined. Discrepancy requires a null under decoding noise and controls using deliberately compatible and incompatible examples. An implementation on synthetic data separates these cases at $\mathrm{AUC}=\overlapIncompatibleAuc$, on a $95\%$ interval of $[\overlapIncompatibleAucLow,\overlapIncompatibleAucHigh]$ resampled by trial; the compatible construction scores $\overlapCompatibleAuc$ on an interval containing one half. Shared-prior shrinkage can nevertheless manufacture agreement, and regional bias can manufacture disagreement. Per-patch decoding diagnostics detect these failures; the compatibility statistic alone does not.

Phase agreement cannot replace that measurement. On constructed content, phase carries a share \overlapPhaseShareBuilt{} of the content entropy when content is built from it and \overlapPhaseShareFree{} when it is not. A phase-derived statistic reports compatibility on phase-locked patches whose local descriptions disagree. The implemented content statistic detects their incompatibility. These controls validate a measurement on known constructions; a neural content model and decoder remain to be established.

Choosing the cover also chooses a candidate subject: trivial descriptions can agree while carrying little content, and a partition can conceal disagreement. The useful choice must therefore be constrained by what neural activity encodes. The exact composition assumes compatibility of its supplied cover. The content mechanisms above identify additional hypotheses under which agreement can arise; they leave the empirical choice of content and the connection to that cover explicit.

\subsection{Estimator calibration on exploratory EEG}

The concentration range and the dependence structure decide whether the concentration--coherence relation can be tested at all. Under the specified log-density estimator and bias-floor criterion, discrimination at the observed concentration ceiling $a=\designObservedMax$ requires \designSitesIndependent{} independent sites in the scanned designs, rising to \designSitesClustered{} when sites share a mean in groups of \designSitesPerCluster. These counts describe that estimator and dependence model; the intended recording requires its own calibration. Supplemental Material reports the bin-count controls and the full dependence-aware design.

That ceiling comes from exercising the estimators on public propofol-sedation EEG \cite{bajwa2025}, pooling channels in 100-ms windows. Bipolar and common-reference montages produce different residuals. Bipolar-montage scalp EEG sits in the linear Bessel regime ($a_{\max} = \designObservedMax$, deviation from $a/2 < 1\%$). The exercise confirms the estimator is well-behaved in this regime but does not test the nonlinear prediction. The bipolar results are compatible with the Bessel relation and with monotone alternatives that agree to this precision over the sampled range. Temporal pooling changes the estimand, and sedation recordings leave the awakening hypothesis untested.

An informative dataset therefore needs sufficient concentration range or precision, calibrated estimators and an observation model for dependence, source mixing and reference choice. Intracranial recordings (ECoG, sEEG or LFP) or narrowband burst analysis may reach a more informative concentration range, but that range must be measured. At the observed ceiling, the specified design requires \designSitesIndependent{} independent sites or \designSitesClustered{} sites under its clustered dependence model; these are design calculations, not sample requirements transferable to another recording. Public mouse calcium imaging provides thousands of spatially located neurons across waking, sleep and anesthesia \cite{oomoto2026}, but its 7.65\,Hz frame rate is below the phase-estimation band used here. Site count, state coverage and bandwidth are separate requirements.

\section{The cortical hypothesis and an awakening test}
\label{sec:prediction}

The cortical proposal identifies a physical source of coupling and asks whether its changes help explain recovery. The tests in Section~\ref{sec:observations} determine what observations must distinguish before a phase trace can support this identification.

\subsection{Physical identification and calibration}
\label{sec:scaling}

The cortical hypothesis identifies part of effective coupling with endogenous extracellular electromagnetic interactions. Fields can modulate spike timing \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}, and field models propose coordination of distributed neural information \cite{pinotsis2022,mcfadden2020}. Phase organization under propofol motivates studying this regime \cite{bastos2021}, but does not measure coupling.

The formal field predicate requires a jointly continuous kernel, a normalized substrate, coupling equal to the kernel average, and no single site providing more than half that strength. Toy constructions satisfy it. A cortical realization must specify the scale at which the kernel and its reduction apply: calcium imaging reports different state-dependent modularity patterns at single-cell and mesoscale resolution \cite{kiyooka2026}.

Cortical columns are the obvious candidate unit, and they answer one of the two demands the formalism makes. As the oscillator index they are a serious proposal: they are delineated by response properties and anatomy rather than by the agreement they would be used to establish \cite{mountcastle1997}, and a phase can be estimated from a column-sized territory at the electrode spacings used for cortical wave measurements \cite{townsend2015}. As the content cover they are a poor one, for three separate reasons. A parcellation into non-overlapping columns is a disjoint cover, which satisfies the compatibility obligation without constraining anything (Section~\ref{sec:unity}). The cover condition additionally requires the patches to exhaust the substrate, while much of association cortex has no accepted columnar parcellation. And the column's standing as a canonical cortical unit is contested \cite{horton2005,rakic2008}. Offering columns for both roles at once therefore supplies the oscillator index; the content cover remains an open empirical question, which the bridge assumption E78 records.

The spatial reduction is also testable. A row-sum aggregation at zero diagonal tracks the uniform-coupling control over the tested spatial decay range, while frequency heterogeneity displaces the operational threshold. The fit window biases both estimates relative to $2D$; agreement with the control is therefore the supported conclusion. Supplemental Material reports the reduction, its numerical error and the distinct assumptions needed for the kernel-average identification in Eq.~\eqref{eq:installed-coupling-bound}.

The field commitment requires physical evidence beyond a choice of mathematical representation, and the available support comparisons do not supply it. A finite matrix spread over positive-mass cells preserves its total coupling and phase correlation exactly, while point-supported vanishing under an atomless measure and a disadvantage from excluding a better-correlated pair concern specified support constraints; Section~\ref{sec:gpu} reads the same comparisons against a digital candidate. Neither supplies energy prices.

A spike-time shift alone supplies no coupling rate. The dimensionless product of population size $N$, timing sensitivity $\mathcal{T}$, field amplitude $E$ and frequency $f$ needs a conversion rate $\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N \mathcal{T} E f.
 \label{eq:keff}
\end{equation}
This rate describes how induced timing shifts accumulate into drift. Neither it nor the installation factor $\kappa$ follows from a heat bound. Testing the physical threshold requires independent estimates of the interaction kernel, its aggregation and diffusion in the same cortical regime.

\subsection{Extracellular geometry and recovery}

The proposed control variable is extracellular geometry. Extracellular volume fraction is approximately $\alpha=0.2$ at tortuosity $\lambda\approx1.6$ \cite{sykova2008}; a sleep study reports a rise from roughly $0.14$ to $0.23$ \cite{xie2013}. Below $10$\,kHz, cortical tissue conducts approximately resistively at $0.3$--$0.6$\,S\,m$^{-1}$ \cite{barbour2017}. At fixed transmembrane current density, an effective-medium description predicts that a larger extracellular fraction raises conductivity and reduces field amplitude.

The coupling-gated hypothesis consequently predicts that extracellular contraction on awakening raises $E$ and, holding the other factors in Eq.~\eqref{eq:keff} fixed, raises effective coupling. This argument supplies a direction; the geometry--coupling relation, its magnitude and its role in human recovery require measurement.

For a smooth crossing at nonzero speed, evaluating the stationary branch along the changing control gives a deterministic tracking approximation that fails at onset in the tested crossing control:
\begin{equation}
 r_{\mathrm{ss}}(K(t))\sim\sqrt{\frac{\dot K(t_0)}{D}}\,(t-t_0)^{1/2},
 \qquad K(t_0)=K_c,\quad \dot K(t_0)>0.
 \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
This is a deterministic tracking approximation from Eq.~\eqref{eq:onset} at fixed $D$; the crossing controls in Section~\ref{sec:ramp} show that it fails as an observed-onset approximation under the tested initialization. A separation of geometry and phase time scales can support tracking away from threshold. The awakening hypothesis needs a calibrated dynamical prediction for an operational onset above the finite-size fluctuation floor. Crossing $K_c$ alone does not supply a recovery latency.

\subsection{A protocol for the awakening experiment}
\label{sec:protocol}

Neural inertia describes a behavioural barrier and hysteresis between anaesthetic induction and emergence \cite{friedman2010}; recovery can pass through metastable activity states \cite{hudson2014,proekt2018}. The $v^{1/2}$ exponent of an ideal delayed bifurcation is generic \cite{baer1989,berglund2002} and cannot alone discriminate a field mechanism from a synaptic one. A possible discriminator is spatial onset relative to independently estimated local field coupling and network connectivity. The present mean-field model has no spatially varying $\kappa$ and derives no nucleation order, so a field-based onset map needs a separate model specified before the observations are inspected.

For that comparison, partition the recording into prespecified regions $i$ and measure pre-onset order $r_i(t_0)$ and diffusion $D_i$. Define a field score $h_i=N_i\mathcal{T}_iE_i f_i$ from independent pre-onset field and timing measurements, and a connectivity score $c_i$ from a separately estimated structural network. For the same measured emergence drive $g(t)$, compare $K_i^{F}(t)=a_Fg(t)h_i$ with $K_i^{C}(t)=a_Cg(t)c_i$. Each model has one fitted scale. The local first-harmonic approximation predicts
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_i^{M}(t)=r_i(t_0)\exp\!\left(\int_{t_0}^{t}[K_i^{M}(s)/2-D_i] \,ds\right),
 \qquad M\in\{F,C\}.
 \label{eq:spatial-onset-comparator}
\end{equation}
Predicted onset is the first crossing of a region-specific threshold fixed from pre-onset fluctuations. Fit the two scales on training sessions, then compare held-out onset times and site order under the same observation model. Larger field scores predict earlier onset only when baseline order and diffusion do not reverse the ranking. This declared approximation omits interregional coupling and saturation; a successful ranking would support its field covariate, not prove ephaptic causation. Supplemental Material specifies the controls and failure conditions.

A within-subject protocol can manipulate emergence rate, with spatially resolved recordings to compare the proposed field and connectivity models:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Manipulate emergence rate.} Vary the propofol infusion taper across sessions within the same subject, recording drug concentrations or a validated pharmacokinetic proxy to quantify the manipulation.
\item \textbf{Measure behavioural recovery.} Record time-to-response to a standard stimulus as the independent behavioural endpoint, rather than estimating $\Delta K$ directly.
\item \textbf{Resolve spatial onset.} Identify anatomical locations where coherence first crosses the baseline-scaled threshold and score the two maps of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spatial-onset-comparator} on held-out sessions. The two covariate maps are fixed before the outcome sessions are inspected.
\item \textbf{Match physical controls.} Confirm that the observed coherence-to-amplitude relation holds under the specific local diffusion $D$ and observation noise, estimable from the quadratic variation of resolved phase traces.
\end{enumerate}

Predeclare the recording window, regional fluctuation thresholds, and a shared timing-error model. Fit the two scales and that observation model on training sessions only; on held-out sessions, score the observed site-onset times and sites that never cross by the same regional predictive rule. Report site order as a secondary outcome and behavioural time-to-response separately. Sessions without a crossing are censored observations, not zero delays or exclusions. Compare the two scores only where independently estimated field and connectivity maps predict distinguishable onset patterns at the recording resolution. A held-out field-model advantage would support that specified spatial predictor; it would not identify an ephaptic cause, shared content or experience. Supplemental Material gives the controls and pilot quantities needed to judge whether the comparison is informative.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Unity and a minimal self as interpretive motivation}
\label{sec:commitment}

We propose the state glued from compatible local descriptions as a formal
correlate of conscious unity, and its accurate internal reconstruction as a
correlate of a minimal self. In the cup example, this would mean one description
that preserves the cup's relation to the perceiver as well as the scene.
Compatibility permits distinct contents and uncertainty; the shared
quantities must be chosen from independent evidence about what each region
encodes. The reconstruction need not be a verbal or autobiographical account.
Its proposed relation to experienced perspective remains an interpretation.
Neither identification is among the eight hypotheses in
Table~\ref{tab:summary}, and satisfying them would not measure experience.

\subsection{What a test of the full proposal must establish}
\label{sec:full-test}

A test needs separate estimates of effective coupling and diffusion, shared
content on overlapping regions, and reconstruction from a specified encoding
at a declared fidelity. The cover, relevant state family and decoder must be
fixed without selecting them for agreement. An independent experiential
endpoint is needed to evaluate the interpretation; absence of a report alone
does not define absence of experience. A unified episode with incompatible
decoded descriptions would challenge the unity identification. A unified
episode lacking the proposed self-encoding would challenge the minimal-self
claim. Conversely, satisfying the mathematical conditions without such an
episode would challenge their sufficiency as correlates.

For the physical route, cortical actuator modes and prices, the
geometry--coupling relation and a dynamical onset model remain to be measured.
The field/connectivity comparison in Section~\ref{sec:protocol} evaluates one
spatial onset prediction. Controlled changes in coordination and independently
decoded content agreement would address the representational connection.
The synthetic decoder and EEG exercises calibrate parts of these measurements;
they do not identify a neural content code or test experience.

\subsection{Explanatory scope}
\label{sec:scope}

Integrated-information, global-workspace and predictive-processing accounts
organize unity, access and modelling differently
\cite{tononi2016,baars1997,dehaene2014,friston2010}. This account specifies
conditional relations among coupling, local content and reconstruction. Its
formal field condition requires a continuous kernel, a normalized substrate,
a specified average coupling and noise, and a site-contribution bound. Those
conditions do not identify an electromagnetic substrate; the extracellular
interpretation needs the separate calibration of
Section~\ref{sec:prediction}. The joint witness shows that the formal
hypotheses can hold together in a declared model. A cortical realization and
an experiential interpretation require additional evidence.

The remaining explanatory question is whether these relations occur in neural
activity and matter for experience. The framework identifies candidate
correlates and tests of their physical and representational premises. It does
not explain why satisfying them would be accompanied by experience.
