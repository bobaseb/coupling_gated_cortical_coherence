\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing
A field account of cortical coordination holds that spatially distributed neural processes are coupled in part through the endogenous extracellular electromagnetic field. We ask what such an account must assume to reach a globally consistent, self-referential state, and what it predicts before it gets there. Its physical core is a noisy mean-field phase model whose coupling drifts through a synchronization threshold. If recovery of coherence after sleep is gated by that crossing, it is not free to take an arbitrary time course. For as long as the phase distribution tracks the stationary branch, it must show a square-root onset and a von Mises concentration--coherence relation with no fitted shape parameter. The tracking condition is part of the prediction, and the simulations measure its cost: ramp speed sets the apparent onset exponent, so an exponent fitted to a driven recovery trace reports the drive as much as the mechanism. A coupling plasticity descending a squared-drift objective preserves coherence while moving away from the environmental structure it should learn, below a shuffled-label comparison: dissipation minimization does not imply representational learning. A Lean~4 formalization composes finite capacity, Landauer's bound, the noisy Kuramoto transition, sheaf gluing and Banach's theorem into one conditional theorem, isolating the eight hypotheses it consumes; a common toy model satisfies all eight. We defend as a philosophical commitment, not a theorem, the identification of the glued section with the unity of a conscious episode and of its reflexive fixed point with a minimal self. Testing the account requires independent calibration of effective cortical coupling and an awakening experiment separating slow coupling change from alternative recovery mechanisms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Neural activity changes across sleep, awakening and anesthesia, but a change in synchrony does not by itself identify the physical interaction responsible for it. A field account makes a stronger proposal: spatially distributed neural processes are coordinated partly through the endogenous extracellular electromagnetic field, and changes in the medium can change that coordination \cite{mcfadden2002,mcfadden2020,pockett2012}. Ephaptic effects provide a possible interaction mechanism \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}. Whether that mechanism is sufficient to support the collective state such accounts require remains an empirical question.

Such an account has three requirements, and they order what follows. Coupling must be strong enough to sustain coherent collective activity; the local descriptions that activity carries must be compatible enough to glue into a single one; and that glued state must support an accurate model of itself. The first requirement is physical and testable with current methods, and most of this article is about testing it. The second and third are where the mathematics is, and where the assumptions accumulate.

The first requirement admits a definite claim. If cortical coupling is slowly gated---if recovery of coherence after sleep is driven by a coupling parameter drifting through a synchronization threshold---then the recovery is not free to take an arbitrary time course. For a recovery slow enough that the phase distribution tracks the stationary branch, the theory fixes a square-root onset and a one-parameter relation between phase concentration and coherence, with no fitted shape parameter. That is a constraint an experiment can meet or violate. How slow is slow enough is itself part of the claim, and our simulations bound it from the other side, by showing which parts of the constraint survive finite populations and finite ramp speeds and which do not.

The contribution has four parts. The first is the coupling-gated recovery hypothesis itself: a stationary coherent branch with square-root onset, a von Mises concentration--coherence relation, and an experimental protocol with failure conditions declared in advance (Sections~\ref{sec:field} and \ref{sec:prediction}). The second is a constraint on how such data may be analyzed. The apparent onset exponent measured from a finite ramp is a function of ramp speed across the sweep of Section~\ref{sec:numerics}, so an exponent fitted to a recovery trace measures the ramp at least as much as the mechanism---and that holds for any analysis reading a critical exponent off a driven transition, not only for ours. The third is a negative result about plasticity: a coupling update descending a squared-drift objective preserves coherence while moving \emph{away} from the environmental structure it is expected to learn, below a shuffled-label comparison. Dissipation minimization does not imply representational learning. The fourth is a machine-checked composition (Section~\ref{sec:chain}) in which finite information capacity, Landauer's bound, a predictive dissipation bound, the noisy Kuramoto transition, sheaf gluing and Banach's fixed-point theorem yield one conditional theorem, with the eight hypotheses that composition consumes isolated and named; a common toy model satisfies all eight, so their conjunction is not empty.

A prose table of assumptions would list the same eight items, so the fourth contribution needs a word about method. What the Lean development adds is that it makes the list non-negotiable. The characteristic failure in an argument of this shape is not a false step but a silent change of subject: a theorem about a stationary density read as a claim about a trajectory, an inequality about a register read as a claim about learning, agreement of phases read as compatibility of representations. Type-checking forbids each of those substitutions, and an assumption needed to make a step go through appears as a hypothesis in the term rather than as a word in a sentence. The last of them is not merely forbidden but refuted by a constructed object: the development carries a cover at a single common phase whose local densities disagree where its patches meet, satisfying every other requirement of the gluing theorem and failing compatibility alone (Section~\ref{sec:unity}). That object is what a prose table of assumptions cannot carry. Formalization does not make the assumptions true; it makes them impossible to lose.

The physical model and the formal composition belong in one article because they are two halves of one commitment. The coupling-gated prediction is worth testing under this framework, rather than as one more synchronization model, only because the coherent state it produces is claimed to do further work: to support a globally consistent representation and a reflexive fixed point that we identify with phenomenal unity and a minimal self.

The distinction that does that work is between phases agreeing and descriptions fitting together, and it is concrete. Take two cortical regions whose receptive territories overlap, each carrying a probability distribution over what occupies the shared part. Their oscillators can be locked to the same phase at every instant while one region's distribution on the overlap places an object left of fixation and the other places it right. The phases agree; the descriptions do not, and no single distribution over the shared territory restricts to both. A theory that identifies unity with synchrony cannot mark that difference, and so cannot separate a brain whose regions oscillate together from one whose regions are about the same world. The sheaf condition marks exactly it: local states glue to one global section when their restrictions to shared regions coincide, and otherwise not at all. Coherence enters as the physical condition under which compatibility can be maintained, not as a substitute for it, and the self-prediction map then asks the further question of whether the glued state contains an accurate model of itself. That identification is a commitment, not a theorem, and Section~\ref{sec:commitment} states it, argues for it, and says what would count against it. Its separation from the physical tests is what makes both assessable: the cortical coupling hypothesis and the recovery prediction can fail while the mathematical composition stands, and the composition can stand while the identification is wrong. The established ingredients \cite{landauer1961,still2012,sakaguchi1988,banach1922} therefore appear here for their biological composition, not for any new fundamental physical law.

\section{A coupling-gated model of cortical coherence}
\label{sec:field}

\subsection{Dynamics and stationary solutions}

The finite stochastic model is
\begin{equation}
 d\theta_i=\left[\omega_i+\sum_j K_{ij}(t)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)\right]dt
 +\sqrt{2D}\,dW_i,
 \qquad
 r_N=\left|\frac1N\sum_i e^{i\theta_i}\right|.
\end{equation}
Here $D>0$ is phase diffusion, $W_i$ are independent Wiener processes, and $K_{ij}$ has inverse-time units, with angles treated as dimensionless. The uniform mean-field convention is $K_{ij}=K/N$. Frequency heterogeneity and phase diffusion are separate parameters; a frequency-spread width cannot be substituted for $D$ when applying the identical-frequency stationary theory.

For identical frequencies in their rotating frame, the assumed stationary density is
\begin{equation}
 \rho(\theta)=\frac{e^{a\cos(\theta-\psi)}}{2\pi I_0(a)},
 \qquad a=\frac{Kr}{D},\qquad
 r=R(a):=\frac{I_1(a)}{I_0(a)}.
 \label{eq:selfconsistency}
\end{equation}
The $I_n$ are modified Bessel functions, and $\psi$ is the collective phase. Lean verifies normalization, the density's circular order parameter, and the self-consistency equation. At $K\leq K_c=2D$, the only nonnegative self-consistent order parameter is zero. At $K>K_c$, exactly one positive solution coexists with zero; the positive branch is continuous at threshold and strictly increasing with $K$.

The near-threshold expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r^2}{K-K_c}\longrightarrow\frac1D,
 \qquad r\sim\sqrt{\frac{K-K_c}{D}}.
 \label{eq:onset}
\end{equation}
This is a result about the stationary solution branch. Of the coherent branch we prove existence, uniqueness, continuous emergence at threshold and strict growth in $K$, but not that it is dynamically selected. The dynamical mean-field limit---propagation of chaos---is the missing link between the finite system and the stationary description \cite{sznitman1991}, and no theorem in this development closes it. Exchange symmetry alone does not justify factorization of the joint law, particularly for an ensemble with an unpinned collective phase.

A spatial version replaces the finite sum by an integral against a kernel $K(x,y)$. We normalize the substrate measure to total mass one, so the mean-field strength is a kernel average, not a quantity that grows with the arbitrary measure of the substrate. Reduction of a heterogeneous spatial kernel to a scalar $K$ is an additional modelling step, and the threshold in Eq.~\eqref{eq:selfconsistency} does not automatically apply to every spatial or signed network.

\subsection{Physical identification and calibration}
\label{sec:scaling}

Regular mesh refinement gives convergence of a discrete coupling-energy quadrature to its continuum integral: scalar energy convergence, not convergence of trajectories and not a construction of a physical kernel. The model posits a continuum kernel and separately identifies its cortical realization with endogenous electromagnetic coupling. The formal predicate requires a jointly continuous kernel, a normalized substrate, a mean-field strength equal to its spatial average, and no single site providing more than half of that mean-field coupling. Toy witnesses satisfy these conditions; none is a model calibrated to cortex.

Extracellular fields can modulate spike timing \cite{frohlich2010,anastassiou2015}, and field-based models have been proposed for integrating distributed neural information \cite{pinotsis2022,mcfadden2020}. These observations motivate an effective interaction without establishing its magnitude relative to phase diffusion, its spatial averaging rule, or its causal role in experience; changes in phase organization under propofol \cite{bastos2021} likewise do not measure a crossing of the model's coupling threshold.

The physical-unit conversion is the sharpest open limitation of the framework. An estimate proportional to $N\Delta t f$, using population size, a spike-time shift and oscillation frequency, is dimensionless. It cannot be compared directly with an inverse-time diffusion parameter. A conversion such as
\begin{equation}
 K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N s E f
\end{equation}
requires a rate calibration $\gamma$, where $s$ is the time shift per unit field and $E$ the field amplitude. Supplemental Material sets out why $\gamma$ is not recoverable from the other factors: it states how a per-cycle phase advance accumulates into a drift rate, and the choice that would let it cancel removes the very ratio the threshold test is about. The saved field-strength calculations are conditional on this calibration and on the chosen spatial aggregation; they do not independently establish $K_{\mathrm{eff}}>2D$ in cortex. Determining the interaction kernel and phase diffusion from the same physical system is necessary before the threshold becomes a quantitative biological claim; Section~\ref{sec:protocol} makes that measurement the first item of the proposed experiment.

\section{Recovery after awakening: a conditional prediction}
\label{sec:prediction}

Sleep-associated changes in extracellular space and clearance motivate a possible slow control of coupling \cite{xie2013}. We hypothesize that recovery after awakening includes a change in effective coupling $K(t)$, mediated by extracellular geometry, on a slower timescale than phase dynamics. Neither the monotonic relation between geometry and coupling nor its role in human sleep inertia follows from the cited physiological observation; the model requires both to be measured.

If $K(t)$ rises through $K_c$ and the phase distribution tracks the coherent stationary branch, Eq.~\eqref{eq:onset} predicts
\begin{equation}
 r(t)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\dot K(t_0)}{D}}\,(t-t_0)^{1/2},
 \qquad t>t_0,\quad \dot K(t_0)>0.
 \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
The ideal stationary description has a subthreshold interval followed by continuous onset. Saturation requires a specified subsequent coupling trajectory; monotonic coupling alone does not determine a logistic or any unique sigmoid. The onset time is fixed by $K(t_0)=K_c$ only if $K(t)$ is independently known. When coupling is inferred from the same coherence trace, this is not a parameter-free prediction of latency.

Critical slowing down limits the adiabatic prediction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:recovery} near threshold, and a finite population carries nonzero fluctuating order below it. An observable onset therefore requires a predeclared threshold above the finite-size floor, and a finite-rate trajectory need not show an infinite-slope cusp. Section~\ref{sec:ramp} quantifies the limitation.

\subsection{Distributional and temporal tests}

The stationary model makes a second prediction: phase concentration and coherence obey $r=I_1(a)/I_0(a)$, with no fitted shape parameter. Its applicability depends on the stationary-density assumption and the observation model, and the relation is common to the whole von Mises family, so agreement with it identifies neither ephaptic coupling nor this framework against alternative synchronization models. Within the same ansatz $a/r=K/D$ for $r>0$, so the limiting threshold ratio follows from the density relation instead of testing it, and ratios near the finite-size floor are especially unstable.

The estimators must be independent, which is the constraint most easily violated in practice. Estimating $a$ by inverting the Bessel ratio from $r$ would enforce the relation being tested, and linear statistics on wrapped phases estimate neither quantity.

\subsection{A protocol for the awakening experiment}
\label{sec:protocol}

The requirements above define a design rather than a single measurement, and the design is where the content of the hypothesis lives. An adequate test needs all six of the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Coupling and diffusion in the same units.} Estimate $K$ and $D$ from the same cortical regime, in inverse-time units, separating phase diffusion from quenched frequency spread and from observation noise. Without this the threshold $K>2D$ is not an evaluable statement about cortex.
\item \textbf{Geometry calibrated to coupling.} A diffusion-MRI observable or an astrocytic signal is an indicator of extracellular geometry, not a measurement of $K$. Its mapping to effective coupling must be established separately, with its own uncertainty.
\item \textbf{Phase estimation at adequate spatial resolution.} At least 100 spatial samples per estimate. Estimate $r$ by the circular resultant length and $a$ by an unweighted regression of log-density on the cosine of phase relative to the circular mean. With fewer sites, pooling over time changes the estimand to a spatiotemporal distribution and must be stated as such.
\item \textbf{An independent recovery endpoint.} Behavioral recovery measured separately from the phase trace, so that latency is not defined by the signal whose latency is at issue.
\item \textbf{A predeclared onset criterion.} A coherence threshold fixed above the finite-size floor before unblinding, chosen in the knowledge that a finite ramp smears the cusp.
\item \textbf{Model comparison under matched observation.} Coupling-gated recovery compared against exponential, flexible sigmoidal and other slow-recovery models under identical temporal filtering and observation noise. Latency alone cannot distinguish the mechanism, because many processes create delays.
\end{enumerate}

The design fails informatively in three ways: failure of the stationary phase-density relation over a sufficiently broad concentration range; absence of the proposed coupling--geometry association; or a recovery trace better explained by an alternative after finite-rate and observation effects are accounted for. Two apparent failures are not failures. A linear apparent onset is evidence against the adiabatic approximation only where that approximation is independently justified, and it does not falsify the field hypothesis under arbitrary ramp speeds. The absence of a fold in the stationary equation excludes stationary coexistence of two positive branches in this model, but does not exclude finite-rate hysteresis or adjudicate theories of ignition.

\section{Simulation results}
\label{sec:numerics}

The simulations characterize the finite-size, finite-rate regime that the stationary theory leaves open, and they bear on whether the prediction of Section~\ref{sec:prediction} is measurable at all. Two of them, Sections~\ref{sec:ramp} and \ref{sec:plasticity}, illustrate methodological issues that also arise in analyses of driven transitions and dissipation-driven plasticity. Each is a finite run at its sampled parameters and not a general statement, and none discharges a hypothesis of the composition in Section~\ref{sec:chain}. Each run uses explicit seeds and compact saved summaries, and the numerical values below are generated from those artifacts. The supplement gives parameters, estimators, failure criteria and additional panels. Identical-frequency runs use phase diffusion rather than a quenched frequency-spread surrogate, and order plots display the $1/\sqrt{N}$ reference.

\subsection{Ramp speed sets the apparent onset exponent}
\label{sec:ramp}

The dynamic-ramp study (Fig.~\ref{fig:ramp}) uses $N=2000$, $D=1$ and 32 replicas at four coupling speeds. Escape is the first of three consecutive saved samples at $r\geq0.2$ after threshold. Only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas escape within the fastest window. The three slower ramps have mean coupling delays $\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$, with a fitted delay exponent $\rampDelayExponent$. The apparent onset exponent changes from $\rampOnsetOne$ at the fastest speed to $\rampOnsetFour$ at the slowest.

The exponent recovered by fitting a driven transition is a joint function of the mechanism and the drive rate. An onset exponent read off a recovery trace therefore says little about the underlying transition unless the ramp rate is independently known and the finite-size floor is declared, and that holds for any such analysis, not only for one conducted under this framework. The prediction of Section~\ref{sec:prediction} is the first thing it constrains: the stationary square-root law cannot be imposed on an arbitrary recovery trace, and its absence is not by itself evidence against a coupling-gated mechanism. The specified deviation-based critical ramp speed is not bracketed by the sweep.

The dynamical-selection control shows escape toward a coherent branch above threshold, but retains quantitative finite-size discrepancies. At $K=2.8$, steady mean order is $\selectionSuperOrder$ against the static value $\selectionTheoryOrder$. The fitted growth-rate slope is $\selectionRateSlope$, compared with the theoretical slope $1/2$. The propagation-of-chaos diagnostics show decreasing subcritical dependence with increasing population size. In the supercritical unpinned ensemble, however, pair correlation remains substantial because realizations share a random collective orientation. This negative unconditional result does not settle a conditional mean-field limit after removal of that orientation.

\subsection{Spatial coupling, frustration and calibration}

A periodic two-dimensional sheet with row-normalized exponential coupling remains coherent in the sampled empirical decay-length band under the declared total coupling and frequency spread. At decay lengths $0.1$, $0.2$ and $0.3$~mm, steady order is $\spatialOrderOne$, $\spatialOrderTwo$ and $\spatialOrderThree$. This is one finite parameter regime; fixing total coupling while changing range tests fragmentation, not whether cortex supplies that total coupling.

A row-balanced Dale-law network shows that row balance alone does not guarantee a floor-level incoherent baseline. In a separately specified weak-synapse regime, a uniform modulatory term produces an operational coherence crossing at $K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D$ between $\frustrationCouplingMin$ and $\frustrationCouplingMax$. That ratio is dimensionless; carrying it over to cortex needs the rate calibration $\gamma$ of Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, which neither this sweep nor the spatial control supplies.

\subsection{Squared-drift plasticity does not learn structure}
\label{sec:plasticity}

A coupled system that descends a dissipation-like objective is widely expected to come, in doing so, to mirror the structure of its input; the predictive dissipation bound of Section~\ref{sec:chain} invites that reading. This control tests the expectation directly in the present model, and the expectation fails.

For fixed phases, consider the squared-drift objective
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(K)=\frac1D\int\left[\omega(x)+\int K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)\,d\mu(y)\right]^2d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
It is a quadratic functional of an affine image of the kernel. The formalization constructs the bounded drift operator $A$ and its adjoint on the relevant $L^2$ spaces, obtaining $\nabla\sigma=(2/D)A^*(AK+\omega)$, and the specified gradient flow decreases this objective at fixed phases. The objective is not generally a thermodynamic entropy-production rate, which requires probability-current information. Whether biological couplings follow this flow is an additional modelling question.

The joint control evolves noisy phases with clustered frequencies and nonzero grand-mean drive, while updating symmetric couplings every 50 fast steps. Couplings remain nonnegative, hollow and normalized to a fixed total. The symmetric-edge derivative of $\frac12\sum_i v_i^2$ is $(v_i-v_j)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$, so both endpoints contribute. Resource constraints and evolving phases remove the fixed-phase monotonicity guarantee.

Across three seeds, second-half order lies between $\plasticityOrderMin$ and $\plasticityOrderMax$, and matched frozen-coupling controls maintain similar coherence. The distance to the true cluster template increases in every adaptive run, with worse changes than the shuffled-label comparison (Fig.~\ref{fig:resonance}). The template is a covariance of shared latent cluster signals, not an input to the update or a measured covariance of the constant frequencies.

The objective descends, coherence is maintained, and the coupling matrix moves away from the generating structure---below the level of a random relabelling. Coherence is therefore not a proxy for representational alignment in this regime, and a plateau in the objective establishes neither stationarity of the adaptive process nor a thermodynamic entropy-production rate.

\subsection{The estimation protocol demonstrated on public EEG}

Item 3 of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} specifies estimators whose behavior on real recordings is not obvious, so we exercise them on public propofol-sedation EEG \cite{bajwa2025}. The purpose is to show what the protocol produces and where it is fragile, not to test the awakening hypothesis, for which these data are the wrong design.

Two fragilities appear immediately. The implemented estimator pools 62 channels over a 100-ms window, so it estimates a spatiotemporal distribution rather than an instantaneous spatial one---a departure from the protocol forced by channel count, and one that any scalp implementation will face. Bipolar and common-reference montages give different residuals, making referencing a material analysis choice rather than a preprocessing detail.

The bipolar results are compatible with the Bessel relation over the observed concentration range. That range is too narrow to distinguish the curve from its linear approximation at the observed scatter, so the check is uninformative about the relation itself. An informative test needs a broader concentration range and explicit treatment of source mixing, finite samples and temporal pooling; intracranial or high-density recordings across a genuine state transition are the natural setting.

\section{Thermodynamic constraints and the formal composition}
\label{sec:chain}

The synchronization model of Section~\ref{sec:field} is one link of a longer conditional construction, whose nine nodes are:
\begin{quote}
\singlespacing
\noindent
\textbf{n1} a finite phase space has bounded information capacity; \textbf{n2} a broken symmetry forces a boundary, where the field leaves its vacuum manifold; \textbf{n3} the boundary erases, and erasure costs heat; \textbf{n4} that dissipation bounds nonpredictive memory; \textbf{n5} discrete couplings coarse-grain to a continuum limit; \textbf{n6} a specified physical field realizes that limiting coupling; \textbf{n7} above threshold the field carries a unique coherent order parameter; \textbf{n8} compatible local states glue to a unique global section; \textbf{n9} that section is the unique fixed point of a self-prediction map.
\end{quote}
Node-level results are established or cited; what the composition consumes are the eight implications between them. Figure~\ref{fig:chain} shows where they sit and what kind of assumption each one is, and Table~\ref{tab:summary} states them, classified by their role in the composition rather than by their plausibility. In particular, finite capacity does not imply a symmetry-breaking boundary: n1 and n2 are independent roots, even though the composition can accept an implication between them as an argument.

\subsection{Capacity, boundaries and predictive memory}

For a finite state space $X$, Shannon entropy satisfies $H(p)\leq\log|X|$, with equality only for the uniform distribution. A stream with more possible histories than $|X|$ cannot be encoded injectively in that register. Separately, a continuous field on a connected substrate that reaches distinct components of a disconnected vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere, the simplest topological-defect construction \cite{kibble1976}. Relating this locus to a particular finite information-processing register is the additional hypothesis E12 carries.

A non-surjective update on a finite register is non-injective. Under the specified system--bath model and thermal assumptions, erasure carries a Landauer heat cost \cite{landauer1961}. Finite capacity does not imply that every update erases, and logical many-to-one processing must be distinguished from its physical implementation \cite{norton2005,shenker2000}. The formalization records the physical heat identity as an obligation of each model and supplies an explicit one-bit eraser satisfying it.

For a system state $X_t$ driven by an environmental signal $S_t$, the predictive dissipation bound is
\begin{equation}
 W_{\mathrm{diss}}\geq k_BT\left[I(X_t;S_t)-I(X_t;S_{t+1})\right].
 \label{eq:predictive}
\end{equation}
Under the no-back-action Markov structure $X_t\to S_t\to S_{t+1}$, data processing makes the bracket nonnegative \cite{still2012}. The bound constrains nonpredictive memory at a given dissipation budget. It does not specify a learning rule, maximize prediction, or require the internal representation to reproduce environmental geometry---the point the plasticity control of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} makes concretely. Connecting this budget to Landauer heat additionally requires nonpredictive information to be bounded by the entropy erased by the register. The formal bridge derives Eq.~\eqref{eq:predictive} from Landauer's bound under that identification; it does not derive the identification.

A bound $D_{KL}\leq\Delta t\,\sigma$ also cannot force $D_{KL}\to0$ unless its right-hand side tends to zero, and Gibbs' inequality proves nonnegativity of relative entropy, not a general identification of relative entropy with physical entropy production. These distinctions prevent an informational inequality from being read as a derivation of environmental learning, Bayesian inference, or semantic content.

\subsection{Compatible states and reflexive fixed points}
\label{sec:unity}

A sheaf formalizes local descriptions and their restriction to smaller regions. If local sections agree on every overlap, the sheaf condition gives a unique global section. In the probability-sheaf construction, this is consistency of local density information, and it is not implied by agreement of oscillator phases: the development includes a counterexample with equal phases but incompatible densities on an overlap. Section~\ref{sec:commitment} rests on this distinction, and the model excludes such configurations only through its compatibility hypothesis.

A thermodynamic cover couples this gluing construction to a phase configuration at a potential minimum. For the deterministic positive-coupling model, convergence to that minimum is proved under an explicit initial basin condition; other equilibria prevent an unconditional conclusion. The stationary noisy order parameter, the deterministic relaxation, and the cover must therefore be connected by E78 rather than identified without a hypothesis. Neither field continuity nor synchronization alone guarantees a globally compatible representation.

\label{sec:self}
A reflexive extension equips global sections with a complete metric and a predictive map formed by restricting a state to a local encoding region and reading that encoding out. If the map contracts and fixes the section supplied by the cover, Banach's theorem gives uniqueness of that same fixed point. The contraction rate is assumed, and the equality between the glued section and the fixed point is part of E89. A coherent order parameter supplies the supercritical inequality required by the stated contraction bound; it does not construct the map or prove that cortical dynamics implements it.

The theorem \texttt{chain} concludes \texttt{UnifiedSelf} from these hypotheses. A common toy witness satisfies all eight, so their conjunction is not empty. The project declares no additional Lean axioms beyond the foundational axioms used by Lean's libraries; physical content resides in hypotheses and model fields. This checks the conditional mathematical composition, not the empirical truth of its premises. Detailed constructions, scope statements and counterexamples are retained in the supplement and source files.

\section{What the construction commits to about consciousness}
\label{sec:commitment}

The composition terminates in a mathematical object, and we take a position on what that object is. The commitment has two clauses. \emph{Unity:} the unique global section glued from compatible local states is the formal correlate of the unity of a conscious episode---the fact that a subject undergoes one experience rather than a collection of co-occurring local ones. \emph{Selfhood:} the fixed point of the self-prediction map on that same section is the formal correlate of a minimal self, a state that is what it takes itself to be. Neither clause is a theorem, and no simulation here supports either. We argue for them, then say what would count against them.

Three features of the construction motivate the identification, and each distinguishes it from a bare synchrony measure.

First, compatibility rather than agreement. Oscillator phases can agree everywhere while the local probabilistic descriptions they carry remain incompatible on their overlaps, and Section~\ref{sec:unity} exhibits that configuration inside the formalization, not only as an illustration. If the unity of experience is a matter of local contents being about one world rather than merely co-timed, then compatibility, not coherence, is the property to look for---and coherence enters as the physical condition under which compatibility can be maintained, which is what E78 asserts.

Second, uniqueness follows from the construction rather than from a separate selection principle. Accounts of conscious unity need something that picks one experience from among the candidates a system could support; integrated information theory postulates exclusion for this purpose \cite{tononi2016}. Here uniqueness arrives twice as a consequence: given a cover whose local sections agree, the sheaf condition gives exactly one global section, and given a contracting self-prediction map, Banach's theorem gives exactly one fixed point. The scope of that claim needs stating exactly, because the stronger reading is available and is not ours. Uniqueness is relative to a cover and a map, and choosing them---which regions, which overlaps, which encoding region, and so where the subject ends---is not supplied by either theorem. It is what E78 and E89 carry. What the construction removes is the need for an additional principle to select among the experiences a fixed cover and map would otherwise admit; it does not remove the need to say which cover and map a given subject has. That is a structural argument, not evidence.

Third, the reflexive clause makes the self a condition rather than an addition. The predictive map restricts a global state to a region encoding it and reads that encoding back out, so a fixed point is a state whose self-model is accurate about the state it models. That condition is available only because the state modelled is a single glued object: a system whose local descriptions do not cohere has no such state for a self-model to be about. Unity and selfhood are therefore one construction read at two levels, which is why \texttt{UnifiedSelf} requires the fixed point to be the glued section itself rather than an unrelated section.

The costs belong with the argument. The commitment does not address why any physical condition is accompanied by experience; it is a proposal about which physical state is the conscious one, not about why states of that kind feel like anything. It is therefore open to the objection that a system could satisfy every condition---compatible local states, a supercritical field, an accurate self-model---while nothing is experienced. We have no reply to that objection and claim none. The identification is also multiply realizable as stated: nothing in the sheaf or fixed-point conditions is specific to cortex or to electromagnetic coupling, so the substrate claim is carried entirely by E56 and E67 and is empirical. No conclusion about artificial systems follows from the formal results.

What could count against the commitment is dissociation. It predicts that the two clauses and the presence of a unified conscious episode go together, so a state in which local cortical descriptions are demonstrably compatible and the field supercritical, while the subject reports no unified experience, would tell against it; so would unified reported experience where compatibility demonstrably fails. Anesthetic emergence, dreaming and dissociative states are the places to look, because they separate coherence from report in ways ordinary waking does not. Such tests need an operational measure of overlap compatibility on neural data, which this article does not supply; constructing one is the main obstacle to making the commitment empirically live rather than merely stated. Supplemental Material specifies what such an observable would have to satisfy---a restriction map, a statistic with a null under decoding noise, validation against configurations whose compatibility is built rather than inferred---and the three ways it would fail silently, one of which is reading compatibility off synchrony and so assuming away the distinction being tested.

The physical tests of Sections~\ref{sec:prediction} and \ref{sec:numerics} are independent of all of this. They concern coupling, coherence and recovery, and can fail while the identification stands, or succeed while it is wrong.

\section{Discussion}

The framework connects thermodynamic information constraints, collective phase organization and compatible local representations, conditionally and with its assumptions named. Its biological content is a coupling-gated recovery hypothesis with defined observables, a stated protocol, and failure conditions declared in advance. Its formal content is a composition whose eight gaps are exhibited rather than argued past, and which can be inhabited by a single model. Its interpretive content is the identification of Section~\ref{sec:commitment}, offered as a commitment with reasons.

The simulations are useful because they restrict the hypothesis rather than support it. Finite ramps smear the stationary onset to the point where a fitted onset exponent chiefly reports the ramp; an unpinned collective phase makes independence diagnostics ambiguous in exactly the supercritical regime the model cares about; and squared-drift plasticity maintains coherence while degrading structural alignment. The second and third of these are results about method and about a common intuition, and they transfer beyond this framework.

The proposal differs in emphasis from accounts based on integrated information \cite{tononi2016}, global workspace dynamics \cite{baars1997,dehaene2014}, or predictive processing \cite{friston2010}. It specifies a candidate interaction medium and a mathematical compatibility condition, and, relative to a given cover and self-prediction map, it derives the uniqueness those accounts postulate. It does not derive the exclusion principle of integrated information, identify every broadcast with a field transition, or derive a generative model from a dissipation bound. Continuous fields can carry incompatible local descriptions and discrete models can admit sheaf constructions, so continuity alone does not settle the combination problem; compatibility is what this account offers in its place.

Two formal results bear on the substrate question, and both admit a stronger reading than they support. At matched coupling resource, a fixed wiring support that excludes a better-correlated pair can be suboptimal---a statement about constrained optimization, not about biology. On an atomless substrate, a kernel supported on finitely many points contributes zero to the chosen continuum coupling integral. That is a property of the measure and the observable, and it cuts against this framework's own coarse-graining step as much as against anything else. It does not imply that digital hardware cannot approximate a field or support consciousness, and no biological-versus-artificial conclusion follows.

The empirical priorities follow directly from Section~\ref{sec:protocol}: estimate effective coupling and diffusion in consistent units, test the proposed relation between coupling and extracellular geometry, and measure recovery against an independently defined behavioral endpoint under matched model comparison. A favorable concentration--coherence relation would support a statistical description, not identify its physical cause; an unfavorable result in a regime satisfying the stated assumptions would constrain that description. Successful synchronization simulations cannot substitute for measurements distinguishing ephaptic interactions from other coupling mechanisms.

The article therefore asks to be judged in three separable parts: a prediction that can be tested, a composition that can be checked, and an identification that can be argued about. Keeping them separate is what makes the whole assessable.
