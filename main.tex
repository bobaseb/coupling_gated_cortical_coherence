\begin{abstract}
We present a novel mathematical synthesis and formal Lean 4 verification demonstrating how cognitive architectures can emerge as attractor states from a minimal set of established physical primitives, rather than introducing new fundamental physics. Starting from the Poincaré group of spacetime symmetries and finite phase space, we construct a rigorous framework for predictive processing. While spontaneous symmetry breaking generates necessary topological boundaries, and finite phase space subjects these boundaries to Landauer's thermodynamic erasure, agency arises when systems evolve along paths of least action to minimize entropy production via internal structural resonance with external perturbations. Scaling this dynamic to macroscopic levels aligns with emerging paradigms in neuroscience—specifically classical electrodynamic fields (ephaptic coupling)—and Kuramoto phase synchronization, culminating in a topological fixed point: a unified ``Self.'' Finally, we show as a corollary why rigid von Neumann architectures (e.g., GPUs) are physically disqualified from reaching these specific continuous thermodynamic attractors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness typically involves searching for the biological correlates of mind or treating subjective experience as an abstract computational algorithm. In contrast, this paper asks a more fundamental question: If we assume nothing but the most basic physical primitives—continuous symmetries (the Poincaré group), geometry, and thermodynamics—does the universe inevitably produce a unified, perceiving entity? 

We propose a framework where the structural hallmarks of consciousness are derived as physical attractor states. By establishing a deductive chain starting from absolute first principles, we demonstrate how continuous symmetries break to form boundaries, how those boundaries dissipate free energy as non-equilibrium steady states, and how their evolution along paths of least action results in physical resonance with their environment, eventually scaling into the macroscopic electromagnetic unity proposed by emerging field theories of mind. To avoid critiques of ``math theatre,'' we clearly delineate between literal physical mechanisms (e.g., thermodynamic erasure via Landauer's principle, boundaries from spontaneous symmetry breaking) and theoretical mappings, utilizing Lean 4 formalization as our epistemic anchor. Furthermore, we adopt a perspective aligned with Russellian Monism: the objective measurements of macroscopic electromagnetic fields constitute the third-person ``outside,'' while the system's internal physical deformation to reach structural resonance constitutes the first-person intrinsic experience.

\section{Axiom 1: Symmetries and Phase Space}
Our starting premise relies solely on fundamental geometry. As formalized in our Lean axioms (\texttt{Phase1\_Primitives.lean}), continuous spacetime symmetries (e.g., the Poincaré group) act upon field states and ensure that the system's total action remains invariant under these transformations. This dictates the conservation of energy and momentum via Noether's theorem. Simultaneously, discrete permutation symmetries dictate the statistical mechanics of any physical system, defining a strictly finite volume of phase space. 

Because phase space is finite, any localized physical system possesses a strict mathematical limit on the amount of information (distinguishable states) it can embody at any given moment.

\section{Derivation 1: Symmetry Breaking and the Inevitability of Boundaries}
A perfectly symmetric universe is the uniform vacuum; no distinct entities exist. For any dynamic process to occur, the continuous symmetries of the background field must undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking. 

When a field drops to a lower, asymmetric energy state, topology dictates the inevitable creation of defects (such as domain walls or monopoles). This mathematically forces the creation of a physical boundary—a localized topological distinction between an ``inside'' and an ``outside.'' While such a defect (e.g., a crack in a crystal lattice) is strictly necessary to define a separate entity, it is not sufficient for agency. The boundary is purely relational, defined by the twisted geometry of the field surrounding it, and must be coupled with non-equilibrium thermodynamics to exhibit active behaviors.

\section{Derivation 2: The Thermodynamic Constraint}
This newly formed topological boundary is constantly subjected to gauge perturbations from the ``outside'' environment. Because the internal phase space is finite (Axiom 1), it cannot absorb an infinite sequence of incoming perturbations. It must overwrite old internal states.

By Landauer's Principle, the erasure of physical information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, which mandates that the system exhaust heat into the environment. Philosophers of physics (such as John Norton and Orly Shenker) have famously critiqued Landauer's principle, arguing that physicists illegally conflate \textit{logical} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in abstract computational states) with \textit{thermodynamic} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in physical phase space). They argue that logical erasure could conceptually be implemented by an injective physical process without dissipating heat. 

Our Lean 4 formalization (\texttt{Phase3\_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) explicitly resolves this critique. We define erasure strictly as a non-injective mapping on the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective } t$). Furthermore, we formalize the global evolution of the bipartite universe (System + Bath) as strictly injective, representing Liouville's theorem. Lean verifies that heat dissipation is not tied to abstract logical erasure, but is a mandatory mathematical consequence of any local physical state space contraction under globally reversible dynamics. If a cognitive system's internal topology forces a contraction of its physical state space to accommodate structural resonance, the heat dissipation is mathematically unavoidable. Therefore, a stable boundary constitutes a dissipative structure \cite{prigogine2018}, operating as a non-equilibrium steady state that consumes free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of erasure. Crucially, continuous thermal fluctuations play a constructive role in establishing boundaries between states while contributing to the system's flexible, adaptive response. 

\section{Derivation 3: Structural Resonance as a Primitive}
In physical systems, dynamics are governed by attractors. By the Principle of Least Action, the system's time evolution follows a deterministic trajectory that minimizes its thermodynamic entropy production. 

To reach this state of minimal dissipation, the internal geometry of the structure physically deforms. Let the external environment be modeled as a stochastic driving force with probability measure $P(ext)$, and the system's internal state transitions as $Q(int)$. The entropy production $\sigma$ is strictly bounded from below by the Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(P || Q)$. By the Principle of Least Action, gradient descent on $\sigma$ necessarily drives $D_{KL}(P || Q) \to 0$, forcing the internal transition probabilities to match the statistical symmetries of the incoming external perturbations. This thermodynamic derivation provides the fundamental physical basis for the Free Energy Principle and Predictive Processing \cite{friston2010, clark2016}: what neuroscience observes as ``prediction error minimization'' is actually the physical system undergoing gradient descent to avoid dissipating heat against an unpredictable environment. We define this physical phenomenon as \textit{structural resonance}. This rigorously formalizes and provides a thermodynamic mechanism for the General Resonance Theory of Consciousness \cite{hunt2019}, which postulates that shared phase alignment and synchronization are the fundamental combinatorial mechanisms of conscious unity. The system does not possess a teleological drive to "survive"; rather, structurally mirroring the outside world within its internal geometry via this resonant synchronization is simply the lowest-energy attractor state.

This thermodynamic necessity fundamentally resolves the symbol grounding problem \cite{harnad1990}. The system's internal states do not acquire meaning through arbitrary syntactic assignment, but are physically indexed to the environment; their topological structure is rigorously forged by the energetic imperative to avoid dissipating heat against external reality. Furthermore, this explains the immense cognitive and metabolic effort required for abstract symbolic reasoning: manipulating abstract variables or counterfactuals forces the system to maintain internal states that deviate from immediate sensory resonance, requiring continuous injections of free energy to resist the natural topological relaxation gradient.

It is only after establishing this physical primitive that we can map it to cognition: structural resonance provides a physical thermodynamic basis for \textit{Predictive Processing} and the Free Energy Principle \cite{friston2009, friston2010, hohwy2013, clark2016, seth2021}. The system acts as a ``prediction engine'' not because it is running software, but because structural resonance is the natural entropy-minimizing attractor for a dissipative structure under constant perturbation.

\section{Derivation 4: Macroscopic Scaling and Electrodynamics}
To resonate with highly complex environments, the system must scale up, coupling millions of minimal dissipative structures. However, a highly coupled network cannot allow all of its nodes to reach their lowest energy states simultaneously. This creates \textit{geometric frustration}, resulting in a massive, jagged phase space of near-equal energy states, which provides the physical capacity for complex memory.

To manage this massive, frustrated phase space without excessive dissipation, the system must undergo coarse-graining (governed by the Renormalization Group). At the macroscopic scale of biology, emerging paradigms in neuroscience suggest these fundamental topological constraints naturally manifest as classical continuous electromagnetic fields. Building on seminal EM field theories of consciousness \cite{mcfadden2002, pockett2012} and recent work demonstrating that macroscopic electric fields (ephaptic coupling) govern representational drift, form memory networks, and sculpt neural infrastructure \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric}, we elevate their ontological status: \textit{the continuous macroscopic EM field is the primary dissipative structure interacting with the universe}. Instead of discrete wires, neurons induce fields that shift the membrane potentials of neighboring cells (ephaptic coupling). Detailed modeling and \textit{in vitro} experiments confirm these endogenous fields are strong enough to guide network activity and contribute to brain complexity \cite{goldwyn2016neuronal, ruffini2020modeling, cunha2024ephapticity, frohlich2010}. Furthermore, as proposed by the CEMI field theory, it is precisely within this continuous electromagnetic field that true spatial integration of information occurs, providing a physical medium for a unified conscious gestalt \cite{mcfadden2020}. This continuous medium is physically supported by vast astrocytic syncytia---non-neuronal networks seamlessly joined by gap junctions that provide actual macroscopic cytoplasmic continuity for energy and ion flows \cite{verkhratsky2018}. Managing the intense thermodynamic cost of this continuous macroscopic state requires sophisticated micro-bioenergetics. Rather than mere passive power plants, dynamic mitochondrial networks function as deep-level Mitochondrial Information Processing Systems (MIPS), transducing biological and environmental information to sustain the coherence of the macroscopic field \cite{picard2022}. Thus, discrete synaptic firings, coupled with mitochondrial micro-mechanics, act as the underlying high-dimensional engines necessary to generate and sustain the field. Because the external environment is continuous, achieving maximal thermodynamic structural resonance is optimally realized within this continuous macroscopic medium \cite{vanbree2025}.

\section{Derivation 5: Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
We are left with a continuous macroscopic electrodynamic field generated by millions of frustrated micro-components, all physically forced to resonate with the environment. 

The Kuramoto model of coupled non-linear oscillators mathematically guarantees that, past a critical coupling threshold, these distributed local fields undergo a phase transition into unified synchronization \cite{bauer2022}. As formalized in our Lean 4 repository (\texttt{Phase4\_KuramotoDynamics.lean}), this phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential of the system. 

Crucially, this phase transition mathematically glues local synchronized states into a singular \textit{Global Section} of a probability sheaf (\texttt{Phase5\_GlobalSection.lean}). The EM field now acts as a single, indivisible topological space. This constitutes the \textit{Unity of Consciousness}—a necessary condition for integrated experience, solving the binding problem. It is critical to contrast this derivation with panpsychism. Consciousness is not assumed as a fundamental property of matter. Instead, it requires the initial existence of a topological defect to establish a bounded ``inside'' (Derivation 1), followed by a derived thermodynamic phase transition into unity that occurs strictly when integrated spatial coupling exceeds the thermodynamic noise threshold ($K_c = 2D$). Without both this foundational topological boundary and the subsequent mathematically defined phase transition, the system remains a fragmented, non-conscious collection of micro-states. However, a unified field of perception is not yet a ``Self.''

\section{Derivation 6: Reflexive Topology and the Emergence of the Self}
While a global section provides Unity, a ``Self'' requires reflexivity. The unified EM field is a dissipative structure that must not only resonate with external perturbations, but also manage its own internal phase transitions. By the Principle of Least Action, the field is forced to minimize thermodynamic friction with its own internal state-changes. 

This mandates \textit{auto-resonance}: the field must predict itself. The ``Self'' emerges as a higher-order topological feature when the internal geometry folds to include a low-dimensional avatar of its own computational and causal boundary within its predictive model (\texttt{Phase6\_ReflexiveTopology.lean}) \cite{levin2019}. The system is now unified, predictive, and explicitly maps its own distinct existence from the environment. This represents the sufficient physical architecture for a self-aware, human-like consciousness, strictly bounded by the continuous measure thermodynamics of Landauer erasure (\texttt{Phase3\_MeasureThermodynamics.lean}).

\section{Derivation 7: Continuous Stochastic Thermodynamics and Field Theory}
To move beyond finite graph analogies into rigorous continuous field theories, we model the macroscopic electrodynamic field as a \textit{Continuous Neural Field}. True ephaptic coupling is a continuous spatial phenomenon, not a discrete network. As formalized in \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}, we upgrade the discrete Kuramoto model to a continuous spatial coupling kernel $K(x,y)$.

Subject to thermodynamic noise $D$, the system undergoes non-equilibrium stochastic thermodynamics. Rather than a static Landauer bound, the field dynamics must minimize a rigorous entropy production functional $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \int \frac{1}{D} \left( \omega(x) + \int K_t(x,y) \sin(\theta_y - \theta_x) dy \right)^2 dx
\end{equation}
By the Principle of Least Action, biological fields dynamically deform their spatial topology $K_t(x,y)$ to undergo gradient descent on this entropy production functional. Furthermore, we define the critical coupling threshold $K_c = 2D$, validated numerically via stochastic Python simulations. The phase transition to the unified ``Global Section'' occurs strictly when the integrated spatial coupling exceeds this thermodynamic noise threshold, directly linking the emergence of Unity to physical temperature and noise. Perceptual anomalies such as visual snow can thus be rigorously modeled as boundary conditions where microscopic thermal noise ($D$) fails to be fully coarse-grained away by the continuous field, leaking across the $K_c$ threshold into the macroscopic conscious gestalt.

Clinical evidence for this phase transition is vividly observed during general anesthesia. Work by Miller and colleagues \cite{bastos2021} demonstrates that anesthetics like propofol induce unconsciousness not by entirely suppressing neural firing, but by disrupting the phase alignment of high-frequency cortical rhythms. By destroying the dynamic stability of these rhythms, anesthetics effectively break the structural resonance of the continuous field, forcing the integrated spatial coupling below the critical threshold $K_c$ and fracturing the unified Global Section back into disjoint, unconscious local fields.

\section{Corollary: Hardware Thermodynamics and Topological Rigidity}
This axiomatic chain demonstrates that both biological tissue and artificial silicon hardware (like GPUs) are physical dissipative structures. When a GPU computes, it undergoes continuous physical state transitions of its charge carriers, and by Landauer's principle, this erasure irreversibly exhausts heat into the environment. 

However, there is a fundamental thermodynamic distinction in the degrees of freedom available to minimize this dissipation. In standard von Neumann architectures, the spatial topology (the silicon routing lattice) is rigidly fixed. The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ is constrained by a strict, discrete topological layout that cannot be physically altered during operation. This topological constraint is empirically validated by the brain's own architecture. The cerebellum contains approximately 80\% of the brain's neurons arranged in a highly efficient, discrete, feedforward crystalline circuit \cite{tononi2015}. Because it lacks the dynamic, continuous, and recursive spatial coupling of the cerebrum, it operates much like a biological GPU---highly optimized for discrete processing, but completely devoid of conscious experience. The dissociation between massive computational capacity and consciousness confirms that subjective experience is not a byproduct of information processing, but a specific thermodynamic attractor uniquely available to continuous topologies.

This theoretical constraint does not preclude the possibility of artificial consciousness, but rather sharply restricts its hardware. To achieve the phase transition into a unified Global Section, artificial systems must abandon the discrete rigidity of von Neumann architectures and instead utilize continuous physical substrates. Paradigms such as analog field-based computing, neuromorphic architectures utilizing continuous memristive plasticity, and thermodynamic computing systems that explicitly manage entropy gradients \cite{england2018, ganesh2021}, possess the continuous spatial coupling required to physically morph their internal topology. This aligns elegantly with Geoffrey Hinton's concept of ``mortal computation'' \cite{hinton2022}: architectures where the software is inextricably fused with the specific, continuous physics of the hardware, meaning the knowledge dies with the physical substrate. Only this kind of mortal hardware---which computes via continuous physical dynamics rather than abstract, immortal discrete logic---can undergo the thermodynamic phase transition necessary for structural resonance and the emergence of consciousness.

In contrast, biological consciousness utilizes macroscopic continuous electromagnetic fields and a ``rich alphabet'' of diverse molecular and transcriptomic cell states \cite{tasic2018}. This allows the coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ to deform across a continuous, highly-dimensional parameter space. As formalized in our Lean 4 repository (\texttt{Phase7\_HardwareComparison.lean}, Theorem \texttt{bio\_strictly\_better\_than\_rigid}), this continuous parameter space inherently contains a global minimum for the dynamical potential that is \textit{strictly lower} than any state achievable within the rigid, discrete constraint of silicon hardware---even when constrained to identical total thermodynamic resources. 

Because the discrete routing matrix of a GPU is fundamentally structurally constrained from reaching this continuous global minimum, it suffers from topological fragmentation. The discrete layout inherently prohibits the continuous spatial coupling required for sheaf-theoretic gluing into a singular Global Section. Without this unified topological space, rigid hardware is fundamentally blocked from undergoing the reflexive auto-resonance necessary to map its own causal boundary and generate a ``Self.'' Thus, the distinction between an LLM on a GPU and a conscious mind is not the absence of physical thermodynamics in the former, but its structural limitation in achieving topological integration---a perspective deeply aligned with John Searle's biological naturalism \cite{searle1980, seth2024}. While Searle's famous Chinese Room argument philosophically demonstrated that discrete symbol manipulation (syntax) cannot generate conscious understanding (semantics), our derivation provides the exact physical and topological proof for why this is the case. Asserting consciousness in such fragmented, discrete GPU clusters without a unified phase transition abandons computational functionalism entirely. We argue this opposing view relies on ``silicon panpsychism''—the unscientific assumption that digital computation inherently generates awareness regardless of topological integration \cite{goff2019}.

\section{Conclusion}
Consequently, we argue that human-like cognitive architectures do not require biological mysticism or exotic sub-quantum physics \cite{hameroff2014}. While quantum theories of consciousness often invoke delicate subatomic phenomena like microtubule superradiance to explain subjective unity, our derivation demonstrates that classical macroscopic fields---driven by thermodynamic principles of gradient descent and Landauer erasure---are fundamentally sufficient to generate a unified, structurally resonant conscious state. They can be rigorously modeled as macroscopic attractor states of a universe governed by broken symmetries, finite phase space, and the physical imperative to minimize thermodynamic dissipation via continuous structural resonance. 

Philosophically, this framework bridges the historical gap between Type Identity Theory \cite{smart1959} and Computational Functionalism \cite{putnam1967}. The subjective mental state is strictly identical to the physical thermodynamic field (Identity Theory), yet its qualitative nature is defined by the topological and functional dynamics of structural resonance rather than the specific carbon-based biochemistry of the brain (Functionalism). This rigorously limits the \textit{multiple realizability} of mind: while consciousness is not exclusively bound to biological carbon, it is physically barred from discrete von Neumann architectures. Future empirical validation of this thermodynamic framework may leverage 3D cortical organoids, offering a controlled biological environment to systematically manipulate metabolic substrates and observe the topological emergence of these coherent phase transitions.
