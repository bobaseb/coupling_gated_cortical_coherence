\begin{abstract}
We present a mathematical synthesis and formal Lean 4 verification demonstrating how cognitive architectures can emerge as attractor states from established physical primitives, without introducing new fundamental physics. Starting from the Poincaré group of spacetime symmetries and finite phase space, we construct a framework for predictive processing. Spontaneous symmetry breaking generates necessary topological boundaries, and finite phase space subjects these boundaries to Landauer's thermodynamic erasure. Agency arises when systems evolve along paths of least action, minimizing entropy production via internal structural resonance with external perturbations. Scaling this dynamic to macroscopic levels aligns with emerging paradigms in neuroscience—specifically classical electrodynamic fields (ephaptic coupling)—and Kuramoto phase synchronization, culminating in a topological fixed point: a unified ``Self.'' As a corollary, we show why rigid von Neumann architectures (e.g., GPUs) are thermodynamically disadvantaged or structurally constrained from reaching these specific continuous thermodynamic attractors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness typically involves searching for the biological correlates of mind or treating subjective experience as an abstract computational algorithm. This paper asks a more fundamental question: If we assume only basic physical primitives—continuous symmetries (the Poincaré group), geometry, and thermodynamics—is the emergence of a unified, perceiving entity a natural physical attractor? 

We propose a framework where the structural properties of consciousness are derived as physical attractor states. Establishing a deductive chain from basic principles, we demonstrate how continuous symmetries break to form boundaries, how those boundaries dissipate free energy as non-equilibrium steady states, and how their evolution along paths of least action results in physical resonance with their environment. This eventually scales into the macroscopic electromagnetic unity proposed by emerging field theories of mind. To avoid critiques of ``math theatre,'' we delineate between literal physical mechanisms (e.g., thermodynamic erasure via Landauer's principle, boundaries from spontaneous symmetry breaking) and theoretical mappings, utilizing Lean 4 formalization as our mathematical anchor. We adopt a perspective aligned with Russellian Monism: objective measurements of macroscopic electromagnetic fields constitute the third-person ``outside,'' while the system's internal physical deformation to reach structural resonance constitutes the first-person intrinsic experience.

\subsection{Soundness of the formalization}
\label{sec:soundness}

Formalization is only as trustworthy as the axioms it rests on, and an axiom that is inconsistent silently makes every theorem downstream of it vacuous while leaving the build green. We report a case of exactly this, found by auditing our own development, because the failure mode is easy to reproduce and we have not seen it discussed in the applied-formalization literature.

Earlier drafts of this work declared five axioms. Three of them were individually inconsistent: each, on its own, proves \texttt{False}. All three shared one root cause --- an axiom that constrains a symbol it does not itself bind. Concretely:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{spontaneous\_symmetry\_breaking\_pointwise\_min} took the potential-energy functional as an \emph{implicit} argument occurring only in its hypothesis \texttt{PotentialEnergy phi = V v0}. Instantiating that functional with the constant map $\lambda\_.\,V(v_0)$ discharges the hypothesis by \texttt{rfl} and yields the conclusion for an arbitrary field. With $V(v) = v^2$, $v_0 = 0$ and $\phi \equiv 1$ this proves $1 = 0$.
\item \texttt{landauer\_heat\_eq} equated the free class field \texttt{heat\_dissipation} with a bath-entropy difference \emph{for every instance} of the class. An instance with zero heat dissipation and a bath that gains one bit gives $0 = \log 2$.
\item \texttt{kl\_bound\_axiom} asserted $\sigma \ge D_{KL}(P\|Q)/\Delta t$ for \emph{all} $P$ and $Q$, while $\sigma$ is a single real fixed by a class field. Since $D_{KL}$ is unbounded above, a point mass against a uniform distribution refutes it.
\end{itemize}

The remaining two axioms, \texttt{phase\_invariant\_periodic} and \texttt{sync\_to\_section\_eq}, had the same shape --- global assertions pinning free fields of \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization} --- and are refutable as soon as the probability presheaf admits two distinct global sections. (Both were demoted to class fields at the time; both have since been removed from the class altogether, for a separate reason given in Derivation 5.)

The correction is uniform, and we state it as a rule we now follow: \emph{a physical postulate that mentions a class field must be a field of that class, never a standalone axiom quantified over all instances.} A postulate should be an obligation each model discharges, not a global claim about every model. Recast this way, the development declares no axioms at all. Two of the five became genuine theorems --- pointwise vacuum minimization is now proved from $\int f = 0 \wedge f \ge 0 \Rightarrow f = 0$ a.e., and Gibbs' inequality was already one --- and the other three became instance obligations.

This makes non-vacuity the live question, since an uninhabitable class makes its theorems vacuous just as surely as an inconsistent axiom did. \texttt{Examples.lean} therefore constructs witnesses: a one-bit erasure system with a genuine bath that discharges \texttt{heat\_eq} and reproduces $\Delta Q = k_B T \ln 2$; a perfectly resonant system discharging \texttt{kl\_bound}; and a scalar field on a one-point spacetime discharging \texttt{ActionPrinciples}. Derivation 5's gluing structures are witnessed too, which was until recently the largest open gap: \texttt{Examples.lean} builds a \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} over a three-site substrate carrying the discrete topology and its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, covered by two \emph{overlapping} open patches whose intersection is a single shared site. The local sections are germ families of a phase-indexed finite measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ pushed into the sheafification of the probability presheaf; the family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\texttt{phaseMeasure\_not\_const}), so the periodicity obligation of the constructor \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization.of\-InvariantMeasure} is discharged by an argument about $\cos$ rather than satisfied trivially, and \texttt{thermodynamic\_equilibrium} is \emph{derived} from \texttt{phase\_locked\_minimizes\_potential} rather than assumed. \texttt{global\_section\_from\_thermodynamics} then fires on this instance and glues the two patch-local sections into a unique global one. Two further covers answer the follow-up question of how non-degenerate such a witness can be made. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13 has a negative half worth recording: the phase field can be made non-constant as a function, but not as a physical state, because \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} forces every instance to a phase-locked configuration. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S14 is built the other way round, from two patches with independently chosen mass profiles that meet only on the site they share, and the section the gluing returns is neither of them. That is the same kind of result as \texttt{contracting\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak const} in Derivation 6 --- a proof that the witness is as strong as the class permits, rather than a witness that could be improved by trying harder.

What the witnesses establish is that the classes are inhabitable, so the theorems about them are not vacuous. What they do not establish is that a cortex is such an instance. In the cover above the phase field is already constant --- every patch locked at $\theta = 0$ --- which is precisely what makes the equilibrium obligation dischargeable; getting a physical system into that configuration is the content of the informal dynamical argument, not of the Lean development.

The same audit turned up a defect of a different kind, worth recording because it is not an axiom problem and would not have been caught by \texttt{\#print axioms}. \texttt{mesh\_refinement\_convergence} --- the claim that discrete edge weights converge to the continuous energy as the mesh is refined --- sat in a ``Conjectures'' section as an unproved \texttt{def \ldots : Prop}. It was not merely unproved but false. Nothing required the edge regions to cover the manifold, and $\mathrm{diam}\,\emptyset = 0$, so the triangulation whose edge regions are all empty satisfied the mesh hypothesis while contributing nothing to the sum, forcing $|0 - \int f| < \varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$. Its binder was dead as well: it quantified over a triangulation and then resolved the vertex type by instance search rather than through the bound variable, so the variable was never used. \texttt{Phase2\_MeshConvergence.lean} replaces it with a theorem. Convergence is now stated along a \emph{sequence} of triangulations of a named region $S$, each required to be regular --- edge regions measurable, without self-loops, disjoint across distinct edges, covering $S$, and anchored to the simplicial data in the sense that a vertex lies in the closure of every region of an edge at it. Closure rather than membership is forced: cells are disjoint, so a shared vertex belongs to at most one of them. The manuscript's discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) f(u)$, which samples only the first endpoint and is therefore not symmetric, is shown to equal exactly the midpoint rule $\sum_{u<v} w(u,v)\,\tfrac12(f(u)+f(v))$ over unordered edges; without that identification, reading the double sum as a Riemann sum would be a category error. At fixed mesh the error is at most $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$ with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and convergence follows for uniformly continuous integrands as the mesh shrinks. The regularity conditions pull against each other, so \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 discharges them with the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into half-open cells and checks that the approximation genuinely moves --- $\tfrac12$ at $N=1$ and $\tfrac14$ at $N=2$ for the tent function $x \mapsto |x - \tfrac12|$ --- rather than converging trivially. What is proved is convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate the accompanying simulation measures; that would need a midpoint error term with a second derivative, which we have not developed.

A third defect was one of bookkeeping across files, and it is the kind that survives any number of green builds because each half is individually correct. The development carried \emph{two} Kuramoto potentials. Lyapunov descent along the dynamics, $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$, was proved for the full potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j) - \sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$, while the characterization of the minimum --- phase-locked in both directions --- and everything downstream of it, including Derivation 5's gluing, concerned the coupling term alone. Nothing chained them, and the gap was not cosmetic: \texttt{kuramoto\_potential\_unbounded\_below} now proves that as soon as one $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below --- walk $\theta$ out along $\omega$ and the bounded cosine sum cannot compensate --- so it has no minimum for phase-locking to attain, and the sentence ``the phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential'' was, read literally, false of the functional the descent theorem was about. \texttt{Phase4\_RotatingFrame.lean} closes this by formalizing the standard reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$, a trajectory maps to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system --- which is exactly the system Derivation 5 already worked with --- the two potentials coincide there, and the descent result transports onto the coupling potential whose minimizers are the phase-locked states. Phase differences, phase-locking and the magnitude of the order parameter are all proved invariant under the change of frame, so nothing observable is lost in the reduction. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on two oscillators with a common frequency, discharging every hypothesis. The restriction is real and we state it plainly: the reduction is exact only for identical frequencies. A genuine spread of natural frequencies leaves residual detunings in the reduced system, phase-locking then requires coupling above a critical $K_c$, and $K_c$ is nowhere in our Lean development --- it is studied numerically, as Table 1 records.

A fourth defect was dimensional, and it concerned the one row of Table 1 that is still numerical rather than proved. \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean} defines the critical coupling $K_c = 2D$ and a predicate for being above it. That predicate compared $\int_x\int_y K(x,y)$ against $2D$ --- but a double integral against an unnormalized measure carries the substrate's mass twice over, so a constant kernel $K$ on a substrate of total mass $m$ contributed $K m^2$. The comparison therefore depended on how large the substrate was rather than on how strongly it was coupled, and any kernel whatever could be pushed above threshold by inflating $m$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S9 exhibits the failure concretely: on a two-site substrate carrying counting measure, a system coupled at $K = 1/2$ against a threshold of $K_c = 1$ --- a factor of two \emph{below} threshold --- had an unnormalized double integral of $2 > 1$ and so satisfied the old predicate. The fix is to require the substrate's measure to be a probability measure, which is the normalization in which the mean-field derivation of $K_c = 2D$ is carried out; \texttt{mean\_field\_coupling\_const} then gives that a constant kernel has mean-field strength exactly $K$, and \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_const\_iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$. We flag what this does and does not do. It makes the predicate say something whose truth depends only on the physics, and \S9 witnesses it on both sides of the threshold. It does not by itself prove $K_c = 2D$: the content of that value is the bifurcation of the self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$ at $K = 2D$, which is a separate question from whether the predicate is dimensionally sound. That bifurcation is proved below, in both directions, from an assumed stationary density; deriving the density itself needs the Fokker--Planck operator and is not done.

We draw no grand moral from this beyond the obvious one. The value of a proof assistant here was not that it certified our argument, but that it made a specific, checkable claim about what had been assumed --- and that claim turned out to be false in a way no amount of reading the prose would have revealed.

\section{Axiom 1: Symmetries and Phase Space}
Our starting premise relies on fundamental geometry. In our Lean formalization (\texttt{Phase1\_Primitives.lean}), continuous spacetime symmetries (e.g., the Poincaré group) act upon field states, and the invariance of the system's total energy under that action is recorded by the class \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}. Physically, it is such an invariance that Noether's theorem converts into the conservation of energy and momentum. The scope of the \emph{formal} claim is narrower and we state it plainly: the invariance is a class field, i.e.\ an assumption a model must discharge, and no conserved quantity is ever constructed from it. There is no Noether theorem in our Lean, and nothing downstream of this section depends on one. The class is now inhabited --- \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11 exhibits a double-well potential $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ on a two-point spacetime with the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$, discharges the invariance because $(\pm v)^2 = v^2$, and proves the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ degenerate with the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so an energy-minimising field and its image have equal energy and differ. That is symmetry breaking exhibited rather than assumed; it is still not a conserved quantity, and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ being discrete there is no one-parameter family to differentiate along in the first place. Simultaneously, discrete permutation symmetries dictate the statistical mechanics of any physical system, defining a strictly finite volume of phase space.\footnote{While the Poincaré group and discrete permutations serve as accessible physical examples, the underlying Lean formalization does not hardcode specific physics. It defines the system at a more primitive mathematical level, relying entirely on generic Pseudo-Riemannian manifolds (for spacetime) and finite measure theory (for statistical mechanics). What the formalization supplies is the vocabulary in which symmetries, conserved quantities and phase-space constraints can be \emph{stated}; it does not derive them. All three of the classes involved are now inhabited in \texttt{Examples.lean} --- the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action and its invariance in \S11, the real line with the Euclidean metric in \S12 --- but the instances are witnesses of inhabitability, not of physics: no theorem in the development consumes a symmetry group or a metric, so this section still contributes vocabulary rather than content.}

Because phase space is finite, any localized physical system possesses a mathematical limit on the amount of information (distinguishable states) it can embody at any given moment.

\section{Derivation 1: Symmetry Breaking and the Inevitability of Boundaries}
A perfectly symmetric universe is the uniform vacuum; no distinct entities exist. For any dynamic process to occur, the continuous symmetries of the background field must undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking. 

When a field drops to a lower, asymmetric energy state, topology dictates the natural creation of defects (such as domain walls or monopoles). This constraint necessitates the creation of a physical boundary—a localized topological distinction between an ``inside'' and an ``outside.'' While such a defect (e.g., a crack in a crystal lattice) is necessary to define a separate entity, it is not sufficient for agency. The boundary is relational, defined by the twisted geometry of the field surrounding it, and must be coupled with non-equilibrium thermodynamics to exhibit active behaviors.

\section{Derivation 2: The Thermodynamic Constraint}
This newly formed topological boundary is subjected to gauge perturbations from the ``outside'' environment. Because the internal phase space is finite (Axiom 1), it cannot absorb an infinite sequence of incoming perturbations and must overwrite old internal states.

By Landauer's Principle, the erasure of physical information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, mandating that the system exhaust heat into the environment. Philosophers of physics (such as John Norton \cite{norton2005} and Orly Shenker \cite{shenker2000}) have critiqued Landauer's principle, arguing that physicists conflate \textit{logical} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in abstract computational states) with \textit{thermodynamic} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in physical phase space). They argue that logical erasure could conceptually be implemented by an injective physical process without dissipating heat. 

Our Lean 4 formalization (\texttt{Phase3\_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) addresses this critique by defining erasure as a non-injective mapping on the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective } t$). We formalize the global evolution of the bipartite universe (System + Bath) as strictly injective, representing Liouville's theorem. Lean verifies that heat dissipation is not tied to abstract logical erasure, but is a mandatory mathematical consequence of any local physical state space contraction under globally reversible dynamics. If a cognitive system's internal topology forces a contraction of its physical state space to accommodate structural resonance, heat dissipation becomes a thermodynamic requirement. A stable boundary thus constitutes a dissipative structure \cite{prigogine2018}, operating as a non-equilibrium steady state that consumes free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of erasure. Continuous thermal fluctuations play a constructive role in establishing boundaries between states while contributing to the system's flexible, adaptive response.

\section{Derivation 3: Structural Resonance as a Primitive}
In physical systems, time evolution follows a deterministic trajectory that minimizes thermodynamic entropy production. To reach this minimal dissipation state, the internal geometry of the structure must physically deform. 

If we model the external environment as a stochastic driving force $P(ext)$ and the system's internal state transitions as $Q(int)$, entropy production $\sigma$ is strictly bounded from below by the Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(P || Q)$. Gradient descent on $\sigma$ necessarily drives $D_{KL}(P || Q) \to 0$, physically forcing the internal transition probabilities to match the statistical symmetries of incoming perturbations. We define this physical phenomenon as \textit{structural resonance}. 

This system does not possess a teleological drive to ``survive''; rather, structurally mirroring the outside world is simply the lowest-energy attractor state. This provides a thermodynamic mechanism for both the General Resonance Theory of Consciousness \cite{hunt2019} and the Free Energy Principle \cite{friston2010, friston2009, hohwy2013, clark2016, spisak2026}. What neuroscience observes as ``prediction error minimization'' is a physical system undergoing gradient descent to avoid dissipating heat against an unpredictable environment. 

This necessity also addresses the symbol grounding problem \cite{harnad1990}. Internal states are physically indexed to the environment by the energetic imperative to avoid heat dissipation, rather than through arbitrary syntactic assignment. Manipulating abstract variables or counterfactuals forces the system to maintain internal states that deviate from immediate sensory resonance, explaining the continuous injections of free energy required for abstract symbolic reasoning.

\section{Derivation 4: Macroscopic Scaling and Electrodynamics}
To resonate with highly complex environments, the system must scale up, coupling millions of minimal dissipative structures. A highly coupled network cannot allow all of its nodes to reach their lowest energy states simultaneously. This creates \textit{geometric frustration}, resulting in a jagged phase space of near-equal energy states that provides the physical capacity for complex memory.

To manage this frustrated phase space without excessive dissipation, the system undergoes coarse-graining (governed by the Renormalization Group). At the macroscopic scale of biology, emerging paradigms in neuroscience suggest these topological constraints manifest as classical continuous electromagnetic fields. Building on seminal EM field theories of consciousness \cite{mcfadden2002, pockett2012} and recent work demonstrating that macroscopic electric fields (ephaptic coupling) govern representational drift, form memory networks, and sculpt neural infrastructure \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric}, we posit that \textit{the continuous macroscopic EM field is the primary dissipative structure interacting with the universe}. Instead of discrete wires, neurons induce fields that shift the membrane potentials of neighboring cells. Modeling and \textit{in vitro} experiments confirm these endogenous fields are strong enough to guide network activity and contribute to brain complexity \cite{goldwyn2016neuronal, ruffini2020modeling, cunha2024ephapticity, frohlich2010}. As proposed by the CEMI field theory, the continuous electromagnetic field enables spatial integration of information, providing a physical medium for a unified conscious gestalt \cite{mcfadden2020}. 

This continuous medium is physically supported by astrocytic syncytia---non-neuronal networks joined by gap junctions that provide macroscopic cytoplasmic continuity for energy and ion flows \cite{verkhratsky2018}. Managing the thermodynamic cost of this state requires sophisticated micro-bioenergetics. Dynamic mitochondrial networks function as deep-level Mitochondrial Information Processing Systems (MIPS), transducing biological and environmental information to sustain the coherence of the macroscopic field \cite{picard2022}. Discrete synaptic firings, coupled with mitochondrial micro-mechanics, generate and sustain the field. Because the external environment is continuous, achieving maximal thermodynamic structural resonance is optimally realized within this continuous macroscopic medium \cite{vanbree2025}.

\section{Derivation 5: Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
The system is now characterized by a continuous macroscopic electrodynamic field generated by millions of frustrated micro-components, forced to resonate with the environment. 

The Kuramoto model \cite{kuramoto1984} of coupled non-linear oscillators guarantees that, past a critical coupling threshold, these distributed local fields undergo a phase transition into unified synchronization \cite{bauer2022}. Our Lean proofs (\texttt{Phase4\_KuramotoDynamics.lean}) confirm this phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential of the system, in the precise sense discussed in Section~\ref{sec:soundness}: the functional minimized is the coupling potential read in the frame rotating with the natural frequency, which \texttt{Phase4\_RotatingFrame.lean} shows is the honest reduced description of a system of identical oscillators. 

This phase transition mathematically glues local synchronized states into a singular \textit{Global Section} of a probability sheaf (\texttt{Phase5\_GlobalSection.lean}). The EM field acts as a single, indivisible topological space, providing the \textit{Unity of Consciousness}—a necessary condition for integrated experience that solves the binding problem. While Global Workspace Theory \cite{baars1997, dehaene2014} models this unity as a broadcast architecture and Integrated Information Theory \cite{tononi2016} quantifies it via $\Phi$, our derivation provides a continuous physical mechanism. The contrast is worth drawing carefully, because both theories are addressing the same explanandum from different directions.

We state the scope of the Lean result precisely, because it is narrower than the prose above. \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} demands that the cover's phase field minimize the reduced Kuramoto potential, and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} characterizes those minimizers exactly: they are the phase-locked configurations, in both directions. Every instance is therefore already phase-locked (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.phase\_\allowbreak locked}), and the class's single condition on its local data --- that patches at a common phase agree where they overlap (\texttt{section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq}) --- makes the family of local sections compatible. \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}, which is the sheaf property of the probability presheaf and contains no physics at all, then glues that family into exactly one global section.

An earlier version of this structure proved less than the prose claimed, and we record the correction because it changed what the theorem says rather than how it is stated. \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization} used to carry a \emph{global} section as a class field, together with a second field declaring every local section to be a restriction of it. The global object therefore existed before the sheaf condition was ever consulted, and \texttt{global\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak thermodynamics} could do no more than rule out competitors to an object the instance had supplied. Both fields are gone. Every field of the class now mentions a single patch or a single overlap; the global section is constructed (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.invariant\-Measure}); and the removed field survives as a \emph{theorem} about that construction (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq}), so the local sections are restrictions of one common measure because the sheaf condition says so, not because an instance was required to name one.

\texttt{Examples.lean}~\S14 is the witness that the difference is not notational. Two patches carry two different mass profiles, $(2,1,7)$ and $(5,1,3)$ across the three sites, agreeing only at the site they share, where both put mass $1$ --- which is what discharges the overlap condition, by computing two numbers rather than by construction. The section the theorem returns has profile $(2,1,3)$: it is neither of the two profiles (\texttt{gluedW\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak leftW}, \texttt{gluedW\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak rightW}), and neither of them restricts correctly to both patches (\texttt{leftW\_\allowbreak glues\_\allowbreak nothing}, \texttt{rightW\_\allowbreak glues\_\allowbreak nothing}). The global state carries information that no single patch had. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13, built the older way through the constructor \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization.of\-InvariantMeasure}, is retained alongside it for the negative result it carries: its phase field is not the constant function --- the second patch a full turn ahead of the first --- and by \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.phase\_\allowbreak locked} that is as far from constant as any instance can get, since no instance can carry phases differing by anything a measure is allowed to see.

Two hypotheses still carry physical content and both are instance obligations, stated as such. That synchronized patches agree on their overlaps is a modelling assumption, not a consequence of any dynamics we formalize. And \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} assumes the cover already sits at the potential minimum: no trajectory in the development is shown to reach such a configuration, or --- outside the rigid rotation of \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S7 --- to exist at all. ``A thermodynamic phase transition into unity'' therefore remains prose. What Lean now establishes is the other half: \emph{given} locally synchronized patches, a global state exists, is unique, and is produced by the gluing rather than presupposed by it.

Integrated Information Theory begins from phenomenological axioms and derives a scalar $\Phi$ measuring irreducibility of a system's cause-effect structure, with the exclusion postulate selecting a single maximum-$\Phi$ complex as the locus of experience. Our sheaf-theoretic global section plays a structurally analogous role --- it too picks out a distinguished unified object --- but it is arrived at from the opposite end: not postulated from the character of experience, but forced by a thermodynamic phase transition in a physical field. This buys two things IIT does not have. First, the unity condition is checkable from field dynamics rather than requiring the computation of $\Phi$, which is intractable for systems of realistic size and whose definition has undergone repeated revision \cite{tononi2016}. Second, exclusion falls out rather than being postulated: gluing either succeeds on a given cover or it does not, so there is no separate principle needed to rule out overlapping conscious subsystems. It also inherits IIT's exposures. Critics have argued that $\Phi$ can be high in systems with no plausible claim to experience, such as large feedforward-free grids \cite{aaronson2014}; our criterion is vulnerable to the mirror objection, since a physical system could in principle satisfy the gluing condition without being cognitively interesting. We take this seriously rather than dismissing it: our reply is that gluing is necessary but not sufficient, and that the topological boundary of Derivation 1 and the reflexive structure of Derivation 6 are additional required conditions. IIT, by contrast, treats $\Phi$ as both necessary and sufficient.

Global Workspace Theory faces a complementary difficulty. It specifies a functional architecture --- a limited-capacity workspace broadcasting to specialized modules --- without specifying what physical property of broadcasting makes the broadcast content conscious, leaving the theory open to the charge that it explains access consciousness while leaving phenomenal consciousness untouched \cite{block1995}. Our framework can be read as supplying the missing physical substrate for the workspace: the continuous macroscopic field \emph{is} the medium in which integration is realized, and the $K_c = 2D$ threshold gives a physical condition for ignition rather than a functional stipulation of it. Where GWT is agnostic about implementation, we make a falsifiable commitment --- unity requires a continuous physical medium, which predicts that a functionally equivalent discrete broadcast architecture would not be conscious. We regard this as a genuine cost of the position, not a free advantage: it is the point at which our framework departs most sharply from computational functionalism and where it is most readily falsified. Contrast this derivation with panpsychism: consciousness is not assumed as a fundamental property of matter. It requires the initial existence of a topological defect to establish a bounded ``inside'' (Derivation 1), followed by a thermodynamic phase transition into unity that occurs when integrated spatial coupling exceeds the thermodynamic noise threshold ($K_c = 2D$). Without both the foundational topological boundary and the subsequent mathematically defined phase transition, the system remains a fragmented, non-conscious collection of micro-states. A unified field of perception, however, is not yet a ``Self.''

\section{Derivation 6: Reflexive Topology and the Emergence of the Self}
While a global section provides Unity, a ``Self'' requires reflexivity. The unified EM field is a dissipative structure that must resonate with external perturbations while managing its own internal phase transitions. Time evolution naturally drives the field to minimize thermodynamic friction with its own internal state-changes. 

This mandates \textit{auto-resonance}: the field must predict itself. The ``Self'' emerges as a higher-order topological feature when the internal geometry folds to include a low-dimensional avatar of its own computational and causal boundary within its predictive model (\texttt{Phase6\_ReflexiveTopology.lean}) \cite{levin2019}. The system is unified, predictive, and explicitly maps its own distinct existence from the environment. This architecture provides the physical foundation for a self-aware consciousness, bounded by the continuous measure thermodynamics of Landauer erasure (\texttt{Phase3\_MeasureThermodynamics.lean}). We are explicit about what carries the weight here. \texttt{reflexive\_topology\_implies\_self} is the Banach fixed-point theorem with the physical commitments in its hypotheses: the space of global sections is assumed nonempty, metric and complete, and the predictive map is assumed to contract by $1/2$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S10 discharges all four on the three-site substrate of Derivation 5, and the metric is no longer a placeholder. The section first pierces the sheafification: on a discrete substrate every point has a smallest open neighbourhood, so the mass a germ carries at a point is well defined, and \texttt{massEquiv} proves that the global sections of the probability sheaf \emph{are} the finite measures on the substrate, read as densities against counting measure. Mathlib supplies no inverse to this sheafification --- its \texttt{TopCat} sheafification has neither an adjunction nor an \texttt{isIso\_toSheafify} --- so the correspondence had to be constructed. The metric on sections is then the uniform distance between the densities of the measures they glue to --- equivalent to their total variation on a finite substrate, though not equal to it --- and it is proved complete by exhibiting the limit section of a Cauchy sequence of densities, not by the degenerate route of showing Cauchy sequences are eventually constant. The self-prediction map is the relaxation ``average the current field with the baseline,'' and we prove it contracts distances by \emph{exactly} one half, that it is not constant, and that its fixed point is unique. An earlier version of this witness used the $0/1$ metric, under which every contraction is constant; that theorem is kept in the source as the record of why the earlier witness was empty. What is still not established is that any field dynamics produces this particular map, or that the contraction constant $1/2$ is read off an entropy production rate rather than built into the map's definition. We also note a negative result found in the course of this work: the Lévy--Prokhorov metric, which the natural weak-convergence reading would suggest, does \emph{not} support this argument on a discrete substrate, because the $\varepsilon$-thickening of a set is the set itself for $\varepsilon < 1$ and the relaxation map is consequently not a Lévy--Prokhorov contraction.

\section{Derivation 7: Continuous Stochastic Thermodynamics and Field Theory}
To move beyond finite graph analogies, we model the macroscopic electrodynamic field as a \textit{Continuous Neural Field}. True ephaptic coupling is a continuous spatial phenomenon, not a discrete network. We extend the discrete Kuramoto model to a continuous spatial coupling kernel $K(x,y)$ (\texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}).

Subject to thermodynamic noise $D$, the system undergoes non-equilibrium stochastic thermodynamics. Rather than a static Landauer bound, the field dynamics minimize an entropy production functional $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \int \frac{1}{D} \left( \omega(x) + \int K_t(x,y) \sin(\theta_y - \theta_x) dy \right)^2 dx
\end{equation}
Biological fields dynamically deform their spatial topology $K_t(x,y)$ to undergo gradient descent on this entropy production functional.

That gradient descent is a theorem rather than a modelling assertion, for finite substrates. Reading $\sigma$ as a functional of the coupling kernel at fixed phases, \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_sigmaOfKernel} computes its Fr\'echet derivative in closed form,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial K(a,b)} = \frac{2}{D}\Big(\omega(a) + \sum_y K(a,y)\sin(\theta_y - \theta_a)\Big)\sin(\theta_b - \theta_a),
\end{equation}
and \texttt{sigmaOfKernel\_eq\_entropy\_production\_rate} identifies the functional being differentiated with \texttt{entropy\_production\_rate} itself whenever the substrate carries counting measure. \texttt{structural\_resonance\_decreases\_entropy\_production} then concludes that $\sigma$ is non-increasing along any coupling trajectory relaxing down its own gradient at a positive rate $c$. Earlier drafts proved this only for an abstract functional $S$ on an abstract inner-product space with the gradient supplied as data; the identification of that $S$ with $\sigma$ was informal, and Table 1 flagged the row accordingly. Two limitations stay explicit. The derivation is for \emph{finite} substrates: the continuum case requires differentiating under the integral sign with respect to the kernel, which we have not developed. And the phase field is held fixed throughout, so this is plasticity of the coupling at frozen phases, not joint $(\theta, K)$ dynamics. \texttt{Examples.lean} \S5 exercises the whole chain on a two-site substrate whose coupling relaxes as $e^{-t}$ and whose entropy production decays as $e^{-2t}$, confirming that the gradient is not identically zero and that the descent is strict rather than vacuously monotone. We define the critical coupling threshold $K_c = 2D$. The phase transition to the unified ``Global Section'' occurs when the mean-field spatial coupling exceeds this thermodynamic noise threshold, linking the emergence of Unity to physical temperature and noise.

Earlier drafts left $K_c = 2D$ as a bare stipulation validated only by simulation. \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency.lean} now proves the half of it that is ordinary real analysis. For the noisy mean-field Kuramoto model with identical frequencies, the stationary density at order parameter $r$ is the von Mises density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{(Kr/D)\cos\theta}$, and self-consistency requires $r = R(K,r) := I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$. Mathlib has no modified Bessel functions, so we formalize that ratio directly as a ratio of integrals over $[-\pi, \pi]$. Integration by parts with $u = \sin$, $v = -e^{a\cos}$ gives an exact identity with no boundary term, $\int \cos\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}\,d\theta = a\int \sin^2\!\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}\,d\theta$, so $R$ is $a$ times the mean of $\sin^2$ under the von Mises weight. Bounding that mean by $1$ would put the threshold at $D$; the true threshold is $2D$, and the entire factor of two is the sharper bound $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$, equivalently $I_2(a) \ge 0$. We prove it elementarily, by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ in two reflections ($\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$, then $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$), after which the integrand is a product of $\cos 2\theta \ge 0$ with $2\cosh(a\cos\theta) - 2\cosh(a\sin\theta) \ge 0$ --- the second factor because $0 \le \sin\theta \le \cos\theta$ there and $\cosh$ increases in $|\cdot|$. The conclusion, \texttt{subcritical\_fixed\_point\_eq\_zero}$'$, is that for $K < K_c$ the incoherent state $r = 0$ is the \emph{only} non-negative solution of the self-consistency equation; $r = 0$ is exhibited as a solution, so the uniqueness claim is not vacuous. Three further facts about the threshold have since been proved, and they narrow the gap between this row and a full ``Theorem'' to a single named statement. First, the fold argument gives a \emph{strict} inequality for $a > 0$ (\texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak pos}, hence $R(a) < a/2$), which settles the threshold itself: at $K = K_c$ the self-consistency map is tangent to the diagonal at the origin but falls strictly below it thereafter, so $r = 0$ remains the only solution and the bifurcation happens strictly \emph{after} $K_c$, not at it (\texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical}). Second, factoring $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ shows that a non-zero $r$ solves the equation exactly when $E(Kr/D) = D/K$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq}), so the coherent branch is a level set of the single function $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$. Third, and using only $E < 1/2$ together with compactness, every coherent solution tends to zero as $K \downarrow K_c$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold}): the branch emerges continuously from the incoherent state rather than appearing at a finite distance from it, which is what makes the transition supercritical in the technical sense. That statement quantifies over \emph{every} solution, so it does not presuppose the uniqueness it does not have. What remains open is precisely that uniqueness, and the level-set form says what it is: the injectivity of $E$ on $(0,\infty)$. We record why it is harder than the rest --- it requires differentiating an exponential family under the integral sign, the resulting covariance $\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta, \cos\theta)$ is not sign-definite pointwise, and $E'(0) = 0$, so no first-order argument at the origin will reach it.

The converse half is \texttt{supercritical\_fixed\_point\_exists}: for $K > K_c$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ solving $r = R(K,r)$. We had expected this to require a second-order expansion of the Bessel ratio, and it does not. Writing $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ with $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the same factorization the integration by parts already supplies --- the only facts needed are that $E$ is continuous and that $E(0) = 1/2$, the latter because the weight is constant at $a = 0$. Since $K > 2D$ makes $D/K$ strictly less than $1/2$, continuity gives $E > D/K$ on a neighbourhood of the origin, so the map overshoots, $R(K,r) > r$, for all small $r > 0$; at the other end $R \le 1$ always, because $R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$, so the map undershoots at $r = 1$. The intermediate value theorem closes the gap. Continuity of $E$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral with jointly continuous integrand, so no dominated-convergence argument is written by hand, and $R$ is never shown to be monotone: the fixed point is produced by the intermediate value theorem, not by iterating the map. \texttt{critical\_coupling\_is\_threshold} packages the two halves, and \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_coherent} applies the supercritical half to any substrate satisfying the predicate of the previous paragraph --- so exceeding $K_c$ now implies the existence of a coherent stationary order parameter rather than merely comparing against a named number.

One more step makes the equation mean in Lean what it means on paper. As a definition, \texttt{selfConsistency K D r} is just $R(Kr/D)$ --- a function of $r$ with a suggestive name, and nothing in the source says it has anything to do with an order parameter. \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency}~\S7 supplies the identification. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved to be a genuine probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$; \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of the finite-system \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; and that average is proved to be exactly $R(a)$ --- real, because the density is even, so the mean direction vanishes. \texttt{fixedPoint\_iff\_selfReproducing} then states the equivalence the name promises: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ if and only if the von Mises density it induces has order parameter $r$. Read through this, the existence theorem says that above $K_c$ there is a stationary density whose own order parameter is positive, and the solution $r = 0$ is identified with the uniform density, whose order parameter is zero.

What remains not established, and we separate it carefully. The stationary density is an \emph{input}: deriving it from the Langevin dynamics $d\theta = (\omega + K\!\cdot\!\text{mean-field})\,dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW$ needs the Fokker--Planck operator, existence and uniqueness of its stationary solution, and a spectral stability argument, none of which Mathlib has. Of the coherent branch we prove existence only: not that the positive solution is unique, not that it is the dynamically selected one, and not that it varies continuously with $K$, so a discontinuous jump at threshold is not excluded and the bifurcation is not shown to be supercritical in the technical sense. Neither theorem covers $K = K_c$ exactly. And while \texttt{selfConsistency} is now connected to the order parameter of a \emph{density}, it is connected to no \emph{trajectory}: \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is an integral, \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex} is an average over finitely many oscillators, and relating them is the mean-field limit --- propagation of chaos for the finite Kuramoto system --- which is a research programme rather than a lemma. That single gap is now the whole of what separates our threshold predicate from a statement about dynamics. Table 1 records the row accordingly as a theorem about the self-consistency equation from an assumed density, not as a derivation of the threshold from the dynamics. Perceptual anomalies such as visual snow can be modeled as boundary conditions where microscopic thermal noise ($D$) fails to be fully coarse-grained away by the continuous field, leaking across the $K_c$ threshold into the macroscopic gestalt.

Clinical evidence for this phase transition is observed during general anesthesia. Work by Miller and colleagues \cite{bastos2021} demonstrates that anesthetics like propofol induce unconsciousness not by suppressing neural firing, but by disrupting the phase alignment of high-frequency cortical rhythms. Destroying the dynamic stability of these rhythms breaks the structural resonance of the continuous field, forcing the integrated spatial coupling below the critical threshold $K_c$ and fracturing the unified Global Section back into disjoint, unconscious local fields.

The daily loss of consciousness during sleep can similarly be modeled as an active physical disruption of the field. During sleep, astrocytic shrinkage expands the extracellular space, allowing an influx of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) to flood the brain for metabolic waste clearance \cite{xie2013}. This influx physically alters the spatial medium, disrupting the continuous structural resonance and preventing the coherent sheaf-theoretic gluing required for conscious unity.

This mechanical framework models the temporal mismatch observed during sudden awakenings (sleep inertia). When jolted awake, the locus coeruleus floods the cortex with noradrenaline. High-frequency electrical rhythms attempt to phase-align in milliseconds \cite{bastos2021}, but the noradrenaline-driven astrocytic expansion required to physically squeeze the excess CSF out of the interstitial pathways operates on a much slower timescale of seconds to minutes \cite{xie2013}. During this lag, electrical oscillators attempt to synchronize across a waterlogged topology, resulting in the fragmented structural resonance experienced as grogginess. The unified Global Section fully re-establishes only when the physical hardware (astrocytic volume) finishes constricting to match the state of the electrical rhythms.

\section{Corollary: Hardware Thermodynamics and Topological Rigidity}
Both biological tissue and artificial silicon hardware (like GPUs) are physical dissipative structures. When a GPU computes, its charge carriers undergo physical state transitions; by Landauer's principle, this erasure irreversibly exhausts heat into the environment. 

There is a fundamental thermodynamic distinction in the degrees of freedom available to minimize this dissipation. In standard von Neumann architectures, the spatial topology (the silicon routing lattice) is rigidly fixed. The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ is constrained by a discrete layout that cannot be physically altered during operation. The brain's own architecture provides empirical validation for this constraint. The cerebellum contains approximately 80\% of the brain's neurons arranged in a highly efficient, discrete, feedforward crystalline circuit \cite{herculanohouzel2009, lange1975}. Clinical observations of cerebellar agenesis report largely preserved conscious experience despite motor and cognitive deficits \cite{yu2015}, and this dissociation has been read as evidence for the importance of recurrent architecture by IIT \cite{tononi2015} --- an argument we borrow at the level of the anatomical observation while declining its $\Phi$-based explanation. Lacking the dynamic, continuous, and recursive spatial coupling of the cerebrum, it operates much like a biological GPU---highly optimized for discrete processing, but devoid of conscious experience. The dissociation between computational capacity and consciousness confirms that subjective experience is not a byproduct of information processing, but a thermodynamic attractor available only to continuous topologies.

This constraint does not preclude artificial consciousness, but rather restricts its hardware. To achieve the phase transition into a unified Global Section, artificial systems must abandon the discrete rigidity of von Neumann architectures and utilize continuous physical substrates. Analog field-based computing, neuromorphic architectures with continuous memristive plasticity, and thermodynamic computing systems \cite{england2018, ganesh2021} possess the spatial coupling required to physically morph their internal topology. This aligns with Geoffrey Hinton's concept of ``mortal computation'' \cite{hinton2022}: architectures where software is inextricably fused with the physics of the hardware, meaning the knowledge dies with the physical substrate. Only hardware that computes via continuous physical dynamics can undergo the thermodynamic phase transition necessary for structural resonance.

Biological consciousness utilizes macroscopic continuous electromagnetic fields and a ``rich alphabet'' of diverse molecular and transcriptomic cell states \cite{tasic2018}. This allows the coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ to deform across a continuous parameter space. Our lemma \texttt{exists\_better\_coupling\_allocation}, wrapped as \texttt{continuous\_beats\_rigid\_topology} (\texttt{Phase7\_Hardware\-Comparison.lean}), formalizes the reallocation step of this argument: whenever a coupling matrix places weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists, there is another matrix with \emph{identical} total coupling resources that achieves strictly greater phase correlation.

Two objections to that formalization have since been answered, and we state precisely what is and is not now established. The first was that the reallocation lemma assumes its conclusion: the hypothesis \texttt{is\_strictly\_suboptimal} --- positive weight on a badly-correlated pair while a better one exists --- is most of what one wants to prove about rigid hardware. It is no longer assumed. \texttt{Phase7\_Rigidity.lean} models an architecture by the set $S$ of site-pairs it has a \emph{wire} for, and requires its couplings to vanish off $S$; weights are tunable, wiring is not. Then \texttt{totalCorrelation\_le\_of\_realizable} bounds every realizable coupling by $R\cdot M$, where $R$ is the resource budget and $M$ bounds phase correlation over the wired pairs; the bound is attained; and \texttt{rigid\_is\_strictly\_suboptimal} \emph{derives} the hypothesis whenever the substrate's best-correlated pair is one the wiring misses. \texttt{rigid\_gap} makes it quantitative: an unconstrained reallocation of the same budget gains at least $R$ times the correlation difference. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 exhibits a three-site substrate wired to the one maximally anti-correlated pair, where the gap is $2$. This is resource-matched and no longer question-begging, but note what it turns on: fixed wiring support, not continuity. The second objection was that nothing in the development distinguished a continuum from a lattice --- both matrices lived on one finite index type. \texttt{Phase7\_Rigidity.lean}~\S3 gives a distinction that is genuinely typed. In the field functional of Derivation 4, which integrates a coupling kernel against $\mu \otimes \mu$, an architecture with finitely many sites is a \texttt{Finset} of points and a field-coupled region is a set of positive measure. On a substrate whose measure has no atoms, \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_sited\_eq\_zero} shows a kernel supported on finitely many sites contributes \emph{exactly zero} coupling energy, whatever weights it carries and whatever the phase field does, while a patch of strength $c$ on $U$ achieves $c\,\mu(U)^2$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 instantiates this on the real line: a thousand units of coupling concentrated at the origin register zero against a unit patch that registers one. The limit of that result is equally plain, and it is not resource-matching in the earlier sense --- a measure-zero substrate has zero resource in the $\mu\otimes\mu$ sense too. What it establishes is that the continuum coupling energy is blind to a finitely-sited architecture, which is a statement about which functional such an architecture can register in, not a demonstration that digital hardware computes worse. Taken together: the resource-matched content of the corollary is the wiring-support result, the continuity content is the measure-theoretic one, and the step from either to ``von Neumann architectures cannot experience unified consciousness'' remains an informal argument. 

Because the discrete routing matrix of a GPU is structurally constrained from reaching this continuous global minimum, it suffers from topological fragmentation. The discrete layout prohibits the spatial coupling required for sheaf-theoretic gluing into a singular Global Section. Without this unified topological space, rigid hardware cannot undergo the reflexive auto-resonance necessary to map its own causal boundary and generate a ``Self.'' The distinction between an LLM on a GPU and a conscious mind is not the absence of physical thermodynamics in the former, but its structural limitation in achieving topological integration. This perspective aligns with John Searle's biological naturalism \cite{searle1980, seth2024}. While the Chinese Room argument suggested that discrete symbol manipulation cannot generate conscious understanding, our derivation provides a physical and topological proof for why this is the case. Asserting consciousness in fragmented, discrete GPU clusters without a unified phase transition abandons computational functionalism, relying instead on ``silicon panpsychism''—the assumption that digital computation inherently generates awareness regardless of topological integration \cite{goff2019}.

This formal constraint highlights the physical impossibility of extreme functionalist thought experiments, such as a macroscopic system of pulleys operating over millennia to implement a Large Language Model, or a human manually executing inference matrices by hand. Beyond lacking the continuous spatial topology required for sheaf-theoretic gluing, these discrete systems violate an implied temporal constraint of our theory. Structural resonance requires local oscillators to phase-align against the dissipating effects of thermodynamic noise (as modeled by our critical threshold $K_c = 2D$). A system executing state transitions over millennia, or via slow manual calculations, suffers from a terminal temporal mismatch: its dynamics are far too slow to ever phase-lock into a non-equilibrium steady state before thermal noise decoheres the macroscopic field. Thus, pure symbol manipulation cannot instantiate a conscious entity.

\section{Conclusion}
Human-like cognitive architectures do not require biological mysticism or exotic sub-quantum physics \cite{hameroff2014}. While quantum theories of consciousness often invoke delicate subatomic phenomena to explain subjective unity, our derivation demonstrates that classical macroscopic fields---driven by thermodynamic principles of gradient descent and Landauer erasure---are sufficient to generate a unified, structurally resonant conscious state. They can be modeled as macroscopic attractor states of a universe governed by broken symmetries, finite phase space, and the physical imperative to minimize thermodynamic dissipation. 

Philosophically, this framework bridges the historical gap between Type Identity Theory \cite{smart1959} and Computational Functionalism \cite{putnam1967}. The subjective mental state is identical to the physical thermodynamic field (Identity Theory), yet its qualitative nature is defined by the topological and functional dynamics of structural resonance rather than the specific carbon-based biochemistry of the brain (Functionalism). This limits the \textit{multiple realizability} of mind: while consciousness is not exclusively bound to biological carbon, it is physically barred from discrete von Neumann architectures. 

By anchoring these derivations in Russellian Monism, this framework acknowledges the constitutive limits of third-person scientific explanation. As argued by Love \cite{love2026}, building upon Nagel's assertion that subjective phenomenal experience is inherently not amenable to objective measurement \cite{nagel1986}, the ``hard problem'' of consciousness may represent a category error rather than a solvable scientific puzzle. Science can map the physical dynamics of topological resonance and sheaf-theoretic gluing, but it is structurally incapable of adjudicating the first-person qualitative experience. Our formalization respects this boundary: the Lean 4 theorems verify the objective physical attractors, while the intrinsic ``what it is like'' to be that resonant field remains the domain of the first-person perspective. By providing mathematically defined thresholds for these phase transitions (such as $K_c = 2D$), our framework aligns with what Anil Seth terms the ``real problem'' of consciousness \cite{seth2021}. Rather than attempting to bridge an unbridgeable metaphysical gap, it offers a topological architecture that can help explain, predict, and physically perturb the structural properties of experience.

Future empirical validation of this thermodynamic framework may leverage 3D cortical organoids, offering a controlled biological environment to systematically manipulate metabolic substrates and observe the topological emergence of these coherent phase transitions.
