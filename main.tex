\begin{abstract}
We present a mathematical synthesis and formal Lean 4 verification of the dependencies in a candidate route from established physical primitives to cognitive attractor states, without introducing new fundamental physics. Starting from the finiteness of a localized system's phase space, we construct a framework for predictive processing; the spacetime symmetries are the setting rather than a premise, and no theorem consumes them. Spontaneous symmetry breaking generates necessary topological boundaries, and finite phase space subjects these boundaries to Landauer's thermodynamic erasure. Agency arises when systems evolve along paths of least action, minimizing entropy production via internal structural resonance with external perturbations. Scaling this dynamic to macroscopic levels aligns with emerging paradigms in neuroscience—specifically classical electrodynamic fields (ephaptic coupling)—and Kuramoto phase synchronization, culminating in a topological fixed point: a unified ``Self.'' We state the claim narrowly, because the subject matter invites a wider reading than the work supports: no new physics is derived here. Every physical result the chain invokes is established---Picard--Lindelöf, Kibble's classification of topological defects, Landauer's erasure bound, Gibbs' inequality, Still's bound on dissipation by nonpredictive information, Barbalat's lemma, Sakaguchi's threshold, Banach's fixed point. What is new is that they are stated in one formal language, their consistency mechanically checked, and the points where the chain relies on stipulation rather than derivation located exactly. One conditional theorem composes eight named hypotheses to its endpoint; the eight unproved steps are its arguments rather than sentences about it, and they are jointly satisfiable rather than jointly empty. The development declares no axioms and every structure carrying physical content is inhabited by a witness; the synchronization step is proved from a dynamics for initial data inside an explicit basin and assumed outside it, and the identification of a global section of the probability sheaf with the unity of experience is a stipulation, marked as such throughout rather than absorbed into the derivation. After the scalar coarse-graining theorem, the framework posits a continuum kernel and identifies it with the brain's endogenous electromagnetic field; these are respectively a modelling assumption and an empirical commitment, and four conditions would falsify the latter. The framework is nonetheless falsifiable in one place that is its own and no rival's: recovery from sudden awakening should follow a delayed sigmoid rather than an exponential, because the variable gating it is astrocytic volume on a timescale of seconds to minutes rather than cortical phase realignment on a timescale of milliseconds; and, more sharply, the concentration and the coherence of the phase distribution must collapse onto a single parameter-free curve, the Bessel ratio, throughout that recovery. As a corollary, we show why rigid von Neumann architectures (e.g., GPUs) are thermodynamically disadvantaged or structurally constrained from reaching these specific continuous thermodynamic attractors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness typically involves searching for the biological correlates of mind or treating subjective experience as an abstract computational algorithm. This paper asks a more fundamental question: If we assume only basic physical primitives—continuous symmetries (the Poincaré group), geometry, and thermodynamics—is the emergence of a unified, perceiving entity a natural physical attractor? 

We propose a framework in which the structural properties of consciousness are organized as candidate physical attractor states. Its narrative runs from continuous symmetries and boundaries, through dissipation and physical resonance, to the macroscopic electromagnetic unity proposed by field theories of mind. This is not a derivation from basic principles: the formal result is a conditional composition schema whose eight unproved arrows are explicit assumptions. To avoid critiques of ``math theatre,'' we delineate between literal physical mechanisms (e.g., thermodynamic erasure via Landauer's principle, boundaries from spontaneous symmetry breaking) and theoretical mappings, utilizing Lean 4 formalization as our mathematical anchor. We adopt a perspective aligned with Russellian Monism: objective measurements of macroscopic electromagnetic fields constitute the third-person ``outside,'' while the system's internal physical deformation to reach structural resonance constitutes the first-person intrinsic experience.

\subsection*{What is new here, and what is not}
A framework paper about consciousness invites a particular suspicion, and it is better answered at the start than at the end. \textbf{No new physics is derived here.} Every physical result the chain invokes is established, and most of it is old: Picard--Lindelöf for the well-posedness of the coupled dynamics \cite{coddington1955}; Kibble's classification of topological defects by the homotopy of the vacuum manifold \cite{kibble1976}; Landauer's bound on the heat cost of erasure \cite{landauer1961}; Gibbs' inequality for the relative entropy \cite{cover2006}; Barbalat's lemma for the asymptotics of a dissipating trajectory \cite{barbalat1959}; the threshold $K_c = 2D$ for the noisy mean-field Kuramoto model \cite{sakaguchi1988}; the bound of Still, Sivak, Bell and Crooks relating dissipation to nonpredictive information \cite{still2012}; the identity of Kawai, Parrondo and Van den Broeck equating dissipated work with the relative entropy between forward and time-reversed ensembles \cite{kawai2007}; and Banach's fixed-point theorem \cite{banach1922}. A physicist reading this paper will recognize all of it and would establish the point in three paragraphs if we did not. Conceding it costs nothing.

What is new is of a different kind, and it is a smaller claim than a theory of consciousness usually makes. These results have been stated in one formal language, their mutual consistency has been mechanically checked, and the places where the chain relies on stipulation rather than derivation have been located exactly. The value of a machine-checked chain is not that any link is novel; it is that the claim being made \emph{about} the chain is itself checkable, and we state it in the form a reader can check rather than in the form that asks to be believed. Four things. The development declares \textbf{no axioms}: \texttt{\#print axioms} on any result reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. Every physical postulate is a field of a class that each model discharges for itself rather than a global assertion, and every such class is inhabited by an explicit witness, so no theorem is vacuous for want of a model. The links form a single \emph{conditional composition theorem} --- \texttt{chain}, in \texttt{Chain.lean} --- with \textbf{eight named hypotheses}: four formalization gaps, two modelling assumptions and two physical commitments. Its conclusion is \texttt{UnifiedSelf}: the particular section produced by sheaf gluing is the unique fixed point of self-prediction, rather than Unity and Self being witnessed by unrelated sections. The n8$\to$n9 hypothesis explicitly carries both the nonlinear Lipschitz law and this state identification; neither is derived from field dynamics. The theorem \texttt{chain\_hypotheses\_jointly\_satisfiable} discharges all eight on one toy substrate, which rules out inconsistency but is not evidence that one cortical mechanism realizes the composition. The seams --- where physics stops and interpretation begins --- are thereby fixed in the source rather than negotiable in the prose.

The schema exposes two missing arrows. The capacity bound of the first link has no hypotheses at all, so an implication from it to the existence of a boundary is equivalent to assuming the boundary outright: those two links are independent starting points and not consecutive steps. What finiteness does imply directly is the heat cost, skipping the boundary entirely. Neither of the two arrows that are theorems is one the framework's usual narration draws. Section~\ref{sec:soundness} reports the corresponding soundness result: three tempting global axiom shapes each prove \texttt{False}, from one shared root cause.

The framework is not thereby unfalsifiable, and one of its predictions is its own rather than a rival's. Section~\ref{sec:prediction} argues that recovery from sudden awakening should follow a \emph{delayed sigmoid} rather than an exponential, because the variable gating recovery is astrocytic volume and cerebrospinal-fluid clearance, on a timescale of seconds to minutes, while the cortical rhythms whose realignment is usually taken to constitute the recovery move in milliseconds. That is a claim about functional form; it is fittable against data of a kind already collected; and a purely electrical account of the same recovery predicts a different form. Because a delayed sigmoid is what any slow saturating gate would give, the section also states the sharper claim the same mathematics supports: at every instant of the recovery the concentration and the coherence of the phase distribution must lie on the fixed curve $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$, the same curve for every subject, with no free parameters and with the threshold read off the ratio $a/r$ rather than fitted.

\subsection{Soundness of the formalization}
\label{sec:soundness}

Formalization is only as trustworthy as the axioms it rests on, and an axiom that is inconsistent silently makes every theorem downstream of it vacuous while leaving the build green. We report a case of exactly this, found by auditing our own development, because the failure mode is easy to reproduce and we have not seen it discussed in the applied-formalization literature.

The failure has one shape, and naming it is more useful than cataloguing its instances: \emph{an axiom that constrains a symbol it does not itself bind}. Such a declaration is not a postulate about one system. It is a constraint on every model of the class the symbol belongs to, including models built to violate it, and a class that has such a model is thereby inconsistent. Three concrete cases, each of which proves \texttt{False} on its own:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{spontaneous\_symmetry\_breaking\_pointwise\_min} took the potential-energy functional as an \emph{implicit} argument occurring only in its hypothesis \texttt{PotentialEnergy phi = V v0}. Instantiating that functional with the constant map $\lambda\_.\,V(v_0)$ discharges the hypothesis by \texttt{rfl} and yields the conclusion for an arbitrary field. With $V(v) = v^2$, $v_0 = 0$ and $\phi \equiv 1$ this proves $1 = 0$.
\item \texttt{landauer\_heat\_eq} equated the free class field \texttt{heat\_dissipation} with a bath-entropy difference \emph{for every instance} of the class. An instance with zero heat dissipation and a bath that gains one bit gives $0 = \log 2$.
\item \texttt{kl\_bound\_axiom} asserted $\sigma \ge D_{KL}(P\|Q)/\Delta t$ for \emph{all} $P$ and $Q$, while $\sigma$ is a single real fixed by a class field. Since $D_{KL}$ is unbounded above, a point mass against a uniform distribution refutes it.
\end{itemize}

Two further declarations of the same shape are worth recording because their refutation is different in kind. \texttt{phase\_invariant\_periodic} and \texttt{sync\_to\_section\_eq}, as global assertions pinning free fields of \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization}, are refutable as soon as the probability presheaf admits two distinct global sections --- so the refutation needs no arithmetic at all, only that the sheaf is not trivial. Neither is a postulate in the development: the periodicity condition is an obligation of the constructor that builds a cover from an invariant measure, and \texttt{sync\_to\_section\_eq} is a theorem about the global section the sheaf condition produces (Derivation 5).

The rule that prevents it is uniform, and we state it as the rule we follow: \emph{a physical postulate that mentions a class field must be a field of that class, never a standalone axiom quantified over all instances.} A postulate is then an obligation each model discharges, not a global claim about every model. Under that rule the development declares no axioms at all. Two of the statements above are not postulates in the first place --- pointwise vacuum minimization is proved from $\int f = 0 \wedge f \ge 0 \Rightarrow f = 0$ a.e., and Gibbs' inequality is Mathlib's --- and the genuinely physical ones, Landauer's heat identity and the two information bounds, are instance obligations.

This makes non-vacuity the live question, since an uninhabitable class makes its theorems vacuous just as surely as an inconsistent axiom did. \texttt{Examples.lean} therefore constructs witnesses: a one-bit erasure system with a genuine bath that discharges \texttt{heat\_eq} and reproduces $\Delta Q = k_B T \ln 2$; a perfectly resonant system discharging \texttt{kl\_bound}; and a scalar field on a one-point spacetime discharging \texttt{ActionPrinciples}. Derivation 5's gluing structures are the most demanding case and are witnessed too: \texttt{Examples.lean} builds a \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} over a three-site substrate carrying the discrete topology and its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, covered by two \emph{overlapping} open patches whose intersection is a single shared site. The local sections are germ families of a phase-indexed finite measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ pushed into the sheafification of the probability presheaf; the family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\texttt{phaseMeasure\_not\_const}), so the periodicity obligation of the constructor \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization.of\-InvariantMeasure} is discharged by an argument about $\cos$ rather than satisfied trivially, and \texttt{thermodynamic\_equilibrium} is \emph{derived} from \texttt{phase\_locked\_minimizes\_potential} rather than assumed. \texttt{global\_section\_from\_thermodynamics} then fires on this instance and glues the two patch-local sections into a unique global one. Two further covers answer the follow-up question of how non-degenerate such a witness can be made. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13 has a negative half worth recording: the phase field can be made non-constant as a function, but not as a physical state, because \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} forces every instance to a phase-locked configuration. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S14 is built the other way round, from two patches with independently chosen mass profiles that meet only on the site they share, and the section the gluing returns is neither of them. That is the same kind of result as \texttt{contracting\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak const} in Derivation 6 --- a proof that the witness is as strong as the class permits, rather than a witness that could be improved by trying harder.

What the witnesses establish is that the classes are inhabitable, so the theorems about them are not vacuous. What they do not establish is that a cortex is such an instance. In the cover above the phase field is already constant --- every patch locked at $\theta = 0$ --- which is precisely what makes the equilibrium obligation dischargeable; getting a physical system into that configuration is the content of the informal dynamical argument, not of the Lean development.

The same audit turned up a defect of a different kind, worth recording because it is not an axiom problem and would not have been caught by \texttt{\#print axioms}. \texttt{mesh\_refinement\_convergence} --- the claim that discrete edge weights converge to the continuous energy as the mesh is refined --- sat in a ``Conjectures'' section as an unproved \texttt{def \ldots : Prop}. It was not merely unproved but false. Nothing required the edge regions to cover the manifold, and $\mathrm{diam}\,\emptyset = 0$, so the triangulation whose edge regions are all empty satisfied the mesh hypothesis while contributing nothing to the sum, forcing $|0 - \int f| < \varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$. Its binder was dead as well: it quantified over a triangulation and then resolved the vertex type by instance search rather than through the bound variable, so the variable was never used. \texttt{Phase2\_MeshConvergence.lean} replaces it with a theorem. Convergence is now stated along a \emph{sequence} of triangulations of a named region $S$, each required to be regular --- edge regions measurable, without self-loops, disjoint across distinct edges, covering $S$, and anchored to the simplicial data in the sense that a vertex lies in the closure of every region of an edge at it. Closure rather than membership is forced: cells are disjoint, so a shared vertex belongs to at most one of them. The manuscript's discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) f(u)$, which samples only the first endpoint and is therefore not symmetric, is shown to equal exactly the midpoint rule $\sum_{u<v} w(u,v)\,\tfrac12(f(u)+f(v))$ over unordered edges; without that identification, reading the double sum as a Riemann sum would be a category error. At fixed mesh the error is at most $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$ with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and convergence follows for uniformly continuous integrands as the mesh shrinks. The regularity conditions pull against each other, so \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 discharges them with the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into half-open cells and checks that the approximation genuinely moves --- $\tfrac12$ at $N=1$ and $\tfrac14$ at $N=2$ for the tent function $x \mapsto |x - \tfrac12|$ --- rather than converging trivially. What is proved is convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate the accompanying simulation measures; that would need a midpoint error term with a second derivative, which we have not developed.

A third defect was one of bookkeeping across files, and it is the kind that survives any number of green builds because each half is individually correct. The development carried \emph{two} Kuramoto potentials. Lyapunov descent along the dynamics, $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$, was proved for the full potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j) - \sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$, while the characterization of the minimum --- phase-locked in both directions --- and everything downstream of it, including Derivation 5's gluing, concerned the coupling term alone. Nothing chained them, and the gap was not cosmetic: \texttt{kuramoto\_potential\_unbounded\_below} now proves that as soon as one $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below --- walk $\theta$ out along $\omega$ and the bounded cosine sum cannot compensate --- so it has no minimum for phase-locking to attain, and the sentence ``the phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential'' was, read literally, false of the functional the descent theorem was about. \texttt{Phase4\_RotatingFrame.lean} closes this by formalizing the standard reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$, a trajectory maps to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system --- which is exactly the system Derivation 5 already worked with --- the two potentials coincide there, and the descent result transports onto the coupling potential whose minimizers are the phase-locked states. Phase differences, phase-locking and the magnitude of the order parameter are all proved invariant under the change of frame, so nothing observable is lost in the reduction. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on two oscillators with a common frequency, discharging every hypothesis. The restriction is real and we state it plainly: the reduction is exact only for identical frequencies. A genuine spread of natural frequencies leaves residual detunings in the reduced system, phase-locking then requires coupling above a critical $K_c$, and that $K_c$ --- Kuramoto's threshold for a spread of frequencies, not the noise threshold $2D$ of Section~\ref{sec:field} --- is nowhere in our Lean development. It is studied numerically, in Figure~\ref{fig:kuramoto}, as Table~S1 records.

A fourth defect was dimensional, and it concerned the one row of Table~S1 that is still numerical rather than proved. \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean} defines the critical coupling $K_c = 2D$ and a predicate for being above it. That predicate compared $\int_x\int_y K(x,y)$ against $2D$ --- but a double integral against an unnormalized measure carries the substrate's mass twice over, so a constant kernel $K$ on a substrate of total mass $m$ contributed $K m^2$. The comparison therefore depended on how large the substrate was rather than on how strongly it was coupled, and any kernel whatever could be pushed above threshold by inflating $m$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S9 exhibits the failure concretely: on a two-site substrate carrying counting measure, a system coupled at $K = 1/2$ against a threshold of $K_c = 1$ --- a factor of two \emph{below} threshold --- has an unnormalized double integral of $2 > 1$, and would satisfy any predicate stated with that integral. The fix is to require the substrate's measure to be a probability measure, which is the normalization in which the mean-field derivation of $K_c = 2D$ is carried out; \texttt{mean\_field\_coupling\_const} then gives that a constant kernel has mean-field strength exactly $K$, and \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_const\_iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$. We flag what this does and does not do. It makes the predicate say something whose truth depends only on the physics, and \S9 witnesses it on both sides of the threshold. It does not by itself prove $K_c = 2D$: the content of that value is the bifurcation of the self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$ at $K = 2D$, which is a separate question from whether the predicate is dimensionally sound. That bifurcation is proved below, in both directions, from an assumed stationary density; deriving the density itself needs the Fokker--Planck operator and is not done.

We draw no grand moral from this beyond the obvious one. The value of a proof assistant here was not that it certified our argument, but that it made a specific, checkable claim about what had been assumed --- and that claim turned out to be false in a way no amount of reading the prose would have revealed.

\section{Axiom 1: Symmetries as Setting, and the Capacity of a Finite Phase Space}
Our starting premise relies on fundamental geometry. In our Lean formalization (\texttt{Phase1\_Primitives.lean}), continuous spacetime symmetries (e.g., the Poincaré group) act upon field states, and the invariance of the system's total energy under that action is recorded by the class \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}. Physically, it is such an invariance that Noether's theorem converts into the conservation of energy and momentum. The scope of the \emph{formal} claim is narrower and we state it plainly: the invariance is a class field, i.e.\ an assumption a model must discharge, and no conserved quantity is ever constructed from it. There is no Noether theorem in our Lean, and nothing downstream of this section depends on one. The class is now inhabited --- \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11 exhibits a double-well potential $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ on a two-point spacetime with the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$, discharges the invariance because $(\pm v)^2 = v^2$, and proves the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ degenerate with the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so an energy-minimising field and its image have equal energy and differ. That is symmetry breaking exhibited rather than assumed; it is still not a conserved quantity, and $\mathbb{Z}_2$ being discrete there is no one-parameter family to differentiate along in the first place. Simultaneously, discrete permutation symmetries dictate the statistical mechanics of any physical system, defining a strictly finite volume of phase space.\footnote{While the Poincaré group and discrete permutations serve as accessible physical examples, the underlying Lean formalization does not hardcode specific physics. It defines the system at a more primitive mathematical level, relying entirely on generic Pseudo-Riemannian manifolds (for spacetime) and finite measure theory (for statistical mechanics). What the formalization supplies is the vocabulary in which symmetries, conserved quantities and phase-space constraints can be \emph{stated}; it does not derive them. All three of the classes involved are now inhabited in \texttt{Examples.lean} --- the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action and its invariance in \S11, the real line with the Euclidean metric in \S12 --- but the instances are witnesses of inhabitability, not of physics: no theorem in the development consumes a symmetry group or a metric, and that half of the premise is setting rather than content. The other half of the premise is not setting, and the paragraphs below are what it does.}

Because phase space is finite, any localized physical system possesses a mathematical limit on the amount of information (distinguishable states) it can embody at any given moment. That sentence is the section's contribution to the chain, and three theorems carry it, in \texttt{Phase1\_PhaseSpaceCapacity.lean}, whose only hypothesis about the system is that its state space is finite: no symmetry group, no metric, no action functional appears anywhere in them.

The first is the capacity bound itself. A probability distribution $p$ on a finite state space $X$ satisfies $H(p) = -\sum_x p(x)\log p(x) \le \log|X|$ (\texttt{shannon\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak log\_\allowbreak card}), with equality if and only if $p$ is uniform (\texttt{shannon\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak log\_\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak iff}). The ``only if'' half is the part worth stating: it says $\log|X|$ is the exact capacity rather than a convenient over-estimate, so a system holding any distribution other than the flat one over its states holds strictly less than its size suggests. The proof is Gibbs' inequality against the uniform law --- $\log x \le x - 1$ applied at $x = 1/(|X|\,p(x))$, with the $p(x) = 0$ case carrying the slack that makes the equality condition sharp --- and it is elementary. What was missing here was the statement, not the difficulty.

The second says the limit binds. Let the boundary absorb a stream of perturbations drawn from an alphabet $P$; a stream of length $k$ has $|P|^k$ possible histories. Once $|X| < |P|^k$, the map from histories to final states cannot be injective (\texttt{absorb\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak injective}), so two distinct histories leave the system in the same state, and the entropy of the source strictly exceeds anything the system can hold (\texttt{source\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak exceeds\_\allowbreak capacity}). The update map is arbitrary and is allowed to be reversible at every individual step; the collision is forced by counting alone, which is the sense in which the premise, and not the model of the dynamics, is doing the work.

The third is what Derivation~2 consumes, and it is the one place where finiteness does something no other hypothesis could. On a finite state space, a map that fails to reach every state is not injective (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak erasure\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak surjective}) --- which is exactly the erasure condition Landauer's bound requires --- so any update of a finite phase space leaving some state unreachable dissipates strictly positive heat (\texttt{finite\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak dissipates}). On an infinite phase space the step simply fails: the successor map on $\mathbb{N}$ misses $0$ and is injective. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S19 records that failure next to the witnesses, so the finiteness hypothesis is fenced rather than assumed to be load-bearing, and runs all three results on a single bit: the fair coin attains $\log 2$, a $3/4$--$1/4$ bit falls strictly short, two binary perturbations already outrun a one-bit memory, and \S1's one-bit thermodynamic system pays heat for a state it cannot reach.

None of this derives the finiteness of phase space. Finiteness is assumed, as it is in any statistical-mechanical treatment, and the Poincar\'e group remains an illustration in prose. What has been removed is the gap between assuming the premise and using it.

\section{Derivation 1: Symmetry Breaking and the Inevitability of Boundaries}
A perfectly symmetric universe is the uniform vacuum; no distinct entities exist. For any dynamic process to occur, the continuous symmetries of the background field must undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking. 

When a field drops to a lower, asymmetric energy state, topology dictates the natural creation of defects (such as domain walls or monopoles). This constraint necessitates the creation of a physical boundary—a localized topological distinction between an ``inside'' and an ``outside.'' While such a defect (e.g., a crack in a crystal lattice) is necessary to define a separate entity, it is not sufficient for agency. The boundary is relational, defined by the twisted geometry of the field surrounding it, and must be coupled with non-equilibrium thermodynamics to exhibit active behaviors.

That first sentence is a claim about topology, and it needs a different argument from the energy claim beside it. The energy half --- an energy-minimizing field sits in the vacuum manifold almost everywhere (\texttt{pointwise\_\allowbreak vacuum\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak min}) --- together with the observation that a boundary defect \emph{obstructs} extension over a contractible interior, produces no defect: both are statements about a field already given. What produces one is \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In}: if the substrate is connected and a continuous field takes values in two \emph{different connected components} of the vacuum manifold $M$, then somewhere the field is not in $M$ at all. That excluded region is the wall. This is the $\pi_0$ case of Kibble's classification of topological defects by the homotopy of the vacuum manifold \cite{kibble1976}, and the proof is elementary in exactly the way it is elementary in the physics literature: the continuous image of a connected space is connected, a connected subset of $M$ lies inside one component of $M$, and the field's range would be such a subset if it never left $M$. No hypothesis mentions energy, a potential, or a symmetry; connectedness of the substrate and disconnectedness of the vacuum are the whole of it.

We formalize the $\pi_0$ case and stop there. The higher cases --- $\pi_1 \ne 0$ forcing vortex lines, $\pi_2 \ne 0$ forcing monopoles --- are the rest of Kibble's classification and Mathlib's homotopy-group library would support at least the next one; they are available and not taken, because the argument this paper runs uses domain walls and nothing else. The witness is the double well of \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11.1, where the vacuum manifold is \emph{computed} to be exactly $\{-1, 1\}$ (\texttt{wellVacuum\_\allowbreak eq}) rather than merely known to contain both minima, and its disconnection is proved in the form the theorem consumes: no preconnected subset of it contains both, since a preconnected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is an interval and an interval spanning $-1$ and $1$ contains $0$, which is the top of the barrier. \texttt{wellV\_\allowbreak domain\_\allowbreak wall} then quantifies over \emph{every} continuous field on \emph{any} connected substrate that reaches both minima, with no formula for the field anywhere in the statement. That the class is non-empty is checked separately, because a theorem quantified over all fields is worthless if none satisfies its hypotheses: the clipped identity on $\mathbb{R}$ does, and the wall it is forced to have sits at the origin (\texttt{kink\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak notMem}).

\section{Derivation 2: The Thermodynamic Constraint}
This newly formed topological boundary is subjected to gauge perturbations from the ``outside'' environment. Because the internal phase space is finite (Axiom 1), it cannot absorb an infinite sequence of incoming perturbations and must overwrite old internal states.

By Landauer's Principle \cite{landauer1961}, the erasure of physical information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, mandating that the system exhaust heat into the environment. Philosophers of physics (such as John Norton \cite{norton2005} and Orly Shenker \cite{shenker2000}) have critiqued Landauer's principle, arguing that physicists conflate \textit{logical} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in abstract computational states) with \textit{thermodynamic} erasure (a many-to-one mapping in physical phase space). They argue that logical erasure could conceptually be implemented by an injective physical process without dissipating heat. 

Our Lean 4 formalization (\texttt{Phase3\_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) addresses this critique by defining erasure as a non-injective mapping on the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective } t$). We formalize the global evolution of the bipartite universe (System + Bath) as strictly injective, representing Liouville's theorem. Lean verifies that heat dissipation is not tied to abstract logical erasure, but is a mandatory mathematical consequence of any local physical state space contraction under globally reversible dynamics. If a cognitive system's internal topology forces a contraction of its physical state space to accommodate structural resonance, heat dissipation becomes a thermodynamic requirement. The finiteness premise enters at exactly this point. What a modeller can usually check about a physical update is not that it is many-to-one but that some state has become unreachable; on a finite phase space those are the same condition, and \texttt{finite\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak dissipates} is the resulting statement. That identification preserves the Norton--Shenker distinction rather than blurring it: an update that keeps the incoming perturbation on the record is injective and dissipates nothing (\texttt{keepRecord\_\allowbreak injective}), while one that refreshes the register holding it is not (\texttt{absorbStep\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak erasure}). Losing \emph{which} history occurred is forced by counting alone and is free; overwriting the register that held it is what is charged $k_B T \ln 2$. A stable boundary thus constitutes a dissipative structure \cite{prigogine2018}, operating as a non-equilibrium steady state that consumes free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of erasure. Continuous thermal fluctuations play a constructive role in establishing boundaries between states while contributing to the system's flexible, adaptive response.

\section{Derivation 3: Prediction as the Thermodynamic Cost of Memory}
A dissipative structure is driven by its environment. Write $X_t$ for the state of the structure and $S_t$ for the environmental signal driving it, and let the signal evolve by a dynamics of its own, $S_t \to S_{t+1}$, on which the structure does not act. Two quantities are then defined at each step: the \textit{memory} $I(X_t ; S_t)$, the mutual information between the structure's present state and the signal's present value, and the \textit{predictive information} $I(X_t ; S_{t+1})$, which is what that same state says about the signal's next value.

\paragraph{What the entropy-production bound does not give.}
There is a natural route from entropy production to predictive processing, and it does not work. Model the environment as a stochastic driving force $P(\mathrm{ext})$ and the structure's internal transitions as $Q(\mathrm{int})$. Entropy production is then bounded below by $D_{KL}(P || Q)/\Delta t$ --- this is Gibbs' inequality, it is carried in Lean as an instance obligation, and \S3 of the supplement states it. Rearranged it reads $D_{KL}(P || Q) \le \Delta t\,\sigma$, so a system descending on $\sigma$ lowers a \textit{bound} on the divergence, and it is tempting to conclude that the internal transition probabilities come to mirror the environment's statistical symmetries. They do not, because a driven system does not relax to $\sigma = 0$. It relaxes to a non-equilibrium steady state at strictly positive entropy production, and the bound then yields only $D_{KL} \le \Delta t\,\sigma_{\mathrm{NESS}}$, a positive number. The inequality is true and does real work --- combined with Gibbs it gives $\sigma \ge 0$ --- but no limit may be read off it, and a framework that wants predictive processing has to get it from somewhere else. It gets it from the bound below.

\paragraph{Where predictive processing comes from: a published bound.}
Still, Sivak, Bell and Crooks \cite{still2012} prove that for a system driven by an environmental signal the work dissipated over one step of the drive obeys
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{diss}} \;\ge\; k_B T \left[\, I(X_t ; S_t) \;-\; I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \,\right].
\end{equation}
The bracket is the \textit{nonpredictive} information: what the structure retains about the signal's present that says nothing about the signal's future. Dissipation is bounded below by it. Memory that fails to predict is not merely useless to the structure; it is thermodynamic waste, and the waste is quantified in energy.

\paragraph{Three consequences, and they are theorems rather than readings.}
The bound is carried in Lean as a class field, \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.\allowbreak still\_bound} (\texttt{Phase3\_PredictiveThermodynamics}), for the same reason every other physical postulate here is: its \texttt{axiom} form, quantified over all joint laws with the dissipated work fixed, is refutable, and \texttt{Examples}~\S18.4 refutes it. What is \textit{derived} is everything downstream.

First, \textit{the bracket is non-negative}, and this is proved rather than assumed. If the structure does not act on its environment, then $X_t \to S_t \to S_{t+1}$ is a Markov chain and the data processing inequality gives $I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \le I(X_t ; S_t)$: a system cannot know more about the signal's future than about its present (\texttt{predictiveInfo\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak mutualInfo}). Mathlib carries the Kullback--Leibler divergence but no mutual information, so the latter is defined here as the divergence of the joint law from the product of its marginals, which is the standard definition and makes the library's data processing lemma apply directly.

Second, \textit{the second law follows} instead of being assumed. Dissipation is bounded below by a quantity now known to be non-negative, so $W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge 0$ (\texttt{dissipatedWork\_\allowbreak nonneg}). This mirrors the role Gibbs' inequality plays for the entropy-production bound, with one difference that matters: Gibbs' inequality compares a system to an arbitrary reference distribution, whereas the data processing inequality compares the system to its own future, which is a quantity selection can act on.

Third, and this is the point of the replacement, \textit{the inference now runs in the valid direction}. The quantity of interest sits on the small side of the inequality: $I(X_t;S_t) - I(X_t;S_{t+1}) \le W_{\mathrm{diss}}/k_B T$. No limit is required. Any bound on dissipation is immediately a bound on wasted memory, so a structure held to an energy budget is thereby held to a prediction standard: this is \texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak dissipation}. In the idealised quasi-static case $W_{\mathrm{diss}} = 0$, the theorem \texttt{predictive\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak dissipation} gives that every retained bit is predictive. This is a weaker statement than the one it replaces, and it is the one that is true.

\paragraph{The hypothesis is load-bearing, and we exhibit what breaks without it.}
The data processing step requires that the structure not write to its environment. In the formalization this is not an assumption stated beside the theorem but the shape of the evolution itself: the signal's step is applied to the pair as the parallel kernel $\mathrm{id} \parallel \kappa$, which acts as the identity on the system coordinate and through $\kappa$ on the signal coordinate, so no channel from $X_t$ to $S_{t+1}$ exists. \texttt{Examples}~\S18.3 shows the restriction is not decoration: a Markov kernel on the \textit{pair} which copies the system's state into the signal carries an independent two-bit law to a perfectly correlated one, raising mutual information from zero to a positive value. An agent that steers its environment can indeed know more about that environment's future than about its present, and Still's bound is not about such an agent.

\paragraph{Both regimes are exhibited, not just described.}
\texttt{Examples}~\S18 builds two systems over one correlated two-bit joint law, differing only in the signal's dynamics. When the signal is frozen, every remembered bit is a predictive bit, the nonpredictive information is zero, and the instance is permitted to dissipate nothing --- with the memory itself proved strictly positive, so the conclusion is about a system that does remember something. When the signal is redrawn from its stationary law at each step, the predictive information is exactly zero, the \textit{entire} memory is nonpredictive, and the instance is \textit{forced} to dissipate $k_B T$ times its whole mutual information. Establishing that the mutual information of the witness is neither zero nor infinite is where the work of that section lies.

\paragraph{An equality, where the entropy-production route had only a bound.}
Kawai, Parrondo and Van den Broeck \cite{kawai2007} show that the average dissipated work is exactly $k_B T$ times the relative entropy between the forward and time-reversed phase-space densities. Carried here as a predicate on an instance rather than as a further postulate, it replaces the abstract dissipation field with a quantity an experiment can estimate --- the statistical distinguishability of a recording of the system from the same recording run backwards --- and yields the statement that the irreversibility of the dynamics is at least the amount of memory that fails to earn its keep (\texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak arrow}).

\paragraph{What this does not establish.}
It does not deliver the Free Energy Principle. Still's bound says that prediction is thermodynamically favoured; it says nothing about hierarchical generative models, variational free energy, or Bayesian inference, and we do not claim to have derived any of them. The Free Energy Principle \cite{friston2010, friston2009, hohwy2013, clark2016, spisak2026} and the General Resonance Theory of Consciousness \cite{hunt2019} are consonant with what is proved here, which is a weaker relation than derivation and the only one we assert. Nor does it solve the symbol grounding problem \cite{harnad1990}. Maximal predictive information is compatible with the structure storing a lossy, compressed or otherwise unrecognisable function of the signal; a state that predicts is not thereby a state that means. The narrower claim is the one worth making: whatever internal states a physical structure holds, those that do not help predict its environment are paid for in heat, so the pressure toward states indexed to the environment is energetic rather than semantic. We continue to call the phenomenon \textit{structural resonance}, and there is no teleological drive to ``survive'' in it: predicting the environment is simply the cheaper of the two ways to hold a memory.

\section{Macroscopic Scaling: A Theorem and a Commitment}
\label{sec:scaling}
This is the chain's fourth step, and it is deliberately not numbered as a derivation. Three steps of different kinds meet here and are easy to run together: convergence of a scalar energy, which is a theorem; introduction of a continuum coupling kernel, which is a modelling assumption; and identification of that kernel with the brain's endogenous electromagnetic field, which is an empirical claim about cortex. Neither later step inherits the licence of the first. Kept apart, the empirical claim is the framework's central commitment and the place where it is most easily wrong --- which is where a claim of this kind belongs.

\subsection*{The formal half: coarse-graining is a theorem, and a narrow one}
A network of discretely coupled units has a coupling energy that is a symmetric double sum over pairs. \texttt{Phase2\_MeshConvergence.lean} proves that this sum converges to a continuous integral: along a sequence of regular triangulations of a region $S$ whose edge regions shrink to points, the discrete energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_u\sum_v \mu(R_{uv})\, f(x_u)$ converges to $\int_S f\,d\mu$ (\texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement\_\allowbreak convergence}), witnessed on the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ in \texttt{Examples}~\S6. The identification that makes this a coarse-graining rather than an arbitrary Riemann sum is \texttt{sum\_sum\_mul\_of\_symm}: because the summand is symmetric, the double sum is exactly the midpoint rule over unordered edges, so the discrete energy is a quadrature of the continuous one rather than a different quantity that happens to converge to it.

What this establishes is worth stating exactly, because it is less than the phrase ``coarse-graining, governed by the Renormalization Group'' suggests. It is a statement about an \textit{energy functional}, not about a dynamics: nothing here shows that trajectories of the discrete Kuramoto system converge to solutions of a field equation, and no renormalization-group flow is constructed. The theorem converges one real number, the energy; it does not produce a scalar density, an electromagnetic field or a kernel of two continuum points. The continuous object used later, \texttt{ContinuousNeuralField}, is \textit{posited} with a coupling kernel $K$. A genuine kernel limit first needs new discrete architecture over $M\times M$, since \texttt{edge\_region} supplies only subsets of $M$; only after such a kernel exists does the separate propagation-of-chaos problem for the dynamics arise.

\subsection*{The modelling and empirical steps: positing and identifying the kernel}
The formal half supplies scalar energy convergence and no kernel. The field sections therefore posit a symmetric continuum kernel as a modelling input. Which physical variable in cortex realizes that independently supplied kernel is a further empirical question, and we state our answer as a commitment:

\begin{quote}
\textit{The model identifies its posited continuum kernel with the endogenous electromagnetic field of mammalian cortex.}
\end{quote}

This is a claim about biology, not a consequence of anything above it. It is what makes the framework falsifiable and it is what we expect to be attacked.

\paragraph{What supports it.} Neurons do not only signal through synapses: transmembrane currents generate extracellular fields that shift the membrane potentials of neighbouring cells, a mechanism known as ephaptic coupling. Endogenous fields of $1$--$5$ mV/mm have been shown \textit{in vitro} to entrain spike timing \cite{frohlich2010, anastassiou2015}; realistic head models put mesoscopic ephaptic effects within the range where they can bias network dynamics \cite{ruffini2020modeling}; cable-theoretic treatments show field coupling is sufficient for coincidence detection in the auditory brainstem \cite{goldwyn2016neuronal}; and stable electric fields have been argued to carry information that survives representational drift in the underlying spiking population \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric, cunha2024ephapticity}. The field is spatially continuous and superposes contributions from large populations, which is the property the framework needs: it is a medium in which spatially separated activity is combined without a further mechanism for combining it. This is the integrative role the CEMI field theory assigns it \cite{mcfadden2002, mcfadden2020, pockett2012}.

\paragraph{What the evidence supports, and what it does not.} The endogenous field's effect on any individual neuron is \textit{modulatory}. Anastassiou and Koch report that endogenous fields of $1$--$5$ mV/mm shift spike timing by one to three milliseconds \cite{anastassiou2015}, an effect that is real, reproducible, and subthreshold: it biases when a cell fires; it does not decide whether it fires. Nothing in the work cited above supports calling the field the \textit{primary} dissipative structure of the system, and we do not claim it. The claim is that the field is \textit{modulatory in its effect on individual neurons and integrative in its spatial reach} --- weak per synapse, and the only variable in the system that is defined everywhere at once. What the framework needs is the reach, not the strength, and the two are easy to conflate.

\paragraph{Why reach is the property that matters.} A theory of consciousness needs an account of why many local processes make one experience, and most supply it by stipulation or by a measure. The account here is structural, and it is the framework's strongest argument. The field is the only variable in the system that is \emph{defined everywhere at once}. Unity is not asserted of it; unity is what a field is. That is what makes Derivation 5's sheaf-theoretic step available at all --- local states glue to one global section because there is one object over which gluing can happen --- so the answer to the combination problem is a piece of geometry rather than a postulate. Compare ``integration'' in Integrated Information Theory, which is a number, or ``broadcast'' in Global Workspace Theory, which is a metaphor about a workspace. It is also what the empirical identification above is carrying: if the posited continuum coupling is realized by something that is \emph{not} defined everywhere at once --- by synaptic connectivity, say --- then this argument is unavailable, and the framework loses the property that distinguishes it from its rivals rather than merely losing a mechanism.

\paragraph{Its critics.} The objection with the longest history is that the fields are too weak to matter, and it has been pressed from within the field-theoretic camp: Pockett, who has herself defended an electromagnetic theory of consciousness \cite{pockett2012}, catalogues its difficulties \cite{pockett2002} --- the measurement problem of relating gross field structure to reported experience, the absence of a constant relationship between the two, and the question of whether the field does causal work or merely reflects it. The quantitative case for scepticism has strengthened since. V\"or\"oslakos and colleagues measured intracranial fields during transcranial stimulation in rats and human cadavers and found that the great majority of applied current is shunted through the scalp, so that conventional protocols deliver fields at or below the $1$ V/m range in cortex \cite{voroslakos2018}. That is a bound on \textit{applied} fields rather than endogenous ones, but it constrains how much causal work a field of that magnitude can be doing, and it is the reason we do not claim the field drives spiking.

\paragraph{What would falsify it.} We state the failure conditions rather than leaving them to a referee.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Epiphenomenality.} If the endogenous field can be cancelled or shunted --- by a conductive medium that nulls the extracellular potential without altering transmembrane currents --- and neither behaviour nor report changes, the identification fails. This is the decisive experiment and it is, in principle, available.
\item \textit{A magnitude gap.} If the spatial integration the framework requires demands field effects an order of magnitude above the $1$--$5$ mV/mm measured in cortex, the commitment fails on arithmetic. The relevant number is not whether ephaptic coupling exists but whether its coupling strength suffices to place the system above the critical coupling $K_c$ of Derivation~7.
\item \textit{A timescale mismatch in the wrong direction.} The prediction of Section~\ref{sec:prediction} turns on the field's dynamics being fast relative to the astrocytic and clearance variables. If recovery from sleep inertia tracks an exponential set by the electrical timescale rather than the delayed sigmoid the slow variable predicts, the framework loses the one prediction that is its own.
\item \textit{Dissociation of field coherence from reported unity.} If large-scale field coherence is preserved across states in which experiential unity is lost --- or absent in states where it is retained --- the identification of the global section with unified experience has no physical carrier.
\end{enumerate}
A framework that survives all four is not thereby true. It is merely still standing, which is more than most theories of consciousness arrange to be able to lose.

\subsection*{Geometric frustration, and the regime our theorems actually cover}
The system's memory capacity is standardly grounded in \textit{geometric frustration}: a highly coupled network cannot bring all its nodes to their lowest energy simultaneously, producing a jagged landscape of near-degenerate states with the capacity to store many patterns. That argument is standard and it is not what this development formalizes. Confronting the discrepancy is the honest course, so we do it here rather than in a footnote.

Frustration requires competing couplings --- in the Kuramoto setting, a sign structure that no phase assignment can satisfy on every edge at once. The formal setting admits this: \texttt{KuramotoSystem} carries $A : V \to V \to \mathbb{R}$, symmetric and otherwise unconstrained, so signed couplings are representable. The question is therefore not whether the development can speak about a frustrated network but \textit{where along the chain} the sign of $A$ is constrained, and that question has an exact answer. Positivity is not a hypothesis distributed through the development; it is a \textit{field of one class}. \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} (\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak GlobalSection}) demands \texttt{A\_pos} of every instance, so it is demanded at the node where local sections are glued and at the edge that builds a cover, and it is demanded nowhere upstream of that node. The theorems that feed the class carry it with them --- \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} assumes $A_{ij} > 0$, and the characterization of the minimizers as the phase-locked configurations inherits it, while the bridge of \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak EquilibriumBridge} and the convergence theorem of \texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak RotatingFrame}~\S7 require the strictly stronger uniform bound $0 < a \le A_{ij}$, since $a$ enters the {\L}ojasiewicz constant. A network with all-positive couplings is an unfrustrated ferromagnet whose potential has one minimum up to a global phase shift. It is the opposite of a jagged landscape, and it has no capacity to store multiple patterns at all.

Locating the constraint that precisely narrows the objection, and the narrower version is the one we accept. It is not that the framework describes an unfrustrated brain throughout; the mean-field reduction of Derivation~7 has no sign structure to be frustrated at all, since its coupling is a scalar $K$ rather than a matrix. It is that \textbf{one step} of the framework --- the gluing of Derivation~5 --- describes an unfrustrated system, and every step before it does not care.

The two halves are worth listing by name, because the surviving half is larger than the concession usually made. What holds with no assumption whatever on the sign of the coupling is the entire dissipative account: well-posedness on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (\texttt{is\_kuramoto\_trajectory\_exists}, \texttt{\_unique}); the Lyapunov identity itself, that the potential's time derivative is minus the summed squared velocity (\texttt{dV\_dt\_le\_zero}), which is the theorem the whole descent argument rests on and which mentions no positivity hypothesis; covariance under passage to a rotating frame; monotone descent of the potential (\texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone}) and its convergence to a finite limit (\texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}); finiteness of the dissipation integral; and --- through Barbalat's lemma --- that the motion stops along every trajectory (\texttt{velocity\_sq\_tendsto\_zero}). Every one of these is stated for an arbitrary symmetric $A$, so a frustrated network runs, dissipates, and comes to rest inside this development exactly as an unfrustrated one does. What requires positivity is the identification of the resting place: the three minimization results of \texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak Kuramoto\-Dynamics}, which between them characterize the potential's minimizers as exactly the phase-locked configurations; the class field \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.A\_pos}; and the convergence theorem \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}. \textit{That} the motion stops is frustration-agnostic; \textit{where} it stops is not, and the claims this framework makes about the destination all live on the positive side of that line. On the continuum side the accounting differs slightly: \texttt{phase\_locked\_minimizes\_entropy\_of\_symm} assumes only that the kernel is symmetric and so tolerates signed kernels, but it is conditional on a dynamically phase-locked field existing --- which is precisely what frustration may prevent.

We therefore scope the claim rather than repeat it. The memory capacity of a frustrated cortical network is not supported by any theorem here, and we do not assert it as part of the chain. \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak TwistedGluing} tests the natural repair: let local states agree on overlaps up to a phase and ask whether their offset cocycle is a coboundary. The result is negative in the physically immediate case. Locked, twisted and splay configurations all assign a phase $\phi_i$ to every patch, hence their offsets are $\phi_i-\phi_j$, automatically a coboundary (\texttt{isCoboundary\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}); the family therefore glues after patchwise rotation (\texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}). Frustration alone supplies no non-trivial obstruction and hence no content label.

There is, however, a measured cortical quantity with the right topological shape to motivate a different construction. Primate LFP phase maps contain phase singularities whose surrounding electrode loops have integer winding number; these complex waves covary with local spiking activity \cite{townsend2015}. Human cortical spiral centres and rotation directions are also task-dependent and classify cognitive tasks \cite{xu2023}. The observable is the winding of a circle-valued phase around a point where its amplitude vanishes, not an independently chosen circle-valued offset between patches. Its natural transition data are the integer multiples of $2\pi$ relating \emph{local real-valued lifts} of phase on overlaps. \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak PhaseLifts} formalizes the measured loop at this discrete level: \texttt{loopWinding\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} proves that transitions from one global real lift telescope to zero, while \texttt{threeElectrodeWinding\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} witnesses a three-patch loop with winding one and no global lift. This is not the earlier \texttt{PhaseObstruction}, whose circle-valued offsets from absolute patch phases necessarily telescope. Cortical phase singularities therefore supply a biologically grounded, non-trivial lift obstruction, but the evidence does not show that winding labels phenomenal content. A content-sensitive empirical test remains new work outside the chain.

\section{Derivation 5: Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
The system is now characterized by a continuous macroscopic electrodynamic field generated by millions of coupled micro-components, forced to resonate with the environment. Following Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, the coupling regime assumed from here on is the unfrustrated one --- all couplings positive --- which is what every theorem below requires and what the frustrated network of the memory argument is not. 

The Kuramoto model \cite{kuramoto1984} of coupled non-linear oscillators guarantees that, past a critical coupling threshold, these distributed local fields undergo a phase transition into unified synchronization \cite{bauer2022}. Our Lean proofs (\texttt{Phase4\_KuramotoDynamics.lean}) confirm this phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential of the system, in the precise sense discussed in Section~\ref{sec:soundness}: the functional minimized is the coupling potential read in the frame rotating with the natural frequency, which \texttt{Phase4\_RotatingFrame.lean} shows is the honest reduced description of a system of identical oscillators. 

The phase transition supplies synchronized phases. A separate compatibility hypothesis says that the corresponding local states agree on every overlap; given that hypothesis, the sheaf condition mathematically glues them into a singular \textit{Global Section} (\texttt{Phase5\_GlobalSection.lean}). The EM field acts as a single, indivisible topological space, providing the \textit{Unity of Consciousness}—a necessary condition for integrated experience. While Global Workspace Theory \cite{baars1997, dehaene2014} models this unity as a broadcast architecture and Integrated Information Theory \cite{tononi2016} quantifies it via $\Phi$, the framework here proposes a continuous physical mechanism whose overlap condition remains a modelling input. The contrast is worth drawing carefully, because all three approaches address the same explanandum from different directions.

We state the scope of the Lean result precisely, because it is narrower than the prose above. \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} demands that the cover's phase field minimize the reduced Kuramoto potential, and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} characterizes those minimizers exactly: they are the phase-locked configurations, in both directions. Every instance is therefore already phase-locked (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.phase\_\allowbreak locked}), and the class's single condition on its local data --- that patches at a common phase agree where they overlap (\texttt{section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq}) --- makes the family of local sections compatible. \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}, which is the sheaf property of the probability presheaf and contains no physics at all, then glues that family into exactly one global section.

The shape of the class is what makes this an emergence theorem rather than a uniqueness theorem, and the difference is easy to lose. Were \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization} to carry a \emph{global} section as a class field, together with a second field declaring every local section to be a restriction of it, the global object would exist before the sheaf condition was ever consulted, and \texttt{global\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak thermodynamics} could do no more than rule out competitors to an object the instance had supplied. It carries neither. Every field of the class mentions a single patch or a single overlap; the global section is constructed (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.invariant\-Measure}); and the statement that the local sections are restrictions of one common measure is a \emph{theorem} about that construction (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq}), true because the sheaf condition says so, not because an instance was required to name a measure.

\texttt{Examples.lean}~\S14 is the witness that the difference is not notational. Two patches carry two different mass profiles, $(2,1,7)$ and $(5,1,3)$ across the three sites, agreeing only at the site they share, where both put mass $1$ --- which is what discharges the overlap condition, by computing two numbers rather than by construction. The section the theorem returns has profile $(2,1,3)$: it is neither of the two profiles (\texttt{gluedW\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak leftW}, \texttt{gluedW\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak rightW}), and neither of them restricts correctly to both patches (\texttt{leftW\_\allowbreak glues\_\allowbreak nothing}, \texttt{rightW\_\allowbreak glues\_\allowbreak nothing}). The global state carries information that no single patch had. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13, built instead through the constructor \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization.of\-InvariantMeasure}, sits alongside it for the negative result it carries: its phase field is not the constant function --- the second patch a full turn ahead of the first --- and by \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.phase\_\allowbreak locked} that is as far from constant as any instance can get, since no instance can carry phases differing by anything a measure is allowed to see.

Two hypotheses carry physical content. One of them is an obligation on every instance: that synchronized patches agree on their overlaps is a modelling assumption, not a consequence of any dynamics we formalize. The other, \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium}, asks that the cover already sit at the potential minimum, and no theorem says an \emph{arbitrary} system's dynamics takes it there. ``A thermodynamic phase transition into unity'' is therefore doing causal work that no general Lean result supports, though not work without any support at all: \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S15 exhibits a two-oscillator trajectory that starts a quarter turn out of phase and converges to the global minimum of the dynamic potential, with the order parameter tending to $1$. That trajectory is not an isolated construction: \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak cauchy\_\allowbreak problem} proves the Kuramoto initial value problem well posed on all of $\mathbb{R}$, so through every initial state there passes exactly one trajectory, and the exhibited one is the only solution through its own. Nor is its settling down peculiar to it: along every trajectory of a zero-frequency system the dynamic potential converges, and the total dissipation $\int_0^\infty \sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$ is finite and equal to the potential's total drop (\texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}, \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak tendsto}). The motion also stops: a finite integral does not by itself force its integrand to zero, but the dissipation rate is Lipschitz in time, so Barbalat's lemma applies, and every trajectory satisfies $\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}; Mathlib has no Barbalat, so it is proved here as \texttt{tendsto\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak lipschitz\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak le}). That last step --- locating where the motion stops --- is taken conditionally, and by a route that avoids the LaSalle invariance principle, which Mathlib does not carry. LaSalle is the tool for a system with a decreasing functional and nothing else; this one has more, and the extra structure is what makes the conclusion reachable without it. The flow is a gradient flow of the dynamic potential, so $\dot V = -\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$ is an identity rather than an inequality; the minimisers of $V$ are characterised exactly; and on the region where every pair of phases lies within a quarter turn a Polyak--\L{}ojasiewicz estimate holds, $2a^2 W \le (\sum_{ij}A_{ij})\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$, where $W$ is the potential's excess above its minimum and $a>0$ is a lower bound for the coupling (\texttt{lojasiewicz\_\allowbreak estimate}). Its proof is two extremal indices and the identity $\sin^2 D = (1-\cos D)(1+\cos D)$: the site carrying the largest phase is pulled backwards at rate at least $a\sin D$, where $D$ is the spread, while the excess is at most $\tfrac12(\sum_{ij}A_{ij})(1-\cos D)$. The compactness objection dissolves as well, because the invariant region is produced by the Lyapunov function rather than assumed: a single pair contributes $\tfrac12 a(1-\cos)$ to the excess, so an excess below $a/2$ forces every pairwise cosine positive for all time, and continuity on the connected half-line then prevents any pair from changing branch (\texttt{spread\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak small}). Descent plus the estimate integrates to $W(t) \le W(0)e^{-ct}$ with $c = 2a^2/\sum_{ij}A_{ij}$ (\texttt{excess\_\allowbreak decay}), the rate makes each $|\dot\theta_i|$ exponentially small and hence integrable on $[0,\infty)$, and a trajectory with integrable velocity converges. The conclusion is \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}: if the phases start within a quarter turn of each other and the initial excess is below $a/2$, the trajectory converges to a phase-locked configuration which is a \emph{global} minimiser of the dynamic potential, with order parameter $r^2 = 1$. That is the configuration \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} postulates, reached rather than assumed. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17 fires it on three sites with initial data $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, where the Kuramoto system has no closed-form solution: the trajectory comes from the existence theorem and its limit from the convergence theorem, so neither is written down.

\texttt{Phase5\_EquilibriumBridge.lean} closes the gap that remained between the two results. \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.of\-Convergent\-Trajectory} takes a cover whose phase field is the limit of such a trajectory and returns an instance whose \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} field is discharged \emph{by that convergence}; the cover's coupling matrix is the coupling matrix of the dynamics rather than a separately chosen one, so the system whose equilibrium the class asserts and the system whose trajectory is run are the same system. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17.1 is the resulting witness: three patches over the three-site substrate, each carrying its own mass profile on its own open set, above a phase field that no line of the construction writes down. The trajectory that reaches it starts at $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, which is provably not phase-locked (\texttt{trioCover\_\allowbreak start\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak locked}), so on this cover the configuration Derivation 5 requires is arrived at rather than posited. The state the three patches glue to has profile $(2,1,3)$ and is none of the three profiles the instance carries (\texttt{trioW\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak glued}), so one cover now carries both the emergence reading of the gluing and the derived reading of the equilibrium. What is still assumed on it is the overlap agreement, and nothing else.

\paragraph{The step that carries the positivity.} \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} demands one thing more, and it is worth naming here rather than only in the limitations section, because this is the step at which it enters the chain. The class field \texttt{A\_pos} requires every coupling to be strictly positive. Nothing upstream of this node requires it: the trajectory exists, the potential descends, the dissipation integral is finite and the motion stops for an arbitrary symmetric coupling matrix, signed entries included. The unfrustrated regime is therefore not an assumption the framework makes about the brain throughout; it is an assumption Derivation~5 makes about the configuration it glues, and it is the one place a frustrated network would part company with the development. The consequences are set out under \textit{Geometric frustration} above.

The two hypotheses cannot be dropped, and this is the honest scope of the result. Splay and twisted configurations are equilibria of the same flow, so ``every trajectory reaches the unified state'' is false as a general statement, and the initial-data condition is the standard arc condition under which the true statement holds. What is claimed is therefore conditional --- unity is reached from configurations already sufficiently coherent --- and not that an arbitrary initial state is carried into unity. What Lean establishes about the gluing itself is the other half: \emph{given} overlap-compatible local patches, a global state exists, is unique, and is produced by the gluing rather than presupposed by it.

Integrated Information Theory begins from phenomenological axioms and derives a scalar $\Phi$ measuring irreducibility of a system's cause-effect structure, with the exclusion postulate selecting a single maximum-$\Phi$ complex as the locus of experience. Our sheaf-theoretic global section plays a structurally analogous role --- it too picks out a distinguished unified object --- but it is arrived at from the opposite end: not postulated from the character of experience, but forced by a thermodynamic phase transition in a physical field. This buys two things IIT does not have. First, the unity condition is checkable from field dynamics rather than requiring the computation of $\Phi$, which is intractable for systems of realistic size and whose definition has undergone repeated revision \cite{tononi2016}. Second, exclusion falls out rather than being postulated: gluing either succeeds on a given cover or it does not, so there is no separate principle needed to rule out overlapping conscious subsystems. It also inherits IIT's exposures. Critics have argued that $\Phi$ can be high in systems with no plausible claim to experience, such as large feedforward-free grids \cite{aaronson2014}; our criterion is vulnerable to the mirror objection, since a physical system could in principle satisfy the gluing condition without being cognitively interesting. We take this seriously rather than dismissing it: our reply is that gluing is necessary but not sufficient, and that the topological boundary of Derivation 1 and the reflexive structure of Derivation 6 are additional required conditions. IIT, by contrast, treats $\Phi$ as both necessary and sufficient.

Global Workspace Theory faces a complementary difficulty. It specifies a functional architecture --- a limited-capacity workspace broadcasting to specialized modules --- without specifying what physical property of broadcasting makes the broadcast content conscious, leaving the theory open to the charge that it explains access consciousness while leaving phenomenal consciousness untouched \cite{block1995}. Our framework can be read as supplying the missing physical substrate for the workspace: the continuous macroscopic field \emph{is} the medium in which integration is realized, and the $K_c = 2D$ threshold gives a physical condition for ignition rather than a functional stipulation of it. Where GWT is agnostic about implementation, we make a falsifiable commitment --- unity requires a continuous physical medium, which predicts that a functionally equivalent discrete broadcast architecture would not be conscious. We regard this as a genuine cost of the position, not a free advantage: it is the point at which our framework departs most sharply from computational functionalism and where it is most readily falsified. Contrast this derivation with panpsychism: consciousness is not assumed as a fundamental property of matter. It requires the initial existence of a topological defect to establish a bounded ``inside'' (Derivation 1), followed by a thermodynamic phase transition into unity that occurs when integrated spatial coupling exceeds the thermodynamic noise threshold ($K_c = 2D$). Without both the foundational topological boundary and the subsequent mathematically defined phase transition, the system remains a fragmented, non-conscious collection of micro-states. A unified field of perception, however, is not yet a ``Self.''

\section{Derivation 6: Reflexive Topology and the Emergence of the Self}
\label{sec:self}
While a global section provides Unity, a ``Self'' requires reflexivity. The unified EM field is a dissipative structure that must resonate with external perturbations while managing its own internal phase transitions. Time evolution naturally drives the field to minimize thermodynamic friction with its own internal state-changes. 

This mandates \textit{auto-resonance}: the field must predict itself. The ``Self'' emerges as a higher-order topological feature when the internal geometry folds to include a low-dimensional avatar of its own computational and causal boundary within its predictive model (\texttt{Phase6\_ReflexiveTopology.lean}) \cite{levin2019}. The system is unified, predictive, and explicitly maps its own distinct existence from the environment. This architecture provides the physical foundation for a self-aware consciousness, bounded by the continuous measure thermodynamics of Landauer erasure (\texttt{Phase3\_MeasureThermodynamics.lean}).

\paragraph{What carries the weight.} \texttt{reflexive\_topology\_implies\_self} is the Banach fixed-point theorem with the physical commitments in its hypotheses: the space of global sections is assumed nonempty, metric and complete, and the predictive map is assumed to contract. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S10 discharges all four on the three-site substrate of Derivation 5, and the metric is constructed rather than posited. The section first pierces the sheafification: on a discrete substrate every point has a smallest open neighbourhood, so the mass a germ carries at a point is well defined, and \texttt{massEquivOn} proves that over \emph{any} open the sections of the probability sheaf \emph{are} the finite measures on that open, read as densities against counting measure --- the global case being $U = \top$. Mathlib supplies no inverse to this sheafification --- its \texttt{TopCat} sheafification has neither an adjunction nor an \texttt{isIso\_toSheafify} --- so the correspondence had to be constructed. The metric on sections is then the uniform distance between the densities of the measures they glue to --- equivalent to their total variation on a finite substrate, though not equal to it --- and it is proved complete by exhibiting the limit section of a Cauchy sequence of densities, not by the degenerate route of showing Cauchy sequences are eventually constant. The choice of metric is load-bearing rather than incidental: under the $0/1$ metric on sections every contraction is constant (\texttt{contracting\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak const}), so a witness built there would discharge every hypothesis of the theorem and say nothing. That theorem is kept in the source, because which metrics make the fixed point trivial is part of what the fixed point is.

\paragraph{The map is the avatar, or there is no map.} A reflexive boundary could be defined with the predictive map as free data sitting beside the avatar, and the reason it is not is a theorem rather than a preference. With the map free, the fixed-point theorem projects it out and never touches the avatar, so replacing the avatar by a constant leaves a legal boundary with the same predictive model and the same Self --- a fixed-point theorem that cannot detect whether the avatar reads the field is Banach's theorem with a consciousness-flavoured label. The structure is built the other way round. A reflexive boundary carries the avatar's \emph{write} --- \texttt{auto\_\allowbreak resonance}, the encoding of the global state into the avatar region --- and its \emph{read-out}, the global state that encoding predicts; the predictive map is their composite and is not a field. The self-model therefore runs through the avatar region or it does not exist. Three consequences follow that the free-data shape cannot state. The Self lies in the range of the read-out (\texttt{self\_\allowbreak mem\_\allowbreak range\_\allowbreak readout}), so it is reconstructed from a single section over the avatar region: the low-dimensional fold this section has always asserted, as a theorem. Under the resonance condition below, the Self satisfies $s = \mathrm{readout}(s|_{\mathrm{avatar}})$ (\texttt{self\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak readout\_\allowbreak restrict}) --- the field is what its own localized self-encoding reconstructs. And blinding the avatar \emph{moves} the Self: the blinded boundary's map is constant, its unique fixed point is the state its stipulated contents extend to, and the original Self is not a fixed point of it (\texttt{not\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak const\-Resonance}, witnessed on the cortex by \texttt{cortex\-Blind\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak ne}). The opposite statement --- that blinding changes nothing --- does not typecheck against this structure, because a contraction hypothesis about a boundary's own map says nothing about the blinded one's.

\paragraph{Auto-resonance as a constraint.} That the avatar's state \emph{is} the presheaf restriction of the global state is stated as a predicate, \texttt{Is\-Restriction\-Resonance}, rather than added to the structure, so that results say which of them need it. In the positive direction, if a family of resonant avatars \emph{covers} the substrate then the global state is determined by their local readings alone (\texttt{eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak avatar\_\allowbreak eq}): unity is not assumed alongside the avatars but recovered from them, by the same sheaf condition that Derivation 5 uses. In the negative direction a constant avatar fails the predicate as soon as the avatar region separates two states of the field. Both halves are witnessed on the three-site substrate, where that separation is proved before anything is claimed for it, so the condition is a constraint there and not a formality.

\paragraph{The contraction law is an independent modelling postulate.} The rate $\mathrm{exp}(-(K - K_c)\tau/2)$ with $K_c = 2D$ (\texttt{resonanceRate}) is the linear relaxation rate of the mean-field order parameter over an interval $\tau$. The framework independently assumes that the avatar-mediated self-prediction map is Lipschitz at this same rate; no oscillator or field theorem derives that bound for \texttt{rb.predict}. Given the postulate, the threshold results determine whether Banach can use it. Above threshold the rate is below one, so the assumed bound yields a unique fixed point (\texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak supercritical}); at or below threshold the rate is at least one, and \texttt{Contracting\-With} at that rate is false for every self-map of every metric space (\texttt{not\_\allowbreak contracting\-With\_\allowbreak resonance\-Rate}). This says only that the Banach route is unavailable below threshold, not that a Self cannot exist there. The postulate is witnessed at $K=3$, $D=1$, $\tau=2\ln 2$, where the chosen read-out achieves the required factor, but designing a witness to realize the factor is not a derivation of self-model dynamics from oscillator dynamics. \texttt{Chain.lean}'s \texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak order\_\allowbreak parameter} consumes the coherent order parameter to recover $K>K_c$; its separate \texttt{E89} hypothesis supplies the Lipschitz law and the fixed-state identification. Derivation 7 therefore discharges the threshold half of the implication and not the contraction postulate.

\paragraph{The fixed point is now the glued state.} The standalone Banach theorem produces a fixed point somewhere in the space of global sections; by itself it does not say that this is the section Derivation~5 glued. \texttt{Chain.lean} now preserves that identity. \texttt{IsUnifiedBy T s} says that $s$ restricts to every local state of the cover $T$, and the endpoint \texttt{UnifiedSelf} says that this same $s$ is the unique fixed point of the reflexive map. The edge hypothesis \texttt{E89} must supply the equality $\texttt{predict }s=s$ as well as the Lipschitz bound; Banach supplies uniqueness but not the equality between the thermodynamic and reflexive constructions. On the witness, the two-patch \texttt{cortexCover} glues exactly to \texttt{cortexState}, already the unique fixed point of the non-constant avatar map. This closes an omission in the formal composition while exposing, rather than removing, the physical identification it requires.

\paragraph{What is still not established.} That any field dynamics produces this particular read-out or performs the restriction; and that this three-site substrate's coupling is $K = 3$ --- the parameters are a choice of units for the witness, not a measurement, and nothing here derives a coupling constant from cortex. We also note a negative result found in the course of this work: the Lévy--Prokhorov metric, which the natural weak-convergence reading would suggest, does \emph{not} support this argument on a discrete substrate, because the $\varepsilon$-thickening of a set is the set itself for $\varepsilon < 1$ and the relaxation map is consequently not a Lévy--Prokhorov contraction.

\section{Derivation 7: Continuous Stochastic Thermodynamics and Field Theory}
\label{sec:field}
To move beyond finite graph analogies, we model the macroscopic electrodynamic field as a \textit{Continuous Neural Field}. True ephaptic coupling is a continuous spatial phenomenon, not a discrete network. We extend the discrete Kuramoto model to a continuous spatial coupling kernel $K(x,y)$ (\texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}).

Subject to thermodynamic noise $D$, the system undergoes non-equilibrium stochastic thermodynamics. Rather than a static Landauer bound, the field dynamics minimize an entropy production functional $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \int \frac{1}{D} \left( \omega(x) + \int K_t(x,y) \sin(\theta_y - \theta_x) dy \right)^2 dx
\end{equation}
Biological fields dynamically deform their spatial topology $K_t(x,y)$ to undergo gradient descent on this entropy production functional.

That gradient descent is a theorem rather than a modelling assertion, for finite substrates. Reading $\sigma$ as a functional of the coupling kernel at fixed phases, \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_sigmaOfKernel} computes its Fr\'echet derivative in closed form,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \sigma}{\partial K(a,b)} = \frac{2}{D}\Big(\omega(a) + \sum_y K(a,y)\sin(\theta_y - \theta_a)\Big)\sin(\theta_b - \theta_a),
\end{equation}
and \texttt{sigmaOfKernel\_eq\_entropy\_production\_rate} identifies the functional being differentiated with \texttt{entropy\_production\_rate} itself whenever the substrate carries counting measure. \texttt{structural\_resonance\_decreases\_entropy\_production} then concludes that $\sigma$ is non-increasing along any coupling trajectory relaxing down its own gradient at a positive rate $c$. Two limitations stay explicit. The derivation is for \emph{finite} substrates, and the obstacle to the continuum case is \emph{not} differentiation under the integral sign with respect to the kernel, which is what the shape of $\sigma$ suggests at first reading. $\sigma$ is not a general functional that happens to be written as an integral --- it is a quadratic functional of an \emph{affine} image of the kernel, since the drift is a fixed $\omega$ plus a linear operator applied to $K$, and $\sigma$ is a constant times the squared norm of the drift. A squared norm is Fr\'echet differentiable on any real inner-product space and an affine map is its own derivative, so the composite differentiates by the chain rule with no limiting argument. \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_quadratic\_of\_affine} states that in full generality, and \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_sigmaOfKernel\_of\_operator} re-derives the finite gradient from it, showing that the closed form displayed above is a convenience rather than the content. What the continuum case needs is therefore one object: the drift map $K \mapsto \left(x \mapsto \int K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)\,dy\right)$ as a bounded operator $L^2(\mu\otimes\mu) \to L^2(\mu)$, which is a Cauchy--Schwarz estimate --- the integral kernel is bounded by $1$, so on a finite measure space the operator norm is at most $\mu(M)^{1/2}$. That object is built. \texttt{kernelCLM} is the operator, for any kernel factor bounded by $1$, with \texttt{opNorm\_\allowbreak kernelCLM\_\allowbreak le} the stated bound; \texttt{continuumDriftCLM} is the instance with the Kuramoto factor $\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)$. One step beyond the estimate is needed and is worth naming, because the estimate alone does not supply it: the descent theorems ask for the derivative as a \emph{gradient vector}, not as an arbitrary bounded functional, and producing one requires the adjoint, which exists because $L^2$ is complete. \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_\allowbreak quadratic\_\allowbreak grad} supplies it for an arbitrary bounded operator between real Hilbert spaces, giving $\nabla\sigma(K) = (2/D)\,A^{*}(AK+\omega)$, so the finite and continuum cases are now the same theorem. \texttt{structural\_\allowbreak resonance\_\allowbreak decreases\_\allowbreak sigma\-Continuum} is the descent result with no finiteness hypothesis in it, and \texttt{sigma\-Continuum\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak integral} identifies the functional being differentiated with the integral \texttt{entropy\_production\_rate} computes. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S20 runs this on Lebesgue measure restricted to $(0,1)$, a substrate that is neither finite nor atomic, so the finite-substrate results of the previous paragraph do not typecheck there at all. The second limitation is unchanged: the phase field is held fixed throughout, so this is plasticity of the coupling at frozen phases, not joint $(\theta, K)$ dynamics. \texttt{Examples.lean} \S5 exercises the whole chain on a two-site substrate whose coupling relaxes as $e^{-t}$ and whose entropy production decays as $e^{-2t}$, confirming that the gradient is not identically zero and that the descent is strict rather than vacuously monotone.

A second result about $\sigma$ concerns where the descent is headed. Minimality of the phase-locked field is straightforward against competitors that share its spatial mean drift, and in that form the theorem is close to Jensen's inequality and no more; the restriction looks as though lifting it would need a model of how the mean drift varies across competitors. It does not. For a \emph{reciprocal} kernel, one with $K(x,y) = K(y,x)$, the restriction is not a restriction: the coupling integrand $K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y - \theta_x)$ is antisymmetric under exchanging its two sites, because $K$ is symmetric while $\sin$ is odd, so Fubini identifies its double integral with its own negative and the total coupling contribution vanishes. The total drift $\int(\omega + \text{coupling})$ is therefore $\int\omega$ whatever the phases are (\texttt{total\_\allowbreak drift\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm}): coupling moves drift between sites but neither creates nor destroys it. Evaluating that conserved quantity at the phase-locked configuration, where the drift is constant, pins its value, and the mean-matching hypothesis follows for every competitor. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm} is the unrestricted statement. Reciprocity is the physically intended condition --- an ephaptic field acts on both sites of a pair alike, and it is the same symmetry \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.A\_symm} already imposes in Derivation 5 --- but it is not a formality, and we prove that rather than assert it. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S16 exhibits a \emph{one-way} coupling on two sites, in which $a$ feels $b$ and $b$ does not feel $a$, whose phase-locked field is strictly beaten: displacing the second phase to $-\pi/2$ cancels the first site's natural drift while adding none at the second, halving $\sigma$. So ``phase-locking minimizes entropy production'' is false for general kernels and true for reciprocal ones, and the hypothesis cannot be removed by working harder.

We define the critical coupling threshold $K_c = 2D$. The phase transition to the unified ``Global Section'' occurs when the mean-field spatial coupling exceeds this thermodynamic noise threshold, linking the emergence of Unity to physical temperature and noise.

The value $K_c = 2D$ can be taken as a bare stipulation validated by simulation, and it is not: \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency.lean} proves the half of it that is ordinary real analysis. For the noisy mean-field Kuramoto model with identical frequencies, the stationary density at order parameter $r$ is the von Mises density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{(Kr/D)\cos\theta}$, and self-consistency requires $r = R(K,r) := I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$. Mathlib has no modified Bessel functions, so we formalize that ratio directly as a ratio of integrals over $[-\pi, \pi]$. Integration by parts with $u = \sin$, $v = -e^{a\cos}$ gives an exact identity with no boundary term, $\int \cos\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}\,d\theta = a\int \sin^2\!\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}\,d\theta$, so $R$ is $a$ times the mean of $\sin^2$ under the von Mises weight. Bounding that mean by $1$ would put the threshold at $D$; the true threshold is $2D$, and the entire factor of two is the sharper bound $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$, equivalently $I_2(a) \ge 0$. We prove it elementarily, by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ in two reflections ($\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$, then $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$), after which the integrand is a product of $\cos 2\theta \ge 0$ with $2\cosh(a\cos\theta) - 2\cosh(a\sin\theta) \ge 0$ --- the second factor because $0 \le \sin\theta \le \cos\theta$ there and $\cosh$ increases in $|\cdot|$. The conclusion, \texttt{subcritical\_fixed\_point\_eq\_zero}$'$, is that for $K < K_c$ the incoherent state $r = 0$ is the \emph{only} non-negative solution of the self-consistency equation; $r = 0$ is exhibited as a solution, so the uniqueness claim is not vacuous. Three further facts about the threshold narrow the gap between this row and a full ``Theorem'' to a single named statement. First, the fold argument gives a \emph{strict} inequality for $a > 0$ (\texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak pos}, hence $R(a) < a/2$), which settles the threshold itself: at $K = K_c$ the self-consistency map is tangent to the diagonal at the origin but falls strictly below it thereafter, so $r = 0$ remains the only solution and the bifurcation happens strictly \emph{after} $K_c$, not at it (\texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical}). Second, factoring $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ shows that a non-zero $r$ solves the equation exactly when $E(Kr/D) = D/K$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq}), so the coherent branch is a level set of the single function $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$. Third, and using only $E < 1/2$ together with compactness, every coherent solution tends to zero as $K \downarrow K_c$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold}): the branch emerges continuously from the incoherent state rather than appearing at a finite distance from it, which is what makes the transition supercritical in the technical sense. That statement quantifies over \emph{every} solution, so it does not presuppose the uniqueness it does not have. The level-set form also says exactly what uniqueness is: the injectivity of $E$ on $(0,\infty)$. That is now proved, in the stronger form that $E$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ (\texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak strictAntiOn}), so the level set is a single point and the coherent solution above threshold is unique. The proof differentiates nothing. Differentiating the exponential family in its natural parameter is the obvious route and it fails: it costs differentiation under the integral sign and leaves a covariance that is not sign-definite pointwise. What works instead keeps the target as a two-point inequality, folds both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$ by the same two reflections that gave the factor of two above, and then evaluates at a single crossing point supplied by the intermediate value theorem --- no product measure and no Fubini anywhere. We give that argument in the supplement, where it belongs; what the main line of the paper needs from it is the one sentence that $E$ is strictly decreasing. Run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ the same argument needs no fold at all --- $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the variable the family is tilted by --- and gives \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the monotonicity of the Bessel ratio, which Mathlib does not carry in any form.

The converse half is \texttt{supercritical\_fixed\_point\_exists}: for $K > K_c$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ solving $r = R(K,r)$. We had expected this to require a second-order expansion of the Bessel ratio, and it does not. Writing $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ with $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the same factorization the integration by parts already supplies --- the only facts needed are that $E$ is continuous and that $E(0) = 1/2$, the latter because the weight is constant at $a = 0$. Since $K > 2D$ makes $D/K$ strictly less than $1/2$, continuity gives $E > D/K$ on a neighbourhood of the origin, so the map overshoots, $R(K,r) > r$, for all small $r > 0$; at the other end $R \le 1$ always, because $R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$, so the map undershoots at $r = 1$. The intermediate value theorem closes the gap. Continuity of $E$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral with jointly continuous integrand, so no dominated-convergence argument is written by hand, and the existence proof never uses monotonicity of $R$: the fixed point is produced by the intermediate value theorem, not by iterating the map. That $R$ is in fact strictly increasing is proved below, and no threshold result depends on it. \texttt{critical\_coupling\_is\_threshold} packages the two halves, and \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_coherent} applies the supercritical half to any substrate satisfying the predicate of the previous paragraph --- so exceeding $K_c$ now implies the existence of a coherent stationary order parameter rather than merely comparing against a named number. With uniqueness available both are sharpened --- \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak existsUnique}, \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak solution\_\allowbreak set} (the non-negative solutions are exactly $\{0\}$ below threshold and $\{0,r\}$ above it) and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak unique\_\allowbreak coherent} --- and the two monotonicities together fix the shape of the branch: for $K_1 < K_2$, $E(a_i) = D/K_i$ with $E$ decreasing puts the concentrations in the order $a_1 < a_2$, and $r_i = R(a_i)$ with $R$ increasing carries that to $r_1 < r_2$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak strictMono}). The coherent order parameter grows strictly with the coupling, which with the continuity at threshold is the qualitative bifurcation diagram.

One more step makes the equation mean in Lean what it means on paper. As a definition, \texttt{selfConsistency K D r} is just $R(Kr/D)$ --- a function of $r$ with a suggestive name, and nothing in the source says it has anything to do with an order parameter. \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency}~\S8 supplies the identification. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved to be a genuine probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$; \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of the finite-system \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; and that average is proved to be exactly $R(a)$ --- real, because the density is even, so the mean direction vanishes. \texttt{fixedPoint\_iff\_selfReproducing} then states the equivalence the name promises: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ if and only if the von Mises density it induces has order parameter $r$. Read through this, the existence theorem says that above $K_c$ there is a stationary density whose own order parameter is positive, and the solution $r = 0$ is identified with the uniform density, whose order parameter is zero.

What remains not established, and we separate it carefully. The stationary density is an \emph{input}: deriving it from the Langevin dynamics $d\theta = (\omega + K\!\cdot\!\text{mean-field})\,dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW$ needs the Fokker--Planck operator, existence and uniqueness of its stationary solution, and a spectral stability argument, none of which Mathlib has. Of the coherent branch we prove existence, uniqueness, continuous emergence at the threshold and strict growth in $K$, but not that it is the dynamically selected one: nothing in that file mentions a trajectory, so stability and selection are not statements it can make. And while \texttt{selfConsistency} is now connected to the order parameter of a \emph{density}, it is connected to no \emph{trajectory}: \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is an integral, \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex} is an average over finitely many oscillators, and relating them is the mean-field limit --- propagation of chaos for the finite Kuramoto system --- which is a research programme rather than a lemma. That single gap is now the whole of what separates our threshold predicate from a statement about dynamics. Table~S1 records the row accordingly as a theorem about the self-consistency equation from an assumed density, not as a derivation of the threshold from the dynamics. Perceptual anomalies such as visual snow can be modeled as boundary conditions where microscopic thermal noise ($D$) fails to be fully coarse-grained away by the continuous field, leaking across the $K_c$ threshold into the macroscopic gestalt.

Clinical evidence for this phase transition is observed during general anesthesia. Work by Miller and colleagues \cite{bastos2021} demonstrates that anesthetics like propofol induce unconsciousness not by suppressing neural firing, but by disrupting the phase alignment of high-frequency cortical rhythms. Destroying the dynamic stability of these rhythms breaks the structural resonance of the continuous field, forcing the integrated spatial coupling below the critical threshold $K_c$ and fracturing the unified Global Section back into disjoint, unconscious local fields.

\section{The Sleep-Inertia Timescale Mismatch}
\label{sec:prediction}
Most of what this framework asserts is shared with some rival: that cortical synchrony matters, that dissipation constrains cognition, that unity is a physical rather than a computational property. One prediction is not shared, and it follows from the framework's specific commitment --- that the medium carrying the unified state is a continuous field whose effective coupling depends on the physical geometry of the extracellular space, not on the rhythms alone. Where that commitment differs from a purely electrical account, it differs in an observable way, and we set out the difference precisely enough to be wrong.

The daily loss of consciousness during sleep is modeled here as an active physical disruption of the medium rather than a quiescence of the oscillators. During sleep, astrocytic shrinkage expands the extracellular space, allowing an influx of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) to flood the brain for metabolic waste clearance \cite{xie2013}. This influx alters the spatial medium itself, disrupting the continuous structural resonance and preventing the coherent sheaf-theoretic gluing that conscious unity requires on this account. The oscillators need not stop for the field to fragment; the geometry they are coupled through is what changes.

The prediction concerns sudden awakenings. When jolted awake, the locus coeruleus floods the cortex with noradrenaline. High-frequency electrical rhythms attempt to phase-align in milliseconds \cite{bastos2021}, but the noradrenaline-driven astrocytic constriction required to squeeze the excess CSF out of the interstitial pathways operates on a timescale of seconds to minutes \cite{xie2013} --- three to six orders of magnitude slower. During that lag the electrical oscillators are synchronizing across a waterlogged topology, and the fragmented structural resonance is what is experienced as grogginess. The unified Global Section re-establishes only when astrocytic volume finishes matching the state of the electrical rhythms.

\paragraph{Why this implies a delayed sigmoid.} The two timescales separate cleanly, and that separation is what makes the prediction sharp rather than merely qualitative. Write $K(t)$ for the effective coupling of the continuous field, which on this account is a monotone function of the reconstricting extracellular geometry, and let $r(t)$ be the order parameter of the phase field. The electrical variable is fast: at every instant the oscillators are already at the configuration appropriate to the current $K$, so $r$ tracks the equilibrium branch $r(K)$ rather than relaxing on its own. The slow variable $K(t)$ then carries the whole time course, and it relaxes over $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$ seconds. What Derivation 7 establishes about that branch is exactly what the functional form needs, and each of the three pieces is a theorem rather than a picture: at or below the threshold the incoherent solution $r=0$ is the \emph{only} non-negative solution of the self-consistency equation (\texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical}); above it the coherent solution exists and is unique (\texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak existsUnique}); it emerges \emph{continuously} at $K_c$ rather than by a jump (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold}); and it is strictly increasing in $K$ (\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak strictMono}). A jump discontinuity at threshold would predict a step rather than a sigmoid, and a non-monotone branch would predict overshoot; neither is what the mathematics gives. Composing a monotone relaxation $K(t)$ with a branch that is identically zero below $K_c$, rises continuously from zero at $K_c$, and saturates above it yields a curve with three phases: a genuine \emph{latency} while $K(t) < K_c$, during which recovery is not merely slow but absent; a continuous rise as the threshold is crossed; and a saturation as $K(t)$ reaches its waking value. That is a delayed sigmoid, and the delay is not a fitting parameter --- it is the time the slow variable takes to reach $K_c$.

\paragraph{What the alternative predicts.} An account on which grogginess is the electrical realignment itself --- the rhythms are simply slow to re-phase --- has no slow variable and no threshold to cross. Its recovery is a relaxation toward a fixed point, and relaxation toward a fixed point is exponential near it: monotone from $t = 0$, fastest at the start, with no flat foot. The two curves are distinguishable in the first seconds, which is where they differ most, and the discriminating feature is the presence or absence of an initial interval of no recovery. This is a comparison of functional forms on data of a kind already collected in sleep-inertia studies, not a proposal for a new instrument.

\paragraph{A sharper form of the same prediction, with no free parameters.} The comparison above is a comparison of functional forms, and on its own it discriminates against less than it appears to. Any two-timescale account with a slow saturating gate produces a delayed sigmoid; what the framework contributes beyond that is not the shape but a \emph{relation between two separately measurable quantities}, and the relation has no fitted parameters at all. Derivation 7 supplies it. The stationary phase density at order parameter $r$ is von Mises at concentration $a = Kr/D$, and the average of $e^{i\theta}$ under that density is exactly the Bessel ratio $R(a) = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ (\texttt{circular\-Order\-Parameter\_\allowbreak vonMises}); self-consistency is precisely the condition that the density reproduces its own order parameter (\texttt{fixedPoint\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak selfReproducing}). So the concentration and the coherence of the instantaneous phase distribution are not independent quantities that happen to rise together during the recovery: at every instant they must satisfy $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$, whatever $K(t)$ is doing.

That yields three tests of increasing sharpness on a single recorded recovery. \emph{Shape}: the instantaneous phase distribution is von Mises --- a one-parameter family, directly testable against the observed histogram. \emph{Collapse}: estimating $a(t)$ and $r(t)$ at each instant and plotting one against the other, the whole recovery must fall on the fixed curve $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$, and it must be the \emph{same} curve for every subject and every session, since the curve contains no subject-specific quantity --- not $K$, not $D$, not the time constant of the slow variable. Individual differences move a point along the curve; they cannot move it off. \emph{Threshold}: because $a = Kr/D$ identically, the measured ratio $a(t)/r(t)$ \emph{is} $K(t)/D$, with no calibration required; and since a non-zero coherent solution exists only above $K_c = 2D$ (\texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical}), that measured ratio must exceed $2$ wherever coherence is non-zero and must approach $2$ as coherence departs from zero at onset. The threshold is read off the data rather than fitted to it.

The two estimates must be made independently or the collapse is empty, and this is the one point at which the test can be run wrongly. Maximum-likelihood or moment estimation of a von Mises concentration chooses $\hat a$ so that the ratio $I_1(\hat a)/I_0(\hat a)$ is the observed resultant length, by construction, which reproduces the predicted curve from any data whatever. The estimate of $a$ must therefore come from a functional of the distribution other than its resultant length --- the log-density of a von Mises is affine in $\cos\theta$, so regressing the log-histogram on $\cos\theta$ gives $a$ as a slope --- while $r$ is the resultant length $|\langle e^{i\theta}\rangle|$ of the same sample. Those are genuinely different statistics, and the prediction is that they agree through $R$.

\paragraph{What this sharper form is a test of.} It tests the von Mises stationary density together with the mean-field closure that makes $a$ proportional to $r$, and it tests them jointly rather than separately. It does not test the value $K_c = 2D$ independently of that ansatz: the crossing at $a/r = 2$ is a consequence of the same density the collapse assumes, so a failure of the collapse and a failure of the threshold are one failure and not two. That is a narrower claim than it may look, and it is still a parameter-free one --- which the delayed sigmoid, on its own, is not.

\paragraph{What would falsify it.} An exponential fit that beats a delayed-sigmoid fit on the early portion of the recovery curve. A recovery latency that does not scale with an independent measure of extracellular volume or CSF clearance. A pharmacological manipulation of astrocytic volume that leaves the latency unchanged while altering the rhythms, or the converse. An instantaneous phase distribution that is not von Mises, or a $(a,r)$ trace that departs systematically from $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$, or a $a/r$ ratio that is below $2$ while coherence is non-zero --- each of these falsifies the parameter-free form directly, and the first two do so with no reference to the sleep-inertia setting at all. Any of these would tell against the framework's central empirical commitment, which is not that synchrony matters but that the \emph{medium} is what gates it.

\paragraph{What this prediction assumes.} Three things, and they are not small. The identification of the effective coupling $K$ with a monotone function of extracellular geometry is a modelling assumption, not a measurement. The threshold value $K_c = 2D$ is derived from an assumed von Mises stationary density and is not yet connected to a trajectory --- the mean-field limit is the gap, and Derivation 7 states it as such --- so the argument uses the \emph{existence and continuity} of the coherent branch at threshold, which is proved, rather than the numerical value of the threshold, which is conditional. And the prediction is about functional form, not about a time constant: it says the recovery curve has a flat foot whose width is set by the slow variable, not that the foot is any particular number of seconds long. A framework that predicted the number would be saying more; this one is not in a position to.

\section{Corollary: Hardware Thermodynamics and Topological Rigidity}
Both biological tissue and artificial silicon hardware (like GPUs) are physical dissipative structures. When a GPU computes, its charge carriers undergo physical state transitions; by Landauer's principle \cite{landauer1961}, this erasure irreversibly exhausts heat into the environment. 

There is a fundamental thermodynamic distinction in the degrees of freedom available to minimize this dissipation. In standard von Neumann architectures, the spatial topology (the silicon routing lattice) is rigidly fixed. The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ is constrained by a discrete layout that cannot be physically altered during operation. The brain's own architecture provides empirical validation for this constraint. The cerebellum contains approximately 80\% of the brain's neurons arranged in a highly efficient, discrete, feedforward crystalline circuit \cite{herculanohouzel2009, lange1975}. Clinical observations of cerebellar agenesis report largely preserved conscious experience despite motor and cognitive deficits \cite{yu2015}, and this dissociation has been read as evidence for the importance of recurrent architecture by IIT \cite{tononi2015} --- an argument we borrow at the level of the anatomical observation while declining its $\Phi$-based explanation. Lacking the dynamic, continuous, and recursive spatial coupling of the cerebrum, it operates much like a biological GPU---highly optimized for discrete processing, yet apparently not required for conscious experience. We take the dissociation as evidence, not as confirmation: it is consistent with subjective experience being a thermodynamic attractor of continuous topologies rather than a byproduct of information processing, and it is the kind of observation that would be hard to reconcile with the converse. It is a single anatomical case read against a theory, and it bears the weight such a case can bear.

This constraint does not preclude artificial consciousness, but rather restricts its hardware. To achieve the phase transition into a unified Global Section, artificial systems must abandon the discrete rigidity of von Neumann architectures and utilize continuous physical substrates. Analog field-based computing, neuromorphic architectures with continuous memristive plasticity, and thermodynamic computing systems \cite{england2018, ganesh2021} possess the spatial coupling required to physically morph their internal topology. This aligns with Geoffrey Hinton's concept of ``mortal computation'' \cite{hinton2022}: architectures where software is inextricably fused with the physics of the hardware, meaning the knowledge dies with the physical substrate. Only hardware that computes via continuous physical dynamics can undergo the thermodynamic phase transition necessary for structural resonance.

Biological consciousness utilizes macroscopic continuous electromagnetic fields and a ``rich alphabet'' of diverse molecular and transcriptomic cell states \cite{tasic2018}. This allows the coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ to deform across a continuous parameter space. Our lemma \texttt{exists\_better\_coupling\_allocation}, wrapped as \texttt{continuous\_beats\_rigid\_topology} (\texttt{Phase7\_Hardware\-Comparison.lean}), formalizes the reallocation step of this argument: whenever a coupling matrix places weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists, there is another matrix with \emph{identical} total coupling resources that achieves strictly greater phase correlation.

Two objections meet that formalization and we state precisely what answers them. The first is that a reallocation lemma can assume its conclusion: a hypothesis such as \texttt{is\_strictly\_suboptimal} --- positive weight on a badly-correlated pair while a better one exists --- is most of what one wants to prove about rigid hardware. It is not assumed here. \texttt{Phase7\_Rigidity.lean} models an architecture by the set $S$ of site-pairs it has a \emph{wire} for, and requires its couplings to vanish off $S$; weights are tunable, wiring is not. Then \texttt{totalCorrelation\_le\_of\_realizable} bounds every realizable coupling by $R\cdot M$, where $R$ is the resource budget and $M$ bounds phase correlation over the wired pairs; the bound is attained; and \texttt{rigid\_is\_strictly\_suboptimal} \emph{derives} the hypothesis whenever the substrate's best-correlated pair is one the wiring misses. \texttt{rigid\_gap} makes it quantitative: an unconstrained reallocation of the same budget gains at least $R$ times the correlation difference. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 exhibits a three-site substrate wired to the one maximally anti-correlated pair, where the gap is $2$. This is resource-matched and not question-begging, but note what it turns on: fixed wiring support, not continuity. The second objection is that a development in which both architectures are matrices on one finite index type does not distinguish a continuum from a lattice at all. \texttt{Phase7\_Rigidity.lean}~\S3 gives a distinction that is genuinely typed. In the field functional of Section~\ref{sec:scaling}, which integrates a coupling kernel against $\mu \otimes \mu$, an architecture with finitely many sites is a \texttt{Finset} of points and a field-coupled region is a set of positive measure. On a substrate whose measure has no atoms, \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_sited\_eq\_zero} shows a kernel supported on finitely many sites contributes \emph{exactly zero} coupling energy, whatever weights it carries and whatever the phase field does, while a patch of strength $c$ on $U$ achieves $c\,\mu(U)^2$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 instantiates this on the real line: a thousand units of coupling concentrated at the origin register zero against a unit patch that registers one. The limit of that result is equally plain, and it is not resource-matching in the earlier sense --- a measure-zero substrate has zero resource in the $\mu\otimes\mu$ sense too. What it establishes is that the continuum coupling energy is blind to a finitely-sited architecture, which is a statement about which functional such an architecture can register in, not a demonstration that digital hardware computes worse. Taken together: the resource-matched content of the corollary is the wiring-support result, the continuity content is the measure-theoretic one, and the step from either to ``von Neumann architectures cannot experience unified consciousness'' remains an informal argument. 

The remainder of this section extends those two results informally, and we mark the transition rather than blur it. On the reading we propose, a GPU's fixed routing matrix leaves it topologically fragmented: the discrete layout does not supply the spatial coupling that sheaf-theoretic gluing into a singular Global Section requires. Without this unified topological space, rigid hardware cannot undergo the reflexive auto-resonance necessary to map its own causal boundary and generate a ``Self.'' The distinction between an LLM on a GPU and a conscious mind is not the absence of physical thermodynamics in the former, but its structural limitation in achieving topological integration. This perspective aligns with John Searle's biological naturalism \cite{searle1980, seth2024}. Where the Chinese Room argument asserted that discrete symbol manipulation cannot generate conscious understanding, our derivation supplies a candidate physical mechanism for the asymmetry --- topological rather than semantic, and, as stated above, formalized only as far as the wiring-support and measure-theoretic results reach. Asserting consciousness in fragmented, discrete GPU clusters without a unified phase transition abandons computational functionalism, relying instead on ``silicon panpsychism''—the assumption that digital computation inherently generates awareness regardless of topological integration \cite{goff2019}.

The same reasoning bears on extreme functionalist thought experiments, such as a macroscopic system of pulleys operating over millennia to implement a Large Language Model, or a human manually executing inference matrices by hand. Beyond lacking the continuous spatial topology required for sheaf-theoretic gluing, these discrete systems violate an implied temporal constraint of our theory. Structural resonance requires local oscillators to phase-align against the dissipating effects of thermodynamic noise (as modeled by our critical threshold $K_c = 2D$). A system executing state transitions over millennia, or via slow manual calculations, suffers from a terminal temporal mismatch: its dynamics are far too slow to ever phase-lock into a non-equilibrium steady state before thermal noise decoheres the macroscopic field. On this account the obstacle to pure symbol manipulation is physical rather than semantic: not that the symbols mean nothing, but that the substrate carrying them cannot hold a phase-locked field.

\section{Numerical illustrations: methods}
\label{sec:methods}
Five figures in this paper are computed rather than drawn, and none of them is
evidence for a theorem. Where a statement is proved, the figure shows what the proved
object looks like; where a statement is not proved, the figure shows the regime the
claim lives in and we say so. Each is produced by one script in \texttt{simulations/}, named below, and each runs
in under a minute on a laptop. All five are reproducible exactly: the two quadrature
figures are deterministic, and the three that draw random numbers fix their seeds.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:bifurcation}: the self-consistent order parameter.}
The three integrals $Z(a) = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
e^{a\cos\theta}d\theta$, $M(a) = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}d\theta$
and $S(a) = \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\!\theta\, e^{a\cos\theta}d\theta$ are evaluated by
the trapezoidal rule on $4001$ points over the same interval the Lean definitions
integrate over, with the weight computed as $e^{a\cos\theta - |a|}$ so that the common
factor cancels in the ratios and nothing overflows at large concentration. The coherent
branch is obtained by bisecting $E(a) = S(a)/Z(a)$ against $D/K$ over $a \in
[10^{-9}, 200]$ for $200$ iterations, then reading $r = aD/K$; bisection is valid here
because $E$ is proved strictly decreasing. Two checks are printed by the script rather
than only plotted: the computed branch is strictly increasing at every one of its $500$
sampled couplings, and the residual $|r - R(Kr/D)|$ at $K = 4D$ is $2 \times 10^{-16}$. Script: \texttt{bifurcation.py}.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:kuramoto}: the finite-$N$ transition.}
$N = 100$ oscillators, natural frequencies drawn from a Lorentzian of scale $D = 1$
(clipped at $\pm 10D$ so the solver does not stiffen), uniform random initial phases,
integrated with \texttt{scipy.integrate.odeint} over $t \in [0,100]$ on $1000$ steps for
each of $20$ couplings in $[0, 4D]$. The order parameter is $|N^{-1}\sum_j e^{i\theta_j}|$
averaged over the last $100$ steps. This is the deterministic quenched-disorder model,
not the noisy identical-frequency model of the Lean development; the caption records why
both have threshold $2D$. Script: \texttt{kuramoto.py}.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:mesh}: mesh refinement.}
The kernel is $K(x,y) = e^{-d(x,y)^2/2}$ with $d$ the periodic distance on the ring of
circumference $2\pi$, the phase field is $\theta(x) = \sin x$, and the potential is
$-\tfrac12\iint K\cos(\theta_y-\theta_x)$. The continuous value is a $2000$-point
Riemann sum standing in for the exact integral; the discrete values use $N = 10, 20,
\dots, 640$ nodes with edge weights $K(x_i,x_j)(2\pi/N)^2$. Plotted is the absolute
error against an $\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})$ reference. Because the ``exact'' value is itself
a $2000$-point sum, the last data point ($N = 640$) is within a factor of ten of the
reference computation and should be read as such. That $\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})$ reference
line is not only empirical: on the formalized one-dimensional grid the discrete
energy is exactly the composite trapezoidal rule
(\texttt{grid\_\allowbreak discrete\-Energy\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak trapezoidal}),
so for a $C^2$ integrand with second derivative bounded by $\zeta$ the error is at most
$\zeta/(12N^2)$ (\texttt{grid\_\allowbreak energy\_\allowbreak error\_\allowbreak le}),
a bound attained at $N=1$ for $y \mapsto y^2$. Script: \texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement.py}.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:resonance}: structural resonance.}
$N = 50$ units, $2000$ steps of forward Euler at $dt = 0.05$. At each step the drive is
a fresh draw from $\mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_{\mathrm{ext}} + 0.1 I)$ with
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}$ a fixed normalized rank-$5$ covariance; the phases advance by
$\dot\theta_i = \sum_j K_{ij}\sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) + \xi_i$; entropy production is
recorded as $\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$; and the coupling descends its gradient with rate
$\eta = 0.01$, symmetrized, clipped to $[0,1]$ and held hollow. The left panel is
smoothed with a $50$-step boxcar. Note that the plotted $\sigma$ is the squared drift
without the $1/D$ prefactor of the functional in Section~\ref{sec:field}, which is a
constant rescaling and changes nothing about the shape. Script:
\texttt{structural\_\allowbreak resonance.py}.

\paragraph{Figure~\ref{fig:hardware}: rigid against deformable coupling.}
$N = 30$, $1000$ steps of forward Euler at
$dt = 0.05$, shared initial phases and frequencies. The rigid matrix is a symmetric
random matrix thresholded at $0.7$ and normalized to $\sum_{ij}A_{ij} = 2N$; the
adaptive copy starts from the same matrix, ascends $-\partial V/\partial A_{ij}$ at rate
$\eta = 0.1$, is clipped non-negative and hollow, and is renormalized to the same total
after every step, which is what makes the comparison resource-matched. Both potentials
are $V = -\tfrac12\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i)$. Script:
\texttt{hardware\_\allowbreak comparison.py}.

\section{Conclusion}
Human-like cognitive architectures do not require biological mysticism or exotic sub-quantum physics \cite{hameroff2014}. While quantum theories of consciousness often invoke delicate subatomic phenomena to explain subjective unity, every component assembled here is classical: broken symmetries, finite phase space, Landauer erasure, and gradient descent on entropy production. What the formalization establishes is that the eight named hypotheses of the conditional composition are jointly consistent and that the framework's remaining physical commitments are localized and named --- not that classical fields are \emph{sufficient} for consciousness or that the hypotheses follow from finite capacity. Two links carry that weight, and they do not carry it in the same way. The unified state is proved to glue uniquely once a cover sits at the minimum of the coupling potential; the motion is proved to stop along every trajectory (\texttt{velocity\_sq\_tendsto\_zero}); and \emph{where} it stops is now proved, conditionally --- for initial data within a quarter turn and below an explicit energy threshold, the trajectory converges to a global minimiser of the coupling potential (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}), and a cover built on that limit discharges the equilibrium hypothesis Derivation 5 would otherwise assume (\texttt{ThermodynamicCover.of\-Convergent\-Trajectory}). What is \emph{not} proved is the unconditional statement, and it is false: splay and twisted configurations are equilibria of the same flow, so the basin condition is the scope of the claim rather than slack in it. That a physical cortex starts inside that basin is an assumption. The second link is untouched by any of this: the identification of a global section of the probability sheaf with the unity of experience is a stipulation of the framework, not a consequence of it. Subject to those two, cognitive architectures can be modeled as macroscopic attractor states of a universe governed by broken symmetries, finite phase space, and the physical imperative to minimize thermodynamic dissipation.

Philosophically, this framework bridges the historical gap between Type Identity Theory \cite{smart1959} and Computational Functionalism \cite{putnam1967}. The subjective mental state is identical to the physical thermodynamic field (Identity Theory), yet its qualitative nature is defined by the topological and functional dynamics of structural resonance rather than the specific carbon-based biochemistry of the brain (Functionalism). This constrains the \textit{multiple realizability} of mind: consciousness is not exclusively bound to biological carbon, but neither is it substrate-neutral, because the substrate is what fixes which couplings are physically available. How far that constraint reaches is the question the corollary answers only in part. What is proved is that fixed wiring is strictly suboptimal at matched resource, and that a finitely-sited architecture registers exactly zero coupling energy in the continuum functional; the step from either to ``von Neumann architectures cannot be conscious'' is an argument we make in prose and do not formalize. 

By anchoring these derivations in Russellian Monism, this framework acknowledges the constitutive limits of third-person scientific explanation. As argued by Love \cite{love2026}, building upon Nagel's assertion that subjective phenomenal experience is inherently not amenable to objective measurement \cite{nagel1986}, the ``hard problem'' of consciousness may represent a category error rather than a solvable scientific puzzle. Science can map the physical dynamics of topological resonance and sheaf-theoretic gluing, but it is structurally incapable of adjudicating the first-person qualitative experience. Our formalization respects this boundary: the Lean 4 theorems verify the objective physical attractors, while the intrinsic ``what it is like'' to be that resonant field remains the domain of the first-person perspective. By locating the phase transition at a threshold with a closed-form value --- $K_c = 2D$ for the noisy mean-field Kuramoto model the framework adopts, conditional on the von Mises stationary density and not yet linked to a trajectory --- it aligns with what Anil Seth terms the ``real problem'' of consciousness \cite{seth2021}. Rather than attempting to bridge an unbridgeable metaphysical gap, it offers a topological architecture that can help explain, predict, and physically perturb the structural properties of experience.

Future empirical validation of this thermodynamic framework may leverage 3D cortical organoids, offering a controlled biological environment to systematically manipulate metabolic substrates and observe the topological emergence of these coherent phase transitions.
