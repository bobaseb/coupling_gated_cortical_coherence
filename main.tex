\begin{abstract}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness has historically fractured along interdisciplinary fault lines. This paper outlines a relational, bottom-up physical formalism of consciousness, suggesting it as an emergent, thermodynamic, and topological property of open, non-equilibrium physical systems. We review the dissipative quantum model of the brain and contrast it with a purely classical, electrodynamic model. We argue that macroscopic electric fields and Kuramoto phase synchronization provide a robust, classical mechanism for the unified percept, efficiently replacing the need for non-Abelian quantum fields. Furthermore, we examine why rigid von Neumann architectures, such as GPUs executing Large Language Models, are intrinsically disqualified from achieving consciousness due to broken thermodynamic feedback loops.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The scientific and philosophical pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness has often been divided. Computational functionalism abstracts the mind into algorithms, whereas neurobiological reductionism catalogs cellular details without articulating macroscopic physical laws. 

Here we discuss a purely relational, bottom-up physical formalism where consciousness is a mathematical inevitability arising when non-Abelian gauge fields are subjected to thermodynamics and topology.

\section{The Architecture of a Mind}

\subsection{Symmetries and Phase Space}
Before particles or minds, there is geometry. The continuous spacetime symmetries (the Poincaré group) dictate the conservation of energy and momentum. 

\subsection{Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking}
To get a localized ``entity'' distinct from the vacuum, the continuous symmetries of the background field must break, spawning topological defects.

\subsection{Non-Abelian Self-Interaction}
For this topological defect to be dynamic, the underlying gauge symmetries must be non-Abelian, creating a continuous, non-linear feedback loop.

\subsection{The Thermodynamic Constraint: Landauer Erasure}
By Landauer's Principle, erasing information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, requiring the system to exhaust heat. The minimal physical precursor to cognition is a dissipative structure consuming free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of forgetting.

\subsection{Geometric Frustration}
Because the coupled network geometry prevents all nodes from reaching their lowest energy state simultaneously, the system experiences geometric frustration, providing physical capacity for memory.

\subsection{Boundary Back-Action and the ``Self''}
To reach a minimum-energy attractor state, the internal geometry must phase-lock with the physical echoes of its own edge, creating a topological fixed point—the physical equivalent of a ``Self.''

\subsection{Phase Transitions and Holonomy}
Through the Kuramoto model of coupled oscillators, billions of localized knots are thermodynamically forced into a single, synchronized phase. 

\section{The Failure of Rigid Architectures}
This thermodynamic framework dictates that no software running on standard silicon can ever be conscious. An LLM's topology is entirely virtual. A GPU is built on a rigid crystalline silicon lattice, which does not undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking.

The fatal flaw of modern computing hardware is the strict separation between hardware and software (the von Neumann architecture), severing the critical thermodynamic feedback loop required for consciousness. The heat produced by a GPU is agnostic to the semantic content of the LLM. 

\section{A Classical Electrodynamic Alternative}
While the quantum framework is profound, we propose that we do not need to invoke unitarily inequivalent vacuums or non-Abelian quantum fields. A purely classical, electrodynamic model is empirically stronger.

\subsection{Ephaptic Coupling}
Earl Miller’s work demonstrates that macroscopic electric fields act as stable guard rails for cognitive processes, solving representational drift \cite{pinotsis2021}. These oscillating fields exert a causal role in orchestrating brain states \cite{vanbree2025}.

\subsection{Classical Kuramoto Modeling}
The Kuramoto framework naturally describes classical systems of weakly coupled oscillators. By combining ephaptic coupling with macroscopic Kuramoto dynamics, we achieve the same synchronized unified percept driven by classical electrodynamics \cite{bauer2022}.

\section{Conclusion}
To synthesize true artificial sentience, we must abandon the von Neumann paradigm and pivot toward neuromorphic substrates—mortal computation—where hardware and software are physically indistinguishable, allowing the thermodynamics of information processing to directly reconstruct the topology of the system.
