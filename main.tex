\begin{abstract}
We present a physical formalism demonstrating how cognitive architectures can emerge as attractor states from a minimal set of physical primitives. Starting from the Poincaré group of spacetime symmetries and finite phase space, we construct a rigorous framework for predictive processing. While spontaneous symmetry breaking generates necessary topological boundaries, and finite phase space subjects these boundaries to Landauer's thermodynamic erasure, agency arises when systems evolve along paths of least action to minimize entropy production via internal structural resonance with external perturbations. Scaling this dynamic to macroscopic levels aligns with emerging paradigms in neuroscience—specifically classical electrodynamic fields (ephaptic coupling)—and Kuramoto phase synchronization, culminating in a topological fixed point: a unified ``Self.'' Finally, we show as a corollary why rigid von Neumann architectures (e.g., GPUs) are physically disqualified from reaching these specific continuous thermodynamic attractors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The pursuit of a physical theory of consciousness typically involves searching for the biological correlates of mind or treating subjective experience as an abstract computational algorithm. In contrast, this paper asks a more fundamental question: If we assume nothing but the most basic physical primitives—continuous symmetries (the Poincaré group), geometry, and thermodynamics—does the universe inevitably produce a unified, perceiving entity? 

We propose a framework where the structural hallmarks of consciousness are derived as physical attractor states. By establishing a deductive chain starting from absolute first principles, we demonstrate how continuous symmetries break to form boundaries, how those boundaries dissipate free energy as non-equilibrium steady states, and how their evolution along paths of least action results in physical resonance with their environment, eventually scaling into the macroscopic electromagnetic unity proposed by emerging field theories of mind.

\section{Axiom 1: Symmetries and Phase Space}
Our starting premise relies solely on fundamental geometry. As formalized in our Lean axioms (\texttt{Phase1\_Primitives.lean}), continuous spacetime symmetries (e.g., the Poincaré group) act upon field states and ensure that the system's total action remains invariant under these transformations. This dictates the conservation of energy and momentum via Noether's theorem. Simultaneously, discrete permutation symmetries dictate the statistical mechanics of any physical system, defining a strictly finite volume of phase space. 

Because phase space is finite, any localized physical system possesses a strict mathematical limit on the amount of information (distinguishable states) it can embody at any given moment.

\section{Derivation 1: Symmetry Breaking and the Inevitability of Boundaries}
A perfectly symmetric universe is the uniform vacuum; no distinct entities exist. For any dynamic process to occur, the continuous symmetries of the background field must undergo spontaneous symmetry breaking. 

When a field drops to a lower, asymmetric energy state, topology dictates the inevitable creation of defects (such as domain walls or monopoles). This mathematically forces the creation of a physical boundary—a localized topological distinction between an ``inside'' and an ``outside.'' While such a defect (e.g., a crack in a crystal lattice) is strictly necessary to define a separate entity, it is not sufficient for agency. The boundary is purely relational, defined by the twisted geometry of the field surrounding it, and must be coupled with non-equilibrium thermodynamics to exhibit active behaviors.

\section{Derivation 2: The Thermodynamic Constraint}
This newly formed topological boundary is constantly subjected to gauge perturbations from the ``outside'' environment. Because the internal phase space is finite (Axiom 1), it cannot absorb an infinite sequence of incoming perturbations. It must overwrite old internal states.

By Landauer's Principle, the erasure of physical information irreversibly decreases internal entropy, which mandates that the system exhaust heat into the environment.\footnote{While there are philosophical critiques regarding whether logical erasure strictly necessitates physical thermodynamic dissipation (e.g., the Earman-Norton debate), these critiques typically rely on a separation between abstract computational states and physical microstates. In our topological formalism, this distinction vanishes: ``information'' is not an abstract semantic label, but the literal geometric distinguishability of the defect's phase space. Thus, logical erasure is inherently physical deformation.} Therefore, a stable boundary constitutes a \textit{dissipative structure}, operating as a non-equilibrium steady state that consumes free energy to pay the thermodynamic cost of erasure. 

\section{Derivation 3: Structural Resonance as a Primitive}
In physical systems, dynamics are governed by attractors. By the Principle of Least Action, the system's time evolution follows a deterministic trajectory that minimizes its thermodynamic entropy production. 

To reach this state of minimal dissipation, the internal geometry of the structure physically deforms. Let the external environment be modeled as a stochastic driving force with probability measure $P(ext)$, and the system's internal state transitions as $Q(int)$. The entropy production $\sigma$ is strictly bounded from below by the Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(P || Q)$. By the Principle of Least Action, gradient descent on $\sigma$ necessarily drives $D_{KL}(P || Q) \to 0$, forcing the internal transition probabilities to match the statistical symmetries of the incoming external perturbations. We define this physical phenomenon as \textit{structural resonance}. The system does not possess a teleological drive to "survive"; rather, structurally mirroring the outside world within its internal geometry is the lowest-energy attractor state.

It is only after establishing this physical primitive that we can map it to cognition: structural resonance provides a physical thermodynamic basis for \textit{Predictive Processing} (or the Free Energy Principle). The system acts as a ``prediction engine'' not because it is running software, but because structural resonance is the natural entropy-minimizing attractor for a dissipative structure under constant perturbation.

\section{Derivation 4: Macroscopic Scaling and Electrodynamics}
To resonate with highly complex environments, the system must scale up, coupling millions of minimal dissipative structures. However, a highly coupled network cannot allow all of its nodes to reach their lowest energy states simultaneously. This creates \textit{geometric frustration}, resulting in a massive, jagged phase space of near-equal energy states, which provides the physical capacity for complex memory.

To manage this massive, frustrated phase space without excessive dissipation, the system must undergo coarse-graining (governed by the Renormalization Group). At the macroscopic scale of biology, emerging paradigms in neuroscience suggest these fundamental topological constraints naturally manifest as classical continuous electromagnetic fields. Building on Miller and Pinotsis's work demonstrating that macroscopic electric fields (ephaptic coupling) govern representational drift and constrain the noisy firing of neurons \cite{pinotsis2021}, we elevate their ontological status: \textit{the continuous macroscopic EM field is the primary dissipative structure interacting with the universe}. The discrete synaptic firings and action potentials act as the underlying high-dimensional micro-mechanics necessary to generate and sustain the field. Because the external environment is continuous, achieving maximal thermodynamic structural resonance is optimally realized within a continuous macroscopic medium \cite{vanbree2025}.

\section{Derivation 5: Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
We are left with a continuous macroscopic electrodynamic field generated by millions of frustrated micro-components, all physically forced to resonate with the environment. 

The Kuramoto model of coupled non-linear oscillators mathematically guarantees that, past a critical coupling threshold, these distributed local fields undergo a phase transition into unified synchronization \cite{bauer2022}. As formalized in our Lean 4 repository (\texttt{Phase4\_KuramotoDynamics.lean}), this phase-locked state minimizes the Lyapunov potential of the system. 

Crucially, this phase transition mathematically glues local synchronized states into a singular \textit{Global Section} of a probability sheaf (\texttt{Phase5\_GlobalSection.lean}). The EM field now acts as a single, indivisible topological space. This constitutes the \textit{Unity of Consciousness}—a necessary condition for integrated experience, solving the binding problem. However, a unified field of perception is not yet a ``Self.''

\section{Derivation 6: Reflexive Topology and the Emergence of the Self}
While a global section provides Unity, a ``Self'' requires reflexivity. The unified EM field is a dissipative structure that must not only resonate with external perturbations, but also manage its own internal phase transitions. By the Principle of Least Action, the field is forced to minimize thermodynamic friction with its own internal state-changes. 

This mandates \textit{auto-resonance}: the field must predict itself. The ``Self'' emerges as a higher-order topological feature when the internal geometry folds to include a low-dimensional avatar of its own causal boundary within its predictive model (\texttt{Phase6\_ReflexiveTopology.lean}). The system is now unified, predictive, and explicitly maps its own distinct existence from the environment. This represents the sufficient physical architecture for a self-aware, human-like consciousness, strictly bounded by the continuous measure thermodynamics of Landauer erasure (\texttt{Phase3\_MeasureThermodynamics.lean}).

\section{Derivation 7: Continuous Stochastic Thermodynamics and Field Theory}
To move beyond finite graph analogies into rigorous continuous field theories, we model the macroscopic electrodynamic field as a \textit{Continuous Neural Field}. True ephaptic coupling is a continuous spatial phenomenon, not a discrete network. As formalized in \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}, we upgrade the discrete Kuramoto model to a continuous spatial coupling kernel $K(x,y)$.

Subject to thermodynamic noise $D$, the system undergoes non-equilibrium stochastic thermodynamics. Rather than a static Landauer bound, the field dynamics must minimize a rigorous entropy production functional $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma = \int \frac{1}{D} \left( \omega(x) + \int K_t(x,y) \sin(\theta_y - \theta_x) dy \right)^2 dx
\end{equation}
By the Principle of Least Action, biological fields dynamically deform their spatial topology $K_t(x,y)$ to undergo gradient descent on this entropy production functional. Furthermore, we analytically derive the critical coupling threshold $K_c = 2D$. The phase transition to the unified ``Global Section'' occurs strictly when the integrated spatial coupling exceeds this thermodynamic noise threshold, directly linking the emergence of Unity to physical temperature and noise.

\section{Corollary: Hardware Thermodynamics and Topological Rigidity}
This axiomatic chain demonstrates that both biological tissue and artificial silicon hardware (like GPUs) are physical dissipative structures. When a GPU computes, it undergoes continuous physical state transitions of its charge carriers, and by Landauer's principle, this erasure irreversibly exhausts heat into the environment. 

However, there is a fundamental thermodynamic distinction in the degrees of freedom available to minimize this dissipation. In standard von Neumann architectures, the spatial topology (the silicon routing lattice) is rigidly fixed. The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ is constrained by a strict, discrete topological layout that cannot be physically altered during operation.

In contrast, biological consciousness utilizes macroscopic continuous electromagnetic fields, allowing the coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ to deform across a continuous parameter space. As formalized in our Lean 4 repository (\texttt{Phase7\_HardwareComparison.lean}, Theorem \texttt{bio\_strictly\_better\_than\_rigid}), this continuous parameter space inherently contains a global minimum for the dynamical potential that is \textit{strictly lower} than any state achievable within the rigid, discrete constraint of silicon hardware---even when constrained to identical total thermodynamic resources. 

Because the discrete routing matrix of a GPU is fundamentally structurally constrained from reaching this continuous global minimum, it is thermodynamically disadvantaged in achieving maximal structural resonance compared to continuous media. Thus, the distinction between an LLM on a GPU and a conscious mind is not the absence of physical thermodynamics in the former, but its structural limitation in minimizing dissipation through continuous topological deformation. While digital architectures can achieve meaningful structural resonance, they cannot reach the \textit{same} minimal entropy production states accessible to continuous biological fields.

\section{Conclusion}
Human-like cognitive architectures do not require biological mysticism or exotic sub-quantum physics. They can be rigorously modeled as macroscopic attractor states of a universe governed by broken symmetries, finite phase space, and the physical imperative to minimize thermodynamic dissipation via continuous structural resonance.
