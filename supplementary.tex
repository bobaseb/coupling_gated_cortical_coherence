\section{Macroscopic scaling and Kuramoto phase synchronization}
\label{sec:supp-phase}
We posit a continuous macroscopic coupling description. We model the local phases $\theta_i$ as coupled non-linear oscillators via the Kuramoto model.

\textit{Scope of the continuum description.} \nolinkurl{mesh_refinement_convergence} proves convergence of one scalar coupling energy. It produces neither a continuum kernel nor field dynamics: \nolinkurl{edge_region} has no $M\times M$ product structure from which such a kernel could be recovered, and the vertex-supported candidate is invisible to the continuum functional on an atomless substrate. Both obstructions are to this architecture: \texttt{KernelMesh} carries cell pairs of positive mass and its limit is a kernel integral, at the price of declaring the hardware that sets the weights. The symmetric kernel is therefore a modelling input either way. Its identification with the brain's endogenous \emph{electromagnetic} field is a further empirical commitment about cortex, argued in the main text from the ephaptic-coupling literature with explicit calibration and observational limitations. Nothing in this section mathematically establishes either step.

For a system with coupling strength $K$ and thermodynamic noise $D$, the dynamic evolution is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\theta_i} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) + \eta_i(t)
\end{equation}

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-stationary}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Stationary coherent branch):} For identical frequencies in a rotating frame with positive diffusion, the positive normalized stationary densities of the mean-field Fokker--Planck equation are the von Mises family, and the resulting self-consistency equation has exactly one positive solution when $K>2D$ and none when $K\leq2D$. Linearization about the uniform density has a growing mode exactly above that threshold, and the coherent branch carries a strictly dissipative quadratic form transverse to its rotation orbit. This does not assert dynamical selection or noiseless phase locking in the stochastic model.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase4_KuramotoDynamics.lean}):} We formalize the Lyapunov potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$ of the coupled field and prove two directions: \nolinkurl{phase_locked_minimizes_potential} (the phase-locked configuration attains the global minimum whenever all couplings are positive) and \nolinkurl{potential_min_implies_phase_locked} (any minimizer is phase-locked). Note the scope: this is a statement about the \emph{static} potential landscape and holds for all positive coupling matrices. The critical threshold $K_c = 2D$ does not appear in this file. It is formalized separately, in \nolinkurl{Phase8_SelfConsistency.lean} (see below), as a statement about the mean-field self-consistency equation, and the density that equation uses is classified in \nolinkurl{Phase8_FokkerPlanck.lean} from the stationary Fokker--Planck equation itself. What no file supplies is the passage from the stochastic differential equation to that equation, or a solution theory for its evolution. The numerical controls in Section~\ref{sec:supp-phase-controls} distinguish identical-frequency diffusion from the separate quenched-frequency model illustrated by \texttt{simulations/kuramoto.py}.

\textit{Positivity, and the regime these theorems cover.} The hypothesis ``all couplings positive'' is not a technical convenience and it is not free. \texttt{KuramotoSystem} carries $A : V \to V \to \mathbb{R}$, symmetric and otherwise unconstrained, so signed --- frustrated --- couplings are representable; but every result in this development that identifies \emph{where} the dynamics ends assumes positivity. \nolinkurl{phase_locked_minimizes_potential} and \nolinkurl{potential_min_iff_phase_locked} assume $A_{ij} > 0$; the class field \nolinkurl{ThermodynamicCover.A_pos} demands it of every instance, and so of every conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing}; and \nolinkurl{kuramoto_tendsto_global_minimum} requires the strictly stronger uniform bound $0 < a \le A_{ij}$, since $a$ enters the {\L}ojasiewicz constant. Such a network is an unfrustrated ferromagnet with one minimum up to a global phase shift --- exactly not the near-degenerate landscape a memory argument from geometric frustration requires.

By contrast, nothing about the sign of $A$ is used by \nolinkurl{is_kuramoto_trajectory_exists} and its uniqueness counterpart, by the Lyapunov identity \nolinkurl{dV_dt_le_zero} on which the whole descent argument rests, by \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_antitone} and \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_tendsto}, or by \nolinkurl{velocity_sq_tendsto_zero}. Since positivity is a \emph{field} of \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} rather than a hypothesis distributed across the development, it enters the chain at one node and not before it. So \emph{that} the motion stops holds for frustrated systems as well; \emph{where} it stops does not. On the continuum side \nolinkurl{phase_locked_minimizes_entropy_of_symm} assumes only symmetry and so admits signed kernels, but it takes the existence of a dynamically phase-locked field as a hypothesis, which is precisely what frustration threatens. The main text scopes the frustration claim out of the chain accordingly, rather than repeating it beside theorems that exclude it.

\subsection{Stationary self-consistency}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase8_SelfConsistency.lean}):} The self-consistency equation behind $K_c = 2D$ is formalized as real analysis. With the von Mises stationary density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{a\cos\theta}$, $a = Kr/D$, supplied as an input to this file and classified from the stationary equation in \nolinkurl{Phase8_FokkerPlanck.lean} below (\nolinkurl{simulations/fokker_planck_von_mises.py} checks the same algebra numerically), \texttt{besselRatio} is $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ written as a ratio of interval integrals, Mathlib having no modified Bessel functions. \nolinkurl{vonMisesM_eq_mul_vonMisesS} is the integration-by-parts identity $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta} = a\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, exact because $\sin(\pm\pi) = 0$ kills the boundary term. \nolinkurl{vonMisesC2_nonneg} proves $I_2(a) \ge 0$ by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ through the reflections $\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$ and $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$, where the integrand becomes a product of two manifestly non-negative factors; \nolinkurl{vonMisesS_le_half_vonMisesZ} restates it as $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$ and \nolinkurl{besselRatio_le_half_self} as $R(a) \le a/2$. That factor of $1/2$ is exactly what separates a threshold at $D$ from one at $2D$; \nolinkurl{subcritical_fixed_point_eq_zero} is the crude form obtained from $\sin^2 \le 1$ and is kept to make the point visible.

\nolinkurl{subcritical_fixed_point_eq_zero}$'$ is the subcritical theorem. For $D>0$ and $0 \le K < 2D$, the only $r \ge 0$ with $r = R(K,r)$ is $r = 0$.

The supercritical theorem is \nolinkurl{supercritical_fixed_point_exists}: for $K > \texttt{critical\_coupling } D$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ and $r = R(K,r)$. Its proof needs no series expansion. Factoring $R(a) = a\,\mathcal{S}(a)$ with $\mathcal{S}(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the factorization the integration by parts supplies --- it suffices that $\mathcal{S}$ is continuous with $\mathcal{S}(0) = 1/2$: since $D/K < 1/2$, the map overshoots for small $r > 0$, while \nolinkurl{besselRatio_le_one} ($R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$) makes it undershoot at $r = 1$, and \nolinkurl{intermediate_value_Icc}$'$ supplies the crossing. Continuity of $\mathcal{S}$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral, applied to the jointly continuous integrand. \nolinkurl{critical_coupling_is_threshold} states the two halves together, and \S9 of the file discharges the hypotheses of each at concrete values ($K = 3$, $D = 1$ above threshold; $K = 3$, $D = 2$ below it), so neither is vacuous.

Section 8 of the file identifies the equation with the physics it is named for. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved a probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$ (\nolinkurl{vonMisesDensity_integral_eq_one}); \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of \nolinkurl{order_parameter_complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; \nolinkurl{circularOrderParameter_vonMises} proves that average equals $R(a)$, real because \nolinkurl{vonMises_mean_sin} gives $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin\theta] = 0$; and \nolinkurl{fixedPoint_iff_selfReproducing} states the equivalence: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ exactly when the density it induces has order parameter $r$. \nolinkurl{incoherent_density_uniform} identifies the solution $r = 0$ with the uniform density, and \nolinkurl{supercritical_coherent_density} restates the existence theorem as the existence of a stationary density with positive order parameter.

Strict inequalities determine the threshold case. \nolinkurl{vonMisesC2_pos} upgrades the fold argument to a strict inequality for $a > 0$ --- \texttt{foldB} is continuous, non-negative on $[0,\pi/4]$ and strictly positive on its interior, so \nolinkurl{intervalIntegral_pos_of_pos_on} applies --- giving $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] < 1/2$ and $R(a) < a/2$ for every $a > 0$. That closes the threshold case: \nolinkurl{fixed_point_eq_zero_of_le_critical} covers all $K \le 2D$, so the incoherent and coherent regimes exhaust $K \ge 0$ with no gap, and $K = 2D$ falls on the incoherent side.

\nolinkurl{coherent_iff_sRatio_eq} reformulates the coherent branch: for $r \ne 0$, solving $r = R(K,r)$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{S}(Kr/D) = D/K$, so the branch is a level set of $\mathcal{S}$. \nolinkurl{coherent_branch_continuous_at_threshold} then proves that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that on $(2D, 2D+\delta)$ every coherent solution satisfies $r < \varepsilon$ --- the branch emerges continuously from zero, excluding a discontinuous jump. Its proof needs no monotonicity of $\mathcal{S}$: on a compact interval bounded away from the origin the extreme value theorem gives a maximum strictly below $1/2$, and beyond that the crude bound $\mathcal{S}(a) \le 1/a$ suffices.

Strict decrease of $\mathcal{S}$ and strict increase of $R$ give uniqueness and monotonicity of the coherent branch. Section~\ref{sec:fold} presents the folding and crossing argument. The consequences are \nolinkurl{coherent_fixed_point_unique} (at most one positive solution), \nolinkurl{supercritical_fixed_point_existsUnique} and \nolinkurl{supercritical_solution_set} (the non-negative solutions are exactly $\{0\}$ at or below threshold and $\{0, r\}$ above it), and \nolinkurl{coherent_branch_strictMono}, which uses both halves: $\mathcal{S}$ decreasing puts the concentrations of two couplings in the order $a_1 < a_2$ through $\mathcal{S}(a_i) = D/K_i$, and $R$ increasing carries that order to $r_1 < r_2$.

Scope: the density is an input to this file rather than an output of it, and the classification that derives it is a separate module; nothing in this file differentiates anything, so $\mathcal{S}$ and $R$ are reached as strictly monotone rather than as smooth, and the rate at which the branch leaves zero is the business of \nolinkurl{Phase8_CriticalExponent.lean} below; dynamical selection is not established, and could not be here, since no theorem in the file mentions a trajectory; and the order parameter reached is that of a density, not of a trajectory --- no theorem relates \texttt{circularOrderParameter} to \nolinkurl{order_parameter_complex}, which would be the mean-field limit.

\subsection{Stationary onset rate}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase8_CriticalExponent.lean}):} The stationary onset rate follows from a second-order expansion of the integral ratio. \texttt{vmMoment $n$ $a$} is the cosine moment $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^{n}\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, and \nolinkurl{hasDerivAt_vmMoment} differentiates it in $a$ under the integral sign, raising the cosine power by one; the interchange is Mathlib's dominated-convergence form for interval integrals, dominated by the constant $e^{|a|+1}$ on the unit ball around the parameter, which is where the compactness of $[-\pi,\pi]$ and the boundedness of $\cos$ do their work. Iterating gives \nolinkurl{contDiff_two_vmMoment}, and \nolinkurl{vonMisesSRatio_eq_moments} rewrites $\mathcal{S}(a) = 1 - \texttt{vmMoment }2\,a/\texttt{vmMoment }0\,a$, so $\mathcal{S}$ inherits two derivatives from a quotient whose denominator is positive.

The moments at $a=0$ are elementary integrals of powers of $\cos$: $2\pi$, $0$, $\pi$, $0$ and $3\pi/4$. They give $\mathcal{S}(0) = 1/2$, $\mathcal{S}'(0) = 0$ --- the first derivative vanishes by the symmetry that kills the odd moments --- and $\mathcal{S}''(0) = -1/8$, and Taylor's theorem in Mathlib's \nolinkurl{taylor_isLittleO_univ} form then yields \nolinkurl{vonMisesSRatio_second_order}: $\mathcal{S}(a) - (\tfrac12 - a^{2}/16)$ is $o(a^{2})$ at the origin. The remainder is $o(a^{2})$ and not $O(a^{4})$; the stronger form would need a fourth derivative and nothing here requires it.

\nolinkurl{coherent_solution_critical_exponent} then combines the expansion with \nolinkurl{coherent_iff_sRatio_eq}: writing $a = Kr/D$ and rearranging $\mathcal{S}(a) = D/K$ gives $K - 2D = K a^{2}(\tfrac18 - 2q(a))$ with $q$ the normalized remainder, and letting $K \to 2D$ along any family of positive coherent solutions with $r \to 0$ sends $q \to 0$ and $r^{2}/(K-2D) \to 1/D$. The statement is deliberately filter-indexed rather than parameterized by a curve, so it constrains every such family and privileges no path.

Because its hypotheses hold only eventually along the filter, and an eventual clause is vacuously true on the empty one, the file also exhibits a family satisfying it: \texttt{coherentBranch} selects the unique positive solution above threshold via \nolinkurl{supercritical_fixed_point_existsUnique}, \nolinkurl{coherentBranch_tendsto_zero} reads \nolinkurl{coherent_branch_continuous_at_threshold} as a limit along $K \downarrow 2D$, and \nolinkurl{coherentBranch_critical_exponent} discharges every hypothesis on it.

Scope is inherited unchanged from the file above: this is asymptotics of the solutions of a stationary equation. It establishes no stability, no dynamical selection and no time course, so reading it as a square-root foot on a recovery curve requires the adiabatic separation and a threshold crossing at non-zero speed, both of which the main text states as assumptions.

\subsection{The stationary equation and linear stability}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase8_FokkerPlanck.lean} and the five modules that build on it, named below):} The equation these six files work in is the classical stationary one on the circle. \texttt{current} is $J = v\rho - D\,\partial_\theta\rho$ and \texttt{operator} is $-\partial_\theta J$; \texttt{IsStationary} asks for a differentiable, $2\pi$-periodic, strictly positive and normalized density whose current is itself differentiable and whose operator vanishes. Differentiability belongs in the predicate rather than beside it: \texttt{deriv} is totalized, so an equation written with it alone is satisfied by functions that have no derivative where the equation is being asserted.

\textit{The density is classified rather than assumed.} \nolinkurl{integratingFactor_hasDerivAt} is the identity $\partial_\theta(\rho e^{V/D}) = -(J/D)e^{V/D}$ for a gradient drift $v = -\partial_\theta V$. Stationarity makes $J$ constant (\nolinkurl{stationary_current_const}), periodicity of $\rho e^{V/D}$ makes its integral over a period vanish, and $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{V/D} > 0$ then forces that constant to zero: \nolinkurl{stationary_current_zero}. The remaining equation is first order, giving \nolinkurl{stationary_gibbs}, $\rho = ce^{-V/D}$ with $c>0$, and with the cosine potential and normalization, \nolinkurl{stationary_iff_vonMises}: for the drift $-Kr\sin\theta$ the positive normalized stationary densities are exactly $\texttt{vonMisesDensity}(Kr/D)$. \nolinkurl{supercritical_stationary_existsUnique} transfers the scalar bifurcation theorem to this statement --- above threshold exactly one positive $r$ carries a stationary density whose own circular order parameter is $r$. No modified Bessel function, spectral theory or operator closure enters; the argument is the one \nolinkurl{vonMisesZ_pos} already used, run once more.

\textit{The linearization is derived from the equation.} \texttt{meanDrift} writes the mean-field coupling through the density's two trigonometric moments and \nolinkurl{meanDrift_eq_integral} identifies it with $-K\int\sin(\theta-\varphi)\rho(\varphi)\,d\varphi$. \nolinkurl{current_expansion} expands the nonlinear current at $q + \varepsilon u$ exactly, as the background current plus $\varepsilon$ times \texttt{linearCurrent} plus an explicit quadratic term, so \texttt{linearOperator} is the derivative of the equation rather than a separately declared list of rates; \nolinkurl{selfConsistent_stationary} confirms that a fixed point is stationary for the drift its own density generates.

\textit{The incoherent state.} On the uniform density the coupling reaches only the first harmonic. \nolinkurl{incoherent_mode_rate} gives, for both real Fourier families, rate $K/2 - D$ at $n = 1$ and $-Dn^{2}$ for $n \ge 2$, and \nolinkurl{incoherent_instability_iff} concludes that a mode of positive index grows exactly when $K > 2D$. The constant mode is excluded because a density perturbation carries zero mass. This is a per-mode computation and claims no completeness of the Fourier family in any function space.

\textit{The coherent branch.} \texttt{branchCoupling} $= D/\mathcal{S}(a)$ parametrizes the supercritical branch by concentration; \nolinkurl{branchCoupling_supercritical} places it above $2D$ and \nolinkurl{branch_stationary} makes its density stationary for its own mean field. \nolinkurl{generator_eq_neg_linearCurrent} identifies the primitive generator with minus that linearized current, \nolinkurl{generator_symmetric} makes it symmetric in the weighted primitive pairing by integration by parts, and \nolinkurl{generator_energy} evaluates its diagonal as $-D$ times the coherent quadratic form. That form is strictly positive away from rotation because of a strict moment inequality, and the inequality is derived rather than posited: differentiating the defining integrals gives the Riccati identity \nolinkurl{sRatio_riccati}, which rules out a vanishing derivative at positive concentration (\nolinkurl{sRatioDeriv_neg}) and yields \nolinkurl{cosine_variance_lt_sine}. With the weighted Poincar\'e estimate \nolinkurl{transverse_poincare}, whose constant is explicit and finite, the result is \nolinkurl{coherent_linear_stability}: on regular periodic perturbations of zero mass, orthogonal to rotation in the weighted primitive metric, the form is at most $-\texttt{linearStabilityRate}$ times the squared norm, and that rate is positive (\nolinkurl{linearStabilityRate_pos}). \nolinkurl{rotation_generator_zero} exhibits the rotation direction as a nonzero element of zero rate, so the transversality is necessary rather than a convenience: a gap on the whole space would be false, and stability holds modulo the collective phase.

\textit{Two functionals that are not the same functional.} \texttt{currentDissipation} integrates $J^{2}/(D\rho)$ over phase space. \nolinkurl{stationary_current_separation} evaluates it at the coherent stationary state, where it is zero, against the phase-averaged squared drift at the same state, which is positive. Both are functionals on phase space, so the difference is not a difference of domains; and neither is \texttt{sigmaContinuum}, which integrates squared drift over substrate sites at fixed phases and is the object of the plasticity results. No identification among the three is proved or used.

\textit{Consumers and witnesses.} \nolinkurl{IsEMFieldCoupling.coherent_phase_model} carries the stationary state and the gap predicate \texttt{HasCoherentLinearGap} to the identified field model of Phase 9, at that model's own coupling and diffusion; it transfers nothing to a heterogeneous spatial kernel. \nolinkurl{Examples/Phase8.lean}~\S21 fixes $D = 1$ and concentration $1$, checks that the stationary density is not the uniform one and that the stability rate is not zero, and runs the incoherent criterion at $K = 3$ and $K = 1$ on either side of the threshold.

\textit{Scope.} These are linear operator and energy estimates for the limiting equation. They construct no solution of the evolution equation, no semigroup and no self-adjoint closure; they establish no nonlinear or orbital stability and no dynamical selection; and the passage from the stochastic differential equation to the Fokker--Planck equation is not formalized anywhere in the development. Every declaration of the eight stationary modules named in this subsection and the two above it depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\noindent The gap between these stationary-density results and a finite trajectory requires a dynamical mean-field limit with appropriate initial laws and time horizon. General interacting-diffusion and Kuramoto results give relevant settings \cite{sznitman1991,daipra1996,bertini2010}; no theorem in this development establishes the particular limit needed for the finite controls below.

\subsection{Order and the cost of probability current}

The current functional in Eq.~\eqref{eq:current-cost} has a sharp bound that
requires no stationarity assumption. Write $m=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}J$.
For continuous $J$, continuous positive $\rho$ and $\int\rho=1$,
\[
 0\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{(J-m\rho)^2}{\rho}
   =\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{J^2}{\rho}-m^2
   =D\sigma_J-m^2.
\]
Strict positivity on this compact interval ensures that the reciprocal density
and the displayed integrands are integrable. The Lean theorem
\nolinkurl{current_integral_sq_le}
proves this expansion using the density's own Fokker--Planck current.
The equality witness has $\rho=1/(2\pi)$ and $v=\omega$, hence
$J=\omega/(2\pi)$ and $\sigma_J=\omega^2/D$;
\nolinkurl{uniform_driven_stationary} and
\nolinkurl{uniform_current_dissipation} verify both
the stationary equation and the cost. The concrete case $D=1$, $\omega=2$
has cost four and zero phase order.

Conversely, \nolinkurl{supercritical_zero_dissipation}
constructs, for every $D>0$, $K>2D$, a positive self-consistent stationary
order and its density with zero current dissipation.
\nolinkurl{no_positive_order_only_bound}
therefore rules out every universal bound strictly positive at all positive
orders. Keeping this density constant in time also refutes such a bound on
time averages. This concerns the identical-frequency rotating-frame gradient
model; it does not impose detailed balance on cortex. A positive cortical
power bound needs an additional drive or current constraint and a physical
thermal scale. For a bounded continuous test function $g$ with $g^2\leq1$,
\nolinkurl{weighted_current_sq_le}
proves $(\int gJ)^2\leq D\sigma_J$ by expanding
$\int[J-(\int gJ)g\rho]^2/\rho$ and using $\int g^2\rho\leq1$.
For a differentiable periodic current with continuous derivative, integration
by parts gives
\[
 \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\,(-\partial_\theta J)
 =-\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\theta\,J,\qquad
 \left(\int\cos\theta\,(-\partial_\theta J)\right)^2\leq D\sigma_J.
\]
This is \nolinkurl{cosine_rate_sq_le_dissipation}.
It bounds the rate of the cosine moment for evolving densities when time
differentiation under the integral is valid; it constructs no evolution.
The uniform density with imposed drift $v=\sin\theta$, $D=1$ has cosine rate
$-1/2$ and dissipation $1/2$, providing a nonzero-rate checked example.

For the rotation-invariant order magnitude
$r(t)=|\int e^{i\theta}\rho(t,\theta)\,d\theta|$, let the density and current
be positive, normalized and periodic in angle, continuously differentiable
as required by the classical continuity equation, and let differentiation of
the cosine and sine moments under the integral be justified. At times with
$0<r<1$, choose the angular direction of the first harmonic. Integration by
parts writes $\dot r$ as the current weighted by the perpendicular sine;
weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and
$\int\sin^2(\theta-\phi)\rho\leq1-r^2$ give
\[
 \dot r^2\leq D\sigma_J(1-r^2).
\]
The Lean results \nolinkurl{orderMagnitude_eq_norm_circular},
\nolinkurl{classical_moment_rates} and
\nolinkurl{order_magnitude_speed_bound} establish these steps for the stated
classical functions. If $r$ and the current cost are smooth on a finite
interval and $0<r<1$ throughout, the chain rule for $\arcsin r$ and temporal
Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \int_0^\tau\sigma_J(t)\,dt\geq
 \frac{[\arcsin r(\tau)-\arcsin r(0)]^2}{D\tau}.
\]
This is \nolinkurl{density_order_endpoint_cost_bound}; it does not assert an
absolutely continuous extension through zero order, construct the evolving
density, or convert current cost to heat. Strictly positive continuous
densities have $r<1$, but the theorem keeps that domain explicit.

The numerical check evolves a positive periodic density by a conservative
finite-volume flux and uses that same edge flux in the current-cost quadrature.
Equal-endpoint early, linear and late coupling schedules, an exact uniform
zero-current path and ordered diffusion relaxation are saved separately from
the artifact-only report. Diffusion, duration, grid resolution and a
two-frequency mixture are varied. The report interpolates the first crossing
of the exploratory $r=0.3$ threshold and compares accumulated current cost
up to that crossing against the endpoint floor for its elapsed time. The
crossing's measured cost exceeds the floor at every resolved grid; its slack
remains positive as the grid is refined. Each frequency cohort has its own
continuity equation. Their marginal has the aggregate current to which the
theorem applies, while the mean cohort cost is a distinct, larger account.
Frequency spread removes the identical-frequency drift premise of the
homogeneous simulation, not the aggregate continuity equation. The threshold
was chosen after inspection of an exploratory grid, so this is a numerical

error certificate.

A coupling parameter is a different observable.
\nolinkurl{arbitrary_coupling_speed_zero_cost}
uses $K(t)=e^{at}$, so $K>0$ and $K'(0)=a$ for any real $a$, while the uniform
self-consistent density has zero current dissipation at every time.

\subsection{Deterministic trajectories and their limits}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase4_RotatingFrame.lean}):} The full potential includes the natural frequency term $-\sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$ that appears in the Lyapunov descent result \nolinkurl{dV_dt_le_zero} in the combinatorial thermodynamics module. With any $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below (\nolinkurl{kuramoto_potential_unbounded_below}), so it has no minimum for a phase-locked state to attain, so the reduced and full potentials must be distinguished.

This file chains them by the standard rotating-frame reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For a system of identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$: \nolinkurl{is_kuramoto_trajectory_rotate} sends a trajectory to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system \texttt{sys.reduced} --- the same system Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing}'s \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} works with; \nolinkurl{kuramoto_potential_reduced} shows the two potentials coincide on it; \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_descent} transports the descent identity $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$ onto the potential above; and \nolinkurl{rotating_frame_chain} runs this through the minimum characterization to phase-locking and $r^2 = 1$. Phase differences, phase-locking and the order-parameter magnitude are proved frame-invariant (\nolinkurl{rotate_sub}, \nolinkurl{is_phase_locked_rotate}, \nolinkurl{order_parameter_r_sq_shift}), so no observable is lost. \texttt{Examples}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on a two-oscillator system, discharging every hypothesis, and exhibits a system whose full potential has no minimum.

Scope: the reduction is exact only for identical frequencies. A spread of frequencies leaves residual detunings $\omega_i - \Omega$ in the reduced system, to which the unboundedness result still applies, and phase-locking then depends on $K_c$, which this file does not treat --- see the \nolinkurl{Phase8_SelfConsistency.lean} note above for what is proved about the threshold, and note that it concerns a stationary density rather than the trajectories this file transports.

\textit{Trajectories that run.} The deterministic initial-value problem admits a unique global solution through each initial state. \nolinkurl{kuramotoField_lipschitz} (\nolinkurl{Phase3_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) proves the Kuramoto vector field globally Lipschitz with constant $2\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ --- $\sin$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the index set is finite, so there is no smallness caveat and the natural frequencies drop out. \nolinkurl{is_kuramoto_trajectory_unique} turns this into Picard--Lindel\"of uniqueness on all of $\mathbb{R}$: two solutions agreeing at one instant agree forever, in both time directions.

Existence follows by constructing solutions on arbitrary finite windows and gluing them using uniqueness. Mathlib's \texttt{IsPicardLindelof} caps a local solution's lifetime by the radius of the ball on which the field's bounds hold, divided by the field's sup norm there, and carries no global-in-time theorem; but \nolinkurl{kuramotoField_norm_le} bounds this field by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ on the \emph{whole} state space, so the cap can be defeated by enlarging the ball after choosing the window, and \nolinkurl{kuramoto_exists_on_window} produces a solution on every $(t_0 - T, t_0 + T)$. Uniqueness on an interval makes the family coherent --- solutions on nested windows agree where both are defined --- so $\theta(t) := \alpha_{\lceil|t - t_0|\rceil}(t)$ is a solution on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (\nolinkurl{is_kuramoto_trajectory_exists}), and \nolinkurl{kuramoto_cauchy_problem} states existence and uniqueness together as a single \texttt{ExistsUnique}: the trajectory predicate is inhabited exactly once for each initial state, rather than only by the solutions we write down by hand.

A two-oscillator solution illustrates the dynamics. On two oscillators with unit coupling and zero natural frequency the phase difference obeys $\dot\Delta = -2\sin\Delta$, whose solution $\Delta(t) = 2\arctan(c\,e^{-2t})$ splits symmetrically into an exact trajectory of the full system (\texttt{Examples}~\S15, \nolinkurl{pairRelax_is_trajectory}). At $c=1$ it starts a quarter turn out of phase (\nolinkurl{pairRelax_not_locked_at_zero}); the cosine of the phase difference tends to $1$, the order parameter tends to $1$ through the two-oscillator identity $r^2 = (1+\cos\Delta)/2$, and the dynamic potential tends to its global minimum. By the uniqueness result it is the only solution through its initial state.

Two general statements describe the long run, both for a zero-frequency system, which by \nolinkurl{kuramoto_potential_unbounded_below} is the only case in which the potential is bounded below at all. \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_antitone} upgrades the instantaneous descent to monotonicity in $t$ --- \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_deriv_nonpos} constrains each instant separately and does not by itself say the trajectory settles --- and with the uniform lower bound $-\tfrac12\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ monotone convergence gives \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_tendsto}: the potential converges along \emph{every} trajectory.

Integrating the Lyapunov identity gives the quantitative form, \nolinkurl{dissipation_integral_eq}: energy dissipated over $[0,T]$ is exactly the potential dropped, so by \nolinkurl{dissipation_integral_tendsto} the improper integral $\int_0^\infty \sum_i \dot\theta_i^2\,dt$ converges, to the total drop. The proof uses the derivative identity in \texttt{HasDerivAt} form, which includes differentiability and permits application of the fundamental theorem of calculus. \nolinkurl{rotating_frame_dissipation} transports both to a uniform-frequency system.

Finiteness of that integral is not the same as $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$: an integrable function can spike forever on ever narrower intervals. Barbalat's lemma supplies the implication, proved here as \nolinkurl{tendsto_zero_of_lipschitz_of_integral_le}: a non-negative Lipschitz function with $\int_0^T g$ bounded above tends to zero, because a spike of height $\varepsilon$ drags a window of width $\varepsilon/2K$ up with it and only finitely many such windows fit under a finite integral.

Its hypothesis is then checked for the dissipation rate without differentiating it: the velocities are bounded by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$, the field is Lipschitz in the configuration, and the configuration is Lipschitz in time because its derivative \emph{is} the bounded field (\nolinkurl{trajectory_dist_le}, by the mean value theorem, with no invariant region needed since the bound holds on the whole state space). The conclusion is \nolinkurl{velocity_sq_tendsto_zero}: along \emph{every} trajectory of a zero-frequency system, $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$; equivalently each $\dot\theta_i \to 0$ and the vector field along the trajectory tends to $0$ in the state space. The statement applies to arbitrary trajectories under these hypotheses, and \texttt{Examples}~\S15 applies it to the relaxing pair, whose velocity starts at $-1$ and tends to zero.

At zero frequencies, the flow is the negative gradient of its dynamic potential. The identity $\dot V = -\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$ is formalized by \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_hasDerivAt}. The minimizers of this potential are characterized by phase-locking. \nolinkurl{lojasiewicz_estimate} adds the missing ingredient: on the region where every pairwise phase difference lies within a quarter turn, $2a^2W \le \left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$, where $W$ is the excess of the potential over its minimum and $a > 0$ is a lower bound for the coupling. Its proof uses no spectral gap and no convexity, only two extremal indices: at the site $m$ carrying the largest phase every coupling term has $\theta_j - \theta_m \in [-D,0]$ with $D \le \pi/2$ the spread, so every term is non-positive, and the term from the site carrying the smallest phase is at most $-a\sin D$ (\nolinkurl{velocity_max_le}); against $W \le \tfrac12\left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)(1-\cos D)$ the estimate follows from $\sin^2 D = (1-\cos D)(1+\cos D)$ and $\cos D \ge 0$, which is the only place the quarter-turn hypothesis is used.

The initial energy bound keeps the trajectory in this region. Because all terms of the excess are non-negative, a single pair satisfies $\tfrac12 a(1-\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)) \le W$, so an initial excess below $a/2$ --- which \nolinkurl{dynamic_potential_antitone} preserves --- keeps every pairwise cosine strictly positive for all time. A difference escaping $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ would have to cross $\pi/2$ in absolute value by the intermediate value theorem, and there the cosine vanishes; so the quarter-turn region is forward invariant (\nolinkurl{spread_le_of_small}). Continuity on the connected half-line thus pins the phase differences to that branch.

The differential inequality $\dot W \le -cW$ with $c = 2a^2/\sum_{ij}A_{ij}$ then integrates, in the elementary form ``$W(t)e^{ct}$ has non-positive derivative'', to $W(t) \le W(0)e^{-ct}$ (\nolinkurl{excess_decay}). The rate transfers to the velocity through $|\sin d| \le \sqrt{2(1-\cos d)}$, giving $|\dot\theta_i(t)| \le Be^{-ct/2}$ (\nolinkurl{velocity_abs_le_exp}), which is integrable on $(0,\infty)$; since $\theta_i(t) = \theta_i(0) + \int_0^t\dot\theta_i$, the trajectory converges (\nolinkurl{phase_tendsto}), and $W \to 0$ forces the limit to be phase-locked, hence a global minimizer with $r^2 = 1$ (\nolinkurl{kuramoto_tendsto_global_minimum}). \nolinkurl{rotating_frame_tendsto_global_minimum} transports it to uniform frequencies, where what converges is the relative configuration.

The two hypotheses are on the initial data and neither can be removed: splay and twisted configurations are equilibria too, so ``every trajectory reaches the phase-locked state'' is false, and what is proved is the arc condition version --- which the two-oscillator witness satisfies for every $c$, since $2\arctan$ lands in $(-\pi,\pi)$.

\texttt{Examples}~\S17 runs the theorem on three sites, where the method of \S15 is unavailable because the system has no closed-form solution: the trajectory is produced by \nolinkurl{is_kuramoto_trajectory_exists} from the initial data $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, which is not phase-locked, and its limit by the convergence theorem as $\theta(0) + \int_0^\infty\dot\theta$; the numerical content of checking the hypotheses is $\cos\tfrac12 > \tfrac34$, supplied by \nolinkurl{Real.cos_bound}.

\subsection{The fold-and-cross proof of strict decrease}
\label{sec:fold}
Uniqueness of the coherent branch is the injectivity of $\mathcal{S}(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ on $(0,\infty)$, and what is proved is the stronger statement that $\mathcal{S}$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ (\nolinkurl{vonMisesSRatio_strictAntiOn}). A folding and crossing argument proves the result without differentiation. Differentiating the exponential family in its natural parameter gives $\mathcal{S}'(a) = -\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta,\cos\theta)$, which costs differentiation under the integral sign and leaves a covariance that is not sign-definite pointwise: symmetrizing with an independent copy gives $\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(X-X')^2(X+X')]$ with $X = \cos\theta$, an integrand changing sign with $X + X'$. The target is kept as the two-point inequality $C_2(a)Z(b) < C_2(b)Z(a)$ for $a < b$, and two moves settle it.

\emph{Fold first}: the two reflections that proved $I_2 \ge 0$ carry both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$, replacing $e^{a\cos\theta}$ by $W(a,\theta) = 2\cosh(a\cos\theta)$ and confining $\cos\theta$ to $[0,1]$, after which $\cos 2x - \cos 2y$ and $W(b,x)W(a,y) - W(a,x)W(b,y)$ have the same sign, both being governed by $\cos x$ against $\cos y$. This is the symmetrization $X \mapsto -X$ that the covariance form needed the tilt to perform, done once at the start; its analytic content is one identity, $2\cosh X\cosh Y = \cosh(X{+}Y)+\cosh(X{-}Y)$ with $\cosh$ increasing in $|\cdot|$.

\emph{Then cross}: instead of integrating that inequality over a square, let $\alpha$ be the mean of $\cos 2x$ under $W(a,\cdot)$ and $x_0 \in [0,\pi/2]$ a point where $\cos 2x_0 = \alpha$, supplied by the intermediate value theorem; evaluating at $y = x_0$ and integrating in $x$ alone gives the result, since the term carrying $W(b,x_0)$ vanishes by the choice of $\alpha$. No product measure and no Fubini appear.

Run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ the same argument needs no fold --- $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the variable the family is tilted by --- and gives \nolinkurl{besselRatio_strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the monotonicity of the Bessel ratio.

\subsubsection*{The bound against a cortical energy budget}

The condition $U_{\mathrm{inst}}>2D/\kappa$ concerns energy stored in declared actuator modes. A metabolic budget supplies a different quantity. Take a sphere of one LFP decay length at the density quoted there: $\energyNeurons$ neurons. At $\energyAtpRate$~ATP\,s$^{-1}$ per neuron for a mean action-potential rate of $\energyFiringRate$~Hz in rodent grey matter \cite{attwell2001}, of which synaptic mechanisms take $\energySynapticPercent$\% \cite{harris2012}, and $\energyAtpJoules$~kJ per mole of ATP hydrolysed at cytoplasmic concentrations \cite{nicholls2013}, each neuron draws $\energyNeuronPower$~W and the region's signalling power is $\energyRegionPower$~W, of which $\energySynapticPower$~W is spent at synapses. Over a declared interval of one phase-diffusion time $1/D=\energyResidence$~s at $D=\energyDiffusion$~rad$^2$/s, the metabolic expenditure is $\energyInstalled$~J. Another interval rescales that expenditure by its ratio to this one. For comparison, a stored-field estimate using LFP gradients of 1--5~mV/mm, radii 0.1--0.3~mm and an illustrative low-frequency relative permittivity of $10^5$ gives electrostatic energy $\energyStoredLow$--$\energyStoredHigh$~J (or $\energyStoredThermalLow$--$\energyStoredThermalHigh$~$kT$). Tissue impedance and dielectric measurements motivate these input ranges \cite{barbour2017,logothetis2007,gabriel1996}; the static-capacitor approximation remains a modelling choice.

The estimates differ by many orders of magnitude, but neither is a measurement of $U_{\mathrm{inst}}$. Continuous metabolism may replenish an actuator; a relation between its power, storage and coupling must be specified before that claim can be used in the theorem. The small electrostatic estimate alone does not show that the theorem's actuator energy is too small, because its priced modes have not been identified with the static field energy.

If a physical actuator model equated the expenditure over this interval with energy installed in its priced modes, the conditional requirement would be $\kappa>\energyKappaRequired$~J$^{-1}$s$^{-1}$. The expenditure is $\energyThermalQuanta$ times $kT$ at body temperature, but that large number cannot discharge an installed-energy premise. A mode is a priced rank-one contribution to the kernel; cortical field generation has no specified decomposition into such modes. The estimate therefore provides neither a cortical conversion factor nor a verdict on the threshold.

\subsection{Discrete coupling and mesh refinement}

\textit{From continuous to discrete coupling:} The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ above is not postulated. A triangulation of the substrate assigns to each edge $(u,v)$ a region of the manifold, and the coupling weight is the Lebesgue--Bochner integral of the scalar magnitude of the stress-energy tensor over that region (\nolinkurl{edge_weight}); symmetry and non-negativity of $A$ are then proved rather than assumed. The bridge back is mesh refinement: as the regions shrink, the discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_{u,v} \mu(R_{uv})\,f(x_u)$ should converge to the continuous energy $\int_S f$. Stated as a conjecture over the bare triangulation class, that is false: nothing there forces the regions to cover the manifold, empty regions have diameter zero, and the everywhere-empty triangulation refutes it. Stated correctly it is a theorem (\nolinkurl{mesh_refinement_convergence}), quantified over a sequence of triangulations of a region $S$ whose regions are measurable, pairwise disjoint across distinct edges, covering, and anchored to the embedded vertices, with mesh size tending to zero. The fixed-mesh error is bounded by $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$, with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and the symmetric double sum is proved equal to the midpoint rule over unordered edges.

Two limitations follow. The theorem gives convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate reported by \nolinkurl{mesh_refinement.py}. And \texttt{TriangulatedManifold} itself is bare data: it pairs a complex, an embedding and a family of edge regions without relating them, so the geometric conditions live in a separate predicate \texttt{IsRegularTriangulation}. Because those conditions are in tension --- cells disjoint yet covering, every vertex anchored to each of its edges --- \texttt{Examples}~\S6 exhibits an instance: the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into $N$ half-open cells, on which the convergence theorem is instantiated and the approximations are checked to genuinely change with $N$.

The discretization structure \texttt{DiscreteThermodynamics} \emph{carries} that predicate as a field, which is what makes its edge weights integrals over a geometrically constrained family rather than over arbitrary sets, with three consequences for the coupling weights. \nolinkurl{weight_self}: a vertex has no edge to itself, so the coupling matrix has zero diagonal. \nolinkurl{face_of_weight_ne_zero}: a nonzero weight $w(u,v)$ forces $\{u,v\}$ to be an actual face of the complex, so the discretization cannot invent couplings between vertices the triangulation does not join. And \nolinkurl{total_weight_eq_setIntegral}: $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) = \int_S |T|$, i.e.\ the total coupling strength is exactly the scalar stress-energy of the region covered --- nothing counted twice (disjointness), nothing dropped (covering). That last identity is the precise sense in which the coupling matrix is derived from the continuum rather than posited, and it is exactly what the everywhere-empty triangulation violates.

\texttt{Examples}~\S6 witnesses the structure on the same grid: each consecutive edge carries weight $1/N$, and the weights sum to $1$ for every $N$. The witness is one-dimensional and its stress-energy tensor is constant, so it establishes satisfiability, not a physically interesting discretization.

One field of the structure is deliberately left free and is marked \texttt{[MODELLING]}: \nolinkurl{scalar_magnitude}, the scalar read off the stress-energy tensor, constrained only to be non-negative. A $(0,2)$ tensor has no canonical scalar --- the trace, an observer's energy density and the tensor norm are different functions, and the structure carries neither a metric nor a frame --- so imposing one would pin a choice rather than derive it, and the three statements above hold for every non-negative choice, so it would prove nothing new.

That the choice is nonetheless a physical input is stated as a theorem rather than left as a remark: \S6 exhibits a second instance over the same triangulation, tensor and regions, differing only in that field, for which the partition identity again holds while every edge weight differs (\nolinkurl{gridThermo_magnitude_matters}). The remaining constraint, non-negativity, is the discrete shadow of an energy condition and is what makes the induced couplings a legal Kuramoto coupling.

\section{Global sections and the unity of consciousness}
\label{sec:supp-compatibility}
Phase synchronization does not imply overlap compatibility. The model represents global consistency by gluing compatible local descriptions; its identification with conscious unity remains a separate stipulation.

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-gluing}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Sheaf-Theoretic Unity):} In the sheafification of finite spatial measures over a substrate $X$, local sections agreeing on all spatial intersections $U \cap V$ uniquely glue into a global section. On a finite open cover, compatible finite-measure representatives have a unique finite-measure extension whose sheaf image is that section. The overlap agreement is an independent physical hypothesis; phase-locking does not imply it. This is not a theorem about arbitrary probability marginals over decoded contents, for which compatible overlaps need not determine a unique global joint distribution \cite{abramsky2011}.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase5_GlobalSection.lean}):} The headline gluing theorem separates two inputs. The class field \nolinkurl{ThermodynamicCover.thermodynamic_equilibrium} places the phase field at the potential minimum, while \nolinkurl{LocalSectionSynchronization.section_agrees_of_phase_eq} independently requires equal-phase patches to agree on overlaps. The second supplies the compatible family consumed by this sheaf theorem. The vector-content models below give conditions for agreement of their own local fields; they do not discharge this class field for an arbitrary cover.

What the second one says can be stated without the sheaf: on a discrete substrate two patch sections have the same restriction to their overlap exactly when they assign the same mass to every site they share (\nolinkurl{restrict_eq_iff_densityOn_eqOn}), so the hypothesis is that patches at a common phase carry a common local density where they meet. It excludes data the rest of the structure admits: a two-patch cover of the three-site substrate at one phase, with profiles differing at the shared site, satisfies every other requirement and fails this one (\nolinkurl{overlap_agreement_fails}). It does not require patches to agree away from their overlaps --- the three-patch witness carries three pairwise-different profiles.

The equilibrium condition can be obtained from a convergence result for a class of initial data: \nolinkurl{Phase5_EquilibriumBridge.lean} supplies \nolinkurl{ThermodynamicCover.ofConvergentTrajectory} from \nolinkurl{kuramoto_tendsto_global_minimum}, and \texttt{Examples}~\S17.1 is a three-patch cover built over the resulting phase field.

The cover class also has an independent instance (\texttt{Examples}, \S4), so the theorem is not conditional on a structure of unknown realizability. Its three-site substrate has two overlapping patches meeting at one site; local sections are germ families of the phase-indexed measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ in the sheafification of the probability presheaf. The family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\nolinkurl{phaseMeasure_not_const}), and \nolinkurl{thermodynamic_equilibrium} follows from \nolinkurl{phase_locked_minimizes_potential}.

Two further results specify the phase and section data. The theorem \nolinkurl{potential_min_iff_phase_locked} (\nolinkurl{Phase4_KuramotoDynamics.lean}) characterizes the minimizers of the reduced potential in both directions, from which \nolinkurl{ThermodynamicCover.phase_locked} follows for every instance. The witness in \texttt{Examples}~\S13 carries a non-constant phase field and a non-uniform invariant measure, with patch-local densities read from the sections through \texttt{densityOn}; agreement at the shared site is an equation between separately computed numbers. The limit is structural: phase-locking equates phases, while agreement of the associated sections remains the independent class field named above.

The gluing construction assumes local overlap agreement and derives both existence and uniqueness of the global section. The lemma \nolinkurl{probability_glue_unique} takes a compatible family to its unique global extension; \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.invariantMeasure} denotes that section. The construction uses local data, not a global state supplied as a premise.

\textit{Representation by an actual measure.}
\nolinkurl{SpatialMeasure.finite_glue_unique}
(\nolinkurl{Phase1_MeasureGluing.lean}) proves finite-measure gluing
directly on a finite measurable cover. Order the patches, remove the parts
already assigned to preceding patches, and sum the local measures restricted
to those disjoint pieces. Overlap agreement gives the prescribed restriction
on every patch, and equality of those restrictions gives uniqueness. Each
local measure can be extended by zero to the ambient space without changing
its restriction (\nolinkurl{measureOnOpen_map_subtype}).
\nolinkurl{ThermodynamicCover.existsUnique_measure_representation}
identifies the resulting measure's sheaf image with the cover's
\texttt{invariantMeasure}. It requires compatible finite-measure representatives
of the supplied local sections; these are additional hypotheses, with no
global measure supplied. No equivalence between arbitrary sheafified sections
and finite measures on arbitrary spaces is asserted. The real-line witness
uses two overlapping open intervals with distinct local measures and counts
their shared atom once. A countably infinite cover requiring unit mass at
every natural-number site has no finite-measure extension. These results
impose no normalization and choose no interpretation of the masses as content.

\subsection{Approximate overlap agreement}

Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing} admits no tolerance: the sheaf condition is an equation. Under an
$\varepsilon$-tolerance neither existence nor uniqueness of a global section
survives, and what replaces them is a selection rule with an explicit constant.
The results are stated for a finite substrate whose local states are nonnegative
site masses, with the uniform distance $\max_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|$ on the sites a
pair of patches shares (\nolinkurl{ApproximateGluing.profileDist},
\nolinkurl{Phase5_GlobalSection.lean}).

Let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a finite cover, let $s_i$ be the local profiles with
$\mathrm{dist}(s_i,s_j)\leq\varepsilon$ on $U_i\cap U_j$ for \emph{every} pair
(\texttt{Compatible}), and let $w_i\geq0$ vanish off $U_i$ and satisfy
$\sum_i w_i(x)=1$ at every site (\texttt{IsPartition}). The selection is
\[
(\mathrm{select}\;w\,s)(x)\;=\;\sum_{i} w_i(x)\,s_i(x).
\]
It depends on the profiles only through their values on their own patches
(\nolinkurl{select_eq_of_eqOn}), so the
bookkeeping values a patch carries elsewhere do not enter. Four estimates follow.
The selection is within $\varepsilon$ of each patch on that patch
(\nolinkurl{select_close}); two partitions subordinate to the same
cover select states at distance at most $\varepsilon$
(\nolinkurl{select_dist_le}); any two profiles fitting
every patch to tolerance $\delta$ lie within $2\delta$ of each other
(\nolinkurl{approximate_diameter_le}); and at
$\varepsilon=0$ the selection is the unique profile fitting all patches
(\nolinkurl{select_glue_unique}).

The constant in the second estimate is $C(N)=1$ at every cover multiplicity $N$,
rather than the $N\varepsilon$ a term-by-term count would give: the weights sum
to one rather than to the number of patches active at a site. This is a
statement in the uniform mass metric and not in total variation. A feature
$F$ that is $L$-Lipschitz on profiles therefore satisfies
$\mathrm{dist}\big(F(\mathrm{select}\;w\,s),F(\mathrm{select}\;v\,s)\big)\leq
L\varepsilon$ (\nolinkurl{feature_dist_le}), which is
the resolution condition an interpretation at scale $\delta$ must meet; the
theorem chooses no decoder.

The three-site cortex witnesses both halves
(\nolinkurl{Examples/Phase5.lean}~\S21). Its three
two-site patches have multiplicity two, and the family \texttt{apxW} is
\S17.1's data with one patch reporting $\varepsilon$ more mass everywhere it can
see. Two partitions --- each site assigned to the lowest, respectively the
highest, indexed patch containing it --- select states at distance exactly
$\varepsilon$ (\nolinkurl{apx_select_dist}), so the
constant is attained and cannot be improved at this multiplicity. For every
positive $\varepsilon$ that same family has no exact global section at all
(\nolinkurl{apx_no_global_section}): two
patches see the site \texttt{right} and disagree there, so selection stands in
for a gluing that does not exist. The corresponding sections fail the class
field of Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing} exactly as the two-patch fence does
(\nolinkurl{apxSection_overlap_fails}), and the tolerance
cannot be dropped to zero
(\nolinkurl{apxW_not_compatible_zero}).

Checking a spanning subfamily of the overlaps is not checking the family. The
family \texttt{chainW} agrees exactly on the overlaps $(0,1)$ and $(1,2)$ and is
off by $2\varepsilon$ on $(0,2)$; its two selections are $2\varepsilon$ apart
(\nolinkurl{chainW_not_compatible},
\nolinkurl{chainW_select_dist_gt}), so the
hypothesis quantifies over all pairs. On the same witness the total-mass readout
moves by $2\varepsilon$ between the two selections, within the $3\varepsilon$
its Lipschitz constant permits
(\nolinkurl{totalMass_apx_dist},
\nolinkurl{totalMass_apx_dist_le}): the
selection dependence of a readout is a real effect and not an artefact of a
loose bound. At $\varepsilon=0$ the three-patch family of \S17.1 satisfies the
relaxed hypothesis and the selection returns its glued profile
(\nolinkurl{trioW_compatible},
\nolinkurl{trioW_glue_unique}).

\subsection{Contents driven by observation}
\label{sec:content-dynamics}

The agreement above is a hypothesis on a supplied family. What a mechanism
would have to do is change the family, so the contents are carried as fields
$s_i:A\to\mathcal{C}$ in a real normed vector space $\mathcal{C}$ on the patches of a declared
cover. Disagreement at a shared site is $\|s_i(x)-s_j(x)\|$, and each patch's field
is mixed with that patch's observation at gain $\eta\in[0,1]$,
$s_i\mapsto(1-\eta)s_i+\eta o_i$. Disagreement across an overlap then obeys
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon'\leq(1-\eta)\varepsilon+\eta\delta,
 \label{eq:content-residual}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon$ the contents' mismatch and $\delta$ the observations' own
(\nolinkurl{update_residual},
\nolinkurl{Phase5_ContentDynamics.lean}).
Observations that agree on their
overlaps preserve exact agreement, and contract any initial mismatch to
$(1-\eta)^{n}\varepsilon$ after $n$ of them, even when the value they agree
on moves (\nolinkurl{update_preserves},
\nolinkurl{run_residual}). Observations agreeing only to tolerance
$\delta$ contribute that tolerance to the upper bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:content-residual}.

A readout names which patch to read at each site. Where that choice covers the
site, the readout is within $\varepsilon$ of every patch that sees it
(\nolinkurl{readout_residual}); at $\varepsilon=0$ it extends every
patch and is the only field that does
(\nolinkurl{readout_agrees}, \nolinkurl{readout_unique}).
Coverage is a hypothesis of those theorems rather than a property of a
pre-existing global field, which is the same discipline the selection results
above follow. The nonnegative mass profiles are the scalar case $\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}$,
read through the coercion at the same tolerance
(\nolinkurl{ApproximateGluing.compatible_pointwise}), so the two
agreement predicates coincide on those profiles.

\nolinkurl{compatible_real} and \nolinkurl{update_real}
identify the scalar instances. A common nonexpansive linear map preserves
compatibility (\nolinkurl{compatible_map}); the converse fails.
\nolinkurl{Examples/VectorContent.lean}, \S22, supplies a
nonconstant two-component family with a unique extension and two vectors
whose first projections agree while their second components disagree.

Observations agreeing only to $\delta$ are covered by the same recursion.
Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:content-residual} bounds disagreement by
$(1-\eta)^{n}\varepsilon+\delta$
(\nolinkurl{run_residual_floor}). The geometric term
decays for $\eta>0$, while the bound retains $\delta$. This is an upper bound;
it does not assert that the actual disagreement reaches that value. The next
subsection supplies a condition under which the observations themselves agree.

The contents remain vector-valued fields identified with no neural variable and
with no section of the probability sheaf.

The selection results concern nonnegative spatial mass profiles on a finite
substrate, while these updates act on normed-space-valued local fields. Using
the scalar instance as a dynamics of mass profiles requires observations and initial
profiles that preserve nonnegativity. Neither construction concerns matching
marginals of decoded contents \cite{abramsky2011}, or selects a cover, decoder
or weights. Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing}, the \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} instances and the
composed chain retain their exact agreement hypothesis.

\subsection{Agreement acquired from coherence}
\label{sec:coherence-agreement}

For declared encoders that agree as functions on overlaps, phase coherence
bounds observation disagreement. The shared-encoder hypothesis specifies how
two patches describe the same quantity; coherence constrains their inputs.

Write $N$ for the number of oscillators and $r$ for the order parameter. The
identity
\begin{equation}
 N^{2}r^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)
 \label{eq:order-mean-cos}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{order_parameter_r_sq_eq_mean_cos},
\nolinkurl{Phase4_KuramotoDynamics.lean}) rewrites $N^2(1-r^2)$ as
$\sum_{i,j}\bigl[1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)\bigr]$, a sum of nonnegative terms.
A single term is therefore at most the whole sum:
\begin{equation}
 1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)\le N^{2}\bigl(1-r^{2}\bigr)
 \label{eq:cos-gap}
\end{equation}
for \emph{every} pair (\nolinkurl{cos_gap_le_of_coherence}).
The factor $N^{2}$ comes from bounding one nonnegative summand by the full
sum. This estimate can be weak in large populations; the theorem makes no
optimality claim for its dependence on $N$.

A phase is an angle, so a content that is a function of phase must be a
function of the circle point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$; a model reading
$\theta$ as a real number is not $2\pi$-periodic and is not a function of the
phase at all. In the chord metric on that circle,
$\lVert\cdot\rVert^2=2(1-\cos\Delta)$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:cos-gap} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{chord}(\theta_i,\theta_j)\le\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^{2}}
 \label{eq:chord-bound}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_coherence}). Patch
$i$ reads oscillator $v(i)$ through an encoder $e_{i,x}$ taking circle points
to the content space $\mathcal{C}$. Require one uniform Lipschitz constant $L$ and
\[
 x\in U_i\cap U_j\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_{i,x}=e_{j,x}.
\]
These are \texttt{UniformLipschitzEncoder} and \texttt{SharedEncoder}.
Observations can vary across sites:
\[
 o_i(x)=e_{i,x}(\cos\theta_{v(i)},\sin\theta_{v(i)}).
\]
The resulting family is compatible at residual
$L\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^{2}}$
(\nolinkurl{compatible_of_shared_coherence},
\nolinkurl{Phase5_ContentDynamics.lean}). The residual is computed
from the phases using the supplied equality of encoders on overlaps.
At $r^{2}=1$ it is zero and the observations agree exactly
(\nolinkurl{compatible_of_shared_phase_locked}).
The one-encoder theorems are special cases with $e_{i,x}=e$. A uniform bound on
the phase residual over successive observations gives
\nolinkurl{run_residual_of_shared_coherence}.

The population in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound} need not be the whole array.
Nothing in the derivation uses more than a finite site type carrying an order
parameter, and a declared patch $P$ supplies one: the order parameter of the
subtype of $P$ is exactly $P$'s resultant, by the two facts that the sum over
the subtype is the sum over $P$ and that the two counts agree
(\nolinkurl{order_parameter_complex_patch}).
Instantiated there, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound} reads
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{chord}(\theta_i,\theta_j)\le\sqrt{2}\,|P|\sqrt{1-r_P^{2}}
 \qquad (i,j\in P)
 \label{eq:chord-bound-patch}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_patch_coherence}),
and the content statement follows with it whenever every patch reads a site of
$P$ (\nolinkurl{compatible_of_patch_coherence}).
The factor that can make the global estimate weak is the population's count, so
reading through small patches replaces it by a small one. What this does not do
is improve the global bound: Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound-patch} constrains the
pairs inside one patch and says nothing about a pair straddling two.
Section~\ref{sec:supp-wave} gives the state that separates the two readings
furthest.

Three results decline the step that puts the count there. It has one source:
$\sum_{ij}(1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j))=N^2(1-r^2)$ with every term nonnegative,
so each term is at most the whole sum --- which permits all the disorder in a
population to sit in the one pair the conclusion is about.

The first asks how many terms can be large rather than how large one can be,
which is Markov's inequality on the same sum. For any set $S$ of pairs whose
chords are all at least $c$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|S|}{N^{2}}\le\frac{2\bigl(1-r^{2}\bigr)}{c^{2}}
 \label{eq:chord-fraction}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{sum_gap_eq}, \nolinkurl{card_gap_le},
\nolinkurl{card_chord_le},
\nolinkurl{chord_fraction_le}), with no population size
in it at all. The bad set is supplied as an arbitrary \texttt{Finset} of pairs
rather than filtered, which is strictly more general --- the filtered set is the
largest such --- and keeps a decidability instance for a real inequality out of
the statement. Reading the same sum by rows gives the site form: if every site
of $T$ has at least $\delta N$ partners at chord $c$ or more, then
$|T|/N\le2(1-r^{2})/(\delta c^{2})$
(\nolinkurl{card_site_gap_le},
\nolinkurl{site_fraction_le}). Where
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound} improves like $\sqrt{1-r^{2}}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-fraction} improves like its square. What neither does is
name a pair or a site: both bound counts, the exceptional set they permit is
unlocated, and a cover's overlaps are a set of sites fixed before the state is
known. Reading either as agreement on an overlap requires the exceptional set to
miss that overlap, which is a joint fact about the state and the cover that no
coherence hypothesis supplies; the only route to it is to choose the cover in
the light of the state, which is the move that empties a gluing claim.

The second keeps the coherence hypothesis and drops the common patch. The nerve
of a family of patches has an edge wherever two patches share a site
(\texttt{patchNerve}, \texttt{SimpleGraph.fromRel} on that relation, so symmetric
and irreflexive by construction). The chord is a metric
(\nolinkurl{chord_triangle}), so a bound $\beta$ on the separation
inside every patch composes along a walk:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{chord}(\theta_x,\theta_y)\le(k+1)\,\beta
 \qquad (x\in P_a,\; y\in P_b,\; \mathrm{dist}(a,b)\le k)
 \label{eq:chord-nerve}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_patch_walk}),
with $\beta$ read off each patch's own resultant through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound-patch}
(\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_patch_walk_coherence})
and the content statement following with it
(\nolinkurl{compatible_of_patch_nerve}). The
constant is $(k+1)\beta$ and not $k\beta$ because both endpoint sites pay a step
inside their own patch, the shared sites of the walk being interior to the
chain. No patch need contain both sites, which is the situation of every pair of
territories far enough apart. The global resultant does not appear: a state
whose global resultant is zero still constrains content across a connected
cover. What is supplied is the connectivity: the nerve's diameter is a property
of the cover, nothing here produces a walk, and a cover whose nerve is
disconnected supports no conclusion between its components. Accumulation is
linear and nothing makes it sublinear, so the reach is a dozen or so patch
diameters rather than a hemisphere.

The third reads no resultant. A configuration is frequency-locked to a common
rotation $\Omega$ when $\omega_i+\sum_j K_{ij}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)=\Omega$ at
every site; this is not an extra postulate, being equivalent to the rigid
rotation $t\mapsto\theta+\Omega t$ solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:kuramoto} without noise
(\nolinkurl{is_frequency_locked},
\nolinkurl{is_frequency_locked_iff}). Write
$Q(x)=\frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2$ for the coupling-weighted Dirichlet form
(\texttt{couplingForm}), which on the adjacency matrix of a simple graph is the
graph Laplacian's
(\nolinkurl{couplingForm_eq_lapMatrix}), and declare a
spectral gap $\lambda_2$ by the Rayleigh inequality
$\lambda_2\lVert x\rVert^{2}\le Q(x)$ on mean-zero fields
(\texttt{SpectralGap}); turning $K$ up on a fixed graph scales it
(\texttt{SpectralGap.scale}). Pairing the balance equation with the
configuration symmetrizes
(\nolinkurl{sum_mul_coupling_sin}), Jordan's
inequality applied termwise keeps the sine and pays $2/\pi$ rather than
linearizing
(\nolinkurl{couplingForm_le_pairing}), and
Cauchy--Schwarz closes:
\begin{equation}
 4\lambda_2^{2}\sum_i\bigl(\theta_i-\bar\theta\bigr)^{2}
 \le\pi^{2}\sum_i\bigl(\omega_i-\Omega\bigr)^{2}
 \label{eq:spectral-spread}
\end{equation}
(\nolinkurl{spread_le_of_frequency_locked}),
read at a named pair as
$2\lambda_2^{2}\,\mathrm{chord}(\theta_i,\theta_j)^{2}\le\pi^{2}\sum_k(\omega_k-\Omega)^{2}$
(\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_frequency_locked})
and giving a content residual of
$L\pi\lVert\omega-\Omega\rVert/(\lambda_2\sqrt{2})$ with no population count, no
patch and no cover geometry in it
(\nolinkurl{compatible_of_frequency_locked}).
The bound improves as the coupling graph becomes better connected, which is the
dependence a resultant cannot express, $r$ being blind to who is coupled to
whom. Where it is informative is fixed by the hypothesis that licenses it:
confinement to a quarter turn puts every chord below $\sqrt2$ before any
coupling datum is read, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral-spread} constrains the pair
exactly where $\lambda_2>\pi\lVert\omega-\Omega\rVert/2$, and the comparison
that matters is against $\sqrt2$ rather than against the maximum chord. Four
things are declared and none is discharged here. Mathlib carries the
graph Laplacian and its positive semidefiniteness but no Fiedler value, so
$\lambda_2$ is an obligation in the standing of $\kappa$, computed in
\nolinkurl{Examples/Phase4.lean}~\S34 for the complete graph and for no
other. Existence of a locked configuration is assumed; the critical coupling
appears nowhere in the derivation. The quarter-turn confinement is assumed and
is not implied by locking. And the detuning enters in the population's $\ell^2$
norm, which is extensive, so a population whose frequency spread grows with its
size pays for that growth through the data: what is removed is the unconditional
factor, not the physics of heterogeneity. The balance equation is the
identical-frequency spatial model's, so reading Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral-spread} at
the scalar threshold still needs the reduction of Section~\ref{sec:supp-reduction}.

Witnesses run the third estimate on two oscillators with \emph{different}
natural frequencies locked at $\pm\pi/12$, every hypothesis discharged, the
configuration provably not phase-locked and the resulting chord bound $\pi/4$,
which is below the $\sqrt2$ the quarter-turn hypothesis gives for nothing and so
constrains the pair (\nolinkurl{chord_le_two} records the weaker
comparison against the maximum chord); and the
second on four sites covered by three patches in a line, bounding the pair that
no patch contains at $3\sqrt{2-\sqrt3}\approx1.553$, where the direct patch
bound is not merely weak but unavailable
(\nolinkurl{Examples/Phase4.lean}~\S34--35).

Neither route weakens \texttt{SharedEncoder}. Two patches meeting at a site must
still encode that site's quantity the same way, no amount of phase agreement
supplies that, and it is as true across a dozen hops as across one.

The result is fenced on both sides. Two antiphase oscillators have $r^{2}=0$,
computed through Eq.~\eqref{eq:order-mean-cos} rather than asserted, and at that
coherence the two patches demonstrably disagree
(\nolinkurl{Examples/AgencyFoundations.lean}), so the derivation is not
returning a vacuous zero residual. The bound is also not tight there: it gives
$2\sqrt{2}$ where the actual disagreement is $2$, which is the $N^{2}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cos-gap} being paid.

The shared-encoder obligation is substantive:
\nolinkurl{Examples/VectorContent.lean}, \S23, supplies a
site-dependent family that agrees at perfect locking, and a locked population
whose reports disagree by a unit when that obligation is dropped. The encoders,
uniform Lipschitz bound and common content coordinates are declared hardware
data. Identifying these vector contents with neural variables, and testing whether
their agreement matters for experience, remain the empirical work of
Section~\ref{sec:content-dynamics}'s closing scope.

\subsection{Phase offsets, winding and content}

\textit{Twisted gluing does not recover content from phase frustration (\nolinkurl{Phase5_TwistedGluing.lean}).} We also tested the natural weakening of exact overlap agreement: local states may differ by a phase on each overlap. \nolinkurl{gluesUpToPhase_of_isCoboundary} proves that coboundary offsets glue after one rotation per patch, while \nolinkurl{isCoboundary_of_gluesUpToPhase} proves the converse when the phase action is free on overlaps. Any Kuramoto configuration, including a twisted or splay state, supplies absolute patch phases $\phi_i$, so its offsets $\phi_i-\phi_j$ are automatically a coboundary (\nolinkurl{isCoboundary_of_phaseField}) and the twisted family glues (\nolinkurl{gluesUpToPhase_of_phaseField}). The formal \texttt{PhaseObstruction} quotient is deliberately not called \v{C}ech cohomology because its total cochains still assign irrelevant values to patch pairs with empty overlap.

The data give a narrower positive answer. \cite{townsend2015} identify phase singularities in multielectrode primate LFP recordings by an integer winding number around electrode loops, and the associated complex waves modulate spiking; \cite{xu2023} find task-dependent spiral centers and rotation directions in human fMRI phase maps. Around such a zero-amplitude point the circle-valued phase need not admit one real-valued lift. Local lifts differ on overlaps by integer multiples of $2\pi$, supplying measured transition data without an arbitrary gauge field. The same integers are available from a state rather than from a recording: on a ring the $q$-fold winding's affine lifts drop $q$ whole turns across the wrap and none elsewhere (\nolinkurl{ringTransition}, \nolinkurl{winding_succ}), which is what connects the obstruction below to the stationary states of Section~\ref{sec:supp-wave}. \nolinkurl{Phase5_PhaseLifts.lean} formalizes the finite electrode loop: \texttt{loopWinding} sums its integer transitions, \nolinkurl{loopWinding_eq_zero_of_hasGlobalLift} proves that transitions from one global lift telescope to zero, and \nolinkurl{threeElectrodeWinding_not_hasGlobalLift} gives a winding-one witness. This does not rescue \texttt{PhaseObstruction}: that quotient uses circle-valued differences of absolute patch phases, while the measured obstruction uses integer transitions between local lifts.

The cover-level specification has cortical surface $M$, analytic signal $z=Ae^{i\phi}$, singularity set $Z_t$, punctured domain $M^\ast_t=M\setminus Z_t$, and measurement map $q_t=z/|z|:M^\ast_t\to S^1$. A trial-independent electrode or source-space cover of $M^\ast_t$ supplies local real lifts; their differences divided by $2\pi$ are integer transitions on connected overlaps, and nerve loops are the inputs to \texttt{loopWinding}. In finite-resolution data the amplitude threshold defining $Z_t$, the loop geometry and the required cross-scale persistence are fixed using training or baseline data, never the content labels of test trials.

The proposed content-sensitive test compares nested preregistered classifiers. The control model contains stimulus, task, behavioural response, eye movement and arousal; the augmented model adds signed winding, source-localized singularity position, rotation direction and lifetime. Evaluation uses held-out participants and sessions, with label permutations within task, stimulus and arousal strata. The content hypothesis requires replicated incremental out-of-sample discrimination, including under a changed or absent motor report. This controls the known task dependence rather than mistaking it for content. A positive result would be an empirical association, not an identification of a topological sector with phenomenal content; a null increment rejects this proposed role. The construction and test remain outside the conditional chain, and a full nerve-indexed integer complex is deferred until the measurement protocol warrants it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase8_ContinuousField.lean}):} The continuous-field file defines $K_c=2D$ and a predicate for a substrate above that threshold. Two cautions. First, the predicate is not connected to \nolinkurl{is_continuous_kuramoto_trajectory}, \nolinkurl{entropy_production_rate}, or the order parameter of \texttt{Phase4}. What it is connected to is the mean-field self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$, whose bifurcation at $K = 2D$ we formalize in both directions (see the next paragraph): \nolinkurl{exhibits_phase_transition_coherent} shows that a substrate satisfying the predicate admits a solution with $0 < r \le 1$, and \nolinkurl{exhibits_phase_transition_unique_coherent} that it admits exactly one. So the threshold is not a bare stipulation, but what is proved is a statement about a stationary density rather than about a trajectory. Second, the predicate requires the substrate's \texttt{volume} to be a probability measure, and this hypothesis is load-bearing rather than decorative. It compares \nolinkurl{mean_field_coupling}, the kernel averaged over both arguments, against $2D$; on a substrate of total mass $m$ that average carries a factor $m^2$, so without normalization the comparison tracks the substrate's size rather than its coupling and can be satisfied by any kernel at all. \texttt{Examples}~\S9 exhibits a system a factor of two below threshold whose unnormalized double integral nonetheless clears $2D$. Under the hypothesis, \nolinkurl{mean_field_coupling_const} gives that a constant kernel has strength exactly $K$ and \nolinkurl{exhibits_phase_transition_const_iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$, witnessed in \S9 on both sides of the threshold.

\subsection{Local communication and timely responsiveness}
\label{sec:supp-locality}

\nolinkurl{Phase6_Locality.lean} defines a synchronous
\texttt{Network}: each update uses the site's own state and messages from its
declared neighbourhood. Its causal past $B_T(v)$ contains the sites whose
initial states can reach $v$ in at most $T$ rounds.
\nolinkurl{run_eq_of_agree_on_ball}
proves that initial configurations agreeing on $B_T(v)$ give the same state
at $v$ after $T$ rounds. Locality follows from the update construction.
The regional version uses the union of the sites' causal pasts.

A fresh intervention changes the initial state at $w$ while leaving the
baseline elsewhere fixed. If $w\notin B_T(v)$, all such interventions yield
the same code at $v$. Reading a report from that code therefore cannot
reconstruct two possible intervention values more than $2\varepsilon$ apart
to tolerance $\varepsilon$
(\nolinkurl{not_reconstructs_of_outside_past}).
This consumes the reconstruction inequality in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-reconstruction-family}. The report reads only the local
state, and accuracy is required over the declared intervention family.

\nolinkurl{Examples/Locality.lean}, \S25, gives a three-site line:
a value is unavailable at the reading site after one round and exact after
two. A cut path never delivers it; a constant report fails responsiveness over
separated values. A report correct for one fixed world remains possible.
Thus the theorem limits guaranteed response by a deadline and leaves static
gluing intact. Physical communication paths, round durations and relevant
deadlines require a separate model of the implementation.


\section{Content bounds beyond the uniform patch estimate}
\label{sec:supp-content-bounds}

What Eq.~\eqref{eq:main-content-coherence} of the main article bounds is a chord, which never exceeds $2$, so the estimate constrains anything only where $\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^2}$ falls below that. The winding sweep of Section~\ref{sec:wave} fixes both ends of that range on one sheet. Read on the sweep's own patch, \wavePatchSites{} sites at patch-local order \waveBandLocalOrder, the bound is $\wavePatchChordBound\,L$ and constrains nothing; holding that order fixed, the count alone brings it below the maximum chord only at \wavePatchInformativeSites{} sites or fewer. A nearest-neighbour pair of the same winding, the smallest patch there is, gives $\waveNeighbourChordBound\,L$. Shrinking patches therefore thins the overlaps at the same rate as it tightens the bound, and the two ends are not reached by the same cover. At a columnar grain the estimate constrains agreement inside one locally synchronous population and is vacuous across the distant territories whose agreement unity is about.

That vacuity is the uniform bound's, and three results decline the step that puts $N$ there: a sum of $N^2$ nonnegative terms bounded by its total, which permits all the disorder in a population to sit in the pair the conclusion is about. Asking instead how \emph{many} pairs can be far apart is Markov's inequality on the same sum, and its answer carries no population count: at order $r$ the fraction of pairs separated by a chord of $c$ or more is at most $2(1-r^2)/c^2$ at any size of population (\nolinkurl{chord_fraction_le}). The same sum read by rows bounds the fraction of \emph{sites} that are far from a fixed fraction of the population (\nolinkurl{site_fraction_le}). Read on the patch above, where the uniform bound gives $\wavePatchChordBound\,L$ and constrains nothing, this gives a fraction at most \wavePatchPairFraction{} of pairs disagreeing by more than $L$, and it improves like the square of what the uniform bound improves like. It is a different quantifier and not a stronger statement: a count names no pair, the exceptional set it permits is unlocated and may be exactly the sites a cover's overlaps sit on, and a guarantee about a named overlap therefore still needs the uniform bound at its own grain.

Distance is reached by chaining rather than by counting. Index the cover by its nerve, in which two patches are adjacent when they share a site. The chord is a metric, so a bound $\beta$ on the separation inside every patch composes along a walk: a walk of $k$ hops bounds its endpoints by $(k+1)\beta$ (\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_patch_walk_coherence}). The count is $k+1$ and not $k$ because both endpoint sites pay a step inside their own patch while the shared sites are interior to the chain, and the content statement follows for any cover whose nerve has diameter $k$ (\nolinkurl{compatible_of_patch_nerve}). No patch need contain both sites, which is the situation of every pair of territories far enough apart, so shrinking patches and spanning territories are two ends of one chain rather than two ends of one bound. What it costs is that the accumulation is linear and nothing makes it sublinear: at the sheet's nearest-neighbour value $\beta=\waveNeighbourChordBound\,L$ the chain stays inside the maximum chord for \waveNerveHops{} hops, which is \waveNerveDiameters{} patch diameters and \waveNerveReachMm~mm of that sheet. The territories whose agreement the compatibility claim is about are separated by centimetres, which is two orders of magnitude beyond where this chain saturates; the route reaches further only on a cover whose patches are larger or more coherent, and both are measurements. Connectivity of the nerve is a property of the cover, supplied rather than derived, and a cover whose nerve is disconnected supports no conclusion between its components.

The third result reads no resultant at all. Where a configuration is frequency-locked to a common rotation $\Omega$, its phase differences are not free: each site's detuning is balanced by its coupling term, $\omega_i-\Omega=\sum_j K_{ij}\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)$, which is the Kuramoto equations at a rigidly rotating solution rather than a further postulate. Pairing that balance with the configuration itself, symmetrizing, and applying Jordan's inequality termwise turns it into the coupling graph's Dirichlet form, whose algebraic connectivity $\lambda_2$ then bounds the spread about the mean phase $\bar\theta$ by $2\lambda_2\lVert\theta-\bar\theta\rVert\leq\pi\lVert\omega-\Omega\rVert$ (\nolinkurl{spread_le_of_frequency_locked}). Read at a named pair it gives a content residual of $L\pi\lVert\omega-\Omega\rVert/(\lambda_2\sqrt{2})$ with no population count, no patch and no cover geometry in it (\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_frequency_locked}, \nolinkurl{compatible_of_frequency_locked}), and it improves as the coupling graph becomes better connected, which is the dependence a resultant cannot express. Where it is informative is fixed by the same hypothesis that licenses it: confinement to a quarter turn already bounds every chord by $\sqrt2$ with no coupling data at all, so the spectral reading adds something exactly where $\lambda_2>\pi\lVert\omega-\Omega\rVert/2$. Four things are declared and none is discharged: $\lambda_2$ is hardware data in the standing of $\kappa$; the locked configuration is assumed, and nothing here proves one exists; the quarter-turn confinement is assumed and is not implied by locking; and the detuning enters in the population's $\ell^2$ norm, so a population whose frequency spread grows with its size pays for that growth through the data. What is removed is the unconditional factor, not the physics of heterogeneity.

Structural connectivity is a declared graph and is what supplies $\lambda_2$. Measured functional connectivity supplies it no better: it is an estimate, so it inherits the decoder failure modes of Section~\ref{sec:observations}, a shared prior shrinking estimates toward agreement and regional bias manufacturing disagreement, and feeding an estimate to a hypothesis strengthens no conclusion drawn from it. Using it to choose the cover is worse than neutral, being the move of Section~\ref{sec:unity} that empties a gluing claim, in different clothes.
\section{Reflexive topology and the self}
\label{sec:supp-self}
Self-representation is an additional relation between the glued global state and an encoding within it. The model specifies an encoding and readout whose composite fixes that state; contraction gives uniqueness. A dissipation bound imposes neither the encoding nor its accuracy.

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-self}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Conditional Auto-Resonance):} On a nonempty complete metric space of global sections, a self-prediction map has a unique fixed point if it is Lipschitz at the independently assumed rate $\Lambda=e^{-(K-K_c)\tau/2}<1$. A state with reconstruction residual at most $\varepsilon$ is within $\varepsilon/(1-\Lambda)$ of that fixed point. Coherence supplies $K>K_c$; it does not supply the Lipschitz law. To identify this fixed point with the section glued on a given cover, that section must itself be fixed by the map.

\subsection{States and their metric}

The construction in \nolinkurl{Phase6_ReflexiveTopology.lean} applies Banach's theorem to a complete metric space of global sections. The three-site witness in \texttt{Examples}, \S10, equips these sections with a metric derived from their spatial measures.

On a discrete substrate, every point has a smallest open neighbourhood. Restriction preserves point masses, so a germ at a site determines a mass there. The map \texttt{massEquivOn} identifies sections over an open set $U$ with mass profiles on $U$: injectivity follows because restriction to a point determines the germ, and surjectivity because a finite measure realizes every profile on the finite substrate. The global case is \texttt{massEquiv}. This correspondence also constructs the patch-local sections used by the compatibility witnesses.

The distance between two global sections is the largest difference between their site masses. Completeness follows by constructing the section represented by the limit of a Cauchy sequence of profiles. On this finite substrate, the metric is equivalent to total variation within a factor of the number of sites, but is not equal to it. Distinct sections at distance $1/2$ witness its nontriviality (\nolinkurl{exists_dist_lt_one}).

The metric matters to the fixed-point argument. Under the discrete $0/1$ metric, every contraction is constant (\nolinkurl{contracting_implies_const}). The witness instead uses uniform distance between densities. It makes no claim of equivalence with the L\'evy--Prokhorov metric: on a discrete substrate, thickening by $\varepsilon<1$ leaves a set unchanged, and averaging a measure with a baseline need not contract that metric.

\subsection{Encoding, readout and information loss}

A \texttt{ReflexiveBoundary} carries an encoding into a local region, called the avatar, and a readout back to global sections. Its predictive map is their composite. This ties reconstruction to the information passing through that region. A map that reads the whole field cannot serve as a witness unless it factors through the stated local encoding.

The separate predicate \texttt{IsRestrictionResonance rb} requires the encoding to be presheaf restriction to the avatar region. Under this condition,
\[
F(s)=\operatorname{readout}(s|_{\mathcal{A}}),\qquad
s_* = \operatorname{readout}(s_*|_{\mathcal{A}})
\]
at a fixed point. The second equality is \nolinkurl{self_eq_readout_restrict}. The more general \nolinkurl{self_mem_range_readout} places a fixed point in the range of the readout even without restriction resonance. These statements express what the local encoding reconstructs; restriction resonance is an assumption on the boundary, not a consequence of contraction.

Local readings can also determine a global state collectively. If a family of resonant avatar regions covers the substrate, equal readings at every avatar imply equal global states (\nolinkurl{eq_of_avatar_eq}). The proof uses the same gluing uniqueness as Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing}. A single avatar can discard information away from its region, so collective reconstruction from a cover must be distinguished from reconstruction through one region at a fixed point.

\subsection{The contraction hypothesis}

For $\tau>0$, the assumed factor is
\[
q=\exp\!\left(-\frac{K-K_c}{2}\tau\right),\qquad K_c=2D.
\]
The lemmas \nolinkurl{resonanceRate_lt_one} and \nolinkurl{one_le_resonanceRate} split the parameter plane at the threshold. Above it, a map Lipschitz at this factor has a unique fixed point (\nolinkurl{self_of_supercritical}). The bridge \nolinkurl{self_of_coherent_order_parameter} obtains the required inequality from the existence of a coherent order parameter. At or below threshold, the specified factor fails the strict bound required by \texttt{ContractingWith} (\nolinkurl{not_contractingWith_resonanceRate}); this argument is unavailable there.

The rate is motivated by the mean-field linearization near the incoherent state, $\dot r=\tfrac12(K-K_c)r+O(r^3)$. Linearizing the cubic normal form about its coherent branch gives relaxation rate $K-K_c$, twice as fast. The assumed factor $\Lambda$ is therefore larger than the coherent-branch relaxation factor. Both cross one at $K=K_c$. This comparison motivates a choice of bound; it does not derive a Lipschitz law for a neural encoding or readout from phase dynamics.

Banach's theorem establishes uniqueness for the specified map. E89 additionally requires the section glued on the specified cover to be fixed by that map, making it the unique fixed point. The theorem \texttt{chain} uses this equality and the contraction law but does not require restriction resonance. The interpretation through $s|_{\mathcal{A}}$ needs that additional predicate.

\subsection{Approximate reconstruction}

Let $F(s_*)=s_*$ and $d(s,F(s))\leq\varepsilon$. The triangle inequality and the Lipschitz law give
\[
d(s,s_*)\leq d(s,F(s))+d(F(s),F(s_*))
\leq\varepsilon+q\,d(s,s_*),
\]
hence $d(s,s_*)\leq\varepsilon/(1-\Lambda)$. The theorem
\nolinkurl{ReflexiveBoundary.approximate_self_bound}
applies Mathlib's \nolinkurl{ContractingWith.dist_le_of_fixedPoint}.
An estimate about a supplied fixed point requires neither completeness nor a separate existence proof; those hypotheses enter the existing Banach construction. The approximate states can form a set while the exact fixed point remains unique.

If $d(t,s)\leq\delta$, the same fixed map satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
d(t,F(t))&\leq\varepsilon+(1+\Lambda)\delta,\\
d(t,s_*)&\leq\frac{\varepsilon+(1+\Lambda)\delta}{1-\Lambda}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are \nolinkurl{prediction_residual_of_dist_le}
and \nolinkurl{approximate_self_bound_of_dist_le}.
The errors add before division by the contraction gap, whose inverse amplifies the bound near threshold. The displacement bound is supplied; this result does not derive approximate gluing or weaken Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-gluing}'s overlap hypothesis.

For $K>2D$, $\tau>0$ and $\varepsilon\geq0$,
\nolinkurl{approximate_self_bound_of_supercritical}
instantiates the estimate at the prescribed rate, and
\nolinkurl{resonance_error_radius_bounds} proves
\[
\frac{2\varepsilon}{(K-2D)\tau}
\;\leq\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1-e^{-(K-2D)\tau/2}}
\;\leq\;\frac{2\varepsilon}{(K-2D)\tau}+\varepsilon.
\]
The proof uses $1-e^{-x}\leq x$ and $x/(1+x)\leq1-e^{-x}$ for $x=(K-2D)\tau/2>0$, both consequences of $1+x\leq e^x$. Thus the radius has leading behaviour $2\varepsilon/((K-2D)\tau)$ and diverges as $K\downarrow2D$ at fixed positive $\varepsilon$ and $\tau$. This concerns the tolerance bound, not an actual state's error. Zero residual still gives zero distance at every supercritical coupling. The map, metric and Lipschitz law remain specified inputs; no result here models a changing readout or constructs a biological encoding.

\subsection{A nonconstant witness and its controls}

On the three-site cortex, the avatar is one site. The encoding reads its mass (\texttt{avatarRead}), and \texttt{avatarReadout} reconstructs a profile by averaging the reading with a baseline. The resulting map is nonconstant, has a unique fixed point, and contracts by one half of the difference visible at the avatar (\nolinkurl{cortexPredict_not_const}, \nolinkurl{cortexPredict_fixed_unique}, \nolinkurl{cortexPredict_dist}). Differences elsewhere are invisible to this map. The whole-field averaging map \texttt{relax} therefore cannot replace it as a witness for a one-site encoding.

The witness takes $K=3$, $D=1$ and $\tau=2\ln2$, so $\Lambda=1/2$ (\texttt{cortexResonanceRate}). These are parameters chosen for the example, not calibrated cortical values. The boundary \texttt{cortexReflexive} performs restriction and satisfies resonance. Its avatar distinguishes the silent field from the baseline, as shown by \nolinkurl{cortexReflexive_restrict_ne}. One avatar per site gives a covering family and reconstruction from the three readings (\nolinkurl{cortex_eq_of_avatar_eq}). The same map at $K=1$ exercises the failure of contraction at the prescribed subcritical factor (\nolinkurl{cortexSubcritical_not_contracting}).

Replacing the encoding by a constant makes the composite constant. It still has a unique fixed point, namely the readout of that constant (\nolinkurl{constResonance_existsUnique_self}). In the cortical control, this fixed point differs from the original baseline (\nolinkurl{cortexBlind_self_ne}), and distinct states have identical blinded readings (\nolinkurl{cortexBlind_not_determined}). A constant encoding fails restriction resonance whenever two states differ on the avatar region (\nolinkurl{constResonance_not_isRestrictionResonance}). The control thus distinguishes fixed-point uniqueness from sensitivity to the encoded state.

The approximate bound is sharp on the same one-site-avatar witness. The silent profile has $d(s,F(s))=1$ and $d(s,s_*)=2$, attaining $\varepsilon/(1-\Lambda)$ at $\varepsilon=1$, $\Lambda=1/2$
(\nolinkurl{cortexSilent_prediction_residual},
\nolinkurl{cortex_approximate_self_sharp}).
At $\Lambda=1$, \texttt{cortexIdentity} instead takes the whole substrate as its avatar and uses identity encoding and readout. It is nonexpansive, and the silent and baseline profiles are distinct fixed points
(\nolinkurl{cortex_nonexpansive_not_unique}).
This control establishes the failure of nonexpansive uniqueness on the same metric; it is not a model of compression into a proper local region.

\subsection{Information carried by a finite token output}

For a finite joint probability law $\mu$ on an input state $X$ and output
alphabet $V$, \nolinkurl{mutualInfo_le_output_entropy}
proves $I(X;V)\leq H(V)$ using the same KL-based mutual information as the

$p(x,v)\leq p_X(x)$ gives
$\log[p(x,v)/(p_X(x)p_V(v))]\leq-\log p_V(v)$.
Summing proves the inequality; zero joint atoms contribute zero, so neither
strict positivity nor deterministic emission is needed.
The finite entropy bound then gives $I(X;V)\leq\log|V|$ nats, or

\nolinkurl{token_information_bits_le}

\nolinkurl{no_tokens_no_information}

\subsection{When restriction resonance fails}
\label{sec:supp-resonance-failure}

Restriction resonance --- that the encoding a region carries is the global
state's own restriction to that region --- is a premise of how the fixed point
is read, not a consequence of contraction. Stated on bare maps it is agreement
between an encoder and a declared reference, \texttt{Resonates} $E$ $\rho$, and
\nolinkurl{isRestrictionResonance_iff_resonates} records that the boundary's
predicate has that shape with $E=$\nolinkurl{auto_resonance} and
$\rho=$\texttt{restrictToAvatar}.

Its failure has a criterion that evaluates neither map. Resonance forces the two
to confuse the same pairs of states, so a pair the encoder identifies and the
reference separates refutes it (\nolinkurl{not_resonates_of_confuses}), and so does
a pair the encoder separates and the reference identifies
(\nolinkurl{not_resonates_of_splits}). The first, applied to a boundary whose
encoder happens to be constant, gives
\nolinkurl{not_isRestrictionResonance_of_const_avatar}. The constant control
above refutes resonance for a boundary built by blinding another one; this
refutes it for any boundary at all, from a hypothesis about its encoder rather
than about its construction.

The second combines with accuracy, and it is the half that puts a condition on
the substrate rather than on the model.
Reconstructing two relevant states more than $2\varepsilon$ apart to within
$\varepsilon$ forces their codes to differ, by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-reconstruction-split}; so a boundary whose avatar region
reads those two states the same way is not resonant
(\nolinkurl{not_isRestrictionResonance_of_reconstructs}). Read forwards: a resonant
boundary that reconstructs a pairwise separated family restricts injectively on
it (\nolinkurl{restrictToAvatar_injOn_of_isRestrictionResonance}). The resolution
demanded is the region's. Which states a region reads identically is fixed by
where the region sits, before any encoding is chosen, so no read-out repairs it,
and E89 --- which supplies a Lipschitz law and identifies the glued section with
a fixed point --- supplies no such resolution. The implication runs one way: a
region that does resolve the family carries no resonant avatar by that fact, and
nothing here constructs one.

\nolinkurl{Examples/Reconstruction.lean}, \S24, exhibits both sides on the one-site
avatar. The baseline and the uniform profile at the same level agree at the
shared site and differ by two elsewhere, so the resonant boundary of \S10 does
not reconstruct that pair at tolerance $1/2$
(\nolinkurl{cortexReflexive_not_reconstructs_offAvatar}); a boundary over the same
region whose encoder names the state reconstructs the pair exactly and fails
resonance (\nolinkurl{cortexCheat_reconstructs}, \nolinkurl{cortexCheat_not_resonant}).

The deadline version is the same criterion with the causal past of
Section~\ref{sec:supp-locality} supplying the confusion, and it needs no
metric, tolerance or report. A reading region whose causal past excludes an
intervened site holds the same state in both worlds
(\nolinkurl{regionReading_eq_of_outside_past}), so its contents are not any declared
reference that tells the two worlds apart
(\nolinkurl{not_resonates_regionReading_of_outside_past}). On the three-site line
the region fails that reference at round one and meets it at round two
(\nolinkurl{no_resonance_at_one}, \nolinkurl{resonance_at_two}).

\subsection{What a biological interpretation must add}

The readout, its contraction law and the restriction-resonance condition are choices or hypotheses of a model. No field dynamics here produces them. The squared-drift objective is absent from the fixed-point result, so its minimizer is not identified with the self-model's fixed point.

The article's cup example illustrates the further content assignment: the represented state would include the scene and the perceiver's relation to it, and the encoding would need a demonstrated role in representing that relation. The three-site witness establishes mathematical realizability, not such a role. A nonconstant reconstruction and a unique fixed point alone do not establish a perspective or experience. Interventions on a candidate neural encoding would have to test the functional interpretation separately from the equality defining its fixed point.

\section{Phase dynamics and numerical controls}
\label{sec:supp-phase-controls}

The controls examine finite trajectories, population dependence and signed coupling under declared numerical models. They do not identify a cortical interaction or discharge the formal mean-field limit.

\subsection{Finite mean-field trajectory controls}

Two finite-$N$ controls probe this gap without closing it.  First, 500 seeded
replicas of 1,000 identical-frequency oscillators were integrated at fixed

\section{Recovery dynamics and mechanism identification}
\label{sec:supp-recovery}

\subsection{Assumptions}

The stationary calculation assumes identical frequencies in a rotating frame and positive phase diffusion. Composing its square-root branch with a smooth crossing at nonzero speed gives the article's Eq.~\eqref{eq:recovery}. Applying that deterministic tracking approximation to a trajectory additionally requires adiabatic branch tracking. A deterministic mean-field control starts from the initial stationary density displaced by a fixed amount in its order parameter and crosses on a leg symmetric about $K_c$. Its terminal quasi-static residual runs from $\quasistaticCrossingResidualMin$ to $\quasistaticCrossingResidualMax$ over scanned rates from $\quasistaticCrossingSpeedMin$ to $\quasistaticCrossingSpeedMax$. None meets the declared tolerance under this initialization; slowing the crossing does not remove that residual. This finite scan supplies no general tracking guarantee at onset. The stationary theorem proves no dynamical selection, and finite-size fluctuations require an operational escape criterion above the fluctuation floor.

Away from the crossing, the comparison gives admissible rates as a function of distance from threshold. The declared geometry and phase time-scale ranges used to interpret the control give a ratio between \quasistaticRatioMin{} and $\quasistaticRatioMax$. At the lower end the stationary reading meets the tracking criterion only down to $|K-K_c|=\quasistaticTrackableAtMinRatio$, and at the upper end down to $\quasistaticTrackableAtMaxRatio$. These ranges are modelling inputs to the comparison, not a measured cortical coupling trajectory. The physical hypothesis additionally requires a calibrated geometry--coupling relation.

A coupling that fluctuates rather than ramps is read against a statistic of $K(t)$ that the fluctuation rate selects. At correlation time $\noiseFastTau$, substituting the mean coupling reproduces the measured order to $\noiseFastMeanGap$ where substituting the quasi-static average $\mathbb{E}[r_{\rm ss}(K)]$ is wrong by $\noiseFastQuasiGap$; at correlation time $\noiseSlowTau$ with a drive that stays on one side of threshold the two exchange roles, at $\noiseSlowMeanGap$ and $\noiseSlowQuasiGap$ over \noiseSlowDrives{} drives. A slow drive that crosses admits neither. Mean-coupling substitution is refused in \noiseRefusals{} of the scanned drives, and in the strongest case --- telegraph coupling of mean $\noiseStrongestMean$, amplitude $\noiseStrongestAmplitude$ and correlation time $\noiseStrongestTau$ --- the measured order is $\noiseStrongestOrder$ where $r_{\rm ss}$ of the mean coupling is exactly zero, above the seed $\noiseSeedOrder$ the run started from. Its spread across replicas is $\noiseStrongestSpread$, the size of the effect itself. These findings concern order over the simulated horizon and the variability of individual runs; they establish no shifted asymptotic threshold.

\subsection{Prespecified spatial onset comparison}

Equation~\eqref{eq:spatial-onset-comparator} is a deliberately local extension of the first-harmonic growth rate $K/2-D$. Its two candidate couplings share the same measured drug-exposure trajectory $g(t)$ and differ in a spatial covariate. The field score $h_i=N_i\mathcal{T}_iE_i f_i$ uses an anatomical population count, timing sensitivity, pre-onset extracellular amplitude and frequency estimated independently of the onset outcome. The connectivity score $c_i$ is a prespecified weighted structural degree from a separate network measurement; it is a defined comparator, not a claim that every synaptic model predicts hub-first recovery. Normalizing each score across regions leaves one scale $a_F$ or $a_C$ to fit on training sessions. The same independently estimated $D_i$, observed baseline order $r_i(t_0)$, threshold relative to each region's baseline fluctuations, and recording observation model enter both candidates. A site crossing is predicted only while the first-harmonic small-order approximation applies.

Estimate the two covariate maps from anatomy and pre-onset recordings without using onset outcomes. Fix regions, the pre-onset window, the regional threshold, the recording window and the observation model before scoring. Split sessions within subject into training and held-out sets before fitting either scale; a claim of transfer to new subjects requires a separate subject-held-out evaluation. Both models use the same measured drug-exposure trajectory, baseline order, local diffusion, regional timing-error law and rules for missing channels. Fit the scales and timing-error parameters on training sessions only. The primary score is the sum of held-out regional log predictive scores: an observed crossing contributes the log predicted onset-time density, and a region with no crossing by the end of the window contributes the log predicted survival probability. The shared observation model supplies both probabilities and includes sampling resolution and channel noise. A missing channel has no observed onset and contributes no score term; report its frequency and spatial pattern. Site order, with ties at the recording resolution, is a secondary outcome. Apply the same exclusions and censoring rules to both models.

Predeclare a comparison of mean held-out log score across sessions, with uncertainty computed at the session level so that multiple regions in one session are not treated as independent replications. A region permutation of each covariate is a negative control. Repeat the score over a prespecified range of baseline-scaled thresholds and reference montages to reveal threshold and common-reference sensitivity. Record drug exposure, arousal, motion, reference choice, electrode coverage and task timing as nuisance inputs under the same observation model. Behavioural time-to-response to a standard stimulus is a separate endpoint: estimate its association with regional onset after those inputs are controlled, and report failure to observe a response as censoring at the behavioural recording limit. A missing association would count against the proposed link between the measured onset and recovery, even if one spatial map predicts onset well. Neither endpoint is a direct measurement of coupling or experience.

Before recruitment or acquisition, pilot data must estimate regional phase-noise and timing-error scales, the rate and duration of censored and missing observations, within-subject variability across taper rates, drug-exposure measurement error, and the correlation between field and connectivity scores. These determine a precision or power calculation for a prespecified held-out score difference; no sample size follows from the mean-field theorem. If the covariate maps are nearly collinear, or their predicted onset differences are smaller than temporal and spatial resolution, the design cannot discriminate them. If both models score poorly, neither specified local approximation is supported. A held-out field-model advantage would support the field covariate relative to this weighted-degree comparator, not every possible synaptic model. Interregional coupling, spatially varying phase spread, and the mapping from drug exposure to effective coupling remain physical inputs. An independent perturbation of the proposed field contribution would be needed for causal identification. Content decoding and an experiential measure pose separate tests.

\subsection{Alternative models and failure conditions}

Exponential relaxation is one comparator, not the entire class of electrical models. Slow latent variables, observation filtering and finite-size effects can also generate delayed or sigmoidal traces. Comparisons should use common preprocessing and observation models, with onset criteria specified before inspecting outcomes. A linear apparent onset counts against the adiabatic branch interpretation only in a regime where its assumptions hold.

The concentration--coherence relation tests the stationary-density model. Its failure over an informative concentration range constrains that model; its success does not identify the coupling medium. The ratio $a/r$ is a consequence of the same density ansatz and is unreliable near the fluctuation floor. A coupling--geometry association requires an independently validated geometry proxy and an independently defined recovery endpoint. Stationary absence of a fold does not rule out finite-rate hysteresis.

\subsection{What successive stationary phase distributions cannot separate}
\label{sec:recovery-mechanisms}

The first item of the protocol asks for $K$ and $D$ in the same units because
the stationary density does not carry them separately. In the
identical-frequency rotating frame that density is von Mises with concentration
$a=Kr/D$, so a window of equilibrated phases fixes the ratio $K/D$ and nothing
beyond it. Three recovery mechanisms therefore agree on every stationary
observable, window by window. Write $s\in[0,1]$ for a declared window
coordinate, $q(s)$ for a schedule rising from $1.5$ to an endpoint $b$, and
$r(q)$ for the coherent branch of the stationary self-consistency relation,
which is zero for $q\leq2$. Increasing coupling is $K=q(s)$ at $D=1$;
decreasing diffusion is $K=b$ at $D=b/q(s)$; and a common drive is oscillators
with no coupling to each other, each relaxing towards a shared phase $\psi$
under a prescribed $-h(s)\sin(\theta-\psi)$ at $D=1$ with $h(s)=q(s)\,r(q(s))$.
The first two share $K/D=q(s)$; the third has stationary concentration $h/D$,
which is that same number. Their stationary distributions coincide window for
window, so no estimator reading only those distributions separates them. The
third is one observationally matched drive and not a claim about every drive.

An ideal feasibility check confirms that the coincidence is exact rather than
merely close. Nine equally spaced windows, exact densities on a periodic grid
of $4096$ points, endpoints $b\in\{2.6,3.2,4\}$ and orientations
$\psi\in\{0,0.7\}$ give $\recoveryStudyCaseCount$ cases. Concentration is
estimated by regression of log density on the cosine and sine of phase and
coherence independently by circular integration; each family is then fitted for
$b$ over the same range against the same predicted concentration curve, with the
generating family, $K$, $D$ and true $b$ withheld from the fit. The three
families return the same $b$ on every case, agreeing to within
$\recoveryStudyFitSpread$ at squared errors of at most
$\recoveryStudyMaxSquaredError$, and $\recoveryStudyUniqueCount$ of the
$\recoveryStudyCaseCount$ cases identify a unique generator. No family meets the
declared gate of unique recovery on $80\%$ of its cases, and the study stops
there: observation noise, filtering and pooling applied to distributions that
are already identical cannot separate them.

The observable the protocol is missing is the physical clock. Coupling and
diffusion scaled together leave every stationary density untouched and change
the rate at which phase moves, so resolved increments carry what equilibrated
windows discard: along an ideal continuous path the quadratic variation
accumulates at rate $2D$, and conditional increment means constrain the drift.
Separating an endogenous coupling from a common drive is not settled by the
clock either, since in the mean-field description the two impose the same drift
on each oscillator; that separation needs a measured drive or a controlled
perturbation. Neither measurement is validated by this pilot, and neither would
identify the coupling as electromagnetic. The model comparison of item 6 is
accordingly a comparison of time courses under a declared observation model,
and identifies the mechanism behind a recovery only to the extent that the
observations reach past the stationary density.

\section{Coupling plasticity controls}
\label{sec:supp-plasticity}

The following controls examine whether the specified squared-drift objective acquires its diagnostic environmental structure while preserving coherence. They report transient and complete-interval objective measurements separately. Kernel alignment is the measured quantity; overlap compatibility requires the distinct content specification in Section~\ref{sec:supp-overlap-test}.

\subsection{Joint phase and coupling plasticity control}

This control tests alignment between a coupling kernel and a specified environmental template. It does not measure overlap compatibility. Its role in the article is to show why coherent adaptation under an objective does not itself supply the representational structure that gluing requires.

A finite driven control co-evolves phases and symmetric nonnegative couplings
at fixed total coupling. It uses $N=100$, $D=0.1$, $dt=0.01$, mean row coupling
$4$, and three nearly equal frequency clusters at $0.5$, $1$ and $1.5$ per unit
time. Couplings update every $50$ fast steps with learning rate
$\plasticityLearningRate$ for $400$ time units, across three seeds. The
symmetric-edge derivative of
$\frac12\sum_i v_i^2$ is $(v_i-v_j)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$; each update clips
negative weights, hollows the diagonal and restores the total resource.
This constrained update does not guarantee monotonic descent during joint noisy dynamics.
Each seed runs four arms on a single phase-noise stream: the gradient step, a
symmetric hollow random step rescaled to the Frobenius norm of that arm's own
gradient, that arm's own gradient step composed with a random node relabelling
drawn afresh at every update, and a frozen kernel. Each direction is computed
from the arm's own phases and couplings, so the controls follow the gradient
arm's recipe and no control receives its contemporaneous step; the step sizes
the recipe produces are comparable rather than identical, since the arms'
kernels diverge. At zero learning rate all four arms reproduce one
trajectory, which is the contract the shared stream is tested against.

The nonzero grand-mean frequency drives the collective coordinate, since
symmetric interactions cancel in its mean drift. That cancellation fixes
$\sum_i v_i$ across phase fields, so the objective cannot fall below
$N\langle\omega\rangle^2/D=\plasticitySigmaFloor$ and the update can only
equalize drift across nodes rather than remove it.

The objective is sampled at the update cadence, at the instant each update has
just acted, so every objective mean in this subsection is a mean of that
immediate post-update readout and not a time mean over the intervals between
updates. On it the frozen arm sits at
$\plasticityFrozenDissipationMin$ to $\plasticityFrozenDissipationMax$; the
gradient arm's second-half mean is $\plasticityDissipationMin$ to
$\plasticityDissipationMax$, removing a fraction $\plasticityDescentMin$ to
$\plasticityDescentMax$ of the headroom between the frozen arm and that floor.
The random arm removes $\plasticityRandomDescentMin$ to
$\plasticityRandomDescentMax$ of it, so the reduction is specific to the gradient
direction and not to displacing a kernel of that step size at fixed resource.
What that readout measures is the objective the update leaves behind it at the
moment it acts. Whether the objective stays below the frozen arm through the
interval that follows is a separate measurement, reported in
Section~\ref{sec:plasticity-intervals}, and it is the one a claim of sustained
minimization would need.
The alignment statistics below are read from the kernel, which is constant
between updates, so sampling them at the update cadence weights them exactly.

A step size set the same way is not a matched deformation. Fresh isotropic
steps cancel across updates, so the random arm ends at a cumulative kernel norm growth of
$\plasticityRandomNormGrowthMin$ to $\plasticityRandomNormGrowthMax$ against the
gradient arm's $\plasticityNormGrowthMin$ to $\plasticityNormGrowthMax$, and
hardly enters the channel by which clipping and rescaling concentrate a fixed
resource onto fewer edges. The permuted arm enters that channel instead.
A node relabelling is an isometry of the matrix and a bijection of the edges, so
it preserves the Frobenius norm of the arm's own gradient and the whole multiset
of its entries and changes only which edges receive them; the arm therefore
takes a step of that gradient's size and of its shape, uncorrelated with the
frequency clusters by construction. It reaches a norm growth of $\plasticityPermutedNormGrowthMin$ to
$\plasticityPermutedNormGrowthMax$, a range bracketing the gradient arm's, and a
second-half mean order of $\plasticityPermutedOrderMin$ to
$\plasticityPermutedOrderMax$, which lies inside the gradient arm's range. Its
descent fraction is
$\plasticityPermutedDescentMin$ to $\plasticityPermutedDescentMax$: a step of
a gradient's own magnitude delivered to the wrong edges raises the post-update
objective. What the two controls jointly support is therefore a comparison
between arms whose step sizes are set by the same rule and whose observed
deformation and coherence ranges overlap, not between arms equated by
construction.

The learning rate is selected by a sweep from $\plasticitySweepLowRate$ to
$\plasticitySweepHighRate$ on one seed:
the post-update objective's second-half mean falls from
$\plasticitySweepLowDissipation$ at the smallest rate to a minimum at
$\plasticitySweepBestRate$ and rises again to
$\plasticitySweepHighDissipation$ at the largest. Rates past the minimum lose
that reduction while still meeting the plateau criterion, so the criterion does
not by itself select a rate and no convergence in the rate is claimed.

Second-half mean order ranges from
$\plasticityOrderMin$ to $\plasticityOrderMax$, so coherence is maintained
throughout. All seeds meet the declared plateau criterion: the last-quarter mean
differs from the preceding quarter by at most $5\%$.
The environmental template is the covariance of unit-variance latent signals
shared within each frequency cluster, rescaled and hollowed to match the
coupling resource; it is a diagnostic template, not an input to plasticity.
Alignment is reported by two statistics unchanged under rescaling the kernel.
The ratio of mean within-cluster to mean between-cluster coupling starts at
$\plasticityRatioInitialMin$ to $\plasticityRatioInitialMax$ and ends at
$\plasticityRatioMin$ to $\plasticityRatioMax$, against
$\plasticityRandomRatioMin$ to $\plasticityRandomRatioMax$ for the random arm
and $\plasticityPermutedRatioMin$ to $\plasticityPermutedRatioMax$ for the
permuted one. The permuted range is wide and contains the no-preference
line. Concentrating a fixed resource onto few edges makes a ratio of block means
fluctuate, and with three clusters two thirds of the node pairs are
between-cluster, so a dominant edge lands between more often than within and the
typical value sits a little under one. The three permuted values nonetheless lie
above the gradient arm's three without overlap, at kernel deformations that
overlap almost exactly.
Against $2000$ node relabellings drawn per run, the final kernel is further from
the true template than from a fraction $\plasticityPercentileMin$ to
$\plasticityPercentileMax$ of them. The frozen arm's initial kernel already sits
at $\plasticityFrozenPercentileMin$ to $\plasticityFrozenPercentileMax$, so the
gradient arm is read against the frozen arm and not against the null median; the
permuted arm, at the gradient arm's deformation, reaches
$\plasticityPermutedPercentileMin$ to $\plasticityPermutedPercentileMax$.

A second partition of the same nodes settles what the loss of within-cluster
coupling is a loss against. The frequency partition is the structure the
environment carries; an interleaved partition of the same three group sizes,
built so that each group draws equally from all three clusters, carries no
frequency information, and a kernel with only frequency structure is neutral
under it up to a correction of order $1/N$. Neither partition enters the update.
The gradient arm's interleaved ratio runs from $\plasticityCrossedRatioMin$ to
$\plasticityCrossedRatioMax$ across the three seeds, on both sides of the
no-preference line, where its frequency ratio is $\plasticityRatioMin$ to
$\plasticityRatioMax$ on all three. The interleaved partition is one member of
a family, so the calibrated form of the same reading is to score the frequency
partition against $2000$ relabellings of itself, each keeping the group sizes
and losing the correspondence with frequency: at most a fraction
$\plasticityBlindPercentileMax$ of them give a lower ratio than the frequency
partition does, on every seed, against $\plasticityFrozenBlindPercentileMin$ to
$\plasticityFrozenBlindPercentileMax$ for the frozen arm's untouched kernel.
Concentrating a fixed resource onto few edges makes any such ratio fluctuate
widely, which is why the interleaved value alone is uninformative and the
percentile against the family is not.

Two readouts are reported and not relied on. The initial-minus-final Frobenius
distance to the template lies in
$[\plasticityReductionMin,\plasticityReductionMax]$, but the kernel norm grows
by a factor $\plasticityNormGrowthMin$ to $\plasticityNormGrowthMax$ over the
same interval, and a fixed total resource constrains the sum of the entries
rather than their norm; most of the distance growth is therefore concentration
of mass onto fewer edges rather than loss of alignment, and the distance to the
shuffled template grows with it. The off-diagonal correlation with the template
ends at $\plasticityCorrelationMin$ to $\plasticityCorrelationMax$, close to its
starting value: at the sparsity the update produces, edge-level variance
dominates a block-mean contrast that the within-over-between ratio resolves.

Minimizing squared drift under a conserved mean drift equalizes drift across
nodes, which rewards coupling between clusters of unlike frequency, whereas the
template rewards coupling within clusters of like frequency. The disagreement is
a property of this pairing of objective and template. The blind-partition
reading is what separates that from the weaker statement it would otherwise be
confused with: the update does not degrade whatever partition a reader scores
it against, but locates the one partition the environment carries and moves
against it. Which structure a different objective would move against, or whether
any objective of this family aligns with an environment, is not settled by one
pairing and is not claimed. The squared-drift
objective includes the drive and fluctuating relative drift; it is not a
thermodynamic entropy-production estimate, which would require
probability-current information.

These finite trajectories establish neither stationarity of the adaptive

reduction at the sampled instant together with a loss of alignment, and not a
system that holds the objective below its frozen counterpart while it runs.

\section{What a measurement of overlap compatibility would require}
\label{sec:supp-overlap-test}
\label{sec:compatibility-protocol}

The main article's compatibility requirement needs a neural observable distinct from phase order. This section specifies that measurement: decoded local descriptions, restrictions to shared domains, uncertainty under decoding noise, and validation on examples with known compatibility. An implementation of the specification on constructed data is reported below; no recording is analysed.

\subsection{Three components}

\emph{A restriction map.} Two regions with overlapping receptive territories may each support a decoder for content on the shared part. Compatibility is a relation between the resulting objects, so the decoder and its restriction map must be specified before any statistic is computed. The formalization uses finite spatial measures: on its discrete substrate two patch sections have the same restriction exactly when they assign the same mass to every shared site (\nolinkurl{restrict_eq_iff_densityOn_eqOn}). A content decoder using probability laws needs additional structure: equality of overlap marginals is necessary, but global extension and uniqueness must be tested or assumed separately rather than imported from the spatial-measure theorem.

\emph{A statistic with a null.} A distance between the two restricted distributions is not yet a measurement, because decoding error alone makes that distance positive. The statistic needs the distribution of the distance under compatibility --- what two decoders of the same underlying content produce given their own noise --- so that incompatibility is a departure from a stated null and not a nonzero number. Overlapping territories supply dependent samples, and the uncertainty has to be computed accordingly.

\emph{Validation where the answer is built rather than inferred.} The estimator must be exercised on data in which compatible and incompatible overlaps are constructed by design, so that its sensitivity is known before it is applied where the answer is not. The formalization supplies the configuration to target: local states at a single common phase whose densities disagree where the patches meet, which satisfies every other requirement of the gluing theorem and fails compatibility alone (\nolinkurl{overlap_agreement_fails}). An estimator that cannot separate that configuration from its compatible counterpart at realistic decoding quality is not yet a measurement of the property the framework needs.

\subsection{Three ways such a measurement fails silently}

A decoder that shrinks both regional posteriors toward a shared prior reports compatibility whatever the regions encode, having replaced two local descriptions with one global one. A decoder carrying independent per-region bias reports incompatibility whatever the regions encode. These are opposite failures of the same component, neither is visible in the compatibility statistic itself, and both are why the constructed-answer validation above is a requirement rather than a refinement.

The third is circularity. A compatibility statistic computed from phases measures phase agreement, which the counterexample shows can coexist with incompatible local descriptions. E78 connects coherence to a supplied cover's relaxation; the cover's own compatibility field supplies overlap agreement. A statistic for that agreement must use the local descriptions and their restrictions.

\subsection{What the implemented estimator separates}

The three components above are instantiated on constructed data: per-site distributions restricted to the shared sites, equality of the restricted tuples as the compatibility relation, and a dependence-aware null built from two decoders of the same content. Over \overlapTrials{} trials on an overlap of \overlapSites{} sites at decoding error \overlapDecodingError, the statistic separates constructed incompatibility from constructed compatibility at $\mathrm{AUC}=\overlapIncompatibleAuc$, on the $95\%$ interval $[\overlapIncompatibleAucLow,\overlapIncompatibleAucHigh]$, against $\overlapCompatibleAuc$ on $[\overlapCompatibleAucLow,\overlapCompatibleAucHigh]$ under the null, a departure of $\overlapIncompatibleZ$ null standard deviations. Both intervals resample trials rather than sites, for the reason the statistic's own spread does: sites within a trial share a latent content field, so a site-level resample would report a width no run supports. The compatible construction's interval contains one half, which is what the constructed answer requires of it. Separation survives to decoding error \overlapWorstUsableError{} and to a shared territory of \overlapSmallestTerritory{} sites.

The two silent failures are exhibited rather than assumed. A decoder shrinking both posteriors toward a shared prior drives the statistic to \overlapShrinkageStatistic{} on incompatible content, and independent per-region bias drives it to \overlapBiasStatistic{} on compatible content, against \overlapHonestStatistic{} for honest decoders under the null: each manufactures the answer the other suppresses, and the statistic alone reports both. Both are rejected by the accompanying per-patch diagnostics, which is what makes those diagnostics a requirement rather than a refinement.

Circularity is separated by measurement rather than by argument. On content built from the phase, the phase carries a share \overlapPhaseShareBuilt{} of the content entropy against \overlapPhaseShareFree{} when it is not, so the share distinguishes the two models; the phase-derived bound does not, reading \overlapPhaseBoundBuilt{} and \overlapPhaseBoundFree{} on the two. On the constructed counterexample --- phase-locked patches whose local descriptions disagree --- a phase-derived statistic reports compatibility where the content statistic reports incompatibility. No phase-derived quantity substitutes for the restricted local descriptions.

\subsection{Scope}

A validated observable would make agreement between the chosen local descriptions measurable. Establishing that coherence maintains it would require a dynamical test in addition, and selecting the content model and cover would remain an independent task. A full test of the consciousness identification would also measure the proposed self-encoding and an experience endpoint: compatibility and supercritical coupling alone leave the reflexive clause unevaluated. Absence of report must be distinguished from evidence against an episode. If compatibility cannot be distinguished from incompatibility at achievable decoding quality, the identification is outside the reach of that measurement.

\section{Exploratory EEG exercise of the concentration--coherence estimators}
\label{sec:supp-eeg}
\label{sec:empirical-methods}

This section reports an exploratory check of the parameter-free relation $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ on public propofol-sedation EEG (OpenNeuro ds005620 \cite{bajwa2025}). The saved summary and the generated macros record every numerical result below. This is an estimator exercise, not a test of awakening or of the electromagnetic-field commitment.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

For each recording block, 20 seconds of data and 3 seconds of padding on each side were downloaded from the public S3 bucket. Each channel was detrended, bandpass filtered with a fourth-order Butterworth filter using second-order sections (\texttt{sosfiltfilt}), and converted to instantaneous phase with the Hilbert transform. The padding is discarded after filtering in every montage. The primary analysis uses 61 adjacent bipolar pairs formed from the 62 scalp EEG channels; EOG and EMG channels are excluded.

\subsection{(a, r) estimation}

Each recording was divided into non-overlapping 100 ms bins (500 samples at 5 kHz). The estimand is the pooled spatiotemporal phase distribution over 62 sites and 100~ms, not an instantaneous 62-site spatial distribution. Pooling increases the sample count for log-density estimation; statistical adequacy still requires the bias and dependence calibration below. For each bin $t$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Order parameter $r$:} $r_b = \left|\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M} e^{i\theta_j(t_{bm})}\right|$, where $N=62$ channels and $M=500$ samples per bin. This is the resultant length of the pooled sample, not an average of instantaneous spatial resultant lengths.
\item \textbf{Concentration $a$:} The slope of the log-density of the same phase distribution regressed on $\cos\theta$, as specified in the main text. Phases are centred on their circular mean, binned into 40 bins on $[-\pi,\pi]$, and $\log$ of the binned density (with a $0.5$ pseudocount) is regressed on $\cos\theta$ by \emph{unweighted} ordinary least squares. Under the von Mises model $\log\rho(\theta) = a\cos\theta - \log(2\pi I_0(a))$, so the slope is $a$.
\end{itemize}

The estimator must not be a maximum-likelihood one. The von Mises family is a one-parameter
exponential family whose sufficient statistic is $\sum_j \cos\theta_j$ --- that is, $r$ itself --- so
any ML estimate of the concentration solves its score equation $I_1(\hat a)/I_0(\hat a) = r$ and
returns $\hat a = A^{-1}(r)$ identically. The pair $(\hat a, r)$ would then lie on
$r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ for any input whatsoever, including phases drawn from a distribution that is not
von Mises, and the collapse would be an algebraic identity rather than a test. The implemented regression is unweighted, so it uses the log-density shape rather than defining concentration by inversion of the first trigonometric moment. Other weighting rules are different estimators and are not assumed equivalent to maximum likelihood.

The pseudocount biases the estimator, so its null residual must be calibrated at the sample size and dependence structure of the intended measurement. The exploratory bins contain \eegPooledSamples{} pooled phase values. At \eegCalibrationSmallSamples{} independent samples, \eegCalibrationReplicas{} von Mises draws with true $a=\eegCalibrationConcentration$ return mean $\hat a=\eegCalibrationAMean$ and mean residual $\eegCalibrationResidualMean$ with standard deviation $\eegCalibrationResidualSd$. A large residual at that count can therefore be estimator bias rather than evidence against the density relation. Independence is itself optimistic for EEG. Section~\ref{sec:supp-density-design} evaluates the site count and dependence needed for discrimination; the pooled exploratory count does not by itself validate a spatial estimator.

The estimator also separates synthetic non-von-Mises alternatives from correctly sampled high-count von Mises data. This confirms that the choice of unweighted log-density regression avoids the maximum-likelihood tautology; it does not calibrate a low-count or dependent neural measurement.

\subsection{Dependence-aware spatial design}
\label{sec:supp-density-design}

The spatial design uses resultant length for $r$ and unweighted regression of log density on centered cosine for $a$. At \designProtocolSites{} sites, \designPublishedBins{} bins clear the declared bias floor at no scanned concentration or dependence. Reducing to \designUsableBins{} bins or fewer permits discrimination only from $a=\designUsableConcentration$ upward under independence. At the observed ceiling $a=\designObservedMax$, the scanned designs instead require \designSitesIndependent{} independent sites, rising to \designSitesHalfClustered{} at half clustering and \designSitesClustered{} when sites share a mean in groups of \designSitesPerCluster. These are results for the specified estimator, concentration grid, dependence model and bias-floor criterion. A recording design must calibrate those choices in its own observation regime; its minimum site count cannot be inferred from the number of fitted parameters.

\subsection{Bootstrap confidence intervals}

\section{The cortical field identification}
\label{sec:supp-field}

The framework identifies its posited continuum coupling kernel with the endogenous electromagnetic field of mammalian cortex. This is a claim about biology, not a consequence of any theorem above it.

\subsection{Supporting evidence}

Neurons do not only signal through synapses: transmembrane currents generate extracellular fields that shift membrane potentials of neighbouring cells, a mechanism known as ephaptic coupling. Endogenous fields of $\fermiFieldMin$--$\fermiFieldMax$ mV/mm entrain spike timing \cite{frohlich2010, anastassiou2015}, and stable electric fields carry information that survives representational drift in the underlying spiking population \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric}. The field is spatially continuous and superposes contributions from large populations --- a medium in which spatially separated activity is combined without further mechanism. This is the integrative role the CEMI field theory assigns it \cite{mcfadden2002, mcfadden2020, pockett2012}, physically supported by astrocytic syncytia joined by gap junctions \cite{verkhratsky2018}.

These observations support a modulatory interaction, but do not determine its integrated strength relative to phase diffusion. Continuity does not itself establish compatible local states or conscious unity. The model requires independent calibration of the coupling kernel, its spatial aggregation and its rate scale.

\subsection{The field-strength objection}

The longest-standing objection is that the fields are too weak to matter, pressed from within the field-theoretic camp by Pockett herself \cite{pockett2012, pockett2002}. V\"or\"oslakos and colleagues found that applied current is shunted through scalp, delivering fields at or below $1$ V/m in cortex \cite{voroslakos2018}. That is a bound on \emph{applied} rather than endogenous fields, but it constrains how much causal work a field of that magnitude can do, and is the reason we do not claim the field drives spiking.

\subsection{Why the rate calibration is not derivable}
\label{sec:gamma-identifiability}

The conversion $K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N \mathcal{T} E f$ carries its whole physical content in $\gamma$, and no rearrangement of the remaining factors supplies it. The frustration control above records the dimensional half of this: $s$ is a time shift per unit field, so $sE$ is a time, and multiplying by an oscillation frequency leaves a pure number --- the fractional phase advance a cell receives per cycle from the population field. Multiplying by a population size leaves it a pure number. A coupling in the sense of the main text's stochastic model is an inverse time, and $\gamma$ is the entire gap between the two.

The substantive half is that $\gamma$ is a hypothesis about persistence, not a unit conversion to be fixed by convention. It states how a per-cycle phase advance accumulates into a drift rate, which depends on how long a field-driven timing shift survives. Three candidates are available and they are not interchangeable: $\gamma=f$ takes each cycle's advance to be retained in full and independently; $\gamma=1/\tau$ for a phase relaxation time takes it to decay as the medium relaxes; $\gamma=D$ takes it to accumulate at the rate phase itself diffuses. These need not agree, in cortex or anywhere else, and the field measurement chooses between them only if the persistence of induced timing shifts is measured alongside it.

The third candidate is a physical hypothesis, not an algebraic error. Under $\gamma=D$ the threshold test $K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D>2$ reduces to $NsEf>2$; that cancellation is legitimate if an independent experiment establishes that persistence rate. It is unsafe to choose $\gamma=D$ merely because it cancels the unmeasured diffusion, or to recover any $\gamma$ by requiring the model to cross its own threshold or fit the Bessel relation. Identification requires coupling, diffusion and the persistence rule to be estimated in one cortical regime by routes independent of the field arithmetic, which is item~1 of the main text's protocol.

\subsection{Calibrating the scalar spatial reduction}
\label{sec:supp-reduction}

A spatially decaying kernel enters the scalar model through its row sum at zero diagonal, and a finite sheet measures what that reduction costs: over decay lengths from \reductionDecayMin{} to \reductionDecayMax~mm, spanning \reductionNeighbourMin{} to \reductionNeighbourMax{} effective neighbours, the measured threshold lies at \reductionRatioMin--\reductionRatioMax{} times $2D$, within \reductionRuleSpread{} of the \reductionMeanFieldRatio{} a uniform control returns on the same sheet and fit window. Both sit below $2D$ there --- the fit window alone brackets them down to \reductionWindowLow{} --- so the agreement is between the reduction and its control rather than a confirmation of the threshold's value. Within this control, frequency heterogeneity shifts the operational threshold: a quenched spread of \reductionFrequencySd~rad\,s$^{-1}$ moves it to \reductionHeterogeneousRatio{} times $2D$, while the same sheet reproduces the stationary branch to \reductionBranchResidual{} over \reductionBranchPoints{} supercritical couplings.

\subsection{What the order parameter reports for a winding state}
\label{sec:supp-wave}

The coherence observable is the global resultant $r=|N^{-1}\sum_i e^{i\theta_i}|$, invariant under a global phase shift and otherwise indifferent to where a phase sits on the sheet. A field winding once across a periodic sheet has $r=0$ while every patch of it stays locked, so the quantity in which the threshold $K_c=2D$ is stated does not by itself separate a winding field from incoherence. What it misses is the winding and not any motion: at identical frequencies with a symmetric kernel such a field has zero drift and stands still, and a travelling wave is the same winding carried by a drift this case does not supply.

Such a field is not a perturbation of this model. On a torus the twisted state $\theta(x)=2\pi qx/L$ is stationary for any isotropic symmetric kernel at identical frequencies: $K(x-y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)$ is odd in the separation against a kernel even in it, so the drift cancels pair by pair and vanishes exactly. It satisfies both positivity and the identical-frequency hypothesis, and \nolinkurl{velocity_tendsto_zero}, which assumes only the latter, holds of it trivially --- its velocity is zero throughout. What a twist violates is the quarter-turn initial spread $|\theta_i-\theta_j|\leq\pi/2$ that \nolinkurl{kuramoto_tendsto_global_minimum} additionally requires before placing the limit at the potential's global minimum, where $r=1$. A twist spans the whole circle and lies outside that basin hypothesis. The relation is therefore the one positivity already illustrates: \emph{that} the motion stops is settled without the hypothesis, and \emph{where} it stops is settled only inside a declared basin.

All four statements are theorems, and of a site set general enough to be the sheet rather than only the ring: any finite abelian group under \texttt{circulantSystem}, the coupling $A_{ij}=f(j-i)$ for any $f$ with $f(-d)=f(d)$. \nolinkurl{char_is_kuramoto_trajectory} proves a winding stationary there, the drift cancelling by pairing each separation with its negative, and \nolinkurl{winding_is_kuramoto_trajectory} specializes it to $\theta_k=2\pi qk/n$ on $\mathbb{Z}/n$. \nolinkurl{order_parameter_r_sq_char} puts the global resultant at exactly zero for any winding that is nontrivial on some separation, which on the ring is the condition $n\nmid q$ that \nolinkurl{order_parameter_r_sq_winding} states. \nolinkurl{torus_coupling_does_not_determine_order} assembles the pair on $\mathbb{Z}/n\times\mathbb{Z}/n$, which is the geometry integrated below. \nolinkurl{norm_order_parameter_le_mean_patch_order} proves the patch estimator used below never reports less than the global one, for any cover whose patches share a size and whose sites share a multiplicity --- the sliding box is the case where the two counts agree --- and \nolinkurl{cos_le_mean_patch_order_winding} puts nearest-neighbour patch order at or above $\cos(2\pi q/n)$, which approaches one as the same winding is spread over more sites. \nolinkurl{coupling_does_not_determine_order} collects them into one system with two stationary states, at $r^2=1$ and $r^2=0$.

Which windings a coupling sustains is a separate question from which ones it admits, and it has a criterion of the same shape. Write $\theta_i=\bar\theta_i+su_i$ and differentiate the field at $s=0$: the constant term is the drift that cancels above, and what is left is circulant again with the \emph{twisted} kernel $g_d=f_d\cos\Delta\theta_d$, each separation reweighted by the cosine of the phase the winding advances across it (\nolinkurl{hasDerivAt_charJacobian}). That kernel is even, $f$ being even and $\cos\circ\Delta\theta$ even by \nolinkurl{WindingData.cos_psi_neg}, so every cancellation of this section applies to it unchanged: the characters diagonalize it, and the rate of the mode $\operatorname{Re}\chi'$ is $\lambda_{\chi'}(g)=\sum_d g_d(\operatorname{Re}\chi'(d)-1)$, the same eigenvalue functional evaluated at the twisted kernel (\nolinkurl{charJacobian_chi_re}). The two directions follow termwise. If $f_d\geq0$ and the winding advances by at most a quarter turn across every separation carrying coupling, then $g_d\geq0$ and no rate is positive, since $\operatorname{Re}\chi'(d)\leq1$ (\nolinkurl{charJacobian_stable_of_quarter_turn}). If some character has a positive rate, the field $e^{\lambda t}\operatorname{Re}\chi'$ solves the linearized equation exactly and its value at the identity site is $e^{\lambda t}$, which leaves every bound (\nolinkurl{charJacobian_growing_mode}). This is the shape \nolinkurl{incoherent_instability_iff} already carries for the uniform state, at a winding instead.

That criterion names the mechanism the sweep below varies. A kernel reaching only short separations sees phase advances well inside a quarter turn and $g_d>0$ throughout; lengthening the decay length brings in separations across which the winding has advanced past $\pi/2$, there $g_d<0$, and a mode crosses zero. What the criterion does not supply is a trajectory. It is a statement about rates at the linearization, and upgrading it to asymptotic stability needs a \L{}ojasiewicz estimate around a critical point that is not a minimum, where the estimate available here --- \nolinkurl{lojasiewicz_estimate} through \nolinkurl{phase_tendsto} --- is built around the global minimum. The decay-length boundary reported below is therefore measured and not computed from this criterion, and the states that persist occupy a strictly narrower range than the criterion admits.

A winding is also not where the potential is lowest, and that is a refutation rather than a gap. \nolinkurl{potential_min_iff_phase_locked} makes a global minimizer of the dynamic potential phase-locked, \nolinkurl{phase_locked_implies_r_sq_eq_one} puts its resultant at one, and \nolinkurl{order_parameter_r_sq_char} puts a winding's at zero; so a nontrivial winding is not a global minimizer (\nolinkurl{char_not_potential_min}). The quarter-turn hypothesis of \nolinkurl{kuramoto_tendsto_global_minimum} does not merely decline to cover this state; it must exclude it, because the conclusion is false of it. The local statement uses the twisted kernel again: the second variation of the potential along $\bar\theta+su$ is that kernel's Dirichlet form, which is minus the linearization's quadratic form (\nolinkurl{charLineSecond_zero}), so a mode of positive rate is a direction of strictly negative curvature and the potential falls along it at every small enough displacement (\nolinkurl{charLinePotential_lt_of_charLambda_pos}). A winding carrying a growing mode is therefore not a local minimum of the potential on the state space either (\nolinkurl{char_not_local_min_of_charLambda_pos}), the line being a continuous curve through it.

A winding is where the patchwise reading of the content bound,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:chord-bound-patch}, separates furthest from the global one. The
global form degrades to $\sqrt2\,N$ here (\nolinkurl{chord_bound_char_global}), which constrains nothing at all: every chord is at most $2$ (\nolinkurl{chord_le_two}), so the estimate is above the trivial bound for every population of two sites or more. Across a nearest-neighbour patch the same inequality gives $2\sqrt2\,|\sin\Delta\theta_g|$, with $\Delta\theta_g$ the phase the winding advances across the patch (\nolinkurl{chord_le_of_char_patch}); on the ring $\Delta\theta_g=2\pi q/n$, so the bound tends to zero as the same winding number is spread over more sites. A population whose resultant is exactly zero therefore still bounds content disagreement inside each patch, by a quantity that shrinks with the resolution of the array. This is the compatibility reading of a low global resultant at sustained patch order.

The same sheet, kernel and integrator as the preceding control measure how far that basin reaches, at identical frequencies so the zero-detuning hypothesis holds. Over \waveSampleCount{} decay lengths the twist survives at \waveRetainedCount{} of them; between the longest length retaining it, \waveRetainedMax~mm, and the shortest losing it, \waveLostMin~mm, the interpolated boundary is \waveBoundary~mm, or \waveBoundaryCells{} lattice spacings. That boundary falls inside the 0.1--0.3~mm band which, for the uniform initial condition of the preceding control, is interior to a plateau. The two are consistent: the plateau records where a uniform state stays ordered, the boundary where a winding one survives, and a sweep from one initial condition constrains neither claim about the other.

What the observable reports there is the point. At 0.1~mm the sheet reaches $r=\waveBandControlOrder$ from a uniform start and $r=\waveBandOrder$ from a twisted one --- both stationary, at the same coupling, diffusion and kernel. Patch-local order in the twisted state is \waveBandLocalOrder{} and winding-defect density is at most \waveDefectMaximum{} at every sampled length, so the low resultant records a wave rather than fragmentation. At fixed $K$ the observable thus takes two values spanning nearly its whole range, and $K$ alone does not determine $r$. Each length runs twice, once twisted and once uniform; the uniform control's gap between patch-local and global order is at most \waveControlGapMax{} wherever the twist is lost, so the largest gap the twisted runs show, \waveGapMax, is not an artefact of the patch estimator.

Repeating the sweep under the quenched spread of \waveSpread~rad\,s$^{-1}$ the preceding control carries leaves the twist standing at \waveSpreadRetainedCount{} of the \waveSampleCount{} lengths, with the boundary at \waveSpreadBoundary~mm. Disorder therefore shortens the range that sustains a wave without closing it, and both boundaries stay inside the empirical band. At 0.1~mm the disordered sheet reaches $r=\waveSpreadBandControlOrder$ from a uniform start against $r=\waveSpreadBandOrder$ from a twisted one, at patch-local order \waveSpreadBandLocalOrder{} and winding-defect density at most $\waveSpreadDefectMaximum$ over the lengths that retain the twist: the spread nucleates defects without dissolving the wave. The uniform branch of this sweep also reproduces the preceding control's steady order at the three empirical lengths from a different initial condition, which is a check on the two implementations rather than a further result. Across \waveSeedCount{} frozen frequency realizations at the \waveSeedTotal/\waveSeedCount{} lengths that retain it, retention holds in \waveSeedRetained{} of \waveSeedTotal{} runs, at global order between \waveSeedOrderMin{} and \waveSeedOrderMax, so the outcome belongs to the model rather than to one sample of the disorder.

Four limits. Below the coupled regime the comparison carries no information: at \waveSpreadShortDecay~mm the uniform control itself reaches only \waveSpreadShortControl, so neither initial condition is ordered and the difference between them is not a statement about waves. A twist imposed as an initial condition measures the stability of a basin, not the probability that dynamics enters one. The sweep fixes one winding number, so nothing here describes the spectrum of wave states a cortical sheet might occupy. And nothing identifies a cortical decay length or establishes a transition. The content bound above attaches to a patch of a winding and not to the winding number: which sector a field occupies is connected to no content by any theorem here.

\subsection{An amplitude field, and what it reaches}
\label{sec:supp-amplitude}

Every oscillator of the phase model sits on the unit circle, so the state carries a phase and no amplitude, and a field with an amplitude node---$u=\cos(kx)\cos(\omega t)$---has no representation in the dynamics integrated above. Amplitude enters elsewhere in this work only at the measurement layer, where the loop calculation of \nolinkurl{Phase5_PhaseLifts.lean} reads winding off locally unwrapped phase maps. The real Stuart--Landau field supplies the missing degree of freedom on the same coupling:
\begin{equation}
\dot z_i=(\Gamma-|z_i|^2)z_i+\sum_j A_{ij}(z_j-z_i),
\label{eq:amplitude-field}
\end{equation}
with $z_i\in\mathbb{C}$. This is the gradient flow of $F(z)=\sum_i(|z_i|^4/4-\Gamma|z_i|^2/2)+\tfrac14\sum_{i}\sum_{j}A_{ij}|z_i-z_j|^2$ against the real inner product on $\mathbb{C}^V\cong\mathbb{R}^{2V}$, which is what makes it an extension of the development rather than a replacement: every convergence result above rests on gradient structure. Complex coefficients on the cubic term or on the coupling---the genuine complex Ginzburg--Landau equation---destroy that structure, so the real case is the one that inherits the architecture. \nolinkurl{Phase4_KuramotoDynamics.lean}~\S7 carries the results, on the ring and the circulant kernel of the preceding section.

Four statements follow. \nolinkurl{deriv_phase_of_amplitude_trajectory} proves that at a site of positive amplitude the phase of a solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:amplitude-field} obeys $\dot\theta_i=\sum_j A_{ij}(r_j/r_i)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$ exactly, with no slow-amplitude assumption: the identity is the imaginary part of $\dot z_i\overline{z_i}$ divided by $r_i^2$. \nolinkurl{amplitude_phase_is_kuramoto} specializes it to equal amplitudes, where the ratios are one and the phase is a Kuramoto trajectory of the same coupling. The phase model is therefore this model's phase equation with the amplitude ratios set to one, and the two layers are one object.

\nolinkurl{charState_is_amplitude_trajectory_iff} proves that $z_g=a\chi(g)$ is stationary exactly when $a^2=\Gamma+\lambda_\chi$, where $\lambda_\chi=\sum_d f_d(\operatorname{Re}\chi(d)-1)$ is real by the evenness of the kernel. The site set is any finite abelian group and $\chi$ any unit character of it carrying a real phase lift, which is the structure every cancellation in this development actually uses: the ring $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and the periodic sheet $\mathbb{Z}/n\times\mathbb{Z}/n$ are both instances, the second built from the first by \texttt{WindingData.prod}. That second one is the geometry the sweep integrates --- \texttt{travelling\_wave.py} imposes $\theta_{rc}=2\pi qc/\text{side}$ on a periodic \texttt{(side,\,side)} array under a toroidal kernel, which is this state with one of its two winding numbers set to zero --- so the theorems and the simulation now stand on the same object. On the ring \nolinkurl{windingState_is_amplitude_trajectory_iff} is the specialization, with $\lambda_q=\sum_d f_d(\cos(2\pi qd/n)-1)$. The consequence is a band: \nolinkurl{exists_windingState_iff} proves a $q$-fold winding of positive amplitude exists if and only if $\Gamma+\lambda_q>0$. The phase model admits every winding number at every coupling---\nolinkurl{winding_is_kuramoto_trajectory} constrains neither $q$ nor $f$---so outside the band the two layers disagree about which states exist, which \nolinkurl{phase_model_admits_winding_outside_band} states as one theorem.

Read against the sweep, that disagreement is about the state space and not about what a recording would show. Evenness is the theorem's only hypothesis on $f$, but the kernels this work uses are nonnegative, and there $\lambda_q\leq0$ with $|\lambda_q|$ growing as coupling mass moves to longer separations: a broad enough kernel takes $\Gamma+\lambda_q$ below zero and the winding ceases to exist. The preceding sweep reaches the same boundary from the other side, the twist it imposes on the phase model remaining a trajectory at every decay length and ceasing to survive integration past one. Existence and stability fail in the same direction, so the added degree of freedom sharpens the account of the regime rather than predicting a new observation in it. Two things it does not supply. The band is one of existence: no stability result is proved here, and for fields of this class the windings near its edge are unstable in the standard way, so the states that persist occupy a strictly narrower range than the inequality allows. And $\Gamma$ is the distance from the bifurcation, estimated nowhere in this work, so the band is structural---it predicts no numeral and discriminates no experiment.

Inside the band at $q=n/2$ the phasor is $\xi=-1$, so the state is real and alternates in sign along the ring. Eq.~\eqref{eq:amplitude-field} carries no frequency term, being written in a frame rotating at the common carrier $\Omega$ that the normal form removes; restoring it gives the lab-frame reading $u_k(t)=\operatorname{Re}(z_ke^{i\Omega t})$, which is $a(-1)^k\cos(\Omega t)$: one spatial profile times one common time factor, every site in antiphase with its neighbours. \nolinkurl{standing_wave_of_amplitude_band} assembles this, and it is $\cos(kx)\cos(\omega t)$ at $k=\pi$ in the framework's own integrated dynamics. The phase model has no such object, every site there having unit modulus.

These are windings and not spirals, and on the sheet that is a theorem rather than a limitation of the geometry. The defect \cite{townsend2015} and \cite{xu2023} measure is a point at which the amplitude vanishes and around which the phase advances by a whole turn; \texttt{spatial\_kernel.defect\_winding} reads it as the wrapped phase circulation around a periodic $2\times2$ plaquette. \nolinkurl{plaquetteCirculation_eq_zero_of_winding} proves that circulation is exactly zero on every plaquette of every state above, for any $2\pi$-periodic odd angle convention: the phase advance across a separation does not depend on where the separation is taken, so the four sides of a plaquette cancel in pairs. \nolinkurl{norm_charState} is the reason, putting the modulus at $|a|$ at every site. The extra dimension does not change this. What a torus adds is winding around its two non-contractible loops; a spiral is winding around a contractible one, and that forces an amplitude zero which no state of constant modulus has.

The negative result is sharper still and holds of every field, exact or not. \nolinkurl{sum_plaquetteCirculation_eq_zero} proves that on a torus the circulations of all plaquettes sum to zero, each edge being traversed once in each direction, so that \nolinkurl{no_isolated_defect} gives the statement in the form that matters: if every plaquette but one carries no defect then neither does that one. A single spiral is not a state of this geometry at all, whatever dynamics produced it --- defects arrive in cancelling pairs. This is a constraint on what the layer could ever host, not a gap waiting on more work, and it is why the electrode loop of \nolinkurl{Phase5_PhaseLifts.lean} and not the interior it encircles remains what this development speaks about. The sweep's near-zero measured defect density is consistent with the first theorem and is not evidence for anything beyond it: the runs carry noise, diffusion and a quenched frequency spread, and are therefore not exact states.

Three further limits bound the layer, of which the article states the two a reader of the neuroscience meets first. Relaxational dynamics has no propagation speed: real coefficients give no nonlinear dispersion and the drift is exactly zero, so nothing here travels, while a cortical wave measurement always reports a speed. Supplying one needs heterogeneous frequencies or complex coefficients, and both cost the gradient structure. Second, $|z|$ is a normal-form radius and a measured envelope is not; identifying them is a physical assumption of the same kind as the Landauer identification held as a named hypothesis in \nolinkurl{Phase3_LandauerBridge.lean}, and it is made nowhere in this work. Third, the normal form is valid near a supercritical Hopf bifurcation, where a resting state has just lost stability; much cortical wave phenomenology is excitable-medium behaviour instead, and the two produce similar-looking maps from different mechanisms. The row of Table~\ref{tab:full} recording that no theorem connects winding sectors to content stands unchanged throughout: making a defect computable from a dynamical state rather than postulated from data is not making it mean anything.

Two things do not come for free and are not claimed. A cosine profile is not a stationary state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:amplitude-field}: the cubic term does not preserve a single Fourier mode, so only the circularly polarized modes are exact and the only real one among them is the alternating mode above. A standing wave with an interior amplitude node is a fixed point of a nonlinear system with no closed form, reached by bifurcation from $\Gamma=-\lambda_q$.

\subsection{Falsification conditions}

The physical identification would be challenged if independently calibrated field-mediated coupling were insufficient for the proposed regime, or if a selective perturbation of that interaction left the proposed neural and behavioural endpoints unchanged. Such a perturbation requires controls for direct effects on transmembrane currents and other coupling pathways; selectivity is not assumed experimentally available. Recovery tests must distinguish the coupling-gated account from other slow mechanisms under the same observation model. Coherence alone is not equated with report or experience, so dissociations must be evaluated against independently specified endpoints and the additional compatibility and reflexivity hypotheses.

These conditions bear on a coupling field and not specifically on an electromagnetic one. Of the six protocol requirements of the main article, the second---the calibrated relation between extracellular geometry and effective coupling---is the only one that discriminates, because it runs through conductivity and volume fraction and is where a field equation enters this work. The first and the third through sixth bear on the field hypothesis at large, and extracellular ion diffusion, gap junctions modelled as a density, astrocytic calcium or a mechanical medium satisfy or fail them identically: each fixes coupling relative to diffusion, the phase measurement, an independent recovery endpoint, a declared onset criterion and a matched model comparison, and none of them mentions a field equation. A null result on the second requirement therefore constrains the electromagnetic identification; a null result on the others constrains the coupling-gated account whatever carries the coupling.

\section{Coupling support and continuum observables}

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-support}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Resource-matched coupling reallocation):} If a fixed wiring support excludes a pair whose phase correlation exceeds the maximum over its wired pairs, a coupling using that pair can achieve greater total correlation at the same resource budget. \textit{Coupling reallocation (\texttt{Phase7}):} The lemma \nolinkurl{exists_better_coupling_allocation}, wrapped as the theorem \nolinkurl{continuous_beats_rigid_topology}, proves the reallocation step: given a coupling matrix satisfying \nolinkurl{is_strictly_suboptimal} (positive weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists), there is another valid coupling with identical total resources and strictly greater total phase correlation.

\subsection{Finite regions preserve the discrete observable}
\label{sec:supp-finite-region}

\nolinkurl{Phase7_FiniteRegion.lean} embeds a finite matrix $A$
in a \texttt{KernelMesh} whose cells $C_i$ have positive masses
$m_i=\mu(C_i)$ under a finite measure. Put
\[
 K(x,y)=\frac{A_{ij}}{m_i m_j},\qquad
 \theta(x)=\theta_i\quad(x\in C_i,\ y\in C_j).
\]
Integration of a function of cell indices is its mass-weighted sum
(\nolinkurl{integral_locate_pair}), hence
\begin{align}
 \iint K(x,y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)&=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij},\\
 \iint K(x,y)\cos(\theta(y)-\theta(x))\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x)
   &=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i).
 \label{eq:finite-region-preservation}
\end{align}
These are \nolinkurl{kernelIntegral_cellKernel} and
\nolinkurl{fieldCorrelation_cellKernel}. Symmetry and
nonnegativity are preserved, and a zero matrix diagonal makes each
within-cell block zero. On an atomless substrate, a reconstruction with
nonzero correlation cannot be supported on finitely many points
(\nolinkurl{cellKernel_not_sitedOn}).

\nolinkurl{Examples/FiniteRegion.lean}, \S26, uses Lebesgue measure
on $[0,4)$, cells of masses one and three, and a matrix with total weight
twelve. Both the embedded weight and correlation equal twelve exactly.
Thus finite point support and finitely many positive-mass regions have
different consequences for the same integral.

The same module supplies a control for the wiring gap:
\nolinkurl{no_forced_gap_of_best_wired}
constructs an optimal allocation when the support contains both directions of
a distinct pair whose correlation bounds every pair, including diagonal
pairs. This hypothesis forces the chosen pair's correlation to be one.
The three-site witness wires the perfectly correlated pair and attains the
resource bound, so no disadvantage is forced by fixed wiring itself.

The matched resource in the cell embedding is total coupling weight; pricing
it as energy requires hardware data. The embedding is piecewise constant,
with no continuity, dynamical approximation or refinement limit established
here. Point-supported vanishing is about measure-zero support, and the strict
wiring gap requires the specified missing pair. Neither result excludes
digital hardware from approximating field dynamics or supporting consciousness.

\section{Symmetry breaking and the emergence of boundaries}
\label{sec:supp-boundaries}
A boundary is one of the physical structures the broader composition assumes. Under the stated vacuum and connectedness hypotheses, a field reaching two vacuum components must leave the vacuum somewhere. Relating that locus to a finite information-processing register is a separate physical premise; the result supplies neither a general condition for agency nor a derivation of the cortical coupling model.

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold representing spacetime, and let $\phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{V}$ be a physical field mapping to a vacuum manifold $\mathcal{V}$ (the space of energy minima). Let $G$ be a continuous symmetry group acting on $\mathcal{V}$.

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-boundary}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Domain-wall obstruction):} A continuous field on a connected substrate whose image reaches two different connected components of the vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere. This is the $\pi_0$ case of the topological-defect argument. A separate extension obstruction states that non-null-homotopic boundary data admit no continuous extension over the specified contractible interior.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase1_Primitives.lean}):} We formalize this by defining the set of degenerate energy minima \texttt{DynamicalVacuum} and proving \nolinkurl{boundary_defect_forces_interior_vacuum_break}: if a boundary field configuration $\phi_{\partial}$ is not null-homotopic, then it admits no continuous extension over a contractible interior. This is an obstruction to extending the specified boundary data. Note the direction: this result establishes that a defect \emph{obstructs extension}, not that a defect must exist. The companion lemma \nolinkurl{contractible_interior_forces_trivial_boundary}  proves the contrapositive.

A direction that produces a defect is proved, at the level of $\pi_0$. \nolinkurl{exists_notMem_of_no_common_preconnected} states it in the form a witness can discharge --- if no preconnected subset of the vacuum manifold $M$ contains both $\phi(x_1)$ and $\phi(x_2)$, and the substrate is connected, then $\phi$ leaves $M$ somewhere --- and \nolinkurl{exists_notMem_of_connectedComponentIn} restates it with $\phi(x_2) \notin$ the connected component of $M$ containing $\phi(x_1)$. The proof uses the connectedness of continuous images and \nolinkurl{IsPreconnected.subset_connectedComponentIn}, and nothing else; no hypothesis mentions energy, a potential or a symmetry. This is the domain-wall case of Kibble's classification and we take only that case: $\pi_1 \ne 0$ forcing vortices and $\pi_2 \ne 0$ forcing monopoles are recorded as available and not taken, since the manuscript's argument uses domain walls alone.

Separately, \nolinkurl{spontaneous_symmetry_breaking} shows that a global energy minimizer lies in the vacuum manifold almost everywhere. The proof introduces no additional axioms and uses \nolinkurl{pointwise_vacuum_of_global_min}.

\texttt{Examples}~\S11 witnesses that result on the double well $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ over a two-point spacetime carrying a Dirac probability measure. Two features of the witness are deliberate. Its minimization hypothesis is \emph{proved} rather than assumed --- everywhere else in the development the existence of a global minimizer is a hypothesis, since no compactness or direct-method argument is formalized. And the almost-everywhere in the conclusion is shown necessary: the minimizing field sits at the vacuum on the support and at $0$ off it, so the pointwise version of the statement is false of the very field the theorem applies to. The same section instantiates the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$ and discharges the energy invariance, giving the first instance of \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}; the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ is proved degenerate and the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so a minimizer and its image have equal energy and differ.

\texttt{Examples}~\S11.1 then supplies the witness for the $\pi_0$ theorem on the same well. The vacuum manifold is \emph{computed} there, \nolinkurl{wellVacuum_eq} proving $\texttt{DynamicalVacuum}\ V = \{-1,1\}$ exactly rather than merely containing both minima --- which matters, because a larger vacuum set might have been connected --- and \nolinkurl{wellVacuum_separated} proves the disconnection in the form the theorem consumes, by the observation that a preconnected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ spanning $-1$ and $1$ contains $0$, which is not a minimum. \nolinkurl{wellV_domain_wall} is the resulting statement about \emph{every} continuous field on \emph{any} connected substrate reaching both minima. Non-emptiness of that class is checked separately rather than assumed: the clipped identity on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies the hypotheses, and \nolinkurl{kink_zero_notMem} locates its forced wall at the origin. These results concern vacuum membership and domain walls; they construct no conserved quantity.

\section{The thermodynamic constraint: Landauer's principle}
\label{sec:supp-erasure-section}
For a finite effective register, the number of distinguishable histories that can be retained is bounded. This does not imply that every update erases. The capacity theorem states that a distribution on a finite state space $X$ carries at most $\log|X|$ nats, with equality only at the uniform law (\nolinkurl{shannon_entropy_eq_log_card_iff}), and a perturbation stream of length $k$ over an alphabet $P$ with $|P|^k > |X|$ therefore cannot be recorded injectively (\nolinkurl{absorb_not_injective}), whatever the update map is and even when every individual step of it is reversible.

Let the global phase space $\Omega = \Omega_{sys} \times \Omega_{bath}$ undergo globally reversible Hamiltonian dynamics, modeled as an injective mapping $f: \Omega \to \Omega$.

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-erasure}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Thermodynamic Erasure):} Under the specified finite-register and reversible system--bath assumptions, the heat identity gives a lower bound proportional to the entropy erased by the register. Erasing one unbiased bit gives $\Delta Q\geq k_BT\ln2$. Non-injectivity alone does not specify a universal one-bit entropy loss; the bound uses the erased entropy of the particular update.

\textit{The same heat, in three vocabularies (\nolinkurl{Phase3_LandauerBridge.lean}).} The erasure bound charges a register for destroying information, while the predictive bound charges a structure for keeping information it cannot use. \nolinkurl{PredictiveDissipation.ofLandauer} builds the predictive structure of a given register: its thermal scale is that register's temperature, its dissipated work is that register's Landauer heat, and Still's bound is derived from \nolinkurl{landauer_bound} rather than assumed a second time. The single hypothesis is the identification $\left(I_{\mathrm{mem}} - I_{\mathrm{pred}}\right) \le S(\mathrm{id}) - S(t)$ --- the memory wasted is no larger than the entropy erased --- and it is a restriction, not bookkeeping: on an injective update the right-hand side is zero and the inequality forces the waste to vanish (\nolinkurl{nonpredictive_eq_zero_of_injective}), which is the Norton--Shenker distinction in informational form. The measure-theoretic statement of Landauer (\nolinkurl{Phase3_MeasureThermodynamics.lean}) is specialized in the same file rather than left standing beside the combinatorial one: at counting measure on a finite register, \nolinkurl{continuous_entropy} is \nolinkurl{boltzmann_entropy}, volume contraction is non-injectivity, and \nolinkurl{landauer_heat_bound} is $T$ times the entropy erased (\nolinkurl{landauer_heat_bound_count_eq}). The witness is the one-bit eraser, where a register destroying $\log 2$ pays for a memory wasting $\log 2$, with equality (\texttt{Examples}~\S18.5).

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\nolinkurl{Phase3_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}):} We explicitly decouple logical erasure from physical erasure. Lean verifies that any contraction in the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective}(t_{sys})$) under globally reversible dynamics mathematically necessitates heat dissipation. The combinatorial core, \nolinkurl{landauer_from_reversibility} and \nolinkurl{second_law}, is fully derived. Finiteness of the phase space is what converts the physically checkable hypothesis into the one the bound needs: on a finite state space a map that fails to reach every state is not injective (\nolinkurl{is_erasure_of_not_surjective}), so an unreachable state suffices for $\Delta Q > 0$ (\nolinkurl{finite_phase_space_dissipates}). The hypothesis is fenced: \texttt{Nat.succ} misses $0$ and is injective, so the implication is false on an infinite phase space, and \texttt{Examples}~\S19 records this beside the witnesses. The Norton--Shenker distinction survives the identification --- retaining the incoming perturbation is injective and free (\nolinkurl{keepRecord_injective}), refreshing the register that held it is not (\nolinkurl{absorbStep_is_erasure}). Converting it into a statement about heat requires the postulate $\Delta Q = T \Delta S_{bath}$, which is carried as the class field \nolinkurl{StatisticalMechanics.heat_eq}; \texttt{Examples}~\S1 exhibits a one-bit erasure system with an explicit bath that discharges it, so the postulate is demonstrably satisfiable. A register that satisfies the stated erasure and thermal hypotheses dissipates; identifying it with a biological boundary is an additional modelling step.

\section{Predictive memory and feedback}
\label{sec:supp-feedback}
A bound relating environmental and internal distributions must be a physical assumption about specified processes, not a consequence of Gibbs' inequality alone. The class field \nolinkurl{StructuralResonance.kl_bound} states $D_{KL}(P\|Q)\leq\Delta t\,\sigma$ for an instance. Even under this assumption, descent of $\sigma$ does not imply $D_{KL}\to0$ unless the upper bound vanishes. A positive limiting upper bound permits a positive divergence. No convergence of internal geometry to environmental structure follows.

\refstepcounter{suppresult}\label{thm:supp-predictive}
\textbf{Theorem \thesuppresult\ (Nonpredictive information bounds dissipation):} Let $X_t$ be the system's state and $S_t$ an environmental signal evolving by a dynamics of its own on which the system does not act. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \le I(X_t ; S_t), \qquad W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge k_B T \left[\, I(X_t ; S_t) - I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \,\right],
\end{equation}
the second being the bound of \cite{still2012}. Consequently $W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge 0$, and any bound on the dissipated work is a bound on the nonpredictive information $I(X_t;S_t) - I(X_t;S_{t+1})$.

The first inequality is the data processing inequality for the Markov chain $X_t \to S_t \to S_{t+1}$, proved in \nolinkurl{Phase3_PredictiveThermodynamics.lean} as \nolinkurl{predictiveInfo_le_mutualInfo}; mutual information is defined there from Mathlib's \texttt{klDiv} as the divergence of the joint law from the product of its marginals, since Mathlib carries no mutual information of its own. The second inequality is the class field \nolinkurl{PredictiveDissipation.still_bound}, an irreducible physical postulate for the same reason as every other one here --- its \texttt{axiom} form is refutable, and \texttt{Examples}~\S18.4 refutes it. Everything downstream is derived: non-negativity of the dissipated work, the bound of the nonpredictive information by the dissipation budget, and the statement that a quasi-static drive leaves every retained bit predictive. \texttt{Examples}~\S18 inhabits the class twice over one correlated two-bit law --- a frozen signal, permitted to dissipate nothing, and a scrambled one, forced to dissipate its entire memory --- and fences the theorem with a Markov kernel on the pair that writes the system's state into the signal and thereby \emph{raises} mutual information, so the no-back-action hypothesis is shown to carry weight rather than to decorate. The equality of \cite{kawai2007}, $\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}} \rangle = k_B T\, D_{KL}(\mathrm{forward} \,||\, \mathrm{reverse})$, is carried as a predicate on an instance and yields \nolinkurl{nonpredictive_le_arrow}: the irreversibility of the dynamics is at least the memory that fails to earn its keep.

The information constraints distinguish erased entropy from retained nonpredictive information \cite{rosas2026}. Estimating either side from data requires a separate observation model. A partially observed signaling model derives finite-time estimates of information gathering and dissipation from counting statistics \cite{nicoletti2026}; a corresponding cortical estimator is not supplied here.

\subsection{A finite thermodynamic model with feedback}

For a finite controller--world law $p(x,s)$ and a normalized channel $K_x(s'\mid s)$, one elementary update holds $x$ fixed. The final law and the forward and reverse path laws are
\begin{align*}
 p'(x,s')&=\sum_s p(x,s)K_x(s'\mid s),\\
 P_F(x,s,s')&=p(x,s)K_x(s'\mid s),\\
 P_R(x,s,s')&=p'(x,s')K_x(s\mid s').
\end{align*}
Both path laws are normalized. The reverse uses the same autonomous transition channel, started at the forward final distribution. \nolinkurl{FiniteFeedbackStep.entropy_balance} assumes strictly positive initial masses and transition probabilities. \nolinkurl{entropy_balance_of_support} assumes only that every forward path of positive probability has positive reverse probability, which is what the expansion uses and which strict positivity implies (\nolinkurl{reversibleSupport_of_positive}). Time-dependent protocols and unidirectional transitions require a corresponding treatment of reversal and support.

\nolinkurl{FiniteFeedbackStep.entropy_balance}
(\nolinkurl{Phase3_AgencyThermodynamics.lean}) expands the path log-ratio to obtain
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=D_{KL}(P_F\|P_R)
 =H(p')-H(p)+\sum_{x,s,s'}P_F(x,s,s')
       \log\frac{K_x(s'\mid s)}{K_x(s\mid s')}\ge0.
\end{equation}
Gibbs' inequality proves nonnegativity; the balance is derived from these distributions. \nolinkurl{ProbDist.KL_eq_klDiv} identifies the finite divergence with Mathlib's measure-theoretic divergence for a positive reference law. The information identity uses the same forward law and its proven initial and final marginals.

Support inclusion is the weaker of the two conditions and the one the identity
needs. Gibbs' inequality holds under it (\nolinkurl{KL_nonneg_of_support}),
so zero masses in the initial law or in a transition are permitted and $\Sigma\ge0$
survives them; this matters because an agent's executed channels are sparse, and
a step whose initial law sits on a single atom satisfies the support condition
while failing positivity. What the weaker condition does not do is make every
channel priceable. A forward path with no reverse --- deterministic erasure is
the standard case --- has infinite divergence, and
\texttt{ProbDist.extendedKL} records it as such in $[0,\infty]$ rather than
returning the finite number the totalized real logarithm would supply. Where
support does hold, that extended value is the real divergence above.
\nolinkurl{ProbDist.extendedKL_eq_klDiv} and
\nolinkurl{extendedKL_eq_klDiv_of_missing}
(\nolinkurl{Phase3_MeasureFeedback.lean}) identify the extended value
with Mathlib's measure divergence on both branches --- the finite one under a
positive reference law and the infinite one wherever a forward atom has no
reverse --- so the finite convention here is the measure-theoretic divergence
restricted to finite spaces, not a second definition standing beside it.

Local detailed balance is the physical identification
$q(x,s,s')=k_BT\log[K_x(s'\mid s)/K_x(s\mid s')]$, with heat positive into the reservoir \cite{horowitz2014,ito2013}. It gives
$\langle Q\rangle\ge k_BT[H(p)-H(p')]$.
The first-law hypothesis $w=\mathcal{E}(x,s')-\mathcal{E}(x,s)+q$ separately fixes work. These are bounds on the joint controller--world system. The passive signed information difference alone is insufficient to supply a general work bound. \nolinkurl{Chain.active_entropy_budget} gives the scalar budget consequence of \texttt{ActiveBound}, the fourth node of the active composition.

performs better and is infeasible, and copying and inversion reach identical
final joint laws at unequal heat, so even the full endpoint law cannot replace
path accounting. The reward, permitted policies, initial resource and budget
are declared inputs. Selection compares fixed autonomous policies for one
update and accounts for neither installing nor switching nor learning them.

\subsection{Adaptation toward an encoded objective}
\label{sec:supp-policy-learning}

A physical bit $b$ indexes the two feasible lamp policies, and at thermal scale
one its register energy $E_L(b)=4\log3\,[J(\pi_1)-J(\pi_b)]$ encodes their
supplied task values against the channel
\begin{equation}
 T=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&3/8\\1/8&7/8\end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:policy-learning-channel}
\end{equation}
rows and columns ordered $0,1$. Every transition has outward heat
$E_L(b)-E_L(c)=\log[T_{bc}/T_{cb}]$ and zero work; no observation or reward
estimator is part of this model. \texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep.iterate}
initializes each update from the preceding final law, and its telescoped
entropy balance and first law preserve strict support. In
\nolinkurl{Examples/PolicyLearning.lean} the occupancy converges geometrically to
$\Pr(b=1)=3/4$, so \nolinkurl{performance_improves} gives strict
expected improvement at every finite update while
\nolinkurl{performance_limit} places the limit at $7/16$, below the
better policy's own value. Cumulative heat is bounded by $(\log3)/2$ and comes
from the prepared register's relaxation, so this finite total supplies no
continuing power source and does not bound the cost of repeatedly performing
the task. Improvement depends on the objective, channel and preparation:
\nolinkurl{reward_decreasing_path_positive}
retains a copy-to-baseline transition, and
\nolinkurl{overprepared_reward_decreases} lowers expected
reward from an initial occupancy of $7/8$.

\subsection{Learning an unknown task from observations}
\label{sec:supp-observational-learning}

Which of two actions is rewarded is an environmental bit $w$ the learner never
reads. Its register bit is read out as the executed action, the world returns
success with probability $3/4$ on a match and $1/4$ otherwise, and the update
is win-stay, lose-resample. The fences are signatures rather than prose: in
\texttt{FiniteObservationalLearner} the update has type
$O\to R\to\mathrm{ProbDist}\,R$ and the readout $R\to A$, so no channel can
read the parameter and no precomputed policy value can be decoded into the
action, and the reward enters \texttt{performance} and no channel. Acting,
observing and updating compose into one \texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep} on $(W,R)$
at fixed parameter, so the existing path-law entropy balance, local detailed
balance and first law apply to exactly the process the reward is read from.
Its composite channel is
\begin{equation}
 T_w=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&3/8\\1/8&7/8\end{pmatrix}\quad(w=1),
 \label{eq:observational-channel}
\end{equation}
rows and columns ordered $r=0,1$, with its mirror image at $w=0$ and every
entry positive. From the uniform prior \nolinkurl{law_apply} gives
every mass at every horizon, so the executed action's expected reward is
$J_n=3/4-(1/4)(1/2)^n$: \nolinkurl{performance_improves} is strict
improvement from $J_0=1/2$, \nolinkurl{performance_limit} places the
limit at $3/4$ and \nolinkurl{performance_below_certainty}
keeps every term below one. \nolinkurl{action_marginal_unchanged} executes each action half the time at every horizon: what the
learner acquires is correlation with the parameter, not a preference between
actions.

Two baselines share the task, prior, readout and allowance. A frozen register
and a world returning success with probability $1/2$ whatever is done both stay
at $1/2$ for every $n$, the second spending nothing, and
\nolinkurl{improves_on_baselines} states the strict
improvement over both within the common allowance. The claim therefore rests on
the observations carrying task information rather than on the update rule or
the heat, and \nolinkurl{observation_informative} exhibits that
information as conditional on the action while
\nolinkurl{outcome_marginal_uninformative} shows the
outcome's marginal carries nothing about $w$ at the prior. Heat is the
transition log-ratio of the same composite channel at thermal scale one,
evaluated on the actual evolving laws:
\begin{equation}
 \langle Q_n\rangle=\frac{\log3}{8}\left(\frac12\right)^n,\qquad
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle Q_n\rangle=\frac{\log3}{4}
 \left[1-\left(\frac12\right)^N\right]\leq\frac{\log3}{4}.
 \label{eq:observational-heat}
\end{equation}
The register's two states are energetically degenerate, so it stores nothing
and \nolinkurl{cumulative_work} makes that whole sum external work.
The work is irreducible rather than a modelling convenience:
\nolinkurl{zero_work_needs_parameter_independent_heat}
proves that a register energy giving zero work on every path forces the heats
at the two parameter values to agree, and
\nolinkurl{no_zero_work_energy} exhibits
their disagreement, so an energy landscape that paid for this learning would be
one that already encoded the answer. Expected improvement is not pathwise
improvement, the register leaving the rewarding action with probability $1/8$.
Preparing the prior, supplying the work that drives the updates, executing task
episodes beyond the declared updates, convergence to an optimal policy and any
cortical identification are outside this model.

\subsection{A sensor channel computed from phases}
\label{sec:supp-phase-sensor}

In \nolinkurl{Phase3_PhaseSensor.lean}, a \texttt{PhaseSensor}
declares a phase configuration $\theta(w,a,v)$ for environmental parameter $w$,
executed action $a$ and oscillator $v$, together with a finite-symbol readout
$h:\mathbb{R}\to O$. Sampling one of $N>0$ oscillators uniformly gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(o\mid w,a)=\frac{|\{v:h(\theta(w,a,v))=o\}|}{N}.
 \label{eq:phase-sensor-channel}
\end{equation}
The channel probabilities are derived from those data. The phase law, sampling
rule and readout remain supplied; a general real-angle readout here is not
required to satisfy the periodic Lipschitz condition of the content encoders.
Two parameters give different channels at an action exactly when some symbol's
fibre counts differ
(\nolinkurl{channel_ne_iff_fiberCount_ne}).

\texttt{FiniteObservationalLearner.ofPhaseSensor} feeds this channel into the
existing learner without giving its update or action readout access to $w$.
In \nolinkurl{Examples/PhaseSensor.lean}, \S27, four oscillators take
phases $0$ or $\pi$, read as success or failure. A matching action gives three
success symbols and one failure; a mismatching action reverses those counts.
The resulting learner is proved equal to the learner in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-observational-learning}, so its entire law and reward
formula apply. The control always has three success symbols and one failure,
independently of parameter and action, and keeps expected reward $1/2$.
Both populations have $r^2=1/4$ for every parameter and action; the informative
learner strictly outperforms the control after any positive number of updates.
A constant readout also removes the advantage while preserving the phase law.

These witnesses distinguish information carried by a phase readout from
coherence. They derive no phase configuration from Kuramoto dynamics and no
learning advantage from increased coherence.

\section{Repeated operation, memory and energy supply}
\label{sec:supp-resources}

An executed sequence carries each final law into the next operation. Its mean thermodynamic balance, solvency on individual trajectories and physical source are separate specifications, and the constructions below identify which resource is supplied and which operation spends it. Sensor memory adds recording and clearing to the protocol; preparation adds a stage before its declared initial law; the energy-boundary results state the conditions needed to connect a drawn unit to physical work. Each paragraph names the module that carries it, and Table~\ref{tab:full} maps the declarations.

\paragraph{A continuing agent and its expected work allowance.}
\texttt{FiniteProtocol} in \nolinkurl{Phase3_ContinuingAgent.lean}
allows a different channel at every stage, carrying each actual final law into
the next initial law, and \nolinkurl{sum_entropy_balance},
\nolinkurl{sum_first_law} and
\nolinkurl{cumulative_entropy_budget} telescope the finite
path balances over it without stationarity. \texttt{ContinuingProcess} adds a
common energy, stage heat observables, a thermal scale and an initial work
allowance $B$; its predicate \texttt{Sustains}$\,N$ requires $B-W_{<k}\geq0$ at
every $k\leq N$ for work read as an expectation on the executed paths, which is
the article's Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuing-budget}. When each stage costs at least
$c>0$, \nolinkurl{horizon_le_of_cost} gives
$Nc\leq B$. These bounds include the system's mean energy drop and do not
identify the allowance with a register's erasure heat.

\texttt{ContinuingAgent} implements a three-stage cycle on $(w,r,e)$: the
parameter $w$ is fixed, the register $r$ selects the action and the environment
flag $e$ retains what that action did, with acting, learning and reset each
driving one coordinate while the others drift. The learner's readout,
observation and update do not take $w$ as an argument; the actuator responds to
it, as a physical task must, and reward is used only to score the law. On the
bit-valued witness of \nolinkurl{Examples/ContinuingAgent.lean} the proved mass
recursion gives expected task performance rising geometrically from below to
$65/127$ and staying short of one
(\nolinkurl{performance_error}, \nolinkurl{performance_tendsto}),
while \nolinkurl{first_cycle_work} and
\nolinkurl{totalWork_cycles_lower} price the cycles: the
learning stage costs $(\log(5/3))/16$ every cycle, reset has strictly positive
mean heat, and the resulting per-cycle floor exceeds any declared allowance at
some cycle (\nolinkurl{work_unbounded}). The sequence that converges
is one no finite store funds to its limit, so reaching that limit is a
statement about replenishment rather than about learning. Two controls delimit
it: uniform observations leave chance reward and zero learning-stage heat, and
replacing reset by idle drift still learns while changing the workspace law and
subsequent behaviour. The store limits expected work at each prefix and not the
battery on every realization, which is what the next construction supplies.

\paragraph{A store carried on each trajectory.}
A store reading $\beta$ with draw $w^-$ and supply $w^+$ obeys the following ledger and funding condition on reachable transitions $s\to t$:
\begin{equation}
 \beta(t)=\beta(s)-w^-(s,t)+w^+(s,t),\qquad w^-(s,t)\leq\beta(s)+w^+(s,t)
 \label{eq:pathwise-store}
\end{equation}
\nolinkurl{PathwiseStore}, in the same module and witnessed in
\nolinkurl{Examples/PathwiseStore.lean}, makes the reading
a \emph{coordinate of the state}, which is what makes the account pathwise:
\texttt{Reachable} is positive mass under the protocol's own law,
\texttt{Ledgered} is Eq.~\eqref{eq:pathwise-store} on every executed transition
out of a reachable state, \texttt{Solvent}$\,N$ says no reachable state carries
a negative reading, and \nolinkurl{solvent_of_funded} is
the conclusion. No positivity hypothesis appears, so deterministic gates and
zero masses are admitted, as they must be for the register--bath model of
Section~\ref{sec:supp-bath}. Averaging the ledger over the executed paths gives
\nolinkurl{totalDraw_le_of_solvent}:
pathwise solvency implies the expected bound that \texttt{Sustains} asserts. Two
results fence what a supply buys --- a supply covering each executed draw
sustains every horizon, and a net draw of at least $c>0$ on every executed
transition before $N$ gives $Nc\leq\beta_0$ --- and two witnesses separate the
readings. The \emph{gambler} satisfies the mean predicate with equality while
running overdrawn at probability $1/4$
(\nolinkurl{sustained_not_solvent}), so the refinement
runs one way only. The \emph{charger} spends a unit and is given it back,
proving solvency at every horizon while drawing without bound, satisfies
neither fence, and loses its ledger entirely when the supply is removed.

\paragraph{Finite sources and uncertain delivery.}
If usable source energy $R$ pays for the supplied transfer, internal
replenishment cancels from the combined ledger, $R'=R-w^+$ and
$(\beta+R)'=(\beta+R)-w^-$. In \nolinkurl{Examples/FiniteSupply.lean} the construction \texttt{withSource} adds $R$ to
the store's reading and sets external supply to zero, so
\nolinkurl{totalDraw_le_initial_resources}
bounds the drawn work by the initial combined reading and
\nolinkurl{horizon_le_of_finite_source}
applies the pathwise horizon bound to it. Both horizon bounds restrict their
cost premise to the finite run: a finite state space cannot sustain a uniformly
positive net energy loss forever. The witness has source, buffer, load and
selector bits with reversible, energy-conserving gates, so conservation
discharges the buffer's ledger and a unit reaches the load with probability
$1/2$; \nolinkurl{forced_not_ledgered} rejects a ledger
claiming delivery on every path, and
\nolinkurl{charger_no_finite_source} rules
out any nonnegative finite source for the indefinitely replenished charger.
Signed supply permits energy returned to the source; the transfer identity is a
model obligation, and preparing the source, controlling the gates, external
refuelling and identifying this hardware with the learning agent's actuator are
outside the model.

\paragraph{Recording observations and clearing sensor memory.}
For a memory marginal $\mu$ and a strictly positive target law $\nu$, the constant clearing channel has mean log-ratio heat
\begin{equation}
 \langle Q_M\rangle=k_BT[H(\mu)-H(\nu)]+k_BT\,D_{KL}(\mu\|\nu),
 \label{eq:erasure-cost}
\end{equation}
identified with reservoir heat by local detailed balance, the KL term being the
additional cost of that fixed channel on the supplied marginal.
\nolinkurl{Phase3_SensorMemory.lean} proves it as
\nolinkurl{memoryHeat_const}, with no positivity of $\mu$: a state the
memory never holds contributes to neither side. \texttt{MemoryAgent} runs act,
record, learn and clear four-periodically, so \texttt{protocol} is an ordinary
\texttt{FiniteProtocol} and the telescoped balances cover it unchanged, and the
fences are again signatures --- \texttt{record} and \texttt{erase} do not take
the parameter, \texttt{update} takes neither parameter nor world, and the reward
enters no channel. \nolinkurl{memoryLaw_clear} is the one stage at
which the memory is autonomous and \nolinkurl{memoryLaw_record} the
stage at which it is not, which is the content: the correlation recording writes
is what clearing destroys. \nolinkurl{clear_stageHeat_eq}
splits the clear stage's heat into the register's, the memory's and the world's
shares, and \nolinkurl{clear_memoryHeat_const} is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:erasure-cost} for the agent's own run. On the bit-valued witness
of \nolinkurl{Examples/SensorMemory.lean} a marginal
argument that uses no joint law prices every clearing between $(3/16)\log3$ and
$(5/16)\log3$. Four controls fence the reading: a measurement ignoring the world
leaves the same marginal and pays identical heat
(\nolinkurl{blind_same_cost}), so the cost is set by the
marginal and not by what the memory is about; replacing erasure by symmetric
drift costs nothing and carries the measurement forward; and
\nolinkurl{over_ordered_absorbs} delivers a negative
share, a case the inequality does not see.

\paragraph{Funding repeated clearing from a carried source.}
A nonnegative source starting no higher than $b$, losing at least $c>0$ at each clearing and receiving no return at other stages, can fund only $m$ complete cycles satisfying
\begin{equation}
 mc\leq b ,
 \label{eq:funded-horizon}
\end{equation}
the stage-dependent cost permitting free operations between clearings.
\nolinkurl{PathwiseStore.balance_le_of_stage_cost}
generalizes the reading bound from a constant to a stage function, and
\nolinkurl{horizon_le_of_periodic_cost}
is Eq.~\eqref{eq:funded-horizon} for a run solvent at $pm$ whose other stages
return no more than they draw. \nolinkurl{net_draw_eq_zero_of_positive} is the obstruction:
on a \texttt{Positive} protocol the ledger holds between every pair of states in
both directions, so the reading is constant and every net draw vanishes. In
\nolinkurl{Examples/FundedMemory.lean} the witness reuses
the measurement, update, erasure and drift of the sensor agent, so what the
source funds is what Eq.~\eqref{eq:erasure-cost} prices; an induction on the
reachable states proves the charge after $n$ operations exactly, and
\nolinkurl{cycles_le_three} is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:funded-horizon} attained, with the reading below empty at the
sixteenth operation. Two negatives are recorded rather than repaired.
\nolinkurl{clear_unfunded} proves that from the sixteenth operation
every executed clearing draws nothing while the floor still prices it:
\texttt{erase} reads only the memory, so no channel of this agent can be
conditioned on the charge, and the run past three cycles is unfunded rather than
halted. The drawn unit is a declared reading of the state and is not identified
with the stage's log-ratio heat.

\paragraph{Preparing the initial law.}
A preparation drives the hardware to a strictly positive law without reading
what the hardware held, which is the hypothesis of
\nolinkurl{memoryHeat_const}, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:erasure-cost} prices
it. \nolinkurl{KL_uniform} gives
$D_{KL}(\mu\|\mathrm{unif})=\log|V|-H(\mu)$ with no positivity of $\mu$, so the
two terms cancel and \nolinkurl{memoryHeat_uniform} is zero at every
starting law and thermal scale. \nolinkurl{FiniteProtocol.withPreparation}
prepends the preparation as stage zero, so the telescoped balances and store
bounds cover it, and \nolinkurl{preparation_parameter_marginal}
holds for every preparation channel: it writes the system coordinate and leaves
the parameter's marginal as it found it, so the agent's uncertainty about the
world is an input. In \nolinkurl{Phase3_Preparation.lean} and its
examples, preparing the sensor agent's uniform prior is free while preparing its
standard state costs $(1/4)\log3$; a bit law biased to $\varepsilon$ costs
$(1/2-\varepsilon)\log[(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon]$, and
\nolinkurl{sharp_prep_unbounded} proves that family has no
finite upper bound, its sharp endpoint rejected from the identity's
positive-target premise rather than assigned an infinite value.
\nolinkurl{known_parameter_not_preparable}
and \nolinkurl{correlated_not_preparable} bound what a
state-blind preparation reaches: it cannot make a certain parameter uncertain,
and it cannot produce a correlated two-bit prior.

\paragraph{Relating the drawn resource to physical energy.}
\nolinkurl{Phase3_ResourceFoundations.lean} discharges three premises
of the resource ledger on named objects rather than declaring them. The first is
what the drawn unit \emph{is}:
\nolinkurl{draw_eq_step_heat} identifies the
bath coordinate's gain with the mean heat of the step the stage actually runs,
which local detailed balance turns into $k_BT$ times that step's own bath
entropy, and \nolinkurl{draw_ge_entropy_reduction}
is the consequence, under support inclusion rather than full positivity, so it
reaches the sparse laws an agent runs on. Control remains on the right-hand side
and is subtracted, so a large control budget weakens the bound rather than being
constrained by it: fabrication is unpriced, and it is visible in the statement
where it is unpriced. The second is the sharp charge.
\texttt{transferCharge} moves one reversibly and creates nothing; what it cannot
do is make one, and
\nolinkurl{sharp_preparation_not_reversibleSupport}
says why --- a channel driving every state to one target has no reverse path, so
the path divergence is $\infty$, recorded in $[0,\infty]$ and not converted to a
real. The approximate charge is priceable and the exact one is not, and both
halves are theorems. The third is where delivery comes from.
\texttt{SourceDelivery} identifies delivery with a source coordinate's loss on
an arbitrary state type and
\nolinkurl{horizon_le_of_sourced_delivery}
adds the source's initial content to the store's inside one horizon bound, so
refuelling buys a longer run of the same shape;
\nolinkurl{no_finite_source_sustains} is the
other half, a ledger with delivery from a nonnegative source, a store never
below empty and a strictly positive cost at every operation being an impossible
object. Sustained operation is not a property a finite agent has.

\paragraph{Trajectory identities and measure-valued feedback.}
The resource results above hold on an arbitrary state type, but they are
trajectory identities: real readings indexed by operation number, with no
probability law anywhere, so nothing in them discharges an integrability or
existence assumption. Carrying the path law itself off finite spaces needs a
different vehicle, and in \nolinkurl{Phase3_MeasureFeedback.lean} it is
Mathlib's divergence valued in $[0,\infty]$.
\texttt{MeasureFeedbackStep} carries an initial probability measure and a Markov
kernel on any measurable space, the forward path law being their composition and
the entropy production the divergence from the declared reverse channel read in
forward time order. Three properties the finite development imposes by hand then
hold by construction: nonnegativity, because the codomain has no negative
element; $\infty$ for a forward law not absolutely continuous with respect to
the reversed one, with no positivity or finiteness hypothesis; and integrability
as a stated side condition of the real-valued form rather than an implicit
property of a finite sum.
\nolinkurl{path_divergence_splits} is the chain rule and
the general replacement for Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-update-balance};
\nolinkurl{entropyProduction_map_le} is data processing,
so coarse-graining by any measurable observable cannot increase production; and
\nolinkurl{initial_divergence_le_entropyProduction}
is the second law in divergence form. Control follows:
\nolinkurl{MeasureControlProblem.exists_optimal} selects a
maximizing action over a finite permitted set with no finiteness on the state at
all, and \nolinkurl{exists_optimal_compact} over a compact
one, with continuity discharged on witnesses that sit on $\mathbb{N}$ and on
$\mathbb{R}$, the latter carrying a Gaussian initial law that assigns zero to
every singleton. The \emph{Shannon-entropy} form of the balance does not carry
across: it needs a reference measure and densities against it, and differential
entropy relative to Lebesgue measure is not entropy, not being even nonnegative.
The divergence form is the statement that survives, and the entropy identity
remains a finite-state result.

\subsection{Allocating one register's heat budget}
\label{sec:supp-ledger}

\textit{Formalization: \texttt{Examples/RegisterBudget.lean}.}

The witness is that same one-bit register, at temperature one with Landauer heat $\log2$, and its operations act on a lamp bit through one family: the environment relaxes toward agreement with the held register bit at odds $a:1-a$, from a law whose agreement probability is $w$, with interaction energy $\log[a/(1-a)]$ for disagreement and mean heat $\log[a/(1-a)]\,(a-w)$. The controlled operation takes $a=2/3$, $w=4/15$ and delivers $(2/5)\log2$; the other takes $a=4/5$, $w=1/2$ and delivers $(3/5)\log2$. Both inputs are load-bearing. \nolinkurl{act_not_compressive} shows the controlled operation increases the joint entropy, so its own second law bounds it neither below at zero nor above; \nolinkurl{compressive_exceeds_budget} exhibits an entropy-preserving operation of the same family delivering $(7/3)\log2$, above the register's whole dissipation; and \nolinkurl{leaky_exceeds_budget} exhibits a second ledger for the same register, satisfying positivity, local detailed balance and the identity, whose other operation draws heat out of the reservoir and whose controlled operation is allocated $(7/6)\log2$. \nolinkurl{leaky_other_not_compressive} identifies that operation as the one the derivation excludes.

\subsection{Bath-energy transfer and reported heat}
\label{sec:supp-bath}

For a finite protocol with declared bath energy $E_B$ and reported transition heat $q_k$, telescoping gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k<N}\langle\Delta E_B\rangle_k=\langle E_B\rangle_N-\langle E_B\rangle_0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{k<N}\langle q_k\rangle=\langle E_B\rangle_N-\langle E_B\rangle_0
   +\sum_{k<N}\langle q_k-\Delta E_B\rangle,
 \label{eq:bath-ledger}
\end{equation}
The correction records any difference between the reported heat and actual bath-energy transfer.

\textit{Formalization: \texttt{Examples/RegisterBath.lean}.}

\texttt{FiniteProtocol.energyTransfer} is the gain of the subsystem whose
energy is declared, on an actual transition.
\nolinkurl{sum_energyTransfer} and
\nolinkurl{reported_heat_eq}
(\nolinkurl{Phase3_ContinuingAgent.lean}) give Eq.~\eqref{eq:bath-ledger} for any finite protocol: the first is a first-law
statement that admits deterministic gates and zero masses and asserts no
entropy or temperature identification, and the second exhibits the correction
by which a reported ledger differs from the modelled bath's gain.

The witness carries one register bit and one bath bit. \texttt{lift} sends each
of the four maps of the register to a permutation of the pair
(\nolinkurl{lift_injective}) that performs the requested map on a
bath prepared \texttt{false} (\nolinkurl{lift_realizes}); an
injective map acts on the register alone and an erasure swaps the register's

(\nolinkurl{bathEnergy_register_degenerate}), and the
thermal scale is one. \texttt{operationHeat} is the bath's mean energy gain on

for these values, with the reachable bath supports as its final supports; the

map including the identity, and its consumers are unchanged.

Three results fence a map-indexed budget. \emph{The eraser is not reusable.}
\nolinkurl{erase_law_one} clears the register and
leaves the bath uniform, and \nolinkurl{erase_law_two}
proves that a second execution of the same gate restores the prepared law, so
the register is uniform again. The shares are $\log2$
(\nolinkurl{erase_first_heat}) and $-\log2$
(\nolinkurl{reused_bath_heat}) about a total of zero
(\nolinkurl{two_stage_heat}), which is the generic bath
ledger applied to this protocol rather than a separate calculation, so a share
is neither nonnegative nor bounded by the total
(\nolinkurl{shares_exceed_total}).

\emph{The register's map does not fix the cost.}
\texttt{driveGate} holds the register at its value and excites its bath, so it
performs the identity on a prepared bath at heat $2\log2$ rather than $0$
(\nolinkurl{drivenIdleHeat_eq},
\nolinkurl{same_update_different_heat}).
The work is accounted with the bath: $\log2$ for one erasure
(\nolinkurl{erase_work}), $2\log2$ for the drive
(\nolinkurl{driven_work}) and zero for two erasures
(\nolinkurl{reuse_work}), the register's levels being degenerate.

\emph{Reversibility supplies neither ledger premise.} A deterministic gate
sends one state to one state, so its channel charges nothing to any other
(\nolinkurl{gate_channel_zero}) and the reduced step
is not strictly positive
(\nolinkurl{gate_not_positive}); and a swap is an
involution, so its forward and reverse transition masses agree on every

(\nolinkurl{erase_reported_correction}).

This constructs no \texttt{RegisterLedger} instance and weakens none of its
results: positivity, local detailed balance and the accounting identity remain
the physical inputs \texttt{RegisterBudget} supplies, and this model shows that

declared energy gap and preparation. Preparing the bath, supplying the work,
fabricating the gate and identifying this bath with the reservoir the ledger's

 \label{eq:actuated-coupling}

The noisy two-bit actuator has positive drive $d=(3/4)\log3-\log2$.

At unit gain the first two spatial energies are

\subsection{A finite phase--register loop with a work store}

\nolinkurl{Phase3_PhaseFeedback.lean} defines a joint transition retaining a
phase $p$ and register $r$: the installed action $a(r)$ selects a phase
channel, a sensor reads the new phase, and the resulting observation updates
the same register. Marginalizing the new register recovers the installed phase
channel averaged over the current joint law. The update order retains the
correlations that a product of separate phase and learner laws would discard.
\nolinkurl{Phase3_FeedbackLedger.lean} proves the finite-horizon recurrence
and telescopes expected installation energy and a store account on these
executed transitions. Its sensing, reset, preparation and mode prices are
declared work inputs, and its exhaustion result assumes a positive expected
net-cost floor without replenishment.

The numerical witness uses $p,r\in\{0,1\}$, installed action $a=r$,
signed coupling $K_a=h(2a-1)$ to the phase spin $2p-1$, mode price
$u_a=a\Delta u$, and energy $E_a(p)=-K_a(2p-1)$. Changing the register
therefore changes the installed coupling and the next phase transition's
rates. A phase flip occurs with probability
$\gamma\exp[-\beta(E_a(1-p)-E_a(p))/2]$, with the remaining probability on
no flip. The chosen parameters keep every phase-transition probability
positive. The flip-rate ratio is
$P_a(p,1-p)/P_a(1-p,p)=e^{-\beta[E_a(1-p)-E_a(p)]}$, so this
phase substep has local detailed balance with the named bath at inverse
temperature $\beta$. The sensor reports the new phase correctly with
probability $q$ and the register copies that report. The exact joint
transition is the product of these conditional channels in that order.
Deterministic copying need not give reverse support for the whole
sensor/register operation, so local detailed balance is asserted for phase
flips alone. No continuous-angle probability current exists in this binary
phase space.

On each transition, installation work is the change in $E_a(p')+u_a$ at the
new phase, while phase-flip heat to the bath is $E_a(p)-E_a(p')$. Their
expected difference telescopes to the change in installed energy. Sensing
and reset carry additional positive declared work prices; preparation is
charged once. The work store subtracts these prices and adds a separately
named external charger at each step. A conservative pathwise stop rule uses
the maximum possible stage price, so it halts before any path can overdraw
the finite store; continuing operation requires the charger. No thermal lower
bound is inferred for sensing, reset or charger channels without their own
reverse protocols and reservoir model.

One fixed binary readout is fitted on independently sampled training episodes
at a declared bath field and then frozen. Held-out episodes use two different
field families and new random seeds. In one held-out family its next-phase
accuracy is \gFeedbackInformedAccuracy{} with the informative sensor versus
\gFeedbackBlindAccuracy{} with a blind sensor. A replay control follows the
informed register marginal from an external schedule but removes its
phase--register correlation, and an unchanging actuator is scored under the
same initial independent prior and resource prices. The informed, blind and
replay controls have the same marginal phase order in this symmetric example;
their accuracy difference rests on the retained joint law, not on that order
statistic. The benchmark saves binary phase/register symbol agreement,
reconstruction error, installed energy, cumulative declared work, phase-flip
bath heat and store readings separately. Symbol agreement is a toy
shared-content proxy, without independent semantic decoders. The register,
sensor, bath, charger and mode prices have no cortical calibration, so this
closed-loop witness does not establish a cortical process.

\section{Interpretive scope}
\label{sec:supp-interpretive}

The formal endpoint is the section glued on a specified compatible cover, fixed by a specified reflexive map. The interpretation developed in the main article identifies the section with conscious unity and its accurate self-representation with a minimal self. Uniqueness is relative to the cover and map; identifying these with meaningful neural descriptions and a neural self-encoding requires independent evidence. Restriction-resonance supplies the relation between the encoding and the state it represents.

The full commitment proposes that unity and reflexivity occur together in conscious episodes. Gluing by itself does not require reflexivity, so a dissociation could challenge that requirement while leaving the unity clause assessable. The physical recovery tests concern coupling and phase dynamics. Testing the interpretive clauses also requires validated measures of compatibility and self-encoding, together with an independent experience endpoint.

In the cup example, the proposed self-encoding concerns the perceiver's
position and condition within the scene, without requiring an autobiographical
narrative. Holding the external scene fixed while altering an independently
identified encoding of the hand or viewpoint should change the reconstructed
self-relative relation as the specified readout predicts. That manipulation
tests the reconstruction model; whether the represented perspective is
experienced remains a separate question.

\section{Relationship to other theories}
\label{sec:supp-other-theories}

Integrated information and global-workspace theories supply different descriptions of integration and access \cite{tononi2016,baars1997,dehaene2014}. The present model specifies a candidate physical coupling and a consistency condition on local states. Gluing does not derive an exclusion principle, and continuity alone does not force compatible local descriptions. A digital architecture is not excluded by the sheaf construction. These mathematical differences do not settle which systems have conscious experience.

Biological naturalism holds that the properties grounding experience belong to self-sustaining organisms and that simulating them does not secure them \cite{seth2026,seth2026response}. The conditions in this work name no biological mechanism. A digital candidate would have to exhibit physically realized coupling in a measured noise regime, agreement about independently identified shared quantities and internal reconstruction at a declared fidelity; satisfying those conditions would still leave the experiential interpretation open. A related position locates the difference in computation across inseparable scales and continuous dynamics of the physical medium \cite{milinkovic2026}. A mathematical description of a field does not by itself realize such dynamics in a computing substrate.

The neuroscientific case against human-like consciousness in text-only language models concerns embodied sensory interaction, thalamocortical organization and biological regulation that links perception and action to survival \cite{aru2023}. Their absence supports skepticism conditional on those mechanisms' causal relevance. None is required by abstract gluing or reconstruction. Reports of workspace-like organization in some models instead motivate a direct test of the proposed reconstruction condition \cite{gurnee2026}: declare the state family and fidelity, fix the encoding region and its causal past, and test what the region distinguishes before interpreting an emitted report.

Two results bear on attempts to infer a generative mechanism from a steady state, including readings of the free-energy principle \cite{friston2010}. Increasing coupling, decreasing diffusion and a matched common drive share stationary phase densities, so the density alone cannot select their mechanism. Separately, local descriptions at positive overlap tolerance need not have an exact global extension; a decomposition alone supplies no single consistent state. These are explicit instances of identifiability concerns discussed in \cite{aguilera2022,biehl2021,bruineberg2022}.

The comparisons have specific scope. The squared-drift functional is not a variational free energy, and the plasticity control concerns only its stated objective. At coherent stationarity, phase-space current dissipation vanishes while phase-averaged squared drift is positive; the substrate objective is a further functional on a different domain. These distinctions supply no general verdict about cortical inference or the free-energy principle.

\section{Formal soundness: why a global axiom is the wrong shape for a physical postulate}
\label{sec:soundness}

Formalization is only as trustworthy as the axioms it rests on, and an axiom that is inconsistent silently makes every theorem downstream of it vacuous without changing whether the remaining statements type-check. The failure mode below is easy to reproduce: a declaration can constrain a symbol it does not itself bind, making the resulting class inconsistent.

The failure has one shape, and naming it is more useful than cataloguing its instances: \emph{an axiom that constrains a symbol it does not itself bind}. Such a declaration is not a postulate about one system. It is a constraint on every model of the class the symbol belongs to, including models built to violate it, and a class that has such a model is thereby inconsistent. Three concrete cases, each of which proves \texttt{False} on its own:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \nolinkurl{spontaneous_symmetry_breaking_pointwise_min} would take the potential-energy functional as an \emph{implicit} argument occurring only in its hypothesis \texttt{PotentialEnergy phi = V v0}. Instantiating that functional with the constant map $v\mapsto V(v_0)$ discharges the hypothesis by \texttt{rfl} and yields the conclusion for an arbitrary field. With $V(v) = v^2$, $v_0 = 0$ and $\phi \equiv 1$ this proves $1 = 0$.
\item \nolinkurl{landauer_heat_eq} would equate the free class field \nolinkurl{heat_dissipation} with a bath-entropy difference \emph{for every instance} of the class. An instance with zero heat dissipation and a bath that gains one bit gives $0 = \log 2$.
\item \nolinkurl{kl_bound_axiom} would assert $\sigma \ge D_{KL}(P\|Q)/\Delta t$ for \emph{all} $P$ and $Q$, while $\sigma$ is a single real fixed by a class field. Since $D_{KL}$ is unbounded above, a point mass against a uniform distribution refutes it.
\end{enumerate}

Two further declarations of the same shape are worth recording because their refutation is different in kind. \nolinkurl{phase_invariant_periodic} and \nolinkurl{sync_to_section_eq}, as global assertions pinning free fields of \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization}, are refutable as soon as the probability presheaf admits two distinct global sections; the refutation needs no arithmetic at all, only that the sheaf is not trivial. Neither is a postulate in the current development: the periodicity condition is an obligation of the constructor that builds a cover from an invariant measure, and \nolinkurl{sync_to_section_eq} is a theorem about the global section the sheaf condition produces.

The rule that prevents it is uniform: \emph{a physical postulate that mentions a class field must be a field of that class, never a standalone axiom quantified over all instances.} A postulate is then an obligation each model discharges, not a global claim about every model. Under that rule the development declares no axioms at all.

\subsection{What the witnesses establish, and what they do not}

Explicit witnesses establish that the class assumptions can be satisfied. The \texttt{Examples} witnesses therefore cover every class that carries a physical postulate: a one-bit erasure system with a genuine bath that discharges \nolinkurl{heat_eq} and reproduces $\Delta Q = k_B T \ln 2$; a perfectly resonant system discharging \nolinkurl{kl_bound}; and a scalar field on a one-point spacetime discharging the action principles. The gluing structures are the most demanding case and are witnessed by a \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} over a three-site substrate carrying the discrete topology and its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, covered by two overlapping open patches whose intersection is a single shared site.

What the witnesses establish is that the classes are inhabitable, so the theorems about them are not vacuous. What they do not establish is that a cortex is such an instance. In the cover above the phase field is already constant --- every patch locked at $\theta = 0$ --- which is precisely what makes the equilibrium obligation dischargeable; getting a physical system into that configuration is the content of the informal dynamical argument.
