\section*{Overview}
This document supplies the formal constructions, numerical methods and exploratory EEG analysis for the conditional field model in the main article. Mathematical results are distinguished from model assumptions and physical identifications. The Lean development uses only its library's foundational axioms; physical postulates are hypotheses or fields of models with explicit witnesses. The soundness section below explains why an unrestricted postulate about model-dependent quantities can be inconsistent. Source code and saved simulation summaries are maintained at \url{https://github.com/bobaseb/physical_primitives_of_mind}.

\section{Symmetry Breaking and the Emergence of Boundaries}
The framework's first physical motif is that a perfectly symmetric universe cannot support agency. The formal result is narrower: under the stated vacuum and connectedness hypotheses, a field reaching two vacuum components must leave the vacuum somewhere.

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold representing spacetime, and let $\phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{V}$ be a physical field mapping to a vacuum manifold $\mathcal{V}$ (the space of energy minima). Let $G$ be a continuous symmetry group acting on $\mathcal{V}$. 

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Domain-wall obstruction):} A continuous field on a connected substrate whose image reaches two different connected components of the vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere. This is the $\pi_0$ case of the topological-defect argument. A separate extension obstruction states that non-null-homotopic boundary data admit no continuous extension over the specified contractible interior.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase1\_\allowbreak Primitives.lean}):} We formalize this by defining the set of degenerate energy minima \texttt{DynamicalVacuum} and proving \texttt{boundary\_\allowbreak defect\_\allowbreak forces\_\allowbreak interior\_\allowbreak vacuum\_\allowbreak break}: if a boundary field configuration $\phi_{\partial}$ is not null-homotopic, then it admits no continuous extension over a contractible interior. This is an obstruction to extending the specified boundary data. Note the direction: this result establishes that a defect \emph{obstructs extension}, not that a defect must exist. The companion lemma \texttt{contractible\_\allowbreak interior\_\allowbreak forces\_\allowbreak trivial\_\allowbreak boundary}  proves the contrapositive.

A direction that produces a defect is proved, at the level of $\pi_0$. \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak common\_\allowbreak preconnected} states it in the form a witness can discharge --- if no preconnected subset of the vacuum manifold $M$ contains both $\phi(x_1)$ and $\phi(x_2)$, and the substrate is connected, then $\phi$ leaves $M$ somewhere --- and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In} restates it with $\phi(x_2) \notin$ the connected component of $M$ containing $\phi(x_1)$. The proof uses the connectedness of continuous images and \texttt{IsPreconnected.\allowbreak subset\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In}, and nothing else; no hypothesis mentions energy, a potential or a symmetry. This is the domain-wall case of Kibble's classification and we take only that case: $\pi_1 \ne 0$ forcing vortices and $\pi_2 \ne 0$ forcing monopoles are recorded as available and not taken, since the manuscript's argument uses domain walls alone. Separately, \texttt{spontaneous\_\allowbreak symmetry\_\allowbreak breaking} shows a global energy minimiser lies in the vacuum manifold almost everywhere; this is proved without additional axioms, the pointwise step being \texttt{pointwise\_\allowbreak vacuum\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak min}. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11 witnesses that result on the double well $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ over a two-point spacetime carrying a Dirac probability measure. Two features of the witness are deliberate. Its minimisation hypothesis is \emph{proved} rather than assumed --- everywhere else in the development the existence of a global minimiser is a hypothesis, since no compactness or direct-method argument is formalized. And the almost-everywhere in the conclusion is shown necessary: the minimising field sits at the vacuum on the support and at $0$ off it, so the pointwise version of the statement is false of the very field the theorem applies to. The same section instantiates the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$ and discharges the energy invariance, giving the first instance of \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}; the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ is proved degenerate and the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so a minimiser and its image have equal energy and differ. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11.1 then supplies the witness for the $\pi_0$ theorem on the same well. The vacuum manifold is \emph{computed} there, \texttt{wellVacuum\_\allowbreak eq} proving $\texttt{DynamicalVacuum}\ V = \{-1,1\}$ exactly rather than merely containing both minima --- which matters, because a larger vacuum set might have been connected --- and \texttt{wellVacuum\_\allowbreak separated} proves the disconnection in the form the theorem consumes, by the observation that a preconnected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ spanning $-1$ and $1$ contains $0$, which is not a minimum. \texttt{wellV\_\allowbreak domain\_\allowbreak wall} is the resulting statement about \emph{every} continuous field on \emph{any} connected substrate reaching both minima. Non-emptiness of that class is checked separately rather than assumed: the clipped identity on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies the hypotheses, and \texttt{kink\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak notMem} locates its forced wall at the origin. No conserved quantity is constructed from any of this, and there is still no Noether theorem.

\section{The Thermodynamic Constraint: Landauer's Principle}
For a finite effective register, the number of distinguishable histories that can be retained is bounded. This does not imply that every update erases. The capacity theorem states that a distribution on a finite state space $X$ carries at most $\log|X|$ nats, with equality only at the uniform law (\texttt{shannon\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak log\_\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak iff}), and a perturbation stream of length $k$ over an alphabet $P$ with $|P|^k > |X|$ therefore cannot be recorded injectively (\texttt{absorb\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak injective}), whatever the update map is and even when every individual step of it is reversible.

Let the global phase space $\Omega = \Omega_{sys} \times \Omega_{bath}$ undergo globally reversible Hamiltonian dynamics, modeled as an injective mapping $f: \Omega \to \Omega$.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Thermodynamic Erasure):} Under the specified finite-register and reversible system--bath assumptions, the heat identity gives a lower bound proportional to the entropy erased by the register. Erasing one unbiased bit gives $\Delta Q\geq k_BT\ln2$. Non-injectivity alone does not specify a universal one-bit entropy loss; the bound uses the erased entropy of the particular update.

\textit{The same heat, in three vocabularies (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak LandauerBridge.lean}).} Derivation 2 charges a register for destroying information and Derivation 3 charges a structure for keeping information it cannot use. \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.\allowbreak ofLandauer} builds the predictive structure of a given register: its thermal scale is that register's temperature, its dissipated work is that register's Landauer heat, and Still's bound is derived from \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak bound} rather than assumed a second time. The single hypothesis is the identification $\left(I_{\mathrm{mem}} - I_{\mathrm{pred}}\right) \le S(\mathrm{id}) - S(t)$ --- the memory wasted is no larger than the entropy erased --- and it is a restriction, not bookkeeping: on an injective update the right-hand side is zero and the inequality forces the waste to vanish (\texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak injective}), which is the Norton--Shenker distinction in informational form. The measure-theoretic statement of Landauer (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak Measure\-Thermodynamics}) is specialised in the same file rather than left standing beside the combinatorial one: at counting measure on a finite register, \texttt{continuous\_\allowbreak entropy} is \texttt{boltzmann\_\allowbreak entropy}, volume contraction is non-injectivity, and \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak bound} is $T$ times the entropy erased (\texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak count\_\allowbreak eq}). The witness is the one-bit eraser, where a register destroying $\log 2$ pays for a memory wasting $\log 2$, with equality (\texttt{Examples}~\S18.5).

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}):} We explicitly decouple logical erasure from physical erasure. Lean verifies that any contraction in the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective}(t_{sys})$) under globally reversible dynamics mathematically necessitates heat dissipation. The combinatorial core, \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak reversibility} and \texttt{second\_\allowbreak law}, is fully derived. Finiteness of the phase space is what converts the physically checkable hypothesis into the one the bound needs: on a finite state space a map that fails to reach every state is not injective (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak erasure\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak surjective}), so an unreachable state suffices for $\Delta Q > 0$ (\texttt{finite\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak dissipates}). The hypothesis is fenced: \texttt{Nat.succ} misses $0$ and is injective, so the implication is false on an infinite phase space, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S19 records this beside the witnesses. The Norton--Shenker distinction survives the identification --- retaining the incoming perturbation is injective and free (\texttt{keepRecord\_\allowbreak injective}), refreshing the register that held it is not (\texttt{absorbStep\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak erasure}). Converting it into a statement about heat requires the postulate $\Delta Q = T \Delta S_{bath}$, which is carried as the class field \texttt{StatisticalMechanics.heat\_\allowbreak eq}; \texttt{Examples.lean} exhibits a one-bit erasure system with an explicit bath that discharges it, so the postulate is demonstrably satisfiable. A register that satisfies the stated erasure and thermal hypotheses dissipates; identifying it with a biological boundary is an additional modelling step.

\section{Structural Resonance and Least Action}
A bound relating environmental and internal distributions must be a physical assumption about specified processes, not a consequence of Gibbs' inequality alone. The class field \texttt{StructuralResonance.kl\_\allowbreak bound} states $D_{KL}(P\|Q)\leq\Delta t\,\sigma$ for an instance. Even under this assumption, descent of $\sigma$ does not imply $D_{KL}\to0$ unless the upper bound vanishes. A positive limiting upper bound permits a positive divergence. No convergence of internal geometry to environmental structure follows.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (Nonpredictive information bounds dissipation):} Let $X_t$ be the system's state and $S_t$ an environmental signal evolving by a dynamics of its own on which the system does not act. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \le I(X_t ; S_t), \qquad W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge k_B T \left[\, I(X_t ; S_t) - I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \,\right],
\end{equation}
the second being the bound of Still, Sivak, Bell and Crooks (\textit{Phys. Rev. Lett.} \textbf{109}, 120604, 2012). Consequently $W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge 0$, and any bound on the dissipated work is a bound on the nonpredictive information $I(X_t;S_t) - I(X_t;S_{t+1})$.

The first inequality is the data processing inequality for the Markov chain $X_t \to S_t \to S_{t+1}$, proved in \texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak PredictiveThermodynamics.lean} as \texttt{predictiveInfo\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak mutualInfo}; mutual information is defined there from Mathlib's \texttt{klDiv} as the divergence of the joint law from the product of its marginals, since Mathlib carries no mutual information of its own. The second inequality is the class field \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.still\_\allowbreak bound}, an irreducible physical postulate for the same reason as every other one here --- its \texttt{axiom} form is refutable, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S18.4 refutes it. Everything downstream is derived: non-negativity of the dissipated work, the bound of the nonpredictive information by the dissipation budget, and the statement that a quasi-static drive leaves every retained bit predictive. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S18 inhabits the class twice over one correlated two-bit law --- a frozen signal, permitted to dissipate nothing, and a scrambled one, forced to dissipate its entire memory --- and fences the theorem with a Markov kernel on the pair that writes the system's state into the signal and thereby \emph{raises} mutual information, so the no-back-action hypothesis is shown to carry weight rather than to decorate. The equality of Kawai, Parrondo and Van den Broeck (\textit{Phys. Rev. Lett.} \textbf{98}, 080602, 2007), $\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}} \rangle = k_B T\, D_{KL}(\mathrm{forward} \,||\, \mathrm{reverse})$, is carried as a predicate on an instance and yields \texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak arrow}: the irreversibility of the dynamics is at least the memory that fails to earn its keep.

The class field in \texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak KLBound.lean} is separate from Gibbs' nonnegativity theorem. The witness \texttt{boolDetuned} attains the assumed bound with $D_{KL}=\sigma=\log2$, showing that a positive divergence is permitted.

The fixed-phase squared-drift functional in \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak ContinuousField.lean} is a quadratic functional of an affine kernel map. The bounded operators \texttt{kernelCLM} and \texttt{continuumDriftCLM}, together with the Hilbert-space adjoint, give $\nabla\sigma=(2/D)A^*(AK+\omega)$. The theorem \texttt{structural\_\allowbreak resonance\_\allowbreak decreases\_\allowbreak sigma\-Continuum} proves descent for the specified gradient flow, with an atomless witness on the unit interval. The identifier \texttt{entropy\_\allowbreak production\_\allowbreak rate} names this squared-drift functional in the source; the name does not establish its identification with thermodynamic entropy production. That interpretation requires probability-current information absent from this calculation.

For a symmetric kernel, antisymmetry of $K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)$ makes its double integral zero. Thus the mean drift is independent of the phase field. A dynamically phase-locked field attains the associated squared-drift lower bound under the stated hypotheses. The theorem \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm} expresses this minimization. A one-way two-site coupling supplies a counterexample without symmetry. These are statements about the objective and its constrained mean, not convergence of joint noisy phase/plasticity dynamics.

\section{Macroscopic Scaling and Kuramoto Phase Synchronization}
We posit a continuous macroscopic coupling description. We model the local phases $\theta_i$ as coupled non-linear oscillators via the Kuramoto model.

\textit{Scope of the continuum description.} \texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement\_\allowbreak convergence} proves convergence of one scalar coupling energy. It produces neither a continuum kernel nor field dynamics: \texttt{edge\_\allowbreak region} has no $M\times M$ product structure from which such a kernel could be recovered, and the vertex-supported candidate is invisible to the continuum functional on an atomless substrate. The later symmetric kernel is therefore a modelling input. Its identification with the brain's endogenous \emph{electromagnetic} field is a further empirical commitment about cortex, argued in the main text from the ephaptic-coupling literature with explicit calibration and observational limitations. Nothing in this section mathematically establishes either step.

For a system with coupling strength $K$ and thermodynamic noise $D$, the dynamic evolution is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\theta_i} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) + \eta_i(t)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem 4 (Stationary coherent branch):} Under the identical-frequency von Mises stationary ansatz, the self-consistency equation has exactly one positive solution when $K>2D$ and none when $K\leq2D$. This does not assert dynamical selection or noiseless phase locking in the stochastic model.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak KuramotoDynamics.lean}):} We formalize the Lyapunov potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$ of the coupled field and prove two directions: \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} (the phase-locked configuration attains the global minimum whenever all couplings are positive) and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} (any minimizer is phase-locked). Note the scope: this is a statement about the \emph{static} potential landscape and holds for all positive coupling matrices. The critical threshold $K_c = 2D$ does not appear in this file. It is formalized separately, in \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak SelfConsistency.lean} (see below), as a statement about the mean-field self-consistency equation with the stationary density assumed; deriving that density from the noise-driven stochastic dynamics would require the spectral theory of the associated Fokker-Planck operator, which is not available in Mathlib. The numerical controls below distinguish identical-frequency diffusion from the separate quenched-frequency model illustrated by \texttt{simulations/kuramoto.py}.

\textit{Positivity, and the regime these theorems cover.} The hypothesis ``all couplings positive'' is not a technical convenience and it is not free. \texttt{KuramotoSystem} carries $A : V \to V \to \mathbb{R}$, symmetric and otherwise unconstrained, so signed --- frustrated --- couplings are representable; but every result in this development that identifies \emph{where} the dynamics ends assumes positivity. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} assume $A_{ij} > 0$; the class field \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.A\_\allowbreak pos} demands it of every instance, and so of every conclusion of Theorem 5; and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} requires the strictly stronger uniform bound $0 < a \le A_{ij}$, since $a$ enters the {\L}ojasiewicz constant. Such a network is an unfrustrated ferromagnet with one minimum up to a global phase shift --- exactly not the near-degenerate landscape a memory argument from geometric frustration requires. By contrast, nothing about the sign of $A$ is used by \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} and its uniqueness counterpart, by the Lyapunov identity \texttt{dV\_\allowbreak dt\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak zero} on which the whole descent argument rests, by \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} and \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}, or by \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}. Since positivity is a \emph{field} of \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} rather than a hypothesis distributed across the development, it enters the chain at one node and not before it. So \emph{that} the motion stops holds for frustrated systems as well; \emph{where} it stops does not. On the continuum side \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm} assumes only symmetry and so admits signed kernels, but it takes the existence of a dynamically phase-locked field as a hypothesis, which is precisely what frustration threatens. The main text scopes the frustration claim out of the chain accordingly, rather than repeating it beside theorems that exclude it.

\textit{Twisted gluing does not recover content from phase frustration (\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak TwistedGluing.lean}).} We also tested the natural weakening of exact overlap agreement: local states may differ by a phase on each overlap. \texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak isCoboundary} proves that coboundary offsets glue after one rotation per patch, while \texttt{isCoboundary\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak gluesUpToPhase} proves the converse when the phase action is free on overlaps. Any Kuramoto configuration, including a twisted or splay state, supplies absolute patch phases $\phi_i$, so its offsets $\phi_i-\phi_j$ are automatically a coboundary (\texttt{isCoboundary\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}) and the twisted family glues (\texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}). The formal \texttt{PhaseObstruction} quotient is deliberately not called \v{C}ech cohomology because its total cochains still assign irrelevant values to patch pairs with empty overlap.

The data give a narrower positive answer. Townsend et al. (2015) identify phase singularities in multielectrode primate LFP recordings by an integer winding number around electrode loops, and the associated complex waves modulate spiking; Xu et al. (2023) find task-dependent spiral centres and rotation directions in human fMRI phase maps. Around such a zero-amplitude point the circle-valued phase need not admit one real-valued lift. Local lifts differ on overlaps by integer multiples of $2\pi$, supplying measured transition data without an arbitrary gauge field. \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak PhaseLifts.lean} formalizes the finite electrode loop: \texttt{loopWinding} sums its integer transitions, \texttt{loopWinding\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} proves that transitions from one global lift telescope to zero, and \texttt{threeElectrodeWinding\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} gives a winding-one witness. This does not rescue \texttt{PhaseObstruction}: that quotient uses circle-valued differences of absolute patch phases, while the measured obstruction uses integer transitions between local lifts.

The cover-level specification has cortical surface $M$, analytic signal $z=Ae^{i\phi}$, singularity set $Z_t$, punctured domain $M^\ast_t=M\setminus Z_t$, and measurement map $q_t=z/|z|:M^\ast_t\to S^1$. A trial-independent electrode or source-space cover of $M^\ast_t$ supplies local real lifts; their differences divided by $2\pi$ are integer transitions on connected overlaps, and nerve loops are the inputs to \texttt{loopWinding}. In finite-resolution data the amplitude threshold defining $Z_t$, the loop geometry and the required cross-scale persistence are fixed using training or baseline data, never the content labels of test trials.

The proposed content-sensitive test compares nested preregistered classifiers. The control model contains stimulus, task, behavioural response, eye movement and arousal; the augmented model adds signed winding, source-localized singularity position, rotation direction and lifetime. Evaluation uses held-out participants and sessions, with label permutations within task, stimulus and arousal strata. The content hypothesis requires replicated incremental out-of-sample discrimination, including under a changed or absent motor report. This controls the known task dependence rather than mistaking it for content. A positive result would be an empirical association, not an identification of a topological sector with phenomenal content; a null increment rejects this proposed role. The construction and test remain outside the conditional chain, and a full nerve-indexed integer complex is deferred until the measurement protocol warrants it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak ContinuousField.lean}):} The continuous-field file defines $K_c=2D$ and a predicate for a substrate above that threshold. Two cautions. First, the predicate is not connected to \texttt{is\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory}, \texttt{entropy\_\allowbreak production\_\allowbreak rate}, or the order parameter of \texttt{Phase4}. What it is connected to is the mean-field self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$, whose bifurcation at $K = 2D$ we formalize in both directions (see the next paragraph): \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak coherent} shows that a substrate satisfying the predicate admits a solution with $0 < r \le 1$, and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak unique\_\allowbreak coherent} that it admits exactly one. So the threshold is not a bare stipulation, but what is proved is a statement about a stationary density rather than about a trajectory. Second, the predicate requires the substrate's \texttt{volume} to be a probability measure, and this hypothesis is load-bearing rather than decorative. It compares \texttt{mean\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak coupling}, the kernel averaged over both arguments, against $2D$; on a substrate of total mass $m$ that average carries a factor $m^2$, so without normalization the comparison tracks the substrate's size rather than its coupling and can be satisfied by any kernel at all. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S9 exhibits a system a factor of two below threshold whose unnormalized double integral nonetheless clears $2D$. Under the hypothesis, \texttt{mean\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak const} gives that a constant kernel has strength exactly $K$ and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak const\_\allowbreak iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$, witnessed in \S9 on both sides of the threshold.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak SelfConsistency.lean}):} The self-consistency equation behind $K_c = 2D$ is formalized as real analysis. With the von Mises stationary density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{a\cos\theta}$, $a = Kr/D$, taken as given (the algebraic step from the stationary Fokker--Planck equation to this density is verified independently at \texttt{simulations/fokker\_\allowbreak planck\_\allowbreak von\_\allowbreak mises.py} in the repository), \texttt{besselRatio} is $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ written as a ratio of interval integrals, Mathlib having no modified Bessel functions. \texttt{vonMisesM\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak mul\_\allowbreak vonMisesS} is the integration-by-parts identity $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta} = a\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, exact because $\sin(\pm\pi) = 0$ kills the boundary term. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak nonneg} proves $I_2(a) \ge 0$ by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ through the reflections $\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$ and $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$, where the integrand becomes a product of two manifestly non-negative factors; \texttt{vonMisesS\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak half\_\allowbreak vonMisesZ} restates it as $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$ and \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak half\_\allowbreak self} as $R(a) \le a/2$. That factor of $1/2$ is exactly what separates a threshold at $D$ from one at $2D$; \texttt{subcritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero} is the crude form obtained from $\sin^2 \le 1$ and is kept to make the point visible. The subcritical theorem is \texttt{subcritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero}$'$: for $0 \le K < \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling } D$ and $D > 0$, the only $r \ge 0$ with $r = R(K,r)$ is $r = 0$. The supercritical theorem is \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak exists}: for $K > \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling } D$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ and $r = R(K,r)$. Its proof needs no series expansion. Factoring $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ with $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the factorization the integration by parts supplies --- it suffices that $E$ is continuous with $E(0) = 1/2$: since $D/K < 1/2$, the map overshoots for small $r > 0$, while \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak one} ($R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$) makes it undershoot at $r = 1$, and \texttt{intermediate\_\allowbreak value\_\allowbreak Icc}$'$ supplies the crossing. Continuity of $E$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral, applied to the jointly continuous integrand. \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak threshold} states the two halves together, and \S9 of the file discharges the hypotheses of each at concrete values ($K = 3$, $D = 1$ above threshold; $K = 3$, $D = 2$ below it), so neither is vacuous. Section 8 of the file identifies the equation with the physics it is named for. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved a probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$ (\texttt{vonMisesDensity\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak one}); \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of \texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; \texttt{circularOrderParameter\_\allowbreak vonMises} proves that average equals $R(a)$, real because \texttt{vonMises\_\allowbreak mean\_\allowbreak sin} gives $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin\theta] = 0$; and \texttt{fixedPoint\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak selfReproducing} states the equivalence: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ exactly when the density it induces has order parameter $r$. \texttt{incoherent\_\allowbreak density\_\allowbreak uniform} identifies the solution $r = 0$ with the uniform density, and \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak density} restates the existence theorem as the existence of a stationary density with positive order parameter. Three results added since sharpen the threshold. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak pos} upgrades the fold argument to a strict inequality for $a > 0$ --- \texttt{foldB} is continuous, non-negative on $[0,\pi/4]$ and strictly positive on its interior, so \texttt{intervalIntegral\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak on} applies --- giving $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] < 1/2$ and $R(a) < a/2$ for every $a > 0$. That closes the threshold case: \texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical} covers all $K \le 2D$, so the incoherent and coherent regimes now exhaust $K \ge 0$ with no gap, and $K = 2D$ falls on the incoherent side. \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq} reformulates the coherent branch: for $r \ne 0$, solving $r = R(K,r)$ is equivalent to $E(Kr/D) = D/K$, so the branch is a level set of $E$. \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold} then proves that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that on $(2D, 2D+\delta)$ every coherent solution satisfies $r < \varepsilon$ --- the branch emerges continuously from zero, excluding a discontinuous jump. Its proof needs no monotonicity of $E$: on a compact interval bounded away from the origin the extreme value theorem gives a maximum strictly below $1/2$, and beyond that the crude bound $E(a) \le 1/a$ suffices. \subsubsection*{Dale-balanced rescue and calibration limits}
Finite simulations separate synaptic balance from the magnitude of fluctuations.
The network has no self-edges, an Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi edge probability of $0.2$,
and excitatory/inhibitory presynaptic fractions of $80/20$.  Each row's positive
and negative weights sum to $g$ and $-g$, respectively.  With identical
frequencies, $D=1$, $N=500$, $g=1$, step size $0.01$ and duration $100$, three
seeded runs give zero-field mean order in
$[\frustrationBaselineMin,\frustrationBaselineMax]$, close to the
$1/\sqrt{N}$ reference.  Controls at $g\in\{0,1,2,4\}$ and
$N\in\{250,500,1000\}$ show why row balance alone is insufficient to prescribe
that reference: signed coupling can amplify finite-size fluctuations.
Order decreases with size in both the weak and strong controls while
$N\langle r^2\rangle$ remains above the independent-phase value of unity;
these finite sizes do not establish an asymptotic scaling law.
The $g=1$ regime is also below the positive-only mean-field threshold, so its
low order does not establish a causal role for inhibition in preventing coherence.

A uniform per-pair term $\epsilon$ adds mean-field strength
$K_{\mathrm{eff}}=N\epsilon$.  The operational crossing, defined by second-half

finite-run variability, not a confidence interval or an infinite-size critical
point.  Equal-duration half-step controls probe numerical sensitivity near the

The field conversion requires an explicit calibration rate $\gamma$:
\[
 E_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}
 {\gamma N_{\mathrm{cortex}}\,\mathrm{shift}\,f}.
\]
Since $\mathrm{shift}\,f$ is dimensionless, $\gamma$ supplies the inverse-time

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak RotatingFrame.lean}):} The potential above omits the natural-frequency term $-\sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$ that appears in the Lyapunov descent result \texttt{dV\_\allowbreak dt\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak zero} in the combinatorial thermodynamics module. The omission is not cosmetic. With any $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak unbounded\_\allowbreak below}), so it has no minimum for a phase-locked state to attain, and the two results concerned different functionals. This file chains them by the standard rotating-frame reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For a system of identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$: \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak rotate} sends a trajectory to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system \texttt{sys.reduced} --- the same system Theorem 5's \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} works with; \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak reduced} shows the two potentials coincide on it; \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak descent} transports the descent identity $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$ onto the potential above; and \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak chain} runs this through the minimum characterization to phase-locking and $r^2 = 1$. Phase differences, phase-locking and the order-parameter magnitude are proved frame-invariant (\texttt{rotate\_\allowbreak sub}, \texttt{is\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak rotate}, \texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak r\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak shift}), so no observable is lost. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on a two-oscillator system, discharging every hypothesis, and exhibits a system whose full potential has no minimum. \textit{Trajectories that run.} The deterministic initial-value problem admits a unique global solution through each initial state. \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak lipschitz} (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) proves the Kuramoto vector field globally Lipschitz with constant $2\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ --- $\sin$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the index set is finite, so there is no smallness caveat and the natural frequencies drop out. \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak unique} turns this into Picard--Lindel\"of uniqueness on all of $\mathbb{R}$: two solutions agreeing at one instant agree forever, in both time directions. Existence follows by constructing solutions on arbitrary finite windows and gluing them using uniqueness. Mathlib's \texttt{IsPicardLindelof} caps a local solution's lifetime by the radius of the ball on which the field's bounds hold, divided by the field's sup norm there, and carries no global-in-time theorem; but \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak norm\_\allowbreak le} bounds this field by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ on the \emph{whole} state space, so the cap can be defeated by enlarging the ball after choosing the window, and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak on\_\allowbreak window} produces a solution on every $(t_0 - T, t_0 + T)$. Uniqueness on an interval makes the family coherent --- solutions on nested windows agree where both are defined --- so $\theta(t) := \alpha_{\lceil|t - t_0|\rceil}(t)$ is a solution on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists}), and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak cauchy\_\allowbreak problem} states existence and uniqueness together as a single \texttt{ExistsUnique}: the trajectory predicate is inhabited exactly once for each initial state, rather than only by the solutions we write down by hand. What existence does not supply is behaviour, and for that a concrete solution is still what we have. On two oscillators with unit coupling and zero natural frequency the phase difference obeys $\dot\Delta = -2\sin\Delta$, whose solution $\Delta(t) = 2\arctan(c\,e^{-2t})$ splits symmetrically into an exact trajectory of the full system (\texttt{Examples.lean}~\S15, \texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak trajectory}). At $c=1$ it starts a quarter turn out of phase (\texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak zero}); the cosine of the phase difference tends to $1$, the order parameter tends to $1$ through the two-oscillator identity $r^2 = (1+\cos\Delta)/2$, and the dynamic potential tends to its global minimum. By the uniqueness result it is the only solution through its initial state. Two general statements describe the long run, both for a zero-frequency system, which by \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak unbounded\_\allowbreak below} is the only case in which the potential is bounded below at all. \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} upgrades the instantaneous descent to monotonicity in $t$ --- \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak deriv\_\allowbreak nonpos} constrains each instant separately and does not by itself say the trajectory settles --- and with the uniform lower bound $-\tfrac12\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ monotone convergence gives \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}: the potential converges along \emph{every} trajectory. Integrating the Lyapunov identity gives the quantitative form, \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak eq}: energy dissipated over $[0,T]$ is exactly the potential dropped, so by \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak tendsto} the improper integral $\int_0^\infty \sum_i \dot\theta_i^2\,dt$ converges, to the total drop. This needed the Lyapunov identity in \texttt{HasDerivAt} form rather than as a statement about \texttt{deriv}, which carries no differentiability information and cannot be fed to the fundamental theorem of calculus. \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak dissipation} transports both to a uniform-frequency system. Finiteness of that integral is not the same as $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$: an integrable function can spike forever on ever narrower intervals. The classical bridge is Barbalat's lemma, which Mathlib does not have, so it is proved here as \texttt{tendsto\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak lipschitz\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak le}: a non-negative Lipschitz function with $\int_0^T g$ bounded above tends to zero, because a spike of height $\varepsilon$ drags a window of width $\varepsilon/2K$ up with it and only finitely many such windows fit under a finite integral. Its hypothesis is then checked for the dissipation rate without differentiating it: the velocities are bounded by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$, the field is Lipschitz in the configuration, and the configuration is Lipschitz in time because its derivative \emph{is} the bounded field (\texttt{trajectory\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}, by the mean value theorem, with no invariant region needed since the bound holds on the whole state space). The conclusion is \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}: along \emph{every} trajectory of a zero-frequency system, $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$; equivalently each $\dot\theta_i \to 0$ and the vector field along the trajectory tends to $0$ in the state space. This is the first asymptotic statement here about an arbitrary trajectory rather than about a witness, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S15 fires it on the relaxing pair, whose velocity starts at $-1$ and is therefore genuinely decaying rather than identically zero. It does not locate the limit. Locating it looks as though it needs the LaSalle invariance principle, which Mathlib does not carry, and a compact invariant set, which the state space $V \to \mathbb{R}$ does not supply; neither is in fact required. LaSalle is what one needs when a decreasing functional is all one has, and here the flow is a gradient flow of the dynamic potential --- $\nabla_i$ of \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak dynamic} is exactly $-\,$\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak velocity} at zero frequencies, which is why \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak hasDerivAt} gives $\dot V = -\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$ as an identity --- whose minimisers are already characterised. \texttt{lojasiewicz\_\allowbreak estimate} adds the missing ingredient: on the region where every pairwise phase difference lies within a quarter turn, $2a^2W \le \left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$, where $W$ is the excess of the potential over its minimum and $a > 0$ is a lower bound for the coupling. Its proof uses no spectral gap and no convexity, only two extremal indices: at the site $m$ carrying the largest phase every coupling term has $\theta_j - \theta_m \in [-D,0]$ with $D \le \pi/2$ the spread, so every term is non-positive, and the term from the site carrying the smallest phase is at most $-a\sin D$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak max\_\allowbreak le}); against $W \le \tfrac12\left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)(1-\cos D)$ the estimate follows from $\sin^2 D = (1-\cos D)(1+\cos D)$ and $\cos D \ge 0$, which is the only place the quarter-turn hypothesis is used. The compactness objection is met without any invariant region put in by hand. Because all terms of the excess are non-negative, a single pair satisfies $\tfrac12 a(1-\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)) \le W$, so an initial excess below $a/2$ --- which \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} preserves --- keeps every pairwise cosine strictly positive for all time. A difference escaping $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ would have to cross $\pi/2$ in absolute value by the intermediate value theorem, and there the cosine vanishes; so the quarter-turn region is forward invariant (\texttt{spread\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak small}). This is branch-pinning by continuity on a connected half-line, and it replaces the quotient by the phase-shift symmetry we had thought necessary. The differential inequality $\dot W \le -cW$ with $c = 2a^2/\sum_{ij}A_{ij}$ then integrates, in the elementary form ``$W(t)e^{ct}$ has non-positive derivative'', to $W(t) \le W(0)e^{-ct}$ (\texttt{excess\_\allowbreak decay}). The rate transfers to the velocity through $|\sin d| \le \sqrt{2(1-\cos d)}$, giving $|\dot\theta_i(t)| \le Be^{-ct/2}$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak abs\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak exp}), which is integrable on $(0,\infty)$; since $\theta_i(t) = \theta_i(0) + \int_0^t\dot\theta_i$, the trajectory converges (\texttt{phase\_\allowbreak tendsto}), and $W \to 0$ forces the limit to be phase-locked, hence a global minimiser with $r^2 = 1$ (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}). \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} transports it to uniform frequencies, where what converges is the relative configuration. The two hypotheses are on the initial data and neither can be removed: splay and twisted configurations are equilibria too, so ``every trajectory reaches the phase-locked state'' is false, and what is proved is the arc condition version --- which the two-oscillator witness satisfies for every $c$, since $2\arctan$ lands in $(-\pi,\pi)$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17 runs the theorem on three sites, where the method of \S15 is unavailable because the system has no closed-form solution: the trajectory is produced by \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} from the initial data $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, which is not phase-locked, and its limit by the convergence theorem as $\theta(0) + \int_0^\infty\dot\theta$; the numerical content of checking the hypotheses is $\cos\tfrac12 > \tfrac34$, supplied by \texttt{Real.cos\_\allowbreak bound}.

\textit{From continuous to discrete coupling:} The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ above is not postulated. A triangulation of the substrate assigns to each edge $(u,v)$ a region of the manifold, and the coupling weight is the Lebesgue--Bochner integral of the scalar magnitude of the stress-energy tensor over that region (\texttt{edge\_\allowbreak weight}); symmetry and non-negativity of $A$ are then proved rather than assumed. The bridge back is mesh refinement: as the regions shrink, the discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_{u,v} \mu(R_{uv})\,f(x_u)$ should converge to the continuous energy $\int_S f$. Stated as a conjecture over the bare triangulation class, that is false: nothing there forces the regions to cover the manifold, empty regions have diameter zero, and the everywhere-empty triangulation refutes it. Stated correctly it is a theorem (\texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement\_\allowbreak convergence}), quantified over a sequence of triangulations of a region $S$ whose regions are measurable, pairwise disjoint across distinct edges, covering, and anchored to the embedded vertices, with mesh size tending to zero. The fixed-mesh error is bounded by $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$, with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and the symmetric double sum is proved equal to the midpoint rule over unordered edges. Two limitations follow. The theorem gives convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate reported by \texttt{simulations/mesh\_\allowbreak refinement.py}. And \texttt{TriangulatedManifold} itself is bare data: it pairs a complex, an embedding and a family of edge regions without relating them, so the geometric conditions live in a separate predicate \texttt{IsRegularTriangulation}. Because those conditions are in tension --- cells disjoint yet covering, every vertex anchored to each of its edges --- \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 exhibits an instance: the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into $N$ half-open cells, on which the convergence theorem is instantiated and the approximations are checked to genuinely change with $N$.

The discretization structure \texttt{DiscreteThermodynamics} \emph{carries} that predicate as a field, which is what makes its edge weights integrals over a geometrically constrained family rather than over arbitrary sets, and three statements follow that were unavailable before. \texttt{weight\_\allowbreak self}: a vertex has no edge to itself, so the coupling matrix has zero diagonal. \texttt{face\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak weight\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak zero}: a nonzero weight $w(u,v)$ forces $\{u,v\}$ to be an actual face of the complex, so the discretization cannot invent couplings between vertices the triangulation does not join. And \texttt{total\_\allowbreak weight\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak setIntegral}: $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) = \int_S |T|$, i.e.\ the total coupling strength is exactly the scalar stress-energy of the region covered --- nothing counted twice (disjointness), nothing dropped (covering). That last identity is the precise sense in which the coupling matrix is derived from the continuum rather than posited, and it is exactly what the everywhere-empty triangulation violates. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 witnesses the strengthened structure on the same grid: each consecutive edge carries weight $1/N$, and the weights sum to $1$ for every $N$. The witness is one-dimensional and its stress-energy tensor is constant, so it establishes satisfiability, not a physically interesting discretization. One field of the structure is deliberately left free and is marked \texttt{[MODELLING]}: \texttt{scalar\_\allowbreak magnitude}, the scalar read off the stress-energy tensor, constrained only to be non-negative. A $(0,2)$ tensor has no canonical scalar --- the trace, an observer's energy density and the tensor norm are different functions, and the structure carries neither a metric nor a frame --- so imposing one would pin a choice rather than derive it, and the three statements above hold for every non-negative choice, so it would prove nothing new. That the choice is nonetheless a physical input is stated as a theorem rather than left as a remark: \S6 exhibits a second instance over the same triangulation, tensor and regions, differing only in that field, for which the partition identity again holds while every edge weight differs (\texttt{gridThermo\_\allowbreak magnitude\_\allowbreak matters}). \subsection{The fold-and-cross proof of strict decrease}
\label{sec:fold}
Uniqueness of the coherent branch is the injectivity of $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ on $(0,\infty)$, and what is proved is the stronger statement that $E$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ (\texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak strictAntiOn}). The main text states the conclusion and the shape of the argument; this is the argument, and it avoids the differentiation route entirely. Differentiating the exponential family in its natural parameter gives $E'(a) = -\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta,\cos\theta)$, which costs differentiation under the integral sign and leaves a covariance that is not sign-definite pointwise: symmetrising with an independent copy gives $\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(X-X')^2(X+X')]$ with $X = \cos\theta$, an integrand changing sign with $X + X'$. Nothing in the proof differentiates anything. The target is kept as the two-point inequality $C_2(a)Z(b) < C_2(b)Z(a)$ for $a < b$, and two moves settle it. \emph{Fold first}: the two reflections that proved $I_2 \ge 0$ carry both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$, replacing $e^{a\cos\theta}$ by $W(a,\theta) = 2\cosh(a\cos\theta)$ and confining $\cos\theta$ to $[0,1]$, after which $\cos 2x - \cos 2y$ and $W(b,x)W(a,y) - W(a,x)W(b,y)$ have the same sign, both being governed by $\cos x$ against $\cos y$. This is the symmetrisation $X \mapsto -X$ that the covariance form needed the tilt to perform, done once at the start; its analytic content is one identity, $2\cosh X\cosh Y = \cosh(X{+}Y)+\cosh(X{-}Y)$ with $\cosh$ increasing in $|\cdot|$. \emph{Then cross}: instead of integrating that inequality over a square, let $\alpha$ be the mean of $\cos 2x$ under $W(a,\cdot)$ and $x_0 \in [0,\pi/2]$ a point where $\cos 2x_0 = \alpha$, supplied by the intermediate value theorem; evaluating at $y = x_0$ and integrating in $x$ alone gives the result, since the term carrying $W(b,x_0)$ vanishes by the choice of $\alpha$. No product measure and no Fubini appear. Run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ the same argument needs no fold --- $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the variable the family is tilted by --- and gives \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the monotonicity of the Bessel ratio, which Mathlib carries in no form.

\section{Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
Phase synchronization does not imply overlap compatibility. The model represents global consistency by gluing compatible local descriptions; its identification with conscious unity remains a separate stipulation.

\textbf{Theorem 5 (Sheaf-Theoretic Unity):} Given a sheaf of probability measures $\mathcal{F}$ over a continuous biological substrate $X$, if local states $P_U \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ agree on all spatial intersections $U \cap V$, they uniquely glue into a Global Section $P_X \in \mathcal{F}(X)$. The overlap agreement is an independent physical hypothesis; phase-locking does not imply it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak GlobalSection.lean}):} The headline gluing theorem separates two inputs. The class field \texttt{Thermodynamic\-Cover.\allowbreak thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} places the phase field at the potential minimum, while \texttt{LocalSection\-Synchronization.\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq} independently requires equal-phase patches to agree on overlaps. Only the second supplies the compatible family consumed by the sheaf theorem; no dynamics in the development derives it. What the second one says can be stated without the sheaf: on a discrete substrate two patch sections have the same restriction to their overlap exactly when they assign the same mass to every site they share (\texttt{restrict\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak densityOn\_\allowbreak eqOn}), so the hypothesis is that patches at a common phase carry a common local density where they meet. It excludes data the rest of the structure admits: a two-patch cover of the three-site substrate at one phase, with profiles differing at the shared site, satisfies every other requirement and fails this one (\texttt{overlap\_\allowbreak agreement\_\allowbreak fails}). It does not require patches to agree away from their overlaps --- the three-patch witness carries three pairwise-different profiles. The equilibrium field is discharged one file over on a class of initial data: \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak Equilibrium\-\allowbreak Bridge.lean} supplies \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.of\-\allowbreak Convergent\-\allowbreak Trajectory} from \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17.1 is a three-patch cover built over the resulting phase field. The cover class also has an independent instance (\texttt{Examples.lean}, \S4), so the theorem is not conditional on a structure of unknown realizability. Its three-site substrate has two overlapping patches meeting at one site; local sections are germ families of the phase-indexed measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ in the sheafification of the probability presheaf. The family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\texttt{phaseMeasure\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak const}), and \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} follows from \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential}. This is a formalized gluing mechanism under an explicit compatibility postulate, not a derivation of compatibility from synchronization. Three results sharpen its scope. First, \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak KuramotoDynamics.lean}) characterizes the minimizers of the reduced potential in both directions, from which \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} follows for every instance. Second, \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13 carries a non-constant phase field and a non-uniform invariant measure, with patch-local densities read from the sections through \texttt{densityOn}; agreement at the shared site is an equation between separately computed numbers. The limit is structural: phase-locking equates phases, while agreement of the associated sections remains the independent class field named above.

The gluing construction assumes local overlap agreement and derives both existence and uniqueness of the global section. The lemma \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique} takes a compatible family to its unique global extension; \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.invariantMeasure} denotes that section. The construction uses local data, not a global state supplied as a premise.

\section{Reflexive Topology and the Self}
To minimize internal thermodynamic friction, the unified Global Section must model its own internal state changes.

\textbf{Theorem 6 (Conditional Auto-Resonance):} On a complete metric space of global sections, a self-prediction map has a unique fixed point if it is Lipschitz at the independently assumed rate $e^{-(K-K_c)\tau/2}<1$. Coherence supplies $K>K_c$; it does not supply the Lipschitz law.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase6\_\allowbreak ReflexiveTopology.lean}):} We formalize the reflexive projection as a contraction on a complete metric space and apply the Banach fixed-point theorem to obtain a unique fixed point --- the ``Self'' as a topological feature rather than a metaphysical addition. The entropy production functional does not appear in this file: the identification of the Banach fixed point with the minimizer of $\sigma$ is an interpretive step made in the main text, and the Lipschitz bound at the mean-field rate is an instance obligation rather than a consequence of field dynamics. Without an instance of \texttt{ReflexiveBoundary} and a metric on \texttt{GlobalSection}, the theorem would be conditional on structures of unknown realizability --- the same failure mode an uninhabited class has. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S10 supplies both on the three-site cortex of \S4, and the metric is built from the measures rather than imposed. Five stages. (i) The germ--measure dictionary: on a discrete substrate every point has a smallest open neighbourhood, so ``mass carried at $x$'' is a well-defined map out of the stalk at $x$ (constructed as the colimit map of a cocone whose cocone law is precisely that restriction preserves point masses); reading a section's germ at each site of an open $U$ gives a map to mass profiles on $U$, and \texttt{massEquivOn} proves it a bijection --- injective because a germ on a discrete space is determined by its restriction to the point, surjective because every prescribed profile is realized by a finite measure on $U$. \texttt{massEquiv} is the case $U = \top$; the general case over an arbitrary open $U$ is what lets Derivation 5's \S14 witness build its patch-local sections over their own patches. Mathlib supplies nothing here: \texttt{TopCat.Presheaf.sheafify} has no adjunction and no \texttt{isIso\_\allowbreak toSheafify}, so the inverse had to be built. (ii) The metric: the distance between two sections is the largest difference between the masses their measures put on a site, i.e.\ the uniform distance between their densities (equivalent to the total variation of the glued measures on a finite substrate, within a factor of the number of sites, but not equal to it). It is complete, proved by exhibiting the limit section of a Cauchy sequence of densities, and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak one} exhibits distinct sections at distance $1/2$. (iii) The Self, built through the avatar. Carrying the predictive map as free data beside the avatar would let a blinded boundary---one whose avatar is replaced by a constant---retain the same predictive model and the same Self, since nothing would tie the map to the avatar. The structure instead carries the avatar's \emph{write} (\texttt{auto\_\allowbreak resonance}) and its \emph{read-out} (\texttt{readout}), and \texttt{predict} is their composite rather than a field, so the self-model runs through the avatar region or it does not exist. On the cortex the map is \texttt{avatarRead} --- the single mass a section over the one-site avatar region carries --- followed by \texttt{avatarReadout}, which returns that mass averaged with the baseline; \texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak dist} gives the exact contraction factor (one half \emph{of what the avatar sees}: the read-out is blind off the avatar region, and that loss is the fold, stated rather than hidden), \texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak const} that it is not constant, \texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak unique} that its fixed point is unique. A map such as \texttt{relax}, averaging the whole field with the baseline, is unavailable as a witness here: it reads every site and so does not factor through a one-site avatar. The $0/1$ metric is retained as \texttt{gsDiscreteMetric} together with \texttt{contracting\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak const}, which proves that under that metric every contraction is constant, so that metric supports no informative fixed point. (iv) The avatar as a constraint. Following the rule that data classes stay bare, the resonance condition is a predicate and not a field: \texttt{IsRestrictionResonance rb} says $\texttt{rb.auto\_\allowbreak resonance}\,s$ is the presheaf restriction of $s$ to the avatar region. Four theorems fix its status. \texttt{eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak avatar\_\allowbreak eq}: if the avatar regions of a family of resonant boundaries cover the substrate, two global states with the same reading at every avatar are equal --- the readings are local, and the global state is reconstructed from them by \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}, the same sheaf condition Derivation 5 uses. \texttt{self\_\allowbreak mem\_\allowbreak range\_\allowbreak readout}: a fixed point lies in the range of the read-out, so the Self is reconstructed from a single section over the avatar region --- the low-dimensional fold, as a theorem. \texttt{self\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak readout\_\allowbreak restrict}: under resonance the Self satisfies $s = \texttt{readout}(s|_{\text{avatar}})$, the field being what its own localized self-encoding reconstructs; this is the statement a structure carrying \texttt{predict} as free data cannot make, since there \texttt{predict} is unrelated to the restriction. And \texttt{not\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak constResonance} is the negative half: the blinded boundary's map is constant, \texttt{constResonance\_\allowbreak existsUnique\_\allowbreak self} names its only fixed point as the state its stipulated contents extend to, and the original Self is not one. The opposite statement, that blinding leaves the Self where it was, does not typecheck against this structure --- a contraction hypothesis about a boundary's own map says nothing about the blinded one's --- and \texttt{constResonance\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak isRestrictionResonance} shows the blinded boundary fails the predicate as soon as two global states differ on the avatar region. On the cortex all of it is exercised: \texttt{cortexReflexive} is built from the restriction and discharges the predicate by \texttt{rfl}; \texttt{cortexReflexive\_\allowbreak restrict\_\allowbreak ne} checks the avatar region separates the silent field from the baseline, so the predicate is not vacuous there; \texttt{siteReflexive} gives one avatar per site, and since the singletons cover, \texttt{cortex\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak avatar\_\allowbreak eq} reconstructs the global state from three local readings; \texttt{cortexBlind\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak ne} proves the baseline is \emph{not} a fixed point of the blinded map, and \texttt{cortexBlind\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak determined} exhibits two distinct states with identical blinded readings. Banach's uniqueness half is \texttt{self\_\allowbreak unique}. (v) The contraction rate, from $K$ and $D$. \texttt{resonanceRate K D } $\tau = \exp(-(K - K_c)\tau/2)$ with $K_c = \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling } D = 2D$ is the linear relaxation factor of the mean-field order parameter over an interval $\tau$. \texttt{resonanceRate\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak one} and \texttt{one\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak resonanceRate} split the parameter plane at Sakaguchi's threshold; \texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak supercritical} derives the unique fixed point from $K > K_c$, and \texttt{Chain.lean}'s \texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak order\_\allowbreak parameter} derives it instead from the \emph{existence} of the coherent order parameter --- a distinction that matters, since $K_c$ is a definition unfolding to $2D$, so the first statement is insensitive to whether the bifurcation is proved and the second is not, and \texttt{not\_\allowbreak contractingWith\_\allowbreak resonanceRate} records that at or below threshold \texttt{ContractingWith} at that rate is false for every self-map of every metric space, so the argument is unavailable rather than merely unproved. The witness sits at $K = 3$, $D = 1$, $\tau = 2\ln 2$, where the rate is exactly the one half the read-out achieves (\texttt{cortexResonanceRate}), and \texttt{cortexSubcritical\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak contracting} runs the negative half on the same map at $K = 1$. Three limitations remain and we state them. The read-out \texttt{avatarReadout} is a modelling choice: no field dynamics in the development produces it, and the Lipschitz bound at the mean-field rate is an instance obligation, not a theorem --- the obligation is stated in the substrate's parameters and its consequence is conditional on the threshold. The identification of this three-site substrate's coupling with $K = 3$ is a choice of units for the witness and not a measurement. Nothing derives the resonance condition either: that a physical avatar performs the restriction is an assumption on an instance, in the same class as the overlap-agreement obligation of Derivation 5. And the Lévy--Prokhorov metric named in the file header is \emph{not} what is constructed: on a discrete substrate the $\varepsilon$-thickening of a set is the set itself for $\varepsilon < 1$, so $\mu \mapsto \tfrac{1}{2}\mu + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_0$ is not a Lévy--Prokhorov contraction there, and Mathlib carries no completeness result for that metric. The uniform distance between densities is the metric this substrate supports; we make no claim that it and Lévy--Prokhorov are equivalent.

\section{Coupling support and continuum observables}
Two mathematical comparisons distinguish a restriction on wiring support from a restriction on the measure used to evaluate a kernel.

\textbf{Theorem 7 (Resource-matched coupling reallocation):} If a fixed wiring support excludes a pair whose phase correlation exceeds the maximum over its wired pairs, a coupling using that pair can achieve greater total correlation at the same resource budget. This is a conditional support comparison, not a general inequality between biological and silicon hardware.

\textit{Coupling reallocation (\texttt{Phase7}):} The lemma \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak better\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak allocation}, wrapped as the theorem \texttt{continuous\_\allowbreak beats\_\allowbreak rigid\_\allowbreak topology}, proves the reallocation step: given a coupling matrix satisfying \texttt{is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} (positive weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists), there is another valid coupling with identical total resources and strictly greater total phase correlation.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase7\_\allowbreak Rigidity.lean}):} Two results specify the scope of the comparison. \emph{(i) Rigidity as a support constraint.} \texttt{RealizableIn S R A} says $A$ is a valid coupling meeting resource budget $R$ and vanishing on every pair the architecture has no wire for --- weights tunable, wiring fixed. \texttt{totalCorrelation\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak realizable} proves no realizable coupling exceeds $R\cdot M$ when $M$ bounds phase correlation over the wired pairs, and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak realizable\_\allowbreak pair} attains it. \texttt{rigid\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} then \emph{derives} \texttt{is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} for every realizable coupling, whenever some pair $(k,l)$ the wiring misses has correlation exceeding $M$: the hypothesis Theorem 7 would otherwise have to assume is a consequence of the wiring pattern. \texttt{rigid\_\allowbreak gap} gives the quantitative form, a gain of at least $R\,(\cos(\theta_l-\theta_k) - M) > 0$ at equal budget. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 witnesses this on three sites wired only to the maximally anti-correlated pair, where the gap is exactly $2$, and runs \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak better\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak allocation} on it with no assumed hypothesis. \emph{(ii) Continuity as a measure-theoretic distinction.} \texttt{fieldCorrelation} integrates a kernel against $\mu\otimes\mu$ as in Derivation 4. A finitely-sited architecture is a \texttt{Finset X}; \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_\allowbreak sited\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero} proves that on an atomless substrate such a kernel contributes exactly zero, for every phase field and every choice of weights, while \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_\allowbreak patchKernel} gives $c\,\mu(U)^2$ for a patch. \texttt{sited\_\allowbreak architecture\_\allowbreak below\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak optimum} combines them; \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 instantiates it on $\mathbb{R}$ with a thousand units of weight at the origin against the unit interval.

\textbf{Scope caveat.} Result (i) is resource-matched and not question-begging, but it turns on fixed wiring support, not on continuity: an architecture wired to the best pair is not beaten. Result (ii) is a genuine type-level separation but is \emph{not} resource-matched, since a measure-zero substrate carries zero resource in the $\mu\otimes\mu$ sense as well; what it establishes is that the continuum coupling energy cannot register a finitely-sited architecture at all, which is a claim about the model's observable rather than about computational power. Neither result formalizes manifold structure or lattice geometry. Neither result establishes that digital hardware cannot approximate field dynamics or support consciousness.

\section{Formal soundness: why a global axiom is the wrong shape for a physical postulate}
\label{sec:soundness}

Formalization is only as trustworthy as the axioms it rests on, and an axiom that is inconsistent silently makes every theorem downstream of it vacuous while leaving the build green. We report a case of exactly this, found by auditing our own development, because the failure mode is easy to reproduce and we have not seen it discussed in the applied-formalization literature.

The failure has one shape, and naming it is more useful than cataloguing its instances: \emph{an axiom that constrains a symbol it does not itself bind}. Such a declaration is not a postulate about one system. It is a constraint on every model of the class the symbol belongs to, including models built to violate it, and a class that has such a model is thereby inconsistent. Three concrete cases, each of which proves \texttt{False} on its own:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{spontaneous\_\allowbreak symmetry\_\allowbreak breaking\_\allowbreak pointwise\_\allowbreak min} took the potential-energy functional as an \emph{implicit} argument occurring only in its hypothesis \texttt{PotentialEnergy phi = V v0}. Instantiating that functional with the constant map $\lambda\,\_\allowbreak .\,V(v_0)$ discharges the hypothesis by \texttt{rfl} and yields the conclusion for an arbitrary field. With $V(v) = v^2$, $v_0 = 0$ and $\phi \equiv 1$ this proves $1 = 0$.
\item \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak eq} equated the free class field \texttt{heat\_\allowbreak dissipation} with a bath-entropy difference \emph{for every instance} of the class. An instance with zero heat dissipation and a bath that gains one bit gives $0 = \log 2$.
\item \texttt{kl\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak axiom} asserted $\sigma \ge D_{KL}(P\|Q)/\Delta t$ for \emph{all} $P$ and $Q$, while $\sigma$ is a single real fixed by a class field. Since $D_{KL}$ is unbounded above, a point mass against a uniform distribution refutes it.
\end{enumerate}

Two further declarations of the same shape are worth recording because their refutation is different in kind. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak invariant\_\allowbreak periodic} and \texttt{sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq}, as global assertions pinning free fields of \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization}, are refutable as soon as the probability presheaf admits two distinct global sections; the refutation needs no arithmetic at all, only that the sheaf is not trivial. Neither is a postulate in the current development: the periodicity condition is an obligation of the constructor that builds a cover from an invariant measure, and \texttt{sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq} is a theorem about the global section the sheaf condition produces.

The rule that prevents it is uniform: \emph{a physical postulate that mentions a class field must be a field of that class, never a standalone axiom quantified over all instances.} A postulate is then an obligation each model discharges, not a global claim about every model. Under that rule the development declares no axioms at all.

\subsection{What the witnesses establish, and what they do not}

Explicit witnesses establish that the class assumptions can be satisfied. \texttt{Examples.lean} therefore constructs witnesses for every class that carries a physical postulate: a one-bit erasure system with a genuine bath that discharges \texttt{heat\_\allowbreak eq} and reproduces $\Delta Q = k_B T \ln 2$; a perfectly resonant system discharging \texttt{kl\_\allowbreak bound}; and a scalar field on a one-point spacetime discharging the action principles. The gluing structures are the most demanding case and are witnessed by a \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} over a three-site substrate carrying the discrete topology and its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, covered by two overlapping open patches whose intersection is a single shared site.

What the witnesses establish is that the classes are inhabitable, so the theorems about them are not vacuous. What they do not establish is that a cortex is such an instance. In the cover above the phase field is already constant --- every patch locked at $\theta = 0$ --- which is precisely what makes the equilibrium obligation dischargeable; getting a physical system into that configuration is the content of the informal dynamical argument.

\subsection{Joint phase and coupling plasticity control}

A finite driven control co-evolves phases and symmetric nonnegative couplings
at fixed total coupling. It uses $N=100$, $D=0.1$, $dt=0.01$, mean row coupling
$4$, and three nearly equal frequency clusters at $0.5$, $1$ and $1.5$ per unit
time. Couplings update every $50$ fast steps with learning rate $0.001$ for
$400$ time units, across three seeds. The symmetric-edge derivative of
$\frac12\sum_i v_i^2$ is $(v_i-v_j)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$; each update clips
negative weights, hollows the diagonal and restores the total resource.
This constrained update does not guarantee monotonic descent during joint noisy dynamics.

Second-half mean order ranges from
$\plasticityOrderMin$ to $\plasticityOrderMax$. The dissipation function

$\plasticityDissipationMin$ to $\plasticityDissipationMax$.
All seeds meet the declared plateau criterion: the last-quarter mean differs

\section{The cortical field identification}

The framework identifies its posited continuum coupling kernel with the endogenous electromagnetic field of mammalian cortex. This is a claim about biology, not a consequence of any theorem above it.

\subsection{Supporting evidence}

Neurons do not only signal through synapses: transmembrane currents generate extracellular fields that shift membrane potentials of neighbouring cells, a mechanism known as ephaptic coupling. Endogenous fields of $1$--$5$ mV/mm entrain spike timing \cite{frohlich2010, anastassiou2015}, and stable electric fields carry information that survives representational drift in the underlying spiking population \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric}. The field is spatially continuous and superposes contributions from large populations --- a medium in which spatially separated activity is combined without further mechanism. This is the integrative role the CEMI field theory assigns it \cite{mcfadden2002, mcfadden2020, pockett2012}, physically supported by astrocytic syncytia joined by gap junctions \cite{verkhratsky2018}.

These observations support a modulatory interaction, but do not determine its integrated strength relative to phase diffusion. Continuity does not itself establish compatible local states or conscious unity. The model requires independent calibration of the coupling kernel, its spatial aggregation and its rate scale.

\subsection{Finite-sheet spatial-decay control}

A finite stochastic control asks whether fixing total coupling while shortening its spatial reach fragments the phase field. On a periodic $128\times128$ sheet spanning 2.0~mm, an exponential kernel was row-normalised to total coupling $K_0=8$ at every decay length; the dynamics used thermodynamic diffusion $0.5$~rad$^2$/s, quenched Gaussian frequency spread $1.5$~rad/s, and 20,000 Euler--Maruyama steps per length. With steady $r>0.2$ as the declared operational coherence criterion, the smallest interpolated decay length above which every larger sampled length remained coherent was \spatialCriticalDecay~mm. The transition-local trajectories were metastable and non-monotone, so that number is an operational boundary rather than a critical-point estimate with statistical precision. At the empirical controls 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3~mm, steady $r$ was respectively \spatialOrderOne, \spatialOrderTwo{} and \spatialOrderThree, and winding-defect density was at most $\spatialDefectMaximum$. Thus this finite, single-seed parameter sweep does not show fragmentation in the empirical decay-length band; its boundary lies about sevenfold below the band's 0.1~mm lower limit. It neither measures cortex nor proves a transition, and it leaves seed dependence and the near-boundary metastability unresolved.

\subsection{Critics}

The longest-standing objection is that the fields are too weak to matter, pressed from within the field-theoretic camp by Pockett herself \cite{pockett2012, pockett2002}. V\"or\"oslakos and colleagues found that applied current is shunted through scalp, delivering fields at or below $1$ V/m in cortex \cite{voroslakos2018}. That is a bound on \emph{applied} rather than endogenous fields, but it constrains how much causal work a field of that magnitude can do, and is the reason we do not claim the field drives spiking.

\subsection{Falsification conditions}

The physical identification would be challenged if independently calibrated field-mediated coupling were insufficient for the proposed regime, or if a selective perturbation of that interaction left the proposed neural and behavioral endpoints unchanged. Such a perturbation requires controls for direct effects on transmembrane currents and other coupling pathways; selectivity is not assumed experimentally available. Recovery tests must distinguish the coupling-gated account from other slow mechanisms under the same observation model. Coherence alone is not equated with report or experience, so dissociations must be evaluated against independently specified endpoints and the additional compatibility and reflexivity hypotheses.

\section{Relationship to other theories}

Integrated information and global-workspace theories supply different descriptions of integration and access \cite{tononi2016,baars1997,dehaene2014}. The present model specifies a candidate physical coupling and a consistency condition on local states. Gluing does not derive an exclusion principle, and continuity alone does not force compatible local descriptions. A digital architecture is not excluded by the sheaf construction. These mathematical differences do not settle which systems have conscious experience.

\section{The sleep-inertia prediction: assumptions, alternatives, and falsifiability}

\subsection{Assumptions}

The effective coupling must depend on extracellular geometry through a separately calibrated relation. The stationary ansatz assumes identical frequencies in a rotating frame and positive phase diffusion. The square-root temporal onset additionally requires a nonzero crossing speed and adiabatic branch tracking, which critical slowing down limits near threshold. The theorem does not prove dynamical selection. Finite-size fluctuations require an operational escape criterion above the fluctuation floor; neither an exactly flat observable foot nor an infinite initial slope is guaranteed.

\subsection{Finite-ramp numerical check}

A finite-$N$ Euler--Maruyama ensemble quantifies the range over which the
adiabatic reading is accurate.  With identical frequencies, $D=1$, $N=2000$,
$32$ seeded replicas and $\dot K=v\in\{10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4}\}$,
escape is defined as the first of three consecutive sampled points at $r\geq
0.2$ after $K_c$.  The fastest leg is right-censored: only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas
escape before $K=2.5$.  All replicas escape at the three slower speeds, with
mean delays $\Delta K=\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$.  Their log--log fit gives
$\Delta K\propto v^{\rampDelayExponent}$, near the predicted exponent $1/2$ at this
resolution.  At fixed $v=10^{-2}$ the delay increases
from $\rampNDelayOne$ through $\rampNDelayTwo$ to $\rampNDelayThree$ as $N$ increases from $500$ through
$2000$ to $8000$, qualitatively agreeing with the predicted $\sqrt{\log N}$
dependence; three sizes are evidence for the trend, not a scaling law.

The early-foot fit $r=A(K-K_0)^{\beta_{\rm eff}}$ over $0.1\leq r\leq0.4$
gives $\beta_{\rm eff}=\rampOnsetOne,\rampOnsetTwo,\rampOnsetThree,\rampOnsetFour$ from fastest to slowest.  Thus
the slow ramps recover the square-root foot, while fast driving pushes the
observable foot toward a linear apparent onset.  A mapping from these dimensionless rates to human recovery times requires an independently calibrated phase diffusion and coupling trajectory. The independently
estimated $(a,r)$ traces remain close to $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ even where the
trajectory lags the stationary branch.  Post-critical RMS deviations are
$\rampCollapseOne,\rampCollapseTwo,\rampCollapseThree,\rampCollapseFour$; using the slowest run as the finite-$N$
floor $\mathrm{Dev}_0$, none reaches $2\mathrm{Dev}_0=\rampCollapseThreshold$.  The specified
critical speed $v^*$ is therefore not bracketed by this sweep.  This numerical
check exhibits finite sampled dynamics only: it proves neither stability nor
dynamical selection and does not discharge the adiabatic assumption.

\subsection{Alternative models and failure conditions}

Exponential relaxation is one comparator, not the entire class of electrical models. Slow latent variables, observation filtering and finite-size effects can also generate delayed or sigmoidal traces. Comparisons should use common preprocessing and observation models, with onset criteria specified before inspecting outcomes. A linear apparent onset counts against the adiabatic branch interpretation only in a regime where its assumptions hold.

The concentration--coherence relation tests the stationary-density model. Its failure over an informative concentration range constrains that model; its success does not identify the coupling medium. The ratio $a/r$ is a consequence of the same density ansatz and is unreliable near the fluctuation floor. A coupling--geometry association requires an independently validated geometry proxy and an independently defined recovery endpoint. Stationary absence of a fold does not rule out finite-rate hysteresis.

\section{Interpretive scope}

The formal endpoint is a globally consistent section fixed by a specified reflexive map. Its identification with phenomenal unity is a philosophical commitment. Neither the mathematical constructions nor the simulations establish which biological or artificial systems have experience. The experimentally assessable content concerns coupling, phase organization, and recovery dynamics.

\section{Empirical test of the (a, r) collapse}
\label{sec:empirical-methods}

This section reports the methods and results of the empirical test of the parameter-free collapse prediction $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ described in the main text. The analysis was performed on a publicly available dataset of human scalp EEG recorded during propofol sedation (OpenNeuro ds005620 \cite{bajwa2025}), comprising 21 subjects with 65-channel recordings at 5 kHz.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

For each recording block, 20--30 seconds of data were downloaded from the public S3 bucket. An additional 3 seconds of padding on each side was downloaded and trimmed after filtering to suppress edge artifacts. Each channel was detrended and bandpass filtered with a 4th-order Butterworth filter in the 4--40 Hz band using second-order sections (\texttt{sosfiltfilt}). The instantaneous phase $\theta_j(t)$ was obtained from the Hilbert transform. Only the 62 scalp EEG channels were used; EOG and EMG channels were discarded.

\subsection{(a, r) estimation}

Each recording was divided into non-overlapping 100 ms bins (500 samples at 5 kHz). The estimand is the pooled spatiotemporal phase distribution over 62 sites and 100~ms, not an instantaneous 62-site spatial distribution. Pooling is required because the independent log-density estimator requires at least 100 phase samples. For each bin $t$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Order parameter $r$:} $r_b = \left|\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M} e^{i\theta_j(t_{bm})}\right|$, where $N=62$ channels and $M=500$ samples per bin. This is the resultant length of the pooled sample, not an average of instantaneous spatial resultant lengths.
\item \textbf{Concentration $a$:} The slope of the log-density of the same phase distribution regressed on $\cos\theta$, as specified in the main text. Phases are centred on their circular mean, binned into 40 bins on $[-\pi,\pi]$, and $\log$ of the binned density (with a $0.5$ pseudocount) is regressed on $\cos\theta$ by \emph{unweighted} ordinary least squares. Under the von Mises model $\log\rho(\theta) = a\cos\theta - \log(2\pi I_0(a))$, so the slope is $a$.
\end{itemize}

The estimator must not be a maximum-likelihood one. The von Mises family is a one-parameter
exponential family whose sufficient statistic is $\sum_j \cos\theta_j$ --- that is, $r$ itself --- so
any ML estimate of the concentration solves its score equation $I_1(\hat a)/I_0(\hat a) = r$ and
returns $\hat a = A^{-1}(r)$ identically. The pair $(\hat a, r)$ would then lie on
$r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ for any input whatsoever, including phases drawn from a distribution that is not
von Mises, and the collapse would be an algebraic identity rather than a test. The implemented regression is unweighted, so it uses the log-density shape rather than defining concentration by inversion of the first trigonometric moment. Other weighting rules are different estimators and are not assumed equivalent to maximum likelihood.

The pseudocount biases the estimator. Against 200 synthetic replicates at this analysis's per-bin sample size ($62$ channels $\times\ 500$
samples $= 31{,}000$ phases, 40 bins), the bias is $\le 0.007$ for $a \le 3$ and reaches $-0.375$ only
at $a = 5$, where the troughs of the distribution empty out and the pseudocount dominates them; the
sampling standard deviation over the same range is $0.008$--$0.048$. These synthetic checks use independent samples; temporal and spatial dependence in EEG can change effective sample size and uncertainty.

The discriminating power of the estimator was verified directly on distributions that are not von
Mises. At the same sample size, a wrapped Cauchy ($\rho = 0.6$), a top-hat arc, and a $50/50$ mixture
of a von Mises with a uniform give residuals $r - I_1(\hat a)/I_0(\hat a)$ of $+0.088$, $-0.117$ and
$+0.075$ respectively, against a worst case of $0.0024$ on genuine von Mises samples --- a separation
of $31.7\times$. A uniform distribution passes, correctly, since it is von Mises at $a = 0$.

\subsection{Bootstrap confidence intervals}

95\% confidence intervals on the mean (a, r) per block were obtained by bootstrap resampling of the time bins (2000 resamples, percentile method). This procedure does not preserve dependence between neighboring bins; its intervals are exploratory rather than a calibrated time-series uncertainty estimate.

\subsection{Narrowband analysis}

The pipeline was run separately on four narrow bands (theta 4--8 Hz, alpha 8--12 Hz, beta 13--30 Hz, gamma 30--40 Hz) and the broad band (4--40 Hz) to test whether the collapse is band-specific.

\subsection{Results}

Residuals are reported per 100 ms bin and then averaged, rather than computed from block-mean $a$ and block-mean $r$; because $I_1/I_0$ is nonlinear, the latter is not the mean residual. The estimator's own noise floor at this sample size is $0.0024$. Window sensitivity gave mean residuals $-0.0261$, $-0.0079$, $+0.0017$, $-0.0019$ and $+0.0001$ at 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100~ms respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:window-sensitivity}); the result is stable over 20--100~ms, but not at 5--10~ms.

The gap between the two montages is the one systematic feature of the results. The common-reference residual is negative in every block and every subject, so the fitted concentration is high relative to the resultant length it accompanies: $r < I_1(a)/I_0(a)$. A common-mode component that simply raised $r$ would push the residual the other way, so that is not the mechanism. The sign instead matches the leptokurtic direction of the synthetic controls above, where a wrapped Cauchy --- peaked at the mode with heavier tails than a von Mises --- gives $-0.117$: a shared reference imposes a sharp common-mode peak on channels that remain broadly distributed around it, which steepens the log-density slope near the mode, and hence the fitted $a$, more than it raises the pooled resultant length. We report this as a candidate mechanism whose direction is consistent with the data, not as a demonstrated one. The bipolar derivation removes the shared component, and the residual falls by roughly an order of magnitude.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The (a, r) collapse to $I_1/I_0$ is consistent with human scalp EEG during propofol sedation, in a bipolar montage and in every frequency band tested. This is a necessary condition for the framework's phase-distribution claim: the von Mises stationary density is compatible with the data.

The strength of that confirmation is limited by the range of $a$ the data occupy, and we state the limit rather than leave it to a referee. Across the eight subjects $a$ spans only $0.302$--$0.542$. Over so short an arc the Bessel ratio is nearly straight: $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ departs from the naive linear form $a/2$ by at most $0.0094$ across that interval, and from $\tanh(a/2)$ by at most $0.0030$, while the per-bin residual scatter is $0.02$--$0.03$. The data are therefore compatible with the proposed relation between $r$ and $a$, but they do not discriminate $I_1/I_0$ from a straight line, and no claim of having done so should be read into them. Discriminating the curve from its tangent needs recordings that reach $a \gtrsim 1.5$, where the two separate by more than $0.17$; an awakening experiment would need to demonstrate that its sampled recovery spans such an informative range.

The result does not test the empirical commitment that the endogenous electromagnetic field is the coupling medium, nor the stipulation that the global section is experience. The coupling-gated awakening prediction remains untested here; these recordings concern propofol sedation rather than recovery from sleep.
