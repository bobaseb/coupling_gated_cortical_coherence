\section*{Overview}
This supplementary document provides the formal mathematical framework underlying the core claims presented in the main manuscript. The theorems below have been formalized and verified using the Lean 4 interactive theorem prover. Formalization ensures that the transitions from foundational physical primitives (symmetries, topology, and thermodynamics) to the complex structural properties of consciousness (boundaries, unity, and reflexivity) are stated precisely rather than metaphorically. It does not by itself make them physically true. The development now declares \emph{no} axioms --- \texttt{\#print axioms} reports only Lean's three foundational ones --- but the physical postulates have not vanished; they have moved into class fields that each instance must discharge, and into the hypotheses of individual theorems. Why a standalone axiom is the wrong shape for a physical postulate, and how such an axiom can prove \texttt{False} while leaving the build green, is the soundness subsection of the main text. Where the Lean statement is weaker than the informal theorem above it, we say so explicitly in the implementation note. The complete Lean 4 source code is publicly available at \url{https://github.com/bobaseb/physical_primitives_of_mind}, and web-accessible documentation is available at \url{https://bobaseb.github.io/physical_primitives_of_mind/}.

\section{Symmetry Breaking and the Emergence of Boundaries}
The framework's first physical motif is that a perfectly symmetric universe cannot support agency. The formal result is narrower: under the stated vacuum and connectedness hypotheses, a field reaching two vacuum components must leave the vacuum somewhere.

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold representing spacetime, and let $\phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{V}$ be a physical field mapping to a vacuum manifold $\mathcal{V}$ (the space of energy minima). Let $G$ be a continuous symmetry group acting on $\mathcal{V}$. 

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Topological Defects from Broken Symmetry):} If the continuous symmetry $G$ is spontaneously broken such that $\mathcal{V} \cong G/H$ (where $H$ is the unbroken subgroup), and the fundamental group $\pi_1(\mathcal{V}) \neq 0$, then field configurations $\phi$ defined on a spatial boundary $S^1 \subset \mathcal{M}$ cannot necessarily be smoothly deformed to a constant vacuum state.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase1\_Primitives.lean}):} We formalize this by defining the set of degenerate energy minima \texttt{DynamicalVacuum} and proving \texttt{boundary\_defect\_forces\_interior\_vacuum\_break}: if a boundary field configuration $\phi_{\partial}$ is not null-homotopic, then it admits no continuous extension over a contractible interior. This geometric obstruction forms the fundamental physical boundary separating an ``inside'' from an ``outside.'' Note the direction: this result establishes that a defect \emph{obstructs extension}, not that a defect must exist. The companion lemma \texttt{contractible\_\allowbreak interior\_\allowbreak forces\_\allowbreak trivial\_\allowbreak boundary} (renamed from \texttt{defect\_inevitability}, whose old name asserted the opposite of its statement) proves the contrapositive.

The direction that \emph{does} produce a defect is now proved, at the level of $\pi_0$. \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak common\_\allowbreak preconnected} states it in the form a witness can discharge --- if no preconnected subset of the vacuum manifold $M$ contains both $\phi(x_1)$ and $\phi(x_2)$, and the substrate is connected, then $\phi$ leaves $M$ somewhere --- and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In} restates it with $\phi(x_2) \notin$ the connected component of $M$ containing $\phi(x_1)$. The proof uses the connectedness of continuous images and \texttt{IsPreconnected.\allowbreak subset\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In}, and nothing else; no hypothesis mentions energy, a potential or a symmetry. This is the domain-wall case of Kibble's classification and we take only that case: $\pi_1 \ne 0$ forcing vortices and $\pi_2 \ne 0$ forcing monopoles are recorded as available and not taken, since the manuscript's argument uses domain walls alone. Theorem~1 as stated above, in terms of $\pi_1$ and a coset space $G/H$, therefore remains \emph{stronger than what is formalized}; what is formalized is its $\pi_0$ analogue, and the gap between them is the higher homotopy, not the mechanism. Separately, \texttt{spontaneous\_symmetry\_breaking} shows a global energy minimiser lies in the vacuum manifold almost everywhere; this is now axiom-free, the pointwise step being \texttt{pointwise\_vacuum\_of\_global\_min}. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11 witnesses that result on the double well $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ over a two-point spacetime carrying a Dirac probability measure. Two features of the witness are deliberate. Its minimisation hypothesis is \emph{proved} rather than assumed --- everywhere else in the development the existence of a global minimiser is a hypothesis, since no compactness or direct-method argument is formalized. And the almost-everywhere in the conclusion is shown necessary: the minimising field sits at the vacuum on the support and at $0$ off it, so the pointwise version of the statement is false of the very field the theorem applies to. The same section instantiates the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$ and discharges the energy invariance, giving the first instance of \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}; the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ is proved degenerate and the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so a minimiser and its image have equal energy and differ. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S11.1 then supplies the witness for the $\pi_0$ theorem on the same well. The vacuum manifold is \emph{computed} there, \texttt{wellVacuum\_eq} proving $\texttt{DynamicalVacuum}\ V = \{-1,1\}$ exactly rather than merely containing both minima --- which matters, because a larger vacuum set might have been connected --- and \texttt{wellVacuum\_separated} proves the disconnection in the form the theorem consumes, by the observation that a preconnected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ spanning $-1$ and $1$ contains $0$, which is not a minimum. \texttt{wellV\_domain\_wall} is the resulting statement about \emph{every} continuous field on \emph{any} connected substrate reaching both minima. Non-emptiness of that class is checked separately rather than assumed: the clipped identity on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies the hypotheses, and \texttt{kink\_zero\_notMem} locates its forced wall at the origin. No conserved quantity is constructed from any of this, and there is still no Noether theorem.

\section{The Thermodynamic Constraint: Landauer's Principle}
Once a boundary is established, it is subjected to external perturbations. Because the internal phase space of any localized physical system is finite, the system must overwrite internal states to adapt. That premise is discharged by theorem rather than by assertion in \texttt{Phase1\_PhaseSpaceCapacity.lean}: a distribution on a finite state space $X$ carries at most $\log|X|$ nats, with equality only at the uniform law (\texttt{shannon\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak log\_\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak iff}), and a perturbation stream of length $k$ over an alphabet $P$ with $|P|^k > |X|$ therefore cannot be recorded injectively (\texttt{absorb\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak injective}), whatever the update map is and even when every individual step of it is reversible.

Let the global phase space $\Omega = \Omega_{sys} \times \Omega_{bath}$ undergo globally reversible Hamiltonian dynamics, modeled as an injective mapping $f: \Omega \to \Omega$.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Thermodynamic Erasure):} If the local transition map on the system $\pi_{sys} \circ f$ is not injective (i.e., multiple initial states map to the same final state, contracting the local phase space), then by the conservation of global phase space volume (Liouville's theorem), the bath phase space must expand, resulting in an irreversible transfer of heat to the environment: $\Delta Q \ge k_B T \ln 2$.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase3\_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}):} We explicitly decouple logical erasure from physical erasure. Lean verifies that any contraction in the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective}(t_{sys})$) under globally reversible dynamics mathematically necessitates heat dissipation. The combinatorial core, \texttt{landauer\_from\_reversibility} and \texttt{second\_law}, is fully derived. Finiteness of the phase space is what converts the physically checkable hypothesis into the one the bound needs: on a finite state space a map that fails to reach every state is not injective (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak erasure\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak surjective}), so an unreachable state suffices for $\Delta Q > 0$ (\texttt{finite\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak dissipates}). The hypothesis is fenced: \texttt{Nat.succ} misses $0$ and is injective, so the implication is false on an infinite phase space, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S19 records this beside the witnesses. The Norton--Shenker distinction survives the identification --- retaining the incoming perturbation is injective and free (\texttt{keepRecord\_\allowbreak injective}), refreshing the register that held it is not (\texttt{absorbStep\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak erasure}). Converting it into a statement about heat requires the postulate $\Delta Q = T \Delta S_{bath}$, which is carried as the class field \texttt{StatisticalMechanics.heat\_eq}; \texttt{Examples.lean} exhibits a one-bit erasure system with an explicit bath that discharges it, so the postulate is demonstrably satisfiable. The boundary is thus forced to operate as a dissipative structure.

\section{Structural Resonance and Least Action}
To avoid catastrophic dissipation, the dissipative structure evolves to minimize entropy production. Let $P(ext)$ be the probability measure of external environmental perturbations, and $Q(int)$ be the internal transition probabilities of the system.

The total entropy production $\sigma$ is bounded from below by the Kullback-Leibler divergence:
\begin{equation}
\sigma \ge \frac{1}{\Delta t} D_{KL}(P(ext) || Q(int))
\end{equation}

\textbf{What the bound above does not support.} One natural reading of it runs: by the Principle of Least Action the system undergoes gradient descent on $\sigma$, forcing $\lim_{t \to \infty} D_{KL}(P || Q) = 0$. That conclusion does not follow. Rearranged it says $D_{KL} \le \Delta t\,\sigma$, so descending on $\sigma$ lowers a \emph{bound} on the divergence; but a driven system settles into a non-equilibrium steady state with $\sigma_{\mathrm{NESS}} > 0$, and the bound then permits any divergence up to $\Delta t\,\sigma_{\mathrm{NESS}}$. The limit is not merely unproved here. It does not follow.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (Nonpredictive information bounds dissipation):} Let $X_t$ be the system's state and $S_t$ an environmental signal evolving by a dynamics of its own on which the system does not act. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \le I(X_t ; S_t), \qquad W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge k_B T \left[\, I(X_t ; S_t) - I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \,\right],
\end{equation}
the second being the bound of Still, Sivak, Bell and Crooks (\textit{Phys. Rev. Lett.} \textbf{109}, 120604, 2012). Consequently $W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge 0$, and any bound on the dissipated work is a bound on the nonpredictive information $I(X_t;S_t) - I(X_t;S_{t+1})$.

The first inequality is the data processing inequality for the Markov chain $X_t \to S_t \to S_{t+1}$, proved in \texttt{Phase3\_PredictiveThermodynamics.lean} as \texttt{predictiveInfo\_le\_mutualInfo}; mutual information is defined there from Mathlib's \texttt{klDiv} as the divergence of the joint law from the product of its marginals, since Mathlib carries no mutual information of its own. The second inequality is the class field \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.still\_bound}, an irreducible physical postulate for the same reason as every other one here --- its \texttt{axiom} form is refutable, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S18.4 refutes it. Everything downstream is derived: non-negativity of the dissipated work, the bound of the nonpredictive information by the dissipation budget, and the statement that a quasi-static drive leaves every retained bit predictive. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S18 inhabits the class twice over one correlated two-bit law --- a frozen signal, permitted to dissipate nothing, and a scrambled one, forced to dissipate its entire memory --- and fences the theorem with a Markov kernel on the pair that writes the system's state into the signal and thereby \emph{raises} mutual information, so the no-back-action hypothesis is shown to carry weight rather than to decorate. The equality of Kawai, Parrondo and Van den Broeck (\textit{Phys. Rev. Lett.} \textbf{98}, 080602, 2007), $\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}} \rangle = k_B T\, D_{KL}(\mathrm{forward} \,||\, \mathrm{reverse})$, is carried as a predicate on an instance and yields \texttt{nonpredictive\_le\_arrow}: the irreversibility of the dynamics is at least the memory that fails to earn its keep.

This is what survives of the claim that ``prediction error minimization'' is a physical thermodynamic attractor: retained information that does not predict is paid for in heat. It is not a derivation of the Free Energy Principle and it does not ground symbols. The information-theoretic KL bound is formalized in \texttt{Phase3\_KLBound.lean} as the class field \texttt{StructuralResonance.kl\_bound} --- an irreducible physical postulate attached to a specific system rather than asserted globally, the earlier \texttt{kl\_bound\_axiom} formulation having been inconsistent. Gibbs' inequality (KL non-negativity) is proved as a genuine theorem from $\log x \le x - 1$; \texttt{structural\_resonance\_bound} restates the axiom in the form $D_{KL} \le \Delta t \cdot \sigma$, and \texttt{discrete\_entropy\_rate\_nonneg} combines the two to derive $\sigma \ge 0$. The gradient-flow half of that reading is formalized against the entropy production functional itself: \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean} \S7 derives the Fr\'echet derivative of $\sigma$ with respect to the coupling kernel, identifies the differentiated functional with \texttt{entropy\_production\_rate} on finite substrates carrying counting measure, and concludes in \texttt{structural\_resonance\_decreases\_entropy\_production} that $\sigma$ is non-increasing along the resulting flow; \texttt{Examples.lean} \S5 exhibits a two-site substrate on which $\sigma$ decays as $e^{-2t}$. The limit $\lim_{t\to\infty} D_{KL} = 0$ is not among the consequences and is claimed nowhere in either document: monotone descent plus the bound $D_{KL} \le \Delta t\,\sigma$ cannot force it, because the descent terminates at a positive steady-state value of $\sigma$. It is tempting to file this as a missing estimate --- a {\L}ojasiewicz or coercivity argument yet to be supplied --- and that would misdescribe it: the gap is in the inference and not in the analysis, so it is closed by replacing the inference, as the theorem above does. The link is also restricted to finite substrates and to a fixed phase field, though the first restriction is narrower than the shape of $\sigma$ suggests. \texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}~\S8 is where that is made precise: $\sigma$ is a quadratic functional of an affine image of the kernel, so \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_quadratic\_of\_affine} differentiates it by the chain rule with no limiting argument, and \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_sigmaOfKernel\_of\_operator} recovers \S7's gradient from that abstract lemma. The continuum case was consequently blocked on exhibiting the drift map as a bounded operator between $L^2$ spaces --- a Cauchy--Schwarz estimate --- rather than on differentiation under the integral sign, and that operator is now built (\texttt{kernelCLM}, \texttt{continuumDriftCLM}, with operator norm at most $\mu(M)^{1/2}$). One further step was needed that the recorded scope had missed: the descent theorems want the derivative as a gradient \emph{vector}, which needs the adjoint, available because $L^2$ is complete. \texttt{hasFDerivAt\_\allowbreak quadratic\_\allowbreak grad} supplies $\nabla\sigma(K) = (2/D)A^{*}(AK+\omega)$ for an arbitrary bounded operator between real Hilbert spaces, so \texttt{structural\_\allowbreak resonance\_\allowbreak decreases\_\allowbreak sigma\-Continuum} is the continuum descent result with no finiteness hypothesis anywhere in it, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S20 runs it on Lebesgue measure restricted to $(0,1)$. A separate result in the same file concerns the target of the descent rather than the descent itself, and it is now unconditional on the class of kernels the framework uses. \texttt{phase\_locked\_achieves\_minimum\_entropy} proved that the phase-locked field minimizes $\sigma$ only among competitors sharing its spatial mean drift, which made the content Jensen's inequality alone; the note recorded that removing the restriction would mean modelling how the mean drift varies with the competitor. It does not. For a symmetric kernel the integrand $K(x,y)\sin(\theta_y-\theta_x)$ is antisymmetric in its two sites, so by Fubini its double integral equals its own negative and vanishes (\texttt{coupling\_integral\_eq\_zero}); the total drift is consequently the same for every phase field (\texttt{total\_drift\_eq\_of\_symm}), the phase-locked configuration fixes its value, and the mean-matching hypothesis is a theorem (\texttt{mean\_drift\_of\_symm}) rather than an assumption. \texttt{phase\_locked\_minimizes\_entropy\_of\_symm} is the resulting statement, quantified over all competitor fields. Symmetry is imposed as a hypothesis rather than a field of \texttt{Continuous\-Neural\-Field}, since the results quantify over instances, and it is load-bearing: \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S16 builds a one-way coupling on the two-site substrate whose phase-locked field is strictly beaten (\texttt{asymSys\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak minimal}, $\sigma = 1/2$ against $1$), with \texttt{asymSys\_mean\_drift\_fails} isolating the mechanism --- there the total drift is not conserved, so the variance bound has nothing to stand on. On the witness side, \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S2 now carries two instances rather than one. The original is perfectly resonant, $D_{KL} = 0$, and so discharges the postulate trivially. The second (\texttt{boolDetuned}) is the one-bit eraser of \S1 driven by a point-mass environment against uniform internal statistics: there $D_{KL} = \log 2 > 0$ and $\sigma = \log 2 > 0$, and \texttt{boolDetuned\_tight} proves the postulate holds with \emph{equality}. The bound is therefore attained and cannot be strengthened to a strict inequality.

\section{Macroscopic Scaling and Kuramoto Phase Synchronization}
As the system scales, it forms a continuous macroscopic field. We model the local phases $\theta_i$ as coupled non-linear oscillators via the Kuramoto model.

\textit{Scope of that sentence.} \texttt{mesh\_refinement\_convergence} proves convergence of one scalar coupling energy. It produces neither a continuum kernel nor field dynamics: \texttt{edge\_region} has no $M\times M$ product structure from which such a kernel could be recovered, and the vertex-supported candidate is invisible to the continuum functional on an atomless substrate. The later symmetric kernel is therefore a modelling input. Its identification with the brain's endogenous \emph{electromagnetic} field is a further empirical commitment about cortex, argued in the main text from the ephaptic-coupling literature and stated with four falsification conditions. Nothing in this section mathematically establishes either step.

For a system with coupling strength $K$ and thermodynamic noise $D$, the dynamic evolution is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\theta_i} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) + \eta_i(t)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem 4 (Critical Phase Transition):} When the integrated spatial coupling $K$ exceeds the thermodynamic noise threshold $K_c = 2D$, the oscillators undergo a sudden phase transition into a synchronized, phase-locked state ($r > 0$).

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_KuramotoDynamics.lean}):} We formalize the Lyapunov potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$ of the coupled field and prove two directions: \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} (the phase-locked configuration attains the global minimum whenever all couplings are positive) and \texttt{potential\_min\_implies\_phase\_locked} (any minimizer is phase-locked). Note the scope: this is a statement about the \emph{static} potential landscape and holds for all positive coupling matrices. The critical threshold $K_c = 2D$ does not appear in this file. It is formalized separately, in \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency.lean} (see below), as a statement about the mean-field self-consistency equation with the stationary density assumed; deriving that density from the noise-driven stochastic dynamics would require the spectral theory of the associated Fokker-Planck operator, which is not available in Mathlib. The threshold is also validated numerically (\texttt{simulations/kuramoto.py}).

\textit{Positivity, and the regime these theorems cover.} The hypothesis ``all couplings positive'' is not a technical convenience and it is not free. \texttt{KuramotoSystem} carries $A : V \to V \to \mathbb{R}$, symmetric and otherwise unconstrained, so signed --- frustrated --- couplings are representable; but every result in this development that identifies \emph{where} the dynamics ends assumes positivity. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} assume $A_{ij} > 0$; the class field \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.A\_pos} demands it of every instance, and so of every conclusion of Theorem 5; and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} requires the strictly stronger uniform bound $0 < a \le A_{ij}$, since $a$ enters the {\L}ojasiewicz constant. Such a network is an unfrustrated ferromagnet with one minimum up to a global phase shift --- exactly not the near-degenerate landscape a memory argument from geometric frustration requires. By contrast, nothing about the sign of $A$ is used by \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} and its uniqueness counterpart, by the Lyapunov identity \texttt{dV\_\allowbreak dt\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak zero} on which the whole descent argument rests, by \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} and \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}, or by \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}. Since positivity is a \emph{field} of \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} rather than a hypothesis distributed across the development, it enters the chain at one node and not before it. So \emph{that} the motion stops holds for frustrated systems as well; \emph{where} it stops does not. On the continuum side \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm} assumes only symmetry and so admits signed kernels, but it takes the existence of a dynamically phase-locked field as a hypothesis, which is precisely what frustration threatens. The main text scopes the frustration claim out of the chain accordingly, rather than repeating it beside theorems that exclude it.

\textit{Twisted gluing does not recover content from phase frustration (\texttt{Phase5\_TwistedGluing.lean}).} We also tested the natural weakening of exact overlap agreement: local states may differ by a phase on each overlap. \texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_of\_isCoboundary} proves that coboundary offsets glue after one rotation per patch, while \texttt{isCoboundary\_of\_gluesUpToPhase} proves the converse when the phase action is free on overlaps. Any Kuramoto configuration, including a twisted or splay state, supplies absolute patch phases $\phi_i$, so its offsets $\phi_i-\phi_j$ are automatically a coboundary (\texttt{isCoboundary\_of\_phaseField}) and the twisted family glues (\texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_of\_phaseField}). The formal \texttt{PhaseObstruction} quotient is deliberately not called \v{C}ech cohomology because its total cochains still assign irrelevant values to patch pairs with empty overlap.

The data give a narrower positive answer. Townsend et al. (2015) identify phase singularities in multielectrode primate LFP recordings by an integer winding number around electrode loops, and the associated complex waves modulate spiking; Xu et al. (2023) find task-dependent spiral centres and rotation directions in human fMRI phase maps. Around such a zero-amplitude point the circle-valued phase need not admit one real-valued lift. Local lifts differ on overlaps by integer multiples of $2\pi$, supplying measured transition data without an arbitrary gauge field. \texttt{Phase5\_PhaseLifts.lean} formalizes the finite electrode loop: \texttt{loopWinding} sums its integer transitions, \texttt{loopWinding\_eq\_zero\_of\_hasGlobalLift} proves that transitions from one global lift telescope to zero, and \texttt{threeElectrodeWinding\_not\_hasGlobalLift} gives a winding-one witness. This does not rescue \texttt{PhaseObstruction}: that quotient uses circle-valued differences of absolute patch phases, while the measured obstruction uses integer transitions between local lifts.

The cover-level specification has cortical surface $M$, analytic signal $z=Ae^{i\phi}$, singularity set $Z_t$, punctured domain $M^\ast_t=M\setminus Z_t$, and measurement map $q_t=z/|z|:M^\ast_t\to S^1$. A trial-independent electrode or source-space cover of $M^\ast_t$ supplies local real lifts; their differences divided by $2\pi$ are integer transitions on connected overlaps, and nerve loops are the inputs to \texttt{loopWinding}. In finite-resolution data the amplitude threshold defining $Z_t$, the loop geometry and the required cross-scale persistence are fixed using training or baseline data, never the content labels of test trials.

The proposed content-sensitive test compares nested preregistered classifiers. The control model contains stimulus, task, behavioural response, eye movement and arousal; the augmented model adds signed winding, source-localized singularity position, rotation direction and lifetime. Evaluation uses held-out participants and sessions, with label permutations within task, stimulus and arousal strata. The content hypothesis requires replicated incremental out-of-sample discrimination, including under a changed or absent motor report. This controls the known task dependence rather than mistaking it for content. A positive result would be an empirical association, not an identification of a topological sector with phenomenal content; a null increment rejects this proposed role. The construction and test remain outside the conditional chain, and a full nerve-indexed integer complex is deferred until the measurement protocol warrants it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_ContinuousField.lean}):} What the continuous-field file does carry is the threshold value itself, \texttt{critical\_coupling D = 2 * D}, and a predicate \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition} for a substrate lying above it. Two cautions. First, the predicate is not connected to \texttt{is\_continuous\_kuramoto\_trajectory}, \texttt{entropy\_production\_rate}, or the order parameter of \texttt{Phase4}. What it is connected to is the mean-field self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$, whose bifurcation at $K = 2D$ we formalize in both directions (see the next paragraph): \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_coherent} shows that a substrate satisfying the predicate admits a solution with $0 < r \le 1$, and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak unique\_\allowbreak coherent} that it admits exactly one. So the threshold is not a bare stipulation, but what is proved is a statement about a stationary density rather than about a trajectory. Second, the predicate requires the substrate's \texttt{volume} to be a probability measure, and this hypothesis is load-bearing rather than decorative. It compares \texttt{mean\_field\_coupling}, the kernel averaged over both arguments, against $2D$; on a substrate of total mass $m$ that average carries a factor $m^2$, so without normalization the comparison tracks the substrate's size rather than its coupling and can be satisfied by any kernel at all. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S9 exhibits a system a factor of two below threshold whose unnormalized double integral nonetheless clears $2D$. Under the hypothesis, \texttt{mean\_field\_coupling\_const} gives that a constant kernel has strength exactly $K$ and \texttt{exhibits\_phase\_transition\_const\_iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$, witnessed in \S9 on both sides of the threshold.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency.lean}):} The self-consistency equation behind $K_c = 2D$ is formalized as real analysis. With the von Mises stationary density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{a\cos\theta}$, $a = Kr/D$, taken as given, \texttt{besselRatio} is $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ written as a ratio of interval integrals, Mathlib having no modified Bessel functions. \texttt{vonMisesM\_eq\_mul\_vonMisesS} is the integration-by-parts identity $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta} = a\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, exact because $\sin(\pm\pi) = 0$ kills the boundary term. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_nonneg} proves $I_2(a) \ge 0$ by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ through the reflections $\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$ and $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$, where the integrand becomes a product of two manifestly non-negative factors; \texttt{vonMisesS\_le\_half\_vonMisesZ} restates it as $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$ and \texttt{besselRatio\_le\_half\_self} as $R(a) \le a/2$. That factor of $1/2$ is exactly what separates a threshold at $D$ from one at $2D$; \texttt{subcritical\_fixed\_point\_eq\_zero} is the crude form obtained from $\sin^2 \le 1$ and is kept to make the point visible. The subcritical theorem is \texttt{subcritical\_fixed\_point\_eq\_zero}$'$: for $0 \le K < \texttt{critical\_coupling } D$ and $D > 0$, the only $r \ge 0$ with $r = R(K,r)$ is $r = 0$. The supercritical theorem is \texttt{supercritical\_fixed\_point\_exists}: for $K > \texttt{critical\_coupling } D$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ and $r = R(K,r)$. Its proof needs no series expansion. Factoring $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ with $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the factorization the integration by parts supplies --- it suffices that $E$ is continuous with $E(0) = 1/2$: since $D/K < 1/2$, the map overshoots for small $r > 0$, while \texttt{besselRatio\_le\_one} ($R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$) makes it undershoot at $r = 1$, and \texttt{intermediate\_value\_Icc}$'$ supplies the crossing. Continuity of $E$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral, applied to the jointly continuous integrand. \texttt{critical\_coupling\_is\_threshold} states the two halves together, and \S9 of the file discharges the hypotheses of each at concrete values ($K = 3$, $D = 1$ above threshold; $K = 3$, $D = 2$ below it), so neither is vacuous. Section 8 of the file identifies the equation with the physics it is named for. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved a probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$ (\texttt{vonMisesDensity\_integral\_eq\_one}); \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; \texttt{circularOrderParameter\_vonMises} proves that average equals $R(a)$, real because \texttt{vonMises\_mean\_sin} gives $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin\theta] = 0$; and \texttt{fixedPoint\_iff\_selfReproducing} states the equivalence: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ exactly when the density it induces has order parameter $r$. \texttt{incoherent\_density\_uniform} identifies the solution $r = 0$ with the uniform density, and \texttt{supercritical\_coherent\_density} restates the existence theorem as the existence of a stationary density with positive order parameter. Three results added since sharpen the threshold. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak pos} upgrades the fold argument to a strict inequality for $a > 0$ --- \texttt{foldB} is continuous, non-negative on $[0,\pi/4]$ and strictly positive on its interior, so \texttt{intervalIntegral\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak on} applies --- giving $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] < 1/2$ and $R(a) < a/2$ for every $a > 0$. That closes the threshold case: \texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical} covers all $K \le 2D$, so the incoherent and coherent regimes now exhaust $K \ge 0$ with no gap, and $K = 2D$ falls on the incoherent side. \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq} reformulates the coherent branch: for $r \ne 0$, solving $r = R(K,r)$ is equivalent to $E(Kr/D) = D/K$, so the branch is a level set of $E$. \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold} then proves that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that on $(2D, 2D+\delta)$ every coherent solution satisfies $r < \varepsilon$ --- the branch emerges continuously from zero, excluding a discontinuous jump. Its proof needs no monotonicity of $E$: on a compact interval bounded away from the origin the extreme value theorem gives a maximum strictly below $1/2$, and beyond that the crude bound $E(a) \le 1/a$ suffices. Section 7 of the file closes uniqueness, which the paragraph above reduced to the injectivity of $E$ on $(0,\infty)$, and closes it in the stronger form \texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak strictAntiOn}: $E$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$. The expected route --- differentiate the exponential family, obtain $E'(a) = -\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta,\cos\theta)$, and show that covariance positive --- is not the route taken, and it was the harder one: it needs differentiation under the integral sign, and the covariance is not sign-definite pointwise, since the symmetrised form $\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(X-X')^2(X+X')]$ with $X = \cos\theta$ has an integrand changing sign with $X+X'$. Instead the target is kept as the two-point inequality $C_2(a)Z(b) < C_2(b)Z(a)$ for $a < b$, and two moves settle it. \emph{Fold first}: \texttt{vonMises\-Z\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak foldW} and \texttt{vonMises\-C2\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak foldA} carry both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$ by the two reflections already used for $I_2 \ge 0$, replacing $e^{a\cos\theta}$ by $W(a,\theta) = 2\cosh(a\cos\theta)$ and confining $\cos\theta$ to $[0,1]$; there \texttt{crossIntegrand\_\allowbreak nonneg} shows $\cos 2x - \cos 2y$ and $W(b,x)W(a,y) - W(a,x)W(b,y)$ have the same sign, both being governed by $\cos x$ against $\cos y$. This is the same $X \mapsto -X$ symmetrisation the covariance form needed, performed once at the start rather than inside a double integral, and its analytic content is one identity: \texttt{cosh\_\allowbreak mul\_\allowbreak cosh\_\allowbreak cross}, i.e.\ $\cosh(ap)\cosh(bq) \le \cosh(bp)\cosh(aq)$ for $0 \le a \le b$, $0 \le q \le p$, from $2\cosh X\cosh Y = \cosh(X{+}Y) + \cosh(X{-}Y)$ and $\cosh$ increasing in $|\cdot|$. \emph{Then cross}: rather than integrating that inequality over a square, \texttt{foldA\_\allowbreak mul\_\allowbreak foldW\_\allowbreak lt} takes $\alpha$ to be the mean of $\cos 2x$ under $W(a,\cdot)$ and $x_0 \in [0,\pi/2]$ a point where $\cos 2x_0 = \alpha$, supplied by \texttt{intermediate\_\allowbreak value\_\allowbreak Icc}$'$; evaluating at $y = x_0$ and integrating in $x$ alone gives the conclusion, the $W(b,x_0)$ term vanishing by the choice of $\alpha$. Strictness comes from \texttt{crossIntegrand\_\allowbreak pos} on a subinterval missing $x_0$. The same crossing argument run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ needs no fold, since $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the tilting variable itself, and gives \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$. The consequences are \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak unique} (at most one positive solution), \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak existsUnique} and \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak solution\_\allowbreak set} (the non-negative solutions are exactly $\{0\}$ at or below threshold and $\{0, r\}$ above it), and \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak strictMono}, which uses both halves: $E$ decreasing puts the concentrations of two couplings in the order $a_1 < a_2$ through $E(a_i) = D/K_i$, and $R$ increasing carries that order to $r_1 < r_2$. Scope: the density is assumed, not derived from the Fokker--Planck equation; $E$ and $R$ are shown strictly monotone but not differentiable, and no rate is proved; dynamical selection is not established, and could not be here, since no theorem in the file mentions a trajectory; and the order parameter reached is that of a density, not of a trajectory --- no theorem relates \texttt{circularOrderParameter} to \texttt{order\_parameter\_complex}, which would be the mean-field limit. Every declaration in the file depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_RotatingFrame.lean}):} The potential above omits the natural-frequency term $-\sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$ that appears in the Lyapunov descent result \texttt{dV\_dt\_le\_zero} of \texttt{Phase3\_CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}. The omission is not cosmetic. With any $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below (\texttt{kuramoto\_potential\_unbounded\_below}), so it has no minimum for a phase-locked state to attain, and the two results concerned different functionals. This file chains them by the standard rotating-frame reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For a system of identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$: \texttt{is\_kuramoto\_trajectory\_rotate} sends a trajectory to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system \texttt{sys.reduced} --- the same system Theorem 5's \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} works with; \texttt{kuramoto\_potential\_reduced} shows the two potentials coincide on it; \texttt{dynamic\_potential\_descent} transports the descent identity $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$ onto the potential above; and \texttt{rotating\_frame\_chain} runs this through the minimum characterization to phase-locking and $r^2 = 1$. Phase differences, phase-locking and the order-parameter magnitude are proved frame-invariant (\texttt{rotate\_sub}, \texttt{is\_phase\_locked\_rotate}, \texttt{order\_parameter\_r\_sq\_shift}), so no observable is lost. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on a two-oscillator system, discharging every hypothesis, and exhibits a system whose full potential has no minimum. Scope: the reduction is exact only for identical frequencies. A spread of frequencies leaves residual detunings $\omega_i - \Omega$ in the reduced system, to which the unboundedness result still applies, and phase-locking then depends on $K_c$, which this file does not treat --- see the \texttt{Phase8\_SelfConsistency.lean} note above for what is proved about the threshold, and note that it concerns a stationary density rather than the trajectories this file transports.

\textit{Trajectories that run.} Descent along a trajectory is worth little if the only trajectory in the development starts at its own limit, which was the case until \S7's rigid rotation was joined by the results below. \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak lipschitz} (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) proves the Kuramoto vector field globally Lipschitz with constant $2\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ --- $\sin$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the index set is finite, so there is no smallness caveat and the natural frequencies drop out. \texttt{is\_kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak unique} turns this into Picard--Lindel\"of uniqueness on all of $\mathbb{R}$: two solutions agreeing at one instant agree forever, in both time directions. Existence is now proved in general, and the gluing that Mathlib does not supply is written out. Mathlib's \texttt{IsPicardLindelof} caps a local solution's lifetime by the radius of the ball on which the field's bounds hold, divided by the field's sup norm there, and carries no global-in-time theorem; but \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak norm\_\allowbreak le} bounds this field by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ on the \emph{whole} state space, so the cap can be defeated by enlarging the ball after choosing the window, and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak on\_\allowbreak window} produces a solution on every $(t_0 - T, t_0 + T)$. Uniqueness on an interval makes the family coherent --- solutions on nested windows agree where both are defined --- so $\theta(t) := \alpha_{\lceil|t - t_0|\rceil}(t)$ is a solution on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (\texttt{is\_kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists}), and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak cauchy\_\allowbreak problem} states existence and uniqueness together as a single \texttt{ExistsUnique}: the trajectory predicate is inhabited exactly once for each initial state, rather than only by the solutions we write down by hand. What existence does not supply is behaviour, and for that a concrete solution is still what we have. On two oscillators with unit coupling and zero natural frequency the phase difference obeys $\dot\Delta = -2\sin\Delta$, whose solution $\Delta(t) = 2\arctan(c\,e^{-2t})$ splits symmetrically into an exact trajectory of the full system (\texttt{Examples.lean}~\S15, \texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak trajectory}). At $c=1$ it starts a quarter turn out of phase (\texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak zero}); the cosine of the phase difference tends to $1$, the order parameter tends to $1$ through the two-oscillator identity $r^2 = (1+\cos\Delta)/2$, and the dynamic potential tends to its global minimum. By the uniqueness result it is the only solution through its initial state. Two general statements about the long run are now proved as well, both for a zero-frequency system, which by \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak unbounded\_\allowbreak below} is the only case in which the potential is bounded below at all. \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} upgrades the instantaneous descent to monotonicity in $t$ --- \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak deriv\_\allowbreak nonpos} constrains each instant separately and does not by itself say the trajectory settles --- and with the uniform lower bound $-\tfrac12\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ monotone convergence gives \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}: the potential converges along \emph{every} trajectory. Integrating the Lyapunov identity gives the quantitative form, \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak eq}: energy dissipated over $[0,T]$ is exactly the potential dropped, so by \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak tendsto} the improper integral $\int_0^\infty \sum_i \dot\theta_i^2\,dt$ converges, to the total drop. This needed the Lyapunov identity in \texttt{HasDerivAt} form rather than as a statement about \texttt{deriv}, which carries no differentiability information and cannot be fed to the fundamental theorem of calculus. \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak dissipation} transports both to a uniform-frequency system. Finiteness of that integral is not the same as $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$: an integrable function can spike forever on ever narrower intervals. The classical bridge is Barbalat's lemma, which Mathlib does not have, so it is proved here as \texttt{tendsto\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak lipschitz\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak le}: a non-negative Lipschitz function with $\int_0^T g$ bounded above tends to zero, because a spike of height $\varepsilon$ drags a window of width $\varepsilon/2K$ up with it and only finitely many such windows fit under a finite integral. Its hypothesis is then checked for the dissipation rate without differentiating it: the velocities are bounded by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$, the field is Lipschitz in the configuration, and the configuration is Lipschitz in time because its derivative \emph{is} the bounded field (\texttt{trajectory\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}, by the mean value theorem, with no invariant region needed since the bound holds on the whole state space). The conclusion is \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}: along \emph{every} trajectory of a zero-frequency system, $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$; equivalently each $\dot\theta_i \to 0$ and the vector field along the trajectory tends to $0$ in the state space. This is the first asymptotic statement here about an arbitrary trajectory rather than about a witness, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S15 fires it on the relaxing pair, whose velocity starts at $-1$ and is therefore genuinely decaying rather than identically zero. It does not locate the limit. Locating it looks as though it needs the LaSalle invariance principle, which Mathlib does not carry, and a compact invariant set, which the state space $V \to \mathbb{R}$ does not supply; neither is in fact required. LaSalle is what one needs when a decreasing functional is all one has, and here the flow is a gradient flow of the dynamic potential --- $\nabla_i$ of \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak dynamic} is exactly $-\,$\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak velocity} at zero frequencies, which is why \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak hasDerivAt} gives $\dot V = -\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$ as an identity --- whose minimisers are already characterised. \texttt{lojasiewicz\_\allowbreak estimate} adds the missing ingredient: on the region where every pairwise phase difference lies within a quarter turn, $2a^2W \le \left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$, where $W$ is the excess of the potential over its minimum and $a > 0$ is a lower bound for the coupling. Its proof uses no spectral gap and no convexity, only two extremal indices: at the site $m$ carrying the largest phase every coupling term has $\theta_j - \theta_m \in [-D,0]$ with $D \le \pi/2$ the spread, so every term is non-positive, and the term from the site carrying the smallest phase is at most $-a\sin D$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak max\_\allowbreak le}); against $W \le \tfrac12\left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)(1-\cos D)$ the estimate follows from $\sin^2 D = (1-\cos D)(1+\cos D)$ and $\cos D \ge 0$, which is the only place the quarter-turn hypothesis is used. The compactness objection is met without any invariant region put in by hand. Because all terms of the excess are non-negative, a single pair satisfies $\tfrac12 a(1-\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)) \le W$, so an initial excess below $a/2$ --- which \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} preserves --- keeps every pairwise cosine strictly positive for all time. A difference escaping $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ would have to cross $\pi/2$ in absolute value by the intermediate value theorem, and there the cosine vanishes; so the quarter-turn region is forward invariant (\texttt{spread\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak small}). This is branch-pinning by continuity on a connected half-line, and it replaces the quotient by the phase-shift symmetry we had thought necessary. The differential inequality $\dot W \le -cW$ with $c = 2a^2/\sum_{ij}A_{ij}$ then integrates, in the elementary form ``$W(t)e^{ct}$ has non-positive derivative'', to $W(t) \le W(0)e^{-ct}$ (\texttt{excess\_\allowbreak decay}). The rate transfers to the velocity through $|\sin d| \le \sqrt{2(1-\cos d)}$, giving $|\dot\theta_i(t)| \le Be^{-ct/2}$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak abs\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak exp}), which is integrable on $(0,\infty)$; since $\theta_i(t) = \theta_i(0) + \int_0^t\dot\theta_i$, the trajectory converges (\texttt{phase\_\allowbreak tendsto}), and $W \to 0$ forces the limit to be phase-locked, hence a global minimiser with $r^2 = 1$ (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}). \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} transports it to uniform frequencies, where what converges is the relative configuration. The two hypotheses are on the initial data and neither can be removed: splay and twisted configurations are equilibria too, so ``every trajectory reaches the phase-locked state'' is false, and what is proved is the arc condition version --- which the two-oscillator witness satisfies for every $c$, since $2\arctan$ lands in $(-\pi,\pi)$. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17 runs the theorem on three sites, where the method of \S15 is unavailable because the system has no closed-form solution: the trajectory is produced by \texttt{is\_kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} from the initial data $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, which is not phase-locked, and its limit by the convergence theorem as $\theta(0) + \int_0^\infty\dot\theta$; the numerical content of checking the hypotheses is $\cos\tfrac12 > \tfrac34$, supplied by \texttt{Real.cos\_bound}.

\textit{From the continuous field to the discrete model (\texttt{Phase2\_SimplicialBridge.lean}, \texttt{Phase2\_MeshConvergence.lean}):} The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ above is not postulated. A triangulation of the substrate assigns to each edge $(u,v)$ a region of the manifold, and the coupling weight is the Lebesgue--Bochner integral of the scalar magnitude of the stress-energy tensor over that region (\texttt{edge\_weight}); symmetry and non-negativity of $A$ are then proved rather than assumed. The bridge back is mesh refinement: as the regions shrink, the discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_{u,v} \mu(R_{uv})\,f(x_u)$ should converge to the continuous energy $\int_S f$. Stated as a conjecture over the bare triangulation class, that is false: nothing there forces the regions to cover the manifold, empty regions have diameter zero, and the everywhere-empty triangulation refutes it. Stated correctly it is a theorem (\texttt{mesh\_refinement\_convergence}), quantified over a sequence of triangulations of a region $S$ whose regions are measurable, pairwise disjoint across distinct edges, covering, and anchored to the embedded vertices, with mesh size tending to zero. The fixed-mesh error is bounded by $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$, with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and the symmetric double sum is proved equal to the midpoint rule over unordered edges. Two limitations are worth stating. The theorem gives convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate reported by \texttt{simulations/mesh\_refinement.py}. And \texttt{TriangulatedManifold} itself is bare data: it pairs a complex, an embedding and a family of edge regions without relating them, so the geometric conditions live in a separate predicate \texttt{IsRegularTriangulation}. Because those conditions are in tension --- cells disjoint yet covering, every vertex anchored to each of its edges --- \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 exhibits an instance: the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into $N$ half-open cells, on which the convergence theorem is instantiated and the approximations are checked to genuinely change with $N$.

The discretization structure \texttt{DiscreteThermodynamics} now \emph{carries} that predicate as a field, which is what makes its edge weights integrals over a geometrically constrained family rather than over arbitrary sets, and three statements follow that were unavailable before. \texttt{weight\_self}: a vertex has no edge to itself, so the coupling matrix has zero diagonal. \texttt{face\_of\_weight\_ne\_zero}: a nonzero weight $w(u,v)$ forces $\{u,v\}$ to be an actual face of the complex, so the discretization cannot invent couplings between vertices the triangulation does not join. And \texttt{total\_weight\_eq\_setIntegral}: $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) = \int_S |T|$, i.e.\ the total coupling strength is exactly the scalar stress-energy of the region covered --- nothing counted twice (disjointness), nothing dropped (covering). That last identity is the precise sense in which the coupling matrix is derived from the continuum rather than posited, and it is exactly what the everywhere-empty triangulation violates. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S6 witnesses the strengthened structure on the same grid: each consecutive edge carries weight $1/N$, and the weights sum to $1$ for every $N$. The witness is one-dimensional and its stress-energy tensor is constant, so it establishes satisfiability, not a physically interesting discretization. One field of the structure is deliberately left free and is now marked \texttt{[MODELLING]}: \texttt{scalar\_magnitude}, the scalar read off the stress-energy tensor, constrained only to be non-negative. A $(0,2)$ tensor has no canonical scalar --- the trace, an observer's energy density and the tensor norm are different functions, and the structure carries neither a metric nor a frame --- so imposing one would pin a choice rather than derive it, and the three statements above hold for every non-negative choice, so it would prove nothing new. That the choice is nonetheless a physical input is stated as a theorem rather than left as a remark: \S6 exhibits a second instance over the same triangulation, tensor and regions, differing only in that field, for which the partition identity again holds while every edge weight differs (\texttt{gridThermo\_\allowbreak magnitude\_\allowbreak matters}). The remaining constraint, non-negativity, is the discrete shadow of an energy condition and is what makes the induced couplings a legal Kuramoto coupling.

\subsection{The fold-and-cross proof that $E$ is strictly decreasing}
\label{sec:fold}
Uniqueness of the coherent branch is the injectivity of $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ on $(0,\infty)$, and what is proved is the stronger statement that $E$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ (\texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak strictAntiOn}). The main text states the conclusion and the shape of the argument; this is the argument, and it avoids the route we had expected to need. Differentiating the exponential family in its natural parameter gives $E'(a) = -\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta,\cos\theta)$, which costs differentiation under the integral sign and leaves a covariance that is not sign-definite pointwise: symmetrising with an independent copy gives $\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(X-X')^2(X+X')]$ with $X = \cos\theta$, an integrand changing sign with $X + X'$. Nothing in the proof differentiates anything. The target is kept as the two-point inequality $C_2(a)Z(b) < C_2(b)Z(a)$ for $a < b$, and two moves settle it. \emph{Fold first}: the two reflections that proved $I_2 \ge 0$ carry both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$, replacing $e^{a\cos\theta}$ by $W(a,\theta) = 2\cosh(a\cos\theta)$ and confining $\cos\theta$ to $[0,1]$, after which $\cos 2x - \cos 2y$ and $W(b,x)W(a,y) - W(a,x)W(b,y)$ have the same sign, both being governed by $\cos x$ against $\cos y$. This is the symmetrisation $X \mapsto -X$ that the covariance form needed the tilt to perform, done once at the start; its analytic content is one identity, $2\cosh X\cosh Y = \cosh(X{+}Y)+\cosh(X{-}Y)$ with $\cosh$ increasing in $|\cdot|$. \emph{Then cross}: instead of integrating that inequality over a square, let $\alpha$ be the mean of $\cos 2x$ under $W(a,\cdot)$ and $x_0 \in [0,\pi/2]$ a point where $\cos 2x_0 = \alpha$, supplied by the intermediate value theorem; evaluating at $y = x_0$ and integrating in $x$ alone gives the result, since the term carrying $W(b,x_0)$ vanishes by the choice of $\alpha$. No product measure and no Fubini appear. Run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ the same argument needs no fold --- $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the variable the family is tilted by --- and gives \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the monotonicity of the Bessel ratio, which Mathlib carries in no form.

\section{Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
Phase synchronization alone is mathematically insufficient for conscious unity; the synchronized local fields must be topologically glued together.

\textbf{Theorem 5 (Sheaf-Theoretic Unity):} Given a sheaf of probability measures $\mathcal{F}$ over a continuous biological substrate $X$, if local states $P_U \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ agree on all spatial intersections $U \cap V$, they uniquely glue into a Global Section $P_X \in \mathcal{F}(X)$. The overlap agreement is an independent physical hypothesis; phase-locking does not imply it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase5\_GlobalSection.lean}):} The headline gluing theorem separates two inputs. The class field \texttt{Thermodynamic\-Cover.\allowbreak thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} places the phase field at the potential minimum, while \texttt{LocalSection\-Synchronization.\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq} independently requires equal-phase patches to agree on overlaps. Only the second supplies the compatible family consumed by the sheaf theorem; no dynamics in the development derives it. The equilibrium field is discharged one file over on a class of initial data: \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak Equilibrium\-\allowbreak Bridge.lean} supplies \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.of\-\allowbreak Convergent\-\allowbreak Trajectory} from \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}, and \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S17.1 is a three-patch cover built over the resulting phase field. The cover class also has an independent instance (\texttt{Examples.lean}, \S4), so the theorem is not conditional on a structure of unknown realizability. Its three-site substrate has two overlapping patches meeting at one site; local sections are germ families of the phase-indexed measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ in the sheafification of the probability presheaf. The family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\texttt{phaseMeasure\_not\_const}), and \texttt{thermodynamic\_equilibrium} follows from \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential}. This is a formalized gluing mechanism under an explicit compatibility postulate, not a derivation of compatibility from synchronization. Three results sharpen its scope. First, \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak KuramotoDynamics.lean}) characterizes the minimizers of the reduced potential in both directions, from which \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} follows for every instance. Second, \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S13 carries a non-constant phase field and a non-uniform invariant measure, with patch-local densities read from the sections through \texttt{densityOn}; agreement at the shared site is an equation between separately computed numbers. The limit is structural: phase-locking equates phases, while agreement of the associated sections remains the independent class field named above.

Third, and this one changed the theorem rather than the witnesses. As stated until 2026-08-30, Theorem 5 in Lean was uniqueness of the global state given the local data, not emergence of a global state from it: the class field \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak invariant\_\allowbreak measure} handed each instance a section over $\top$, and \texttt{sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq} declared each local section to be a restriction of it, so the existence half was assumed and the sheaf condition only excluded competitors. Both fields have been removed. \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization} now constrains its local data only by \texttt{section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq} --- patches at a common phase agree where they overlap --- in which every symbol ranges over one patch or one overlap. The sheaf property is isolated as \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}, a lemma with no physics in it, which takes a compatible family to the unique section over $\top$ restricting to it; \texttt{Thermodynamic\-Cover.\allowbreak invariant\-Measure} is defined as that section, and the statement that every local section is its restriction is a theorem about it, \texttt{Thermodynamic\-Cover.\allowbreak sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq}. An instance may still be built the other way round, from a global measure, through the constructor \texttt{Local\-Section\-Synchronization.\allowbreak of\-Invariant\-Measure}, and \S4 and \S13 are built through it --- proof that the weaker class is genuinely weaker, and the reason those two witnesses glue to the measure they started from. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S14 is built without it: the two patches carry independently chosen mass profiles $(2,1,7)$ and $(5,1,3)$, agreeing only at the shared site where both put mass $1$, and the section the gluing returns has profile $(2,1,3)$. \section{Reflexive Topology and the Self}
To minimize internal thermodynamic friction, the unified Global Section must model its own internal state changes.

\textbf{Theorem 6 (Conditional Auto-Resonance):} On a complete metric space of global sections, a self-prediction map has a unique fixed point if it is Lipschitz at the independently assumed rate $e^{-(K-K_c)\tau/2}<1$. Coherence supplies $K>K_c$; it does not supply the Lipschitz law.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase6\_ReflexiveTopology.lean}):} We formalize the reflexive projection as a contraction on a complete metric space and apply the Banach fixed-point theorem to obtain a unique fixed point --- the ``Self'' as a topological feature rather than a metaphysical addition. The entropy production functional does not appear in this file: the identification of the Banach fixed point with the minimizer of $\sigma$ is an interpretive step made in the main text, and the Lipschitz bound at the mean-field rate is an instance obligation rather than a consequence of field dynamics. Without an instance of \texttt{ReflexiveBoundary} and a metric on \texttt{GlobalSection}, the theorem would be conditional on structures of unknown realizability --- the same failure mode an uninhabited class has. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S10 supplies both on the three-site cortex of \S4, and the metric is built from the measures rather than imposed. Five stages. (i) The germ--measure dictionary: on a discrete substrate every point has a smallest open neighbourhood, so ``mass carried at $x$'' is a well-defined map out of the stalk at $x$ (constructed as the colimit map of a cocone whose cocone law is precisely that restriction preserves point masses); reading a section's germ at each site of an open $U$ gives a map to mass profiles on $U$, and \texttt{massEquivOn} proves it a bijection --- injective because a germ on a discrete space is determined by its restriction to the point, surjective because every prescribed profile is realized by a finite measure on $U$. \texttt{massEquiv} is the case $U = \top$; the general case was added on 2026-08-30 and is what lets Derivation 5's \S14 witness build its patch-local sections over their own patches. Mathlib supplies nothing here: \texttt{TopCat.Presheaf.sheafify} has no adjunction and no \texttt{isIso\_toSheafify}, so the inverse had to be built. (ii) The metric: the distance between two sections is the largest difference between the masses their measures put on a site, i.e.\ the uniform distance between their densities (equivalent to the total variation of the glued measures on a finite substrate, within a factor of the number of sites, but not equal to it). It is complete, proved by exhibiting the limit section of a Cauchy sequence of densities, and \texttt{exists\_dist\_lt\_one} exhibits distinct sections at distance $1/2$. (iii) The Self, built through the avatar (rebuilt 2026-08-31). Until that pass \texttt{ReflexiveBoundary} carried the predictive map as free data beside the avatar, and \texttt{reflexive\_topology\_implies\_self} projected that map out without ever touching the avatar; the consequence was a theorem, \texttt{self\_of\_constResonance}, stating that replacing the avatar by a constant left a legal boundary with the same predictive model and the same Self. The structure now carries the avatar's \emph{write} (\texttt{auto\_resonance}) and its \emph{read-out} (\texttt{readout}), and \texttt{predict} is their composite rather than a field, so the self-model runs through the avatar region or it does not exist. On the cortex the map is \texttt{avatarRead} --- the single mass a section over the one-site avatar region carries --- followed by \texttt{avatarReadout}, which returns that mass averaged with the baseline; \texttt{cortexPredict\_dist} gives the exact contraction factor (one half \emph{of what the avatar sees}: the read-out is blind off the avatar region, and that loss is the fold, stated rather than hidden), \texttt{cortexPredict\_not\_const} that it is not constant, \texttt{cortexPredict\_fixed\_unique} that its fixed point is unique. The previous witness \texttt{relax}, which averaged the whole field with the baseline, could not be kept: it reads every site and so does not factor through a one-site avatar. The earlier $0/1$-metric witness is kept as \texttt{gsDiscreteMetric}, together with \texttt{contracting\_implies\_const}, which proves that under that metric every contraction is constant --- the record of why the earliest witness said nothing. (iv) The avatar as a constraint. Following the rule that data classes stay bare, the resonance condition is a predicate and not a field: \texttt{IsRestrictionResonance rb} says $\texttt{rb.auto\_resonance}\,s$ is the presheaf restriction of $s$ to the avatar region. Four theorems fix its status. \texttt{eq\_of\_avatar\_eq}: if the avatar regions of a family of resonant boundaries cover the substrate, two global states with the same reading at every avatar are equal --- the readings are local, and the global state is reconstructed from them by \texttt{probability\_glue\_unique}, the same sheaf condition Derivation 5 uses. \texttt{self\_mem\_range\_readout}: a fixed point lies in the range of the read-out, so the Self is reconstructed from a single section over the avatar region --- the low-dimensional fold, as a theorem. \texttt{self\_eq\_readout\_restrict}: under resonance the Self satisfies $s = \texttt{readout}(s|_{\text{avatar}})$, the field being what its own localized self-encoding reconstructs; this is the statement a structure carrying \texttt{predict} as free data cannot make, since there \texttt{predict} is unrelated to the restriction. And \texttt{not\_self\_of\_constResonance} is the negative half: the blinded boundary's map is constant, \texttt{constResonance\_existsUnique\_self} names its only fixed point as the state its stipulated contents extend to, and the original Self is not one. The opposite statement, that blinding leaves the Self where it was, does not typecheck against this structure --- a contraction hypothesis about a boundary's own map says nothing about the blinded one's --- and \texttt{constResonance\_not\_isRestrictionResonance} shows the blinded boundary fails the predicate as soon as two global states differ on the avatar region. On the cortex all of it is exercised: \texttt{cortexReflexive} is built from the restriction and discharges the predicate by \texttt{rfl}; \texttt{cortexReflexive\_restrict\_ne} checks the avatar region separates the silent field from the baseline, so the predicate is not vacuous there; \texttt{siteReflexive} gives one avatar per site, and since the singletons cover, \texttt{cortex\_eq\_of\_avatar\_eq} reconstructs the global state from three local readings; \texttt{cortexBlind\_self\_ne} proves the baseline is \emph{not} a fixed point of the blinded map, and \texttt{cortexBlind\_not\_determined} exhibits two distinct states with identical blinded readings. Banach's uniqueness half, which the original statement did not carry, is \texttt{self\_unique}. (v) The contraction rate, from $K$ and $D$. \texttt{resonanceRate K D } $\tau = \exp(-(K - K_c)\tau/2)$ with $K_c = \texttt{critical\_coupling } D = 2D$ is the linear relaxation factor of the mean-field order parameter over an interval $\tau$. \texttt{resonanceRate\_lt\_one} and \texttt{one\_le\_resonanceRate} split the parameter plane at Sakaguchi's threshold; \texttt{self\_of\_supercritical} derives the unique fixed point from $K > K_c$, and \texttt{Chain.lean}'s \texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak order\_\allowbreak parameter} derives it instead from the \emph{existence} of the coherent order parameter --- a distinction that matters, since $K_c$ is a definition unfolding to $2D$, so the first statement is insensitive to whether the bifurcation is proved and the second is not, and \texttt{not\_contractingWith\_resonanceRate} records that at or below threshold \texttt{ContractingWith} at that rate is false for every self-map of every metric space, so the argument is unavailable rather than merely unproved. The witness sits at $K = 3$, $D = 1$, $\tau = 2\ln 2$, where the rate is exactly the one half the read-out achieves (\texttt{cortexResonanceRate}), and \texttt{cortexSubcritical\_not\_contracting} runs the negative half on the same map at $K = 1$. Three limitations remain and we state them. The read-out \texttt{avatarReadout} is a modelling choice: no field dynamics in the development produces it, and the Lipschitz bound at the mean-field rate is an instance obligation, not a theorem --- what changed is that the obligation is now stated in the substrate's parameters and its consequence is conditional on the threshold. The identification of this three-site substrate's coupling with $K = 3$ is a choice of units for the witness and not a measurement. Nothing derives the resonance condition either: that a physical avatar performs the restriction is an assumption on an instance, in the same class as the overlap-agreement obligation of Derivation 5. And the Lévy--Prokhorov metric named in the file header is \emph{not} what is constructed: on a discrete substrate the $\varepsilon$-thickening of a set is the set itself for $\varepsilon < 1$, so $\mu \mapsto \tfrac{1}{2}\mu + \tfrac{1}{2}\mu_0$ is not a Lévy--Prokhorov contraction there, and Mathlib carries no completeness result for that metric. The uniform distance between densities is the metric this substrate supports; we make no claim that it and Lévy--Prokhorov are equivalent.

\section{Topological Rigidity of von Neumann Architectures}
Finally, we compare the continuous coupling matrix $A(x,y)$ of biological fields with the discrete, rigid routing matrix $G_{ij}$ of standard artificial hardware (e.g., GPUs).

\textbf{Theorem 7 (Hardware Thermodynamics):} Because a continuous parameter space contains global minima inaccessible to a discrete lattice under identical thermodynamic constraints, the dynamical potential of a biological neural field is strictly lower than that of rigid silicon hardware: $V(continuous) < V(discrete)$.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase7\_Hardware\-Comparison.lean}):} The lemma \texttt{exists\_better\_coupling\_allocation}, wrapped as the theorem \texttt{continuous\_beats\_rigid\_topology}, proves the reallocation step: given a coupling matrix satisfying \texttt{is\_strictly\_suboptimal} (positive weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists), there is another valid coupling with identical total resources and strictly greater total phase correlation.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase7\_Rigidity.lean}):} Two defects of the above are addressed here. \emph{(i) Rigidity as a support constraint.} \texttt{RealizableIn S R A} says $A$ is a valid coupling meeting resource budget $R$ and vanishing on every pair the architecture has no wire for --- weights tunable, wiring fixed. \texttt{totalCorrelation\_le\_of\_realizable} proves no realizable coupling exceeds $R\cdot M$ when $M$ bounds phase correlation over the wired pairs, and \texttt{exists\_realizable\_pair} attains it. \texttt{rigid\_is\_strictly\_suboptimal} then \emph{derives} \texttt{is\_strictly\_suboptimal} for every realizable coupling, whenever some pair $(k,l)$ the wiring misses has correlation exceeding $M$: the hypothesis Theorem 7 would otherwise have to assume is a consequence of the wiring pattern. \texttt{rigid\_gap} gives the quantitative form, a gain of at least $R\,(\cos(\theta_l-\theta_k) - M) > 0$ at equal budget. \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 witnesses this on three sites wired only to the maximally anti-correlated pair, where the gap is exactly $2$, and runs \texttt{exists\_better\_coupling\_allocation} on it with no assumed hypothesis. \emph{(ii) Continuity as a measure-theoretic distinction.} \texttt{fieldCorrelation} integrates a kernel against $\mu\otimes\mu$ as in Derivation 4. A finitely-sited architecture is a \texttt{Finset X}; \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_sited\_eq\_zero} proves that on an atomless substrate such a kernel contributes exactly zero, for every phase field and every choice of weights, while \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_patchKernel} gives $c\,\mu(U)^2$ for a patch. \texttt{sited\_architecture\_below\_field\_optimum} combines them; \texttt{Examples.lean}~\S8 instantiates it on $\mathbb{R}$ with a thousand units of weight at the origin against the unit interval.

\textbf{Scope caveat.} Result (i) is resource-matched and not question-begging, but it turns on fixed wiring support, not on continuity: an architecture wired to the best pair is not beaten. Result (ii) is a genuine type-level separation but is \emph{not} resource-matched, since a measure-zero substrate carries zero resource in the $\mu\otimes\mu$ sense as well; what it establishes is that the continuum coupling energy cannot register a finitely-sited architecture at all, which is a claim about the model's observable rather than about computational power. Neither result formalizes manifold structure or lattice geometry. Accordingly, the inference to ``von Neumann architectures cannot experience unified consciousness'' remains an informal argument in the main text, now with two verified steps rather than one.
