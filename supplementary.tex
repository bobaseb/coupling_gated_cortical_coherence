\section{Symmetry Breaking and the Emergence of Boundaries}
A boundary is one of the physical structures the broader composition assumes. Under the stated vacuum and connectedness hypotheses, a field reaching two vacuum components must leave the vacuum somewhere. Relating that locus to a finite information-processing register is a separate physical premise; the result supplies neither a general condition for agency nor a derivation of the cortical coupling model.

Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold representing spacetime, and let $\phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{V}$ be a physical field mapping to a vacuum manifold $\mathcal{V}$ (the space of energy minima). Let $G$ be a continuous symmetry group acting on $\mathcal{V}$. 

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Domain-wall obstruction):} A continuous field on a connected substrate whose image reaches two different connected components of the vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere. This is the $\pi_0$ case of the topological-defect argument. A separate extension obstruction states that non-null-homotopic boundary data admit no continuous extension over the specified contractible interior.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase1\_\allowbreak Primitives.lean}):} We formalize this by defining the set of degenerate energy minima \texttt{DynamicalVacuum} and proving \texttt{boundary\_\allowbreak defect\_\allowbreak forces\_\allowbreak interior\_\allowbreak vacuum\_\allowbreak break}: if a boundary field configuration $\phi_{\partial}$ is not null-homotopic, then it admits no continuous extension over a contractible interior. This is an obstruction to extending the specified boundary data. Note the direction: this result establishes that a defect \emph{obstructs extension}, not that a defect must exist. The companion lemma \texttt{contractible\_\allowbreak interior\_\allowbreak forces\_\allowbreak trivial\_\allowbreak boundary}  proves the contrapositive.

A direction that produces a defect is proved, at the level of $\pi_0$. \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak common\_\allowbreak preconnected} states it in the form a witness can discharge --- if no preconnected subset of the vacuum manifold $M$ contains both $\phi(x_1)$ and $\phi(x_2)$, and the substrate is connected, then $\phi$ leaves $M$ somewhere --- and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak notMem\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In} restates it with $\phi(x_2) \notin$ the connected component of $M$ containing $\phi(x_1)$. The proof uses the connectedness of continuous images and \texttt{IsPreconnected.\allowbreak subset\_\allowbreak connected\-Component\-In}, and nothing else; no hypothesis mentions energy, a potential or a symmetry. This is the domain-wall case of Kibble's classification and we take only that case: $\pi_1 \ne 0$ forcing vortices and $\pi_2 \ne 0$ forcing monopoles are recorded as available and not taken, since the manuscript's argument uses domain walls alone.

Separately, \texttt{spontaneous\_\allowbreak symmetry\_\allowbreak breaking} shows that a global energy minimiser lies in the vacuum manifold almost everywhere. The proof introduces no additional axioms and uses \texttt{pointwise\_\allowbreak vacuum\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak min}.

\texttt{Examples}~\S11 witnesses that result on the double well $V(v) = (v^2-1)^2$ over a two-point spacetime carrying a Dirac probability measure. Two features of the witness are deliberate. Its minimisation hypothesis is \emph{proved} rather than assumed --- everywhere else in the development the existence of a global minimiser is a hypothesis, since no compactness or direct-method argument is formalized. And the almost-everywhere in the conclusion is shown necessary: the minimising field sits at the vacuum on the support and at $0$ off it, so the pointwise version of the statement is false of the very field the theorem applies to. The same section instantiates the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ action $v \mapsto \pm v$ and discharges the energy invariance, giving the first instance of \texttt{SymmetryInvariantAction}; the vacuum manifold $\{-1,1\}$ is proved degenerate and the two minima exchanged by the symmetry, so a minimiser and its image have equal energy and differ.

\texttt{Examples}~\S11.1 then supplies the witness for the $\pi_0$ theorem on the same well. The vacuum manifold is \emph{computed} there, \texttt{wellVacuum\_\allowbreak eq} proving $\texttt{DynamicalVacuum}\ V = \{-1,1\}$ exactly rather than merely containing both minima --- which matters, because a larger vacuum set might have been connected --- and \texttt{wellVacuum\_\allowbreak separated} proves the disconnection in the form the theorem consumes, by the observation that a preconnected subset of $\mathbb{R}$ spanning $-1$ and $1$ contains $0$, which is not a minimum. \texttt{wellV\_\allowbreak domain\_\allowbreak wall} is the resulting statement about \emph{every} continuous field on \emph{any} connected substrate reaching both minima. Non-emptiness of that class is checked separately rather than assumed: the clipped identity on $\mathbb{R}$ satisfies the hypotheses, and \texttt{kink\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak notMem} locates its forced wall at the origin. These results concern vacuum membership and domain walls; they construct no conserved quantity.

\section{The Thermodynamic Constraint: Landauer's Principle}
For a finite effective register, the number of distinguishable histories that can be retained is bounded. This does not imply that every update erases. The capacity theorem states that a distribution on a finite state space $X$ carries at most $\log|X|$ nats, with equality only at the uniform law (\texttt{shannon\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak log\_\allowbreak card\_\allowbreak iff}), and a perturbation stream of length $k$ over an alphabet $P$ with $|P|^k > |X|$ therefore cannot be recorded injectively (\texttt{absorb\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak injective}), whatever the update map is and even when every individual step of it is reversible.

Let the global phase space $\Omega = \Omega_{sys} \times \Omega_{bath}$ undergo globally reversible Hamiltonian dynamics, modeled as an injective mapping $f: \Omega \to \Omega$.

\textbf{Theorem 2 (Thermodynamic Erasure):} Under the specified finite-register and reversible system--bath assumptions, the heat identity gives a lower bound proportional to the entropy erased by the register. Erasing one unbiased bit gives $\Delta Q\geq k_BT\ln2$. Non-injectivity alone does not specify a universal one-bit entropy loss; the bound uses the erased entropy of the particular update.

\textit{The same heat, in three vocabularies (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak LandauerBridge.lean}).} Derivation 2 charges a register for destroying information and Derivation 3 charges a structure for keeping information it cannot use. \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.\allowbreak ofLandauer} builds the predictive structure of a given register: its thermal scale is that register's temperature, its dissipated work is that register's Landauer heat, and Still's bound is derived from \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak bound} rather than assumed a second time. The single hypothesis is the identification $\left(I_{\mathrm{mem}} - I_{\mathrm{pred}}\right) \le S(\mathrm{id}) - S(t)$ --- the memory wasted is no larger than the entropy erased --- and it is a restriction, not bookkeeping: on an injective update the right-hand side is zero and the inequality forces the waste to vanish (\texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak injective}), which is the Norton--Shenker distinction in informational form. The measure-theoretic statement of Landauer (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak Measure\-Thermodynamics}) is specialised in the same file rather than left standing beside the combinatorial one: at counting measure on a finite register, \texttt{continuous\_\allowbreak entropy} is \texttt{boltzmann\_\allowbreak entropy}, volume contraction is non-injectivity, and \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak bound} is $T$ times the entropy erased (\texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak count\_\allowbreak eq}). The witness is the one-bit eraser, where a register destroying $\log 2$ pays for a memory wasting $\log 2$, with equality (\texttt{Examples}~\S18.5).

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}):} We explicitly decouple logical erasure from physical erasure. Lean verifies that any contraction in the \textit{physical} state space ($\neg \text{Function.Injective}(t_{sys})$) under globally reversible dynamics mathematically necessitates heat dissipation. The combinatorial core, \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak reversibility} and \texttt{second\_\allowbreak law}, is fully derived. Finiteness of the phase space is what converts the physically checkable hypothesis into the one the bound needs: on a finite state space a map that fails to reach every state is not injective (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak erasure\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak surjective}), so an unreachable state suffices for $\Delta Q > 0$ (\texttt{finite\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak space\_\allowbreak dissipates}). The hypothesis is fenced: \texttt{Nat.succ} misses $0$ and is injective, so the implication is false on an infinite phase space, and \texttt{Examples}~\S19 records this beside the witnesses. The Norton--Shenker distinction survives the identification --- retaining the incoming perturbation is injective and free (\texttt{keepRecord\_\allowbreak injective}), refreshing the register that held it is not (\texttt{absorbStep\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak erasure}). Converting it into a statement about heat requires the postulate $\Delta Q = T \Delta S_{bath}$, which is carried as the class field \texttt{StatisticalMechanics.heat\_\allowbreak eq}; \texttt{Examples}~\S1 exhibits a one-bit erasure system with an explicit bath that discharges it, so the postulate is demonstrably satisfiable. A register that satisfies the stated erasure and thermal hypotheses dissipates; identifying it with a biological boundary is an additional modelling step.

\section{Structural Resonance and Least Action}
A bound relating environmental and internal distributions must be a physical assumption about specified processes, not a consequence of Gibbs' inequality alone. The class field \texttt{StructuralResonance.kl\_\allowbreak bound} states $D_{KL}(P\|Q)\leq\Delta t\,\sigma$ for an instance. Even under this assumption, descent of $\sigma$ does not imply $D_{KL}\to0$ unless the upper bound vanishes. A positive limiting upper bound permits a positive divergence. No convergence of internal geometry to environmental structure follows.

\textbf{Theorem 3 (Nonpredictive information bounds dissipation):} Let $X_t$ be the system's state and $S_t$ an environmental signal evolving by a dynamics of its own on which the system does not act. Then
\begin{equation}
I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \le I(X_t ; S_t), \qquad W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge k_B T \left[\, I(X_t ; S_t) - I(X_t ; S_{t+1}) \,\right],
\end{equation}
the second being the bound of \cite{still2012}. Consequently $W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge 0$, and any bound on the dissipated work is a bound on the nonpredictive information $I(X_t;S_t) - I(X_t;S_{t+1})$.

The first inequality is the data processing inequality for the Markov chain $X_t \to S_t \to S_{t+1}$, proved in \texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak PredictiveThermodynamics.lean} as \texttt{predictiveInfo\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak mutualInfo}; mutual information is defined there from Mathlib's \texttt{klDiv} as the divergence of the joint law from the product of its marginals, since Mathlib carries no mutual information of its own. The second inequality is the class field \texttt{PredictiveDissipation.still\_\allowbreak bound}, an irreducible physical postulate for the same reason as every other one here --- its \texttt{axiom} form is refutable, and \texttt{Examples}~\S18.4 refutes it. Everything downstream is derived: non-negativity of the dissipated work, the bound of the nonpredictive information by the dissipation budget, and the statement that a quasi-static drive leaves every retained bit predictive. \texttt{Examples}~\S18 inhabits the class twice over one correlated two-bit law --- a frozen signal, permitted to dissipate nothing, and a scrambled one, forced to dissipate its entire memory --- and fences the theorem with a Markov kernel on the pair that writes the system's state into the signal and thereby \emph{raises} mutual information, so the no-back-action hypothesis is shown to carry weight rather than to decorate. The equality of \cite{kawai2007}, $\langle W_{\mathrm{diss}} \rangle = k_B T\, D_{KL}(\mathrm{forward} \,||\, \mathrm{reverse})$, is carried as a predicate on an instance and yields \texttt{nonpredictive\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak arrow}: the irreversibility of the dynamics is at least the memory that fails to earn its keep.

\subsection{Agency and the scope of the passive bound}
\label{sec:agency}

The stochastic channels of \texttt{Agency} (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak Agency.lean}) specify one perception--action cycle:
\begin{align*}
 A_t&\sim\pi(\cdot\mid X_t),&
 S_{t+1}&\sim K(\cdot\mid S_t,A_t),\\
 O_{t+1}&\sim G(\cdot\mid S_{t+1}),&
 X_{t+1}&\sim U(\cdot\mid X_t,A_t,O_{t+1}).
\end{align*}
The sampled action is retained through actuation and supplied to the memory update. The history used for predictive information records $(X_t,S_t,S_{t+1})$, keeping memory at its pre-action state. Its present marginal is the input law and its future marginal is the post-action world paired with that pre-action memory. The environmental state must be sufficient for the declared Markov dynamics; an observation alone need not satisfy this condition.

\texttt{Feedback.information\_\allowbreak balance} proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:feedback-information} from the existing KL-based mutual information. Conditional information uses the chain-rule expression $I(X;(S,S'))-I(X;S')$ or $I(X;(S,S'))-I(X;S)$, respectively. Data processing proves both nonnegative under finite joint information, and \texttt{mutualInfo\_\allowbreak finite} discharges that condition on finite discrete spaces, including laws with zero atoms. Real subtraction preserves the sign of their difference. The passive constructor retains the present signal while evolving it through its own kernel; \texttt{passive\_\allowbreak feedback\_\allowbreak zero} and \texttt{passive\_\allowbreak signedWaste} recover the passive information statements exactly. The witness \texttt{writingAgent} selects its own bit as the action, writes it into an initially independent world bit, senses the result and updates its memory. Its signed difference is strictly negative, and its composed cycle equals the explicit write kernel. Conditional dependence identifies causal influence only with an adequate causal model.

\subsection{A finite thermodynamic model with feedback}

For a finite controller--world law $p(x,s)$ and a normalized channel $K_x(s'\mid s)$, one elementary update holds $x$ fixed. The final law and the forward and reverse path laws are
\begin{align*}
 p'(x,s')&=\sum_s p(x,s)K_x(s'\mid s),\\
 P_F(x,s,s')&=p(x,s)K_x(s'\mid s),\\
 P_R(x,s,s')&=p'(x,s')K_x(s\mid s').
\end{align*}
Both path laws are normalized. The reverse uses the same autonomous transition channel, started at the forward final distribution. \texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep.entropy\_\allowbreak balance} assumes strictly positive initial masses and transition probabilities. \texttt{entropy\_\allowbreak balance\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak support} assumes only that every forward path of positive probability has positive reverse probability, which is what the expansion uses and which strict positivity implies (\texttt{reversibleSupport\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak positive}). Time-dependent protocols and unidirectional transitions require a corresponding treatment of reversal and support.

\texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep.entropy\_\allowbreak balance}
(\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak AgencyThermodynamics.lean}) expands the path log-ratio to obtain
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma=D_{KL}(P_F\|P_R)
 =H(p')-H(p)+\sum_{x,s,s'}P_F(x,s,s')
       \log\frac{K_x(s'\mid s)}{K_x(s\mid s')}\ge0.
\end{equation}
Gibbs' inequality proves nonnegativity; the balance is derived from these distributions. \texttt{ProbDist.KL\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak klDiv} identifies the finite divergence with Mathlib's measure-theoretic divergence for a positive reference law. The information identity uses the same forward law and its proven initial and final marginals.

Support inclusion is the weaker of the two conditions and the one the identity
needs. Gibbs' inequality holds under it (\texttt{KL\_\allowbreak nonneg\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak support}),
so zero masses in the initial law or in a transition are permitted and $\Sigma\ge0$
survives them; this matters because an agent's executed channels are sparse, and
a step whose initial law sits on a single atom satisfies the support condition
while failing positivity. What the weaker condition does not do is make every
channel priceable. A forward path with no reverse --- deterministic erasure is
the standard case --- has infinite divergence, and
\texttt{ProbDist.extendedKL} records it as such in $[0,\infty]$ rather than
returning the finite number the totalized real logarithm would supply. Where
support does hold, that extended value is the real divergence above.
\texttt{ProbDist.extendedKL\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak klDiv} and
\texttt{extendedKL\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak klDiv\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak missing}
(\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak MeasureFeedback.lean}) identify the extended value
with Mathlib's measure divergence on both branches --- the finite one under a
positive reference law and the infinite one wherever a forward atom has no
reverse --- so the finite convention here is the measure-theoretic divergence
restricted to finite spaces, not a second definition standing beside it.

Local detailed balance is the physical identification
$q(x,s,s')=k_BT\log[K_x(s'\mid s)/K_x(s\mid s')]$, with heat positive into the reservoir \cite{horowitz2014,ito2013}. It gives
$\langle Q\rangle\ge k_BT[H(p)-H(p')]$.
The first-law hypothesis $w=E(x,s')-E(x,s)+q$ separately fixes work. These are bounds on the joint controller--world system. The passive signed information difference alone is insufficient to supply a general work bound. \texttt{Chain.active\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak budget} gives the scalar budget consequence of \texttt{ActiveBound}, the fourth node of the active composition.

\textit{Complete finite perception--action update.}
\texttt{FiniteFeedbackCycle}, in the same module, carries an actuation step and a memory channel $U_t(y\mid x)$. Its sensing step starts at $p_1^{\mathrm{swap}}(t,x)=p_1(x,t)$, where
\begin{equation}
 p_1(x,t)=\sum_s p_0(x,s)K_x(t\mid s),\qquad
 p_2(y,t)=\sum_x p_1(x,t)U_t(y\mid x).
\end{equation}
The second initial law is constructed from the first final law. The theorem
\texttt{cycle\_law} identifies $p_2$ with the output of the composed
\texttt{Agency.cycle} channels, with action $a=x$ and observation $o=t$.
Strictly positive $p_0,K,U$ give positive substep path laws. At a common
thermal scale $\theta=k_BT>0$, local detailed balance for each channel yields
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_A+\Sigma_M
 =H(p_2)-H(p_0)+\frac{\langle Q_A\rangle+\langle Q_M\rangle}{\theta}\ge0.
 \label{eq:finite-update-balance}
\end{equation}
The intermediate entropy cancels because exchanging coordinates preserves
joint entropy. No intermediate or final law is assumed stationary.
\texttt{entropy\_budget} therefore gives
$H(p_0)-H(p_2)\le B/\theta$ whenever
$\langle Q_A\rangle+\langle Q_M\rangle\le B$.

With one energy observable $E(x,s)$, the separate first-law hypotheses are
$w_A(x,s,t)=E(x,t)-E(x,s)+q_A(x,s,t)$ and
$w_M(t,x,y)=E(y,t)-E(x,t)+q_M(t,x,y)$.
\texttt{mean\_first\_law} cancels the same intermediate expected energy and proves
\begin{equation}
 \langle W_A\rangle+\langle W_M\rangle
 =\mathbb E_{p_2}E-\mathbb E_{p_0}E
   +\langle Q_A\rangle+\langle Q_M\rangle.
\end{equation}
These results cover the two autonomous channel substeps with action and
observation aliases. Extra physical registers, time-dependent control and
learning require specified dynamics and costs. These balances supply no upper
budget; the register ledger below allocates one.

\textit{Reciprocal two-bit witness.} In units $k_BT=1$, let the interaction energy be zero for $x=s$ and $\log3$ for $x\ne s$. Each substep redraws one bit, agreeing with the fixed other bit with probability $3/4$. Actuation updates $s$ using action $a=x$; sensing updates $x$ using observation $o=s$. The action and observation are aliases of existing states and require no separate storage register. Their physical content is the specified pair interaction and thermal updates. The alignment preference is supplied by that interaction.

Starting from independent uniform bits, actuation produces probability $3/8$ on each agreeing pair and $1/8$ on each disagreeing pair. Both marginals remain uniform. The subsequent sensing step preserves this joint law while allowing individual memory transitions. In \texttt{Examples/AgencyThermodynamics.lean}, the exact actuation heat is $(\log3)/4$ and entropy production is $\log2-(\log3)/2>0$; the sensing step has zero mean heat and entropy production. Each substep satisfies detailed balance and the first law with zero external work. The positive actuation heat comes from a decrease in mean interaction energy. The example accounts for relaxation of an initially nonequilibrium law; a maintained supply of useful work or a learning process would require additional dynamics and resources.

\texttt{Examples/AgencyCycle.lean} specializes the general composition, total
heat budget and first law to these same channels and laws. A biased initial
memory law gives an asymmetric intermediate law and a sensing step that changes
the joint distribution, with positive actuation heat. The regression
\texttt{mismatched\_\allowbreak intermediate\_\allowbreak rejected} excludes a
second step initialized without the required coordinate swap; the symmetric
equilibrium law alone cannot detect that error. The reciprocal cycle also
rejects a zero total heat budget.

\textit{Finite goal-directed control.}
\texttt{FiniteControlProblem}, in the same module, supplies a common
initial law $p_0$, world channel $K_a(t\mid s)$, memory channel $U_t(y\mid x)$
and terminal reward $r(y,t)$. A deterministic policy $a=\pi(x)$ induces the
complete update above with $K_x=K_{\pi(x)}$. The theorem \texttt{cycle\_law}
identifies its final law with the channels carrying this explicit policy.
Action remains a function of existing memory, and observation is the world
state. No stochastic action paths are discarded before calculating heat.

The declared reservoir model assigns
$q_A^\pi(x,s,t)=\theta\log[K_{\pi(x)}(t\mid s)/K_{\pi(x)}(s\mid t)]$
and $q_M(t,x,y)=\theta\log[U_t(y\mid x)/U_t(x\mid y)]$.
Their thermal interpretation is the local-detailed-balance modelling input.
The performance $J(\pi)=\sum_{y,t}p_2^\pi(y,t)r(y,t)$ and total mean heat
$Q(\pi)=\langle Q_A^\pi\rangle+\langle Q_M\rangle$ use the same process,
including its policy-dependent intermediate law. For a finite permitted set
$\Pi$ with at least one feasible member, \texttt{exists\_optimal} proves
\begin{equation}
 \exists\pi_*\in\Pi:\quad Q(\pi_*)\le B,\qquad
 J(\pi)\le J(\pi_*)\quad\text{for every }\pi\in\Pi\text{ with }Q(\pi)\le B.
 \label{eq:finite-control}
\end{equation}
Neither strict improvement nor a task objective follows from existence alone.

\texttt{Examples/AgencyControl.lean} supplies a lamp task with all four
deterministic bit policies permitted. Initially $X$ is uniform and independent
of a lamp $S$ with $\Pr(S=1)=1/4$. The fixed world channel sets $S'=a$ with
probability $3/4$; memory then sets $X'=S'$ with probability $3/4$.
The reward is $r(y,t)=\mathbf1_{t=1}$, and the task target is $J\ge1/2$.
At $\theta=1$, use the same energy $E(x,s)=(\log3)\mathbf1_{x\ne s}$ for
every policy, with transition work defined by the two first laws above.
Lean proves the following exact expectations from the finite path laws:

At $B=(\log3)/4$, \texttt{unique\_optimum} identifies copying as the unique

attains the task target. The baseline misses that target despite having the

Both selected substep channels allow state changes, and the selected policy

The reward, permitted policies, initial resource and budget are declared model

accounts for neither installing or switching them nor learning their values.
The result supplies no biological identification, continuing energy source,
register-to-actuator allocation or spatial coupling convergence.

\textit{Learning dynamics of a policy register.}
A specified learning model assigns a physical bit $b$ to the two feasible
lamp policies: $\pi_0$ is the baseline and $\pi_1$ copies memory. At thermal
scale one, choose register energy
\begin{equation}
 E_L(b)=4\log3\,[J(\pi_1)-J(\pi_b)],\qquad
 T=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&3/8\\1/8&7/8\end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:policy-learning-channel}
\end{equation}
with rows and columns ordered $0,1$. Thus $E_L(0)=\log3$ and $E_L(1)=0$.
The energy encodes supplied expected task values; no observations or reward
estimator are part of this model. Every transition has outward heat
$q_L(b,c)=E_L(b)-E_L(c)=\log[T_{bc}/T_{cb}]$ and zero work. This is a
declared autonomous reservoir model whose detailed-balance equality is
checked on all paths.

The construction \texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep.iterate} initializes each update
from the preceding final law. Its \texttt{sum\_entropy\_balance} and
\texttt{sum\_first\_law} telescope entropy and energy over any finite number
of updates, preserving strict support. In
\texttt{Examples/PolicyLearning.lean}, $p_0=\Pr(b_0=1)=1/4$ gives
\begin{align}
 p_{n+1}&=\frac38+\frac12p_n,
 &p_n&=\frac34-\frac12\left(\frac12\right)^n,\\
 J_n&=(1-p_n)J(\pi_0)+p_nJ(\pi_1)
 =\frac7{16}-\frac18\left(\frac12\right)^n.
 \label{eq:policy-learning-reward}
\end{align}
The theorem \texttt{performance\_\allowbreak improves} proves strict expected improvement
at every finite update. \texttt{law\_limit} and \texttt{stationary\_law}
identify convergence to the stationary law with $\Pr(b=1)=3/4$;
\texttt{performance\_\allowbreak limit} gives $J_n\to7/16<1/2=J(\pi_1)$.
These are exact finite-state results, not simulation estimates.

Heat is evaluated on the same evolving forward path laws:
\begin{equation}
 \langle Q^L_n\rangle=\frac{\log3}{4}\left(\frac12\right)^n,
 \qquad
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle Q^L_n\rangle
 =\frac{\log3}{2}\left[1-\left(\frac12\right)^N\right]
 =\langle E_L\rangle_0-\langle E_L\rangle_N.
 \label{eq:policy-learning-heat}
\end{equation}
The cumulative heat is bounded by $(\log3)/2$ and satisfies the summed
KL-based entropy balance. The energy comes from the prepared register's
relaxation; this finite total supplies no continuing power source.

Both decoded policies have lamp task heat $(\log3)/4$ and zero mean
task work, so their mixture retains those prospective expectations for every
$n$. This evaluation presumes the lamp task's prepared initial law,
independent of the policy register. The learning channel executes no task
episodes, resets, reward evaluations or actuator installation, and its heat
does not pay for those operations. In particular, summable register heat
does not bound the cost of repeatedly performing the task.

The regression \texttt{reward\_\allowbreak decreasing\_\allowbreak path\_\allowbreak positive} retains positive
probability of a copy-to-baseline transition. The same channel initialized
with $\Pr(b=1)=7/8$ lowers expected reward
(\texttt{overprepared\_\allowbreak reward\_\allowbreak decreases}). Improvement therefore depends
on the objective, channel and initial preparation. This model establishes
policy adaptation toward encoded task values. The model below supplies no such
values; cortical identification and the spatial coupling convergence of
E45Active require further models.

\textit{Learning an unknown task from observed outcomes.}
Which of two actions is rewarded is an environmental bit $w$ that the learner
never reads. Its register bit $r$ is read out as the executed action $a=r$; the
world returns success with $\Pr(o=1\mid w,a)=3/4$ when $a=w$ and $1/4$
otherwise; and the update is win-stay, lose-resample, so $o=1$ leaves $r$
unchanged and $o=0$ redraws it uniformly. The fences are signatures rather than
prose. In \texttt{FiniteObservationalLearner}, the update has type
$O\to R\to\mathrm{ProbDist}\,R$ and the readout has type $R\to A$: neither takes
the parameter as an argument, so no channel can read it and no precomputed
policy value can be decoded into the action. The reward enters
\texttt{performance} and no channel.

Acting, observing and updating compose into one \texttt{FiniteFeedbackStep} on
$(W,R)$ with the parameter held fixed, so the existing path-law entropy balance,
local detailed balance, first law and their telescoped sums apply to exactly the
process the reward is read from. Its composite channel is
\begin{equation}
 T_w=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&3/8\\1/8&7/8\end{pmatrix}\quad(w=1),
 \label{eq:observational-channel}
\end{equation}
with rows and columns ordered $r=0,1$, and its mirror image at $w=0$; every
entry is positive. From the uniform prior on $(w,r)$, \texttt{law\_\allowbreak apply} gives
every mass of the actual joint law at every horizon, and hence the expected
reward of the action actually executed:
\begin{align}
 \Pr{}_n(w,r)&=\frac38-\frac18\left(\frac12\right)^n\ \ (r=w),
 &\Pr{}_n(w,r)&=\frac18+\frac18\left(\frac12\right)^n\ \ (r\neq w),\\
 J_n&=\frac34-\frac14\left(\frac12\right)^n.
 \label{eq:observational-reward}
\end{align}
\texttt{performance\_\allowbreak improves} proves strict improvement at every update from
$J_0=1/2$, \texttt{performance\_\allowbreak limit} gives $J_n\to3/4$ and
\texttt{performance\_\allowbreak below\_\allowbreak certainty} keeps every $J_n$ below $1$.
\texttt{action\_\allowbreak marginal\_\allowbreak unchanged} shows that each action is executed half the
time at every horizon: what the learner acquires is a correlation with the
parameter, not a preference between actions.

Two baselines share the task, prior, readout and resource allowance.
\texttt{frozen\_\allowbreak performance} holds the register fixed and
\texttt{blind\_\allowbreak performance} replaces the world by one returning success with
probability $1/2$ whatever is done; both stay at $1/2$ for every $n$, and
\texttt{blind\_heat} shows the second spends nothing while remaining strictly
positive. \texttt{improves\_\allowbreak on\_\allowbreak baselines} states the strict improvement over
both within the common allowance. The claim therefore rests on the observations
carrying task information, not on the update rule or on the heat.
\texttt{observation\_\allowbreak informative} exhibits that information as conditional on
the action, $\Pr(o=1\mid w=1,r=1)=3/4$ against $1/4$ at $w=0$, while
\texttt{outcome\_\allowbreak marginal\_\allowbreak uninformative} shows the outcome's marginal carries
nothing about $w$ at the prior.

Heat is the transition log-ratio of the same composite channel at thermal scale
one, $q_w(r,r')=\pm\log3$ on the two register flips and $0$ otherwise, and is
evaluated on the actual evolving laws:
\begin{equation}
 \langle Q_n\rangle=\frac{\log3}{8}\left(\frac12\right)^n,\qquad
 \sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\langle Q_n\rangle=\frac{\log3}{4}
 \left[1-\left(\frac12\right)^N\right]\leq\frac{\log3}{4}.
 \label{eq:observational-heat}
\end{equation}
The register's two states are energetically degenerate, so it stores nothing and
\texttt{cumulative\_\allowbreak work} makes that whole sum external work. The work is
irreducible rather than a modelling convenience.
\texttt{zero\_\allowbreak work\_\allowbreak needs\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak independent\_\allowbreak heat} proves that a register
energy giving zero work on every path forces $q_w=q_{w'}$ for all parameter
values, and \texttt{no\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak work\_\allowbreak energy} exhibits $q_1(0,1)=\log3=-q_0(0,1)$:
an energy landscape that paid for this learning would be one that already
encoded the answer. \texttt{learning\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak budget} closes the accounting by
telescoping the KL-based entropy balance over the same laws, so the joint
entropy the learner removes fits inside $(\log3)/4$.

\texttt{reward\_\allowbreak decreasing\_\allowbreak transition} retains probability $1/8$ that the
register leaves the rewarding action, so expected improvement is not pathwise
improvement, and \texttt{successive\_\allowbreak laws\_\allowbreak differ} rejects a frozen register and
a reset to the prior. Preparing the prior, supplying the work that drives the
updates, executing task episodes beyond the declared updates, fabricating the
readout, convergence to an optimal policy and any cortical identification are
outside this model.

\textit{A continuing agent with a finite work store.}
\texttt{FiniteProtocol} in \texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak ContinuingAgent.lean}
allows a different channel at every stage, carrying each actual final law
into the next initial law. \texttt{sum\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak balance},
\texttt{sum\_\allowbreak first\_\allowbreak law} and
\texttt{cumulative\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak budget} telescope the
existing finite path balances over this sequence without stationarity.
\texttt{ContinuingProcess} adds a common energy, stage heat observables, a
thermal scale and an initial work allowance $B$.
Its predicate \texttt{Sustains}$\,N$ requires
$B-W_{<k}\geq0$ for every $k\leq N$, where work is an expectation on the
executed paths. The resulting bound is the article's
Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuing-budget}; when each stage costs at least $c>0$,
\texttt{horizon\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak cost} gives
$Nc\leq B$. These results include the system's mean energy drop and do not
identify the allowance with a register's erasure heat.

\texttt{ContinuingAgent} implements a three-stage cycle on
$(w,r,e)$: the parameter $w$ is fixed, the register $r$ selects the action,
and the environment flag $e$ retains what that action did. Acting drives the
flag while the register drifts; learning reads the flag, writes the register
and lets the flag drift; reset prepares the flag for reuse while the register
drifts. The learner's readout, observation and update do not take $w$ as an
argument. The actuator responds to $w$, as a physical task must, and reward
is used only to score the law. The observation is marginalized inside the
learning channel, whose thermal interpretation is supplied by local detailed
balance for that composite channel.

In \texttt{Examples/ContinuingAgent.lean}, all five state types are bits and
the initial joint law is uniform. A clear flag is set with probability $3/4$
by the rewarding action $r=w$ and $1/4$ by the other; an already set flag
stays set with probability $3/4$. Observation is correct with probability
$3/4$. Success keeps the register with probability $3/4$ and failure flips
it with that probability. Idle drift keeps a bit with probability $3/4$;
reset sets the flag's probability to $1/4$ independently of its input.
The three composite channels have strictly positive support. With common
energy zero, thermal scale one and stage heat equal to each channel's
forward/reverse transition log-ratio, every path's work equals its heat.

The proved mass recursion gives agreement $a_0=65/128$ after the first
cycle and
\begin{equation}
 a_{m+1}=\frac{65}{128}+\frac{a_m}{128},\qquad
 J_{3(m+1)}=a_m\geq\frac{65}{128}>\frac12.
 \label{eq:continuing-agreement}
\end{equation}
The flag has probability $1/4$ and is independent of parameter and register
at each completed reset. Successive cycle laws differ. These are exact
finite-channel identities, proved in Lean rather than fitted to a simulation.
Solving the recursion gives the error of that agreement exactly,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{65}{127}-J_{3(m+1)}
   =\left(\frac{65}{127}-\frac{65}{128}\right)\left(\frac1{128}\right)^{m},
 \label{eq:continuing-limit}
\end{equation}
so the executed learner's expected task performance rises to $65/127$
geometrically from below and stays short of one
(\texttt{performance\_\allowbreak error}, \texttt{performance\_\allowbreak tendsto}).
The limit is a property of these channels. Nothing here compares it with an
optimal policy, and the convergence is of an expectation along completed cycles
rather than of a realization.
On the same laws, \texttt{first\_\allowbreak cycle\_\allowbreak work} and
\texttt{totalWork\_\allowbreak cycles\_\allowbreak lower} give
\begin{equation}
 W_{<3}=\frac{15}{32}\log3+\frac1{16}\log\frac53,\qquad
 W_{<3m}\geq m\left(\frac3{16}\log3+\frac1{16}\log\frac53\right).
 \label{eq:continuing-cost}
\end{equation}
The learning stage costs $(\log(5/3))/16$ at every cycle, and reset has
strictly positive mean heat. Every prefix of the first cycle fits the declared
$B=4\log3$, while 22 complete cycles exceed it. This is a conservative
exclusion, not the exact exhaustion time. The same per-cycle floor is
positive, so the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuing-cost} exceeds any
declared allowance at some cycle (\texttt{work\_\allowbreak unbounded}): the
sequence that converges in Eq.~\eqref{eq:continuing-limit} is one no finite
store funds to its limit. Reaching that limit is a statement about
replenishment, not about learning.

Two controls delimit the result. Uniform observations leave chance reward
at every completed cycle and zero learning-stage heat. Replacing only reset
by idle drift leaves flag probability $17/32$ after the first cycle, against
$1/4$ with reset, and reduces second-cycle reward from $8385/16384$ to
$4157/8192$. The control still learns; reset restores the specified workspace
law and changes subsequent behaviour. The store limits expected work at each
prefix, not the battery on every realization; the next paragraph set carries a
store on the trajectory instead. Initial-law preparation, the
initial store, microscopic battery and sensor-memory dynamics, fabrication,
optimal-policy convergence and cortical identification remain supplied or
outside the construction. Its indefinite law sequence is a mathematical
continuation, whose funded prefixes are selected by \texttt{Sustains}.

\textit{A store on the trajectory (\texttt{Examples/\allowbreak
Pathwise\allowbreak Store.lean}).} The structure \texttt{Pathwise\allowbreak
Store}, in \texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak Continuing\allowbreak Agent.lean},
carries a finite protocol together with a reading $\beta$ of its work store, a
draw $w^-$ and a supply $w^+$ per transition. The reading is a \emph{coordinate of the state},
which is what makes the account pathwise: \texttt{Reachable}$\,n\,z$ is positive
mass under the protocol's own law at stage $n$, and the three predicates
quantify over those states alone. \texttt{Ledgered} is the article's
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pathwise-store} on every executed transition out of a reachable
state, \texttt{Funded}$\,n$ is the second inequality there, and
\texttt{Solvent}$\,N$ says no reachable state up to $N$ carries a negative
reading. \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak pred\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak
reachable} supplies the one structural fact these need --- a reached state was
reached from a reached state, by a transition of positive mass --- and
\texttt{solvent\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak funded} is the conclusion. No
positivity hypothesis appears: deterministic gates and zero masses are
admitted, as they must be for the reversible gates of the register--bath model
above, and the draw itself may be random.

\texttt{mean\_\allowbreak balance\_\allowbreak eq} averages the ledger over the
executed paths. It is \texttt{sum\_\allowbreak energyTransfer} applied to the
store's own coordinate rather than a second calculation, and with
\texttt{meanBalance\_\allowbreak nonneg\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak solvent}
it gives \texttt{totalDraw\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak
solvent}: pathwise solvency implies the expected bound that
\texttt{ContinuingProcess.Sustains} asserts. Two results fence what a supply
buys. \texttt{solvent\_\allowbreak forall\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak
replenished} is sufficient --- a supply covering each executed draw sustains
every horizon --- and \texttt{horizon\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak
net\_\allowbreak cost} is necessary: a net draw of at least $c>0$ on every
executed transition before $N$ gives $Nc\leq\beta_0$ for a run solvent at $N$. That is the
pathwise form of \texttt{horizon\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak
cost} and, unlike it, constrains each trajectory.

Two witnesses share three readings, $1$, $0$ and $-1$, with only the third
overdrawn. The \emph{gambler} idles or spends a unit with equal probability from
the full reading, and again from the empty one. \texttt{gambler\_\allowbreak
mean\_\allowbreak sustains} shows the existing mean predicate holding over two
stages with $W_{<2}=1$ against a declared store of $1$, and
\texttt{gambler\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak solvent} exhibits the overdrawn
reading at probability $1/4$; \texttt{gambler\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak
funded} locates the failure at the second stage.
\texttt{sustained\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak solvent} states all three at
once, so the refinement above runs one way only: its conclusion holds here,
with equality, and its hypothesis fails. The \emph{charger} spends a unit and is
given it back, deterministically. \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak reach} proves the
overdrawn reading unreachable, \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak funded} and the
generic theorem give \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak solvent} at every horizon, and
\texttt{charger\_\allowbreak totalDraw} evaluates the work drawn over $m$
completed cycles as $m$, so \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak draw\_\allowbreak
unbounded} exceeds any declared allowance at a horizon that is still solvent. It
satisfies neither fence --- \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak
covered} and \texttt{charger\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak uniform\_\allowbreak
cost} --- which is what places it strictly between them, and its supply is
load-bearing: \texttt{unsupplied\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak ledgered} shows
the same protocol and reading, with the supply removed, is not a ledger at all.

The generic supply is an input. A finite source behind it is accounted for
by the following construction, and the measurement's own register by the one
after it; preparing the initial resources remains a distinct task.

\textit{A finite source and uncertain delivery (\texttt{Examples/\allowbreak
Finite\allowbreak Supply.lean}).} The source predicate
\texttt{Source\allowbreak Ledgered}$\,R$ says that the source coordinate's
loss equals the supply on each reachable transition. The construction
\texttt{withSource}$\,R$ adds $R$ to the store's reading and sets external
supply to zero. \texttt{withSource\_\allowbreak ledgered} adds the two
constituent identities to derive Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-source};
\texttt{totalSupply\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak source\_\allowbreak loss}
telescopes the same source's expected loss. If both readings stay nonnegative,
\texttt{totalDraw\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak initial\_\allowbreak resources}
gives $W^-_{<N}\leq\langle\beta+R\rangle_0$.
\texttt{horizon\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak finite\_\allowbreak
source} applies the existing pathwise horizon bound to the combined reading:
a draw of at least $c>0$ on every executed transition before $N$ and an initial combined
reading at most $b$ give $Nc\leq b$. Signed supply permits energy returned to
the source. The transfer identity is a model obligation; no positivity of
masses or entropy inequality is assumed.

\texttt{direct\_\allowbreak draw\_\allowbreak one} and
\texttt{direct\_\allowbreak horizon} instantiate the positive-cost bound with
a unit transferred to a complementary load in one stage, followed by idling.
\texttt{direct\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak uniform\_\allowbreak cost}
rejects a cost premise extending over every natural-numbered stage. Both
horizon bounds restrict their cost premise to the finite run: a finite state
space cannot sustain a uniformly positive net energy loss forever.

The witness has source, buffer and load bits with energy levels $0$ and $1$,
and a selector bit with degenerate levels. It starts the source charged, the
buffer and load empty and the selector uniform. A selector-controlled
source--buffer swap precedes a buffer--load swap; subsequent stages idle.
\texttt{charge\_\allowbreak involutive} and
\texttt{deliver\_\allowbreak involutive} prove reversibility, while
\texttt{charge\_\allowbreak conserves} and
\texttt{deliver\_\allowbreak conserves} prove total-energy conservation.
Supply is the source loss and draw is the load gain, so conservation
discharges the buffer's ledger. \texttt{law\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak coin}
derives the actual law at every horizon: the initial selector is carried
through the gates without resampling. After the two gates, a unit reaches the
load with probability $1/2$ and remains in the source with probability $1/2$.
\texttt{draw\_\allowbreak two} and \texttt{supply\_\allowbreak one} each give
$1/2$ in expectation; \texttt{draw\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak capacity}
bounds every horizon by the initial unit of combined energy.

\texttt{forced\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak ledgered} rejects a ledger
claiming a unit at the delivery stage on every path.
\texttt{empty\_\allowbreak source\_\allowbreak draw} gives zero draw with
the source initially empty, and
\texttt{charger\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak finite\_\allowbreak source}
rules out any nonnegative finite source coordinate for the indefinitely
replenished charger. Solvency after the two gates is idling, and work here is
energy gained by the ideal load. Preparing the source and selector, controlling
the gates, external refuelling and identifying this hardware with the learning
agent's actuator are outside this finite model.

\textit{A sensor memory and its erasure (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak Sensor\allowbreak Memory},
\texttt{Examples/\allowbreak Sensor\allowbreak Memory.lean}).} The elementary
quantity is \texttt{memoryHeat}$\,\theta\,\mu\,c$, the mean log-ratio heat of a
memory channel $c$ on a declared memory law $\mu$. For a channel landing on one
law $\nu$ whatever it is given, \texttt{memoryHeat\_\allowbreak const} proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:erasure-cost} by way of
\texttt{KL\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak cross\_\allowbreak entropy}, which needs
no positivity of $\mu$: a state the memory never holds contributes to neither
side. \texttt{memoryHeat\_\allowbreak ge\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak drop}
is Gibbs applied to that identity, and
\texttt{memoryHeat\_\allowbreak self} is the case $\mu=\nu$.

\texttt{MemoryAgent} carries the prior, readout, actuator, measurement
\texttt{record}, register update, \texttt{erase}, world \texttt{reset}, three
idle drifts and the reward on the joint state $R\times(M\times\mathit{Env})$.
The fences are signatures: \texttt{record} and \texttt{erase} do not take the
parameter, \texttt{update} takes neither the parameter nor the world, and the
reward enters no channel. \texttt{stage} runs act, record, learn and clear

unchanged. \texttt{learn\_\allowbreak register\_\allowbreak independent} states

\texttt{update}$\,m\,r$ at every parameter and every world state.

\texttt{memoryLaw} and \texttt{envLaw} are the marginals, defined through the
regrouping equivalences \texttt{memSplit} and \texttt{envSplit}.
\texttt{memoryLaw\_\allowbreak clear} is the one stage at which the memory is
autonomous --- its marginal evolves by \texttt{erase} alone --- and
\texttt{memoryLaw\_\allowbreak record} is the stage at which it is not, the

is the content: the correlation recording writes is what clearing destroys.
\texttt{clear\_\allowbreak stageHeat\_\allowbreak eq} splits the clear stage's

\texttt{clear\_\allowbreak memoryHeat} identifies the memory's share, averaged
over the executed paths, with \texttt{memoryHeat} on that marginal; and
\texttt{clear\_\allowbreak memoryHeat\_\allowbreak const} is

The witness has bit-valued parameter, register, memory, world and action, a

\section{Macroscopic Scaling and Kuramoto Phase Synchronization}
We posit a continuous macroscopic coupling description. We model the local phases $\theta_i$ as coupled non-linear oscillators via the Kuramoto model.

\textit{Scope of the continuum description.} \texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement\_\allowbreak convergence} proves convergence of one scalar coupling energy. It produces neither a continuum kernel nor field dynamics: \texttt{edge\_\allowbreak region} has no $M\times M$ product structure from which such a kernel could be recovered, and the vertex-supported candidate is invisible to the continuum functional on an atomless substrate. Both obstructions are to this architecture: \texttt{KernelMesh} carries cell pairs of positive mass and its limit is a kernel integral, at the price of declaring the hardware that sets the weights. The symmetric kernel is therefore a modelling input either way. Its identification with the brain's endogenous \emph{electromagnetic} field is a further empirical commitment about cortex, argued in the main text from the ephaptic-coupling literature with explicit calibration and observational limitations. Nothing in this section mathematically establishes either step.

For a system with coupling strength $K$ and thermodynamic noise $D$, the dynamic evolution is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\theta_i} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) + \eta_i(t)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem 4 (Stationary coherent branch):} For identical frequencies in a rotating frame with positive diffusion, the positive normalized stationary densities of the mean-field Fokker--Planck equation are the von Mises family, and the resulting self-consistency equation has exactly one positive solution when $K>2D$ and none when $K\leq2D$. Linearization about the uniform density has a growing mode exactly above that threshold, and the coherent branch carries a strictly dissipative quadratic form transverse to its rotation orbit. This does not assert dynamical selection or noiseless phase locking in the stochastic model.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak KuramotoDynamics.lean}):} We formalize the Lyapunov potential $V(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j} A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$ of the coupled field and prove two directions: \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} (the phase-locked configuration attains the global minimum whenever all couplings are positive) and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} (any minimizer is phase-locked). Note the scope: this is a statement about the \emph{static} potential landscape and holds for all positive coupling matrices. The critical threshold $K_c = 2D$ does not appear in this file. It is formalized separately, in \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak SelfConsistency.lean} (see below), as a statement about the mean-field self-consistency equation, and the density that equation uses is classified in \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak FokkerPlanck.lean} from the stationary Fokker--Planck equation itself. What no file supplies is the passage from the stochastic differential equation to that equation, or a solution theory for its evolution. The numerical controls below distinguish identical-frequency diffusion from the separate quenched-frequency model illustrated by \texttt{simulations/kuramoto.py}.

\textit{Positivity, and the regime these theorems cover.} The hypothesis ``all couplings positive'' is not a technical convenience and it is not free. \texttt{KuramotoSystem} carries $A : V \to V \to \mathbb{R}$, symmetric and otherwise unconstrained, so signed --- frustrated --- couplings are representable; but every result in this development that identifies \emph{where} the dynamics ends assumes positivity. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential} and \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} assume $A_{ij} > 0$; the class field \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.A\_\allowbreak pos} demands it of every instance, and so of every conclusion of Theorem 5; and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} requires the strictly stronger uniform bound $0 < a \le A_{ij}$, since $a$ enters the {\L}ojasiewicz constant. Such a network is an unfrustrated ferromagnet with one minimum up to a global phase shift --- exactly not the near-degenerate landscape a memory argument from geometric frustration requires. By contrast, nothing about the sign of $A$ is used by \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} and its uniqueness counterpart, by the Lyapunov identity \texttt{dV\_\allowbreak dt\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak zero} on which the whole descent argument rests, by \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} and \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}, or by \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}. Since positivity is a \emph{field} of \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} rather than a hypothesis distributed across the development, it enters the chain at one node and not before it. So \emph{that} the motion stops holds for frustrated systems as well; \emph{where} it stops does not. On the continuum side \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak entropy\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak symm} assumes only symmetry and so admits signed kernels, but it takes the existence of a dynamically phase-locked field as a hypothesis, which is precisely what frustration threatens. The main text scopes the frustration claim out of the chain accordingly, rather than repeating it beside theorems that exclude it.

\textit{Twisted gluing does not recover content from phase frustration (\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak TwistedGluing.lean}).} We also tested the natural weakening of exact overlap agreement: local states may differ by a phase on each overlap. \texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak isCoboundary} proves that coboundary offsets glue after one rotation per patch, while \texttt{isCoboundary\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak gluesUpToPhase} proves the converse when the phase action is free on overlaps. Any Kuramoto configuration, including a twisted or splay state, supplies absolute patch phases $\phi_i$, so its offsets $\phi_i-\phi_j$ are automatically a coboundary (\texttt{isCoboundary\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}) and the twisted family glues (\texttt{gluesUpToPhase\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phaseField}). The formal \texttt{PhaseObstruction} quotient is deliberately not called \v{C}ech cohomology because its total cochains still assign irrelevant values to patch pairs with empty overlap.

The data give a narrower positive answer. \cite{townsend2015} identify phase singularities in multielectrode primate LFP recordings by an integer winding number around electrode loops, and the associated complex waves modulate spiking; \cite{xu2023} find task-dependent spiral centres and rotation directions in human fMRI phase maps. Around such a zero-amplitude point the circle-valued phase need not admit one real-valued lift. Local lifts differ on overlaps by integer multiples of $2\pi$, supplying measured transition data without an arbitrary gauge field. \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak PhaseLifts.lean} formalizes the finite electrode loop: \texttt{loopWinding} sums its integer transitions, \texttt{loopWinding\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} proves that transitions from one global lift telescope to zero, and \texttt{threeElectrodeWinding\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak hasGlobalLift} gives a winding-one witness. This does not rescue \texttt{PhaseObstruction}: that quotient uses circle-valued differences of absolute patch phases, while the measured obstruction uses integer transitions between local lifts.

The cover-level specification has cortical surface $M$, analytic signal $z=Ae^{i\phi}$, singularity set $Z_t$, punctured domain $M^\ast_t=M\setminus Z_t$, and measurement map $q_t=z/|z|:M^\ast_t\to S^1$. A trial-independent electrode or source-space cover of $M^\ast_t$ supplies local real lifts; their differences divided by $2\pi$ are integer transitions on connected overlaps, and nerve loops are the inputs to \texttt{loopWinding}. In finite-resolution data the amplitude threshold defining $Z_t$, the loop geometry and the required cross-scale persistence are fixed using training or baseline data, never the content labels of test trials.

The proposed content-sensitive test compares nested preregistered classifiers. The control model contains stimulus, task, behavioural response, eye movement and arousal; the augmented model adds signed winding, source-localized singularity position, rotation direction and lifetime. Evaluation uses held-out participants and sessions, with label permutations within task, stimulus and arousal strata. The content hypothesis requires replicated incremental out-of-sample discrimination, including under a changed or absent motor report. This controls the known task dependence rather than mistaking it for content. A positive result would be an empirical association, not an identification of a topological sector with phenomenal content; a null increment rejects this proposed role. The construction and test remain outside the conditional chain, and a full nerve-indexed integer complex is deferred until the measurement protocol warrants it.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak ContinuousField.lean}):} The continuous-field file defines $K_c=2D$ and a predicate for a substrate above that threshold. Two cautions. First, the predicate is not connected to \texttt{is\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory}, \texttt{entropy\_\allowbreak production\_\allowbreak rate}, or the order parameter of \texttt{Phase4}. What it is connected to is the mean-field self-consistency equation $r = I_1(Kr/D)/I_0(Kr/D)$, whose bifurcation at $K = 2D$ we formalize in both directions (see the next paragraph): \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak coherent} shows that a substrate satisfying the predicate admits a solution with $0 < r \le 1$, and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak unique\_\allowbreak coherent} that it admits exactly one. So the threshold is not a bare stipulation, but what is proved is a statement about a stationary density rather than about a trajectory. Second, the predicate requires the substrate's \texttt{volume} to be a probability measure, and this hypothesis is load-bearing rather than decorative. It compares \texttt{mean\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak coupling}, the kernel averaged over both arguments, against $2D$; on a substrate of total mass $m$ that average carries a factor $m^2$, so without normalization the comparison tracks the substrate's size rather than its coupling and can be satisfied by any kernel at all. \texttt{Examples}~\S9 exhibits a system a factor of two below threshold whose unnormalized double integral nonetheless clears $2D$. Under the hypothesis, \texttt{mean\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak const} gives that a constant kernel has strength exactly $K$ and \texttt{exhibits\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak transition\_\allowbreak const\_\allowbreak iff} reduces the predicate to $K > 2D$, witnessed in \S9 on both sides of the threshold.

\subsection{Stationary self-consistency}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak SelfConsistency.lean}):} The self-consistency equation behind $K_c = 2D$ is formalized as real analysis. With the von Mises stationary density $\rho(\theta) \propto e^{a\cos\theta}$, $a = Kr/D$, supplied as an input to this file and classified from the stationary equation in \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak FokkerPlanck.lean} below (\texttt{simulations/fokker\_\allowbreak planck\_\allowbreak von\_\allowbreak mises.py} checks the same algebra numerically), \texttt{besselRatio} is $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ written as a ratio of interval integrals, Mathlib having no modified Bessel functions. \texttt{vonMisesM\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak mul\_\allowbreak vonMisesS} is the integration-by-parts identity $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta} = a\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin^2\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, exact because $\sin(\pm\pi) = 0$ kills the boundary term. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak nonneg} proves $I_2(a) \ge 0$ by folding $[-\pi,\pi]$ onto $[0,\pi/4]$ through the reflections $\theta \mapsto \pi - \theta$ and $\theta \mapsto \pi/2 - \theta$, where the integrand becomes a product of two manifestly non-negative factors; \texttt{vonMisesS\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak half\_\allowbreak vonMisesZ} restates it as $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] \le 1/2$ and \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak half\_\allowbreak self} as $R(a) \le a/2$. That factor of $1/2$ is exactly what separates a threshold at $D$ from one at $2D$; \texttt{subcritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero} is the crude form obtained from $\sin^2 \le 1$ and is kept to make the point visible.

The subcritical theorem is \texttt{subcritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero}$'$: for $0 \le K < \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling } D$ and $D > 0$, the only $r \ge 0$ with $r = R(K,r)$ is $r = 0$.

The supercritical theorem is \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak exists}: for $K > \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling } D$ there is an $r$ with $0 < r \le 1$ and $r = R(K,r)$. Its proof needs no series expansion. Factoring $R(a) = a\,E(a)$ with $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ --- the factorization the integration by parts supplies --- it suffices that $E$ is continuous with $E(0) = 1/2$: since $D/K < 1/2$, the map overshoots for small $r > 0$, while \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak one} ($R$ is a mean of $\cos\theta$) makes it undershoot at $r = 1$, and \texttt{intermediate\_\allowbreak value\_\allowbreak Icc}$'$ supplies the crossing. Continuity of $E$ is Mathlib's continuity of a parametric interval integral, applied to the jointly continuous integrand. \texttt{critical\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak threshold} states the two halves together, and \S9 of the file discharges the hypotheses of each at concrete values ($K = 3$, $D = 1$ above threshold; $K = 3$, $D = 2$ below it), so neither is vacuous.

Section 8 of the file identifies the equation with the physics it is named for. \texttt{vonMisesDensity} is the normalized weight, proved a probability density on $[-\pi,\pi]$ (\texttt{vonMisesDensity\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak one}); \texttt{circularOrderParameter} is the average of $e^{i\theta}$ against a density, the continuum analogue of \texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak complex} of \texttt{Phase4}; \texttt{circularOrderParameter\_\allowbreak vonMises} proves that average equals $R(a)$, real because \texttt{vonMises\_\allowbreak mean\_\allowbreak sin} gives $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin\theta] = 0$; and \texttt{fixedPoint\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak selfReproducing} states the equivalence: $r$ solves $r = R(K,r)$ exactly when the density it induces has order parameter $r$. \texttt{incoherent\_\allowbreak density\_\allowbreak uniform} identifies the solution $r = 0$ with the uniform density, and \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak density} restates the existence theorem as the existence of a stationary density with positive order parameter.

Strict inequalities determine the threshold case. \texttt{vonMisesC2\_\allowbreak pos} upgrades the fold argument to a strict inequality for $a > 0$ --- \texttt{foldB} is continuous, non-negative on $[0,\pi/4]$ and strictly positive on its interior, so \texttt{intervalIntegral\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak pos\_\allowbreak on} applies --- giving $\mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta] < 1/2$ and $R(a) < a/2$ for every $a > 0$. That closes the threshold case: \texttt{fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak critical} covers all $K \le 2D$, so the incoherent and coherent regimes exhaust $K \ge 0$ with no gap, and $K = 2D$ falls on the incoherent side.

\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq} reformulates the coherent branch: for $r \ne 0$, solving $r = R(K,r)$ is equivalent to $E(Kr/D) = D/K$, so the branch is a level set of $E$. \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold} then proves that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that on $(2D, 2D+\delta)$ every coherent solution satisfies $r < \varepsilon$ --- the branch emerges continuously from zero, excluding a discontinuous jump. Its proof needs no monotonicity of $E$: on a compact interval bounded away from the origin the extreme value theorem gives a maximum strictly below $1/2$, and beyond that the crude bound $E(a) \le 1/a$ suffices.

Strict decrease of $E$ and strict increase of $R$ give uniqueness and monotonicity of the coherent branch. Section~\ref{sec:fold} presents the folding and crossing argument. The consequences are \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak unique} (at most one positive solution), \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak existsUnique} and \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak solution\_\allowbreak set} (the non-negative solutions are exactly $\{0\}$ at or below threshold and $\{0, r\}$ above it), and \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak strictMono}, which uses both halves: $E$ decreasing puts the concentrations of two couplings in the order $a_1 < a_2$ through $E(a_i) = D/K_i$, and $R$ increasing carries that order to $r_1 < r_2$.

Scope: the density is an input to this file rather than an output of it, and the classification that derives it is a separate module; nothing in this file differentiates anything, so $E$ and $R$ are reached as strictly monotone rather than as smooth, and the rate at which the branch leaves zero is the business of \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak CriticalExponent.lean} below; dynamical selection is not established, and could not be here, since no theorem in the file mentions a trajectory; and the order parameter reached is that of a density, not of a trajectory --- no theorem relates \texttt{circularOrderParameter} to \texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak complex}, which would be the mean-field limit. Every declaration in the file depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\subsection{Stationary onset rate}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak CriticalExponent.lean}):} The stationary onset rate follows from a second-order expansion of the integral ratio. \texttt{vmMoment $n$ $a$} is the cosine moment $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^{n}\!\theta\,e^{a\cos\theta}$, and \texttt{hasDerivAt\_\allowbreak vmMoment} differentiates it in $a$ under the integral sign, raising the cosine power by one; the interchange is Mathlib's dominated-convergence form for interval integrals, dominated by the constant $e^{|a|+1}$ on the unit ball around the parameter, which is where the compactness of $[-\pi,\pi]$ and the boundedness of $\cos$ do their work. Iterating gives \texttt{contDiff\_\allowbreak two\_\allowbreak vmMoment}, and \texttt{vonMisesSRatio\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak moments} rewrites $E(a) = 1 - \texttt{vmMoment }2\,a/\texttt{vmMoment }0\,a$, so $E$ inherits two derivatives from a quotient whose denominator is positive.

The moments at $a=0$ are elementary integrals of powers of $\cos$: $2\pi$, $0$, $\pi$, $0$ and $3\pi/4$. They give $E(0) = 1/2$, $E'(0) = 0$ --- the first derivative vanishes by the symmetry that kills the odd moments --- and $E''(0) = -1/8$, and Taylor's theorem in Mathlib's \texttt{taylor\_\allowbreak isLittleO\_\allowbreak univ} form then yields \texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak second\_\allowbreak order}: $E(a) - (\tfrac12 - a^{2}/16)$ is $o(a^{2})$ at the origin. The remainder is $o(a^{2})$ and not $O(a^{4})$; the stronger form would need a fourth derivative and nothing here requires it.

\texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak solution\_\allowbreak critical\_\allowbreak exponent} then combines the expansion with \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak sRatio\_\allowbreak eq}: writing $a = Kr/D$ and rearranging $E(a) = D/K$ gives $K - 2D = K a^{2}(\tfrac18 - 2q(a))$ with $q$ the normalized remainder, and letting $K \to 2D$ along any family of positive coherent solutions with $r \to 0$ sends $q \to 0$ and $r^{2}/(K-2D) \to 1/D$. The statement is deliberately filter-indexed rather than parameterized by a curve, so it constrains every such family and privileges no path.

Because its hypotheses hold only eventually along the filter, and an eventual clause is vacuously true on the empty one, the file also exhibits a family satisfying it: \texttt{coherentBranch} selects the unique positive solution above threshold via \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak point\_\allowbreak existsUnique}, \texttt{coherentBranch\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero} reads \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak branch\_\allowbreak continuous\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak threshold} as a limit along $K \downarrow 2D$, and \texttt{coherentBranch\_\allowbreak critical\_\allowbreak exponent} discharges every hypothesis on it.

Scope is inherited unchanged from the file above: this is asymptotics of the solutions of a stationary equation. It establishes no stability, no dynamical selection and no time course, so reading it as a square-root foot on a recovery curve requires the adiabatic separation and a threshold crossing at non-zero speed, both of which the main text states as assumptions. Every declaration in the file depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\subsection{The stationary equation and linear stability}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak FokkerPlanck.lean} and the five modules that build on it, named below):} The equation these six files work in is the classical stationary one on the circle. \texttt{current} is $J = v\rho - D\,\partial_\theta\rho$ and \texttt{operator} is $-\partial_\theta J$; \texttt{IsStationary} asks for a differentiable, $2\pi$-periodic, strictly positive and normalized density whose current is itself differentiable and whose operator vanishes. Differentiability belongs in the predicate rather than beside it: \texttt{deriv} is totalized, so an equation written with it alone is satisfied by functions that have no derivative where the equation is being asserted.

\textit{The density is classified rather than assumed.} \texttt{integratingFactor\_\allowbreak hasDerivAt} is the identity $\partial_\theta(\rho e^{V/D}) = -(J/D)e^{V/D}$ for a gradient drift $v = -\partial_\theta V$. Stationarity makes $J$ constant (\texttt{stationary\_\allowbreak current\_\allowbreak const}), periodicity of $\rho e^{V/D}$ makes its integral over a period vanish, and $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} e^{V/D} > 0$ then forces that constant to zero: \texttt{stationary\_\allowbreak current\_\allowbreak zero}. The remaining equation is first order, giving \texttt{stationary\_\allowbreak gibbs}, $\rho = ce^{-V/D}$ with $c>0$, and with the cosine potential and normalization, \texttt{stationary\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak vonMises}: for the drift $-Kr\sin\theta$ the positive normalized stationary densities are exactly $\texttt{vonMisesDensity}(Kr/D)$. \texttt{supercritical\_\allowbreak stationary\_\allowbreak existsUnique} transfers the scalar bifurcation theorem to this statement --- above threshold exactly one positive $r$ carries a stationary density whose own circular order parameter is $r$. No modified Bessel function, spectral theory or operator closure enters; the argument is the one \texttt{vonMisesZ\_\allowbreak pos} already used, run once more.

\textit{The linearization is derived from the equation.} \texttt{meanDrift} writes the mean-field coupling through the density's two trigonometric moments and \texttt{meanDrift\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak integral} identifies it with $-K\int\sin(\theta-\varphi)\rho(\varphi)\,d\varphi$. \texttt{current\_\allowbreak expansion} expands the nonlinear current at $q + \varepsilon u$ exactly, as the background current plus $\varepsilon$ times \texttt{linearCurrent} plus an explicit quadratic term, so \texttt{linearOperator} is the derivative of the equation rather than a separately declared list of rates; \texttt{selfConsistent\_\allowbreak stationary} confirms that a fixed point is stationary for the drift its own density generates.

\textit{The incoherent state.} On the uniform density the coupling reaches only the first harmonic. \texttt{incoherent\_\allowbreak mode\_\allowbreak rate} gives, for both real Fourier families, rate $K/2 - D$ at $n = 1$ and $-Dn^{2}$ for $n \ge 2$, and \texttt{incoherent\_\allowbreak instability\_\allowbreak iff} concludes that a mode of positive index grows exactly when $K > 2D$. The constant mode is excluded because a density perturbation carries zero mass. This is a per-mode computation and claims no completeness of the Fourier family in any function space.

\textit{The coherent branch.} \texttt{branchCoupling} $= D/E(a)$ parametrizes the supercritical branch by concentration; \texttt{branchCoupling\_\allowbreak supercritical} places it above $2D$ and \texttt{branch\_\allowbreak stationary} makes its density stationary for its own mean field. \texttt{generator\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak neg\_\allowbreak linearCurrent} identifies the primitive generator with minus that linearized current, \texttt{generator\_\allowbreak symmetric} makes it symmetric in the weighted primitive pairing by integration by parts, and \texttt{generator\_\allowbreak energy} evaluates its diagonal as $-D$ times the coherent quadratic form. That form is strictly positive away from rotation because of a strict moment inequality, and the inequality is derived rather than posited: differentiating the defining integrals gives the Riccati identity \texttt{sRatio\_\allowbreak riccati}, which rules out a vanishing derivative at positive concentration (\texttt{sRatioDeriv\_\allowbreak neg}) and yields \texttt{cosine\_\allowbreak variance\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak sine}. With the weighted Poincar\'e estimate \texttt{transverse\_\allowbreak poincare}, whose constant is explicit and finite, the result is \texttt{coherent\_\allowbreak linear\_\allowbreak stability}: on regular periodic perturbations of zero mass, orthogonal to rotation in the weighted primitive metric, the form is at most $-\texttt{linearStabilityRate}$ times the squared norm, and that rate is positive (\texttt{linearStabilityRate\_\allowbreak pos}). \texttt{rotation\_\allowbreak generator\_\allowbreak zero} exhibits the rotation direction as a nonzero element of zero rate, so the transversality is necessary rather than a convenience: a gap on the whole space would be false, and stability holds modulo the collective phase.

\textit{Two functionals that are not the same functional.} \texttt{currentDissipation} integrates $J^{2}/(D\rho)$ over phase space. \texttt{stationary\_\allowbreak current\_\allowbreak separation} evaluates it at the coherent stationary state, where it is zero, against the phase-averaged squared drift at the same state, which is positive. Both are functionals on phase space, so the difference is not a difference of domains; and neither is \texttt{sigmaContinuum}, which integrates squared drift over substrate sites at fixed phases and is the object of the plasticity results. No identification among the three is proved or used.

\textit{Consumers and witnesses.} \texttt{IsEMFieldCoupling.\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak model} carries the stationary state and the gap predicate \texttt{HasCoherentLinearGap} to the identified field model of Phase 9, at that model's own coupling and diffusion; it transfers nothing to a heterogeneous spatial kernel. \texttt{Examples}~\S21 fixes $D = 1$ and concentration $1$, checks that the stationary density is not the uniform one and that the stability rate is not zero, and runs the incoherent criterion at $K = 3$ and $K = 1$ on either side of the threshold.

\textit{Scope.} These are linear operator and energy estimates for the limiting equation. They construct no solution of the evolution equation, no semigroup and no self-adjoint closure; they establish no nonlinear or orbital stability and no dynamical selection; and the passage from the stochastic differential equation to the Fokker--Planck equation is not formalized anywhere in the development. Every declaration in the six files depends only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

\noindent The gap between these stationary-density results and any statement about a finite trajectory is the dynamical mean-field limit --- propagation of chaos for the Kuramoto system, which is a research programme rather than a lemma \cite{sznitman1991}. No theorem in this development closes it.

\subsection{Finite mean-field trajectory controls}

Two finite-$N$ controls probe this gap without closing it.  First, 500 seeded
replicas of 1,000 identical-frequency oscillators were integrated at fixed
coupling.  Their steady order was \selectionSubOrder{} below threshold,
\selectionCriticalOrder{} at threshold and \selectionSuperOrder{} above
threshold.  The last value lies \selectionStaticResidual{} from the stationary
coherent value \selectionTheoryOrder.  Refining the integration step by a factor
of \selectionDtRefinement{} at the same coupling, duration and tail sampling
moves that steady order to \selectionSuperOrderDtFine, so most of the gap is
Euler--Maruyama bias.  The last refinement still moves it by
\selectionDtLastChange, so the remaining \selectionStaticResidualDtFine{}
bounds the finite-$N$ part rather than measuring it.

Early-growth fits are regressed over a window whose bounds follow the trace: it
opens at $2/\sqrt{N}=\selectionWindowLower$, clear of the finite-size floor,
and closes at half the static branch order, which runs from
\selectionWindowUpperMin{} to \selectionWindowUpperMax{} across the swept
couplings.  The fits give $\lambda=\selectionRateSlope K\selectionRateIntercept$,

assumes, so the finite window can itself contribute to the shortfall from
$1/2$: the step refinement above is a control on stationary order and
apportions no bias in this growth estimator.  The sweep also integrates at the
step whose stationary bias that control measures, and carries finite $N$; this
measurement separates none of the three.  This exhibits finite sampled escape toward the
coherent branch; it proves no stability or dynamical-selection theorem.

Second, 1,000 ensembles were swept from $N=\chaosNMin$ to $N=\chaosNMax$ at

\subsubsection*{Dale-balanced rescue and calibration limits}
Finite simulations separate synaptic balance from the magnitude of fluctuations.
The network has no self-edges, an Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi edge probability of $0.2$,
and excitatory/inhibitory presynaptic fractions of $80/20$.  Each row's positive
and negative weights sum to $g$ and $-g$, respectively.  With identical
frequencies, $D=1$, $N=500$, $g=1$, step size $0.01$ and duration $100$, three
seeded runs give zero-field mean order in
$[\frustrationBaselineMin,\frustrationBaselineMax]$, close to the
$1/\sqrt{N}$ reference.  Controls at $g\in\{0,1,2,4\}$ and
$N\in\{250,500,1000\}$ show why row balance alone is insufficient to prescribe
that reference: signed coupling can amplify finite-size fluctuations.
Order decreases with size in both the weak and strong controls while
$N\langle r^2\rangle$ remains above the independent-phase value of unity;
these finite sizes do not establish an asymptotic scaling law.
The $g=1$ regime is also below the positive-only mean-field threshold, so its
low order does not establish a causal role for inhibition in preventing coherence.

A uniform per-pair term $\epsilon$ adds mean-field strength
$K_{\mathrm{eff}}=N\epsilon$.  The operational crossing, defined by second-half
mean $r=0.2$ with all larger sampled couplings above it, occurs at
$K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D\in[\frustrationCouplingMin,\frustrationCouplingMax]$
across the three seeds (Fig.~\ref{fig:frustration-rescue}).  Each value

\subsection{Deterministic trajectories and their limits}

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak RotatingFrame.lean}):} The full potential includes the natural frequency term $-\sum_i \omega_i\theta_i$ that appears in the Lyapunov descent result \texttt{dV\_\allowbreak dt\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak zero} in the combinatorial thermodynamics module. With any $\omega_i \ne 0$ the full potential is unbounded below (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak unbounded\_\allowbreak below}), so it has no minimum for a phase-locked state to attain, so the reduced and full potentials must be distinguished. This file chains them by the standard rotating-frame reduction $\theta_i \mapsto \theta_i - \Omega t$. For a system of identical natural frequencies $\omega \equiv \Omega$: \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak rotate} sends a trajectory to a trajectory of the zero-frequency system \texttt{sys.reduced} --- the same system Theorem 5's \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} works with; \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak reduced} shows the two potentials coincide on it; \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak descent} transports the descent identity $\dot V = -\sum_i \dot\theta_i^2$ onto the potential above; and \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak chain} runs this through the minimum characterization to phase-locking and $r^2 = 1$. Phase differences, phase-locking and the order-parameter magnitude are proved frame-invariant (\texttt{rotate\_\allowbreak sub}, \texttt{is\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak rotate}, \texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak r\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak shift}), so no observable is lost. \texttt{Examples}~\S7 runs the chain end to end on a two-oscillator system, discharging every hypothesis, and exhibits a system whose full potential has no minimum. Scope: the reduction is exact only for identical frequencies. A spread of frequencies leaves residual detunings $\omega_i - \Omega$ in the reduced system, to which the unboundedness result still applies, and phase-locking then depends on $K_c$, which this file does not treat --- see the \texttt{Phase8\_\allowbreak SelfConsistency.lean} note above for what is proved about the threshold, and note that it concerns a stationary density rather than the trajectories this file transports.

\textit{Trajectories that run.} The deterministic initial-value problem admits a unique global solution through each initial state. \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak lipschitz} (\texttt{Phase3\_\allowbreak CombinatorialThermodynamics.lean}) proves the Kuramoto vector field globally Lipschitz with constant $2\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ --- $\sin$ is $1$-Lipschitz and the index set is finite, so there is no smallness caveat and the natural frequencies drop out. \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak unique} turns this into Picard--Lindel\"of uniqueness on all of $\mathbb{R}$: two solutions agreeing at one instant agree forever, in both time directions.

Existence follows by constructing solutions on arbitrary finite windows and gluing them using uniqueness. Mathlib's \texttt{IsPicardLindelof} caps a local solution's lifetime by the radius of the ball on which the field's bounds hold, divided by the field's sup norm there, and carries no global-in-time theorem; but \texttt{kuramotoField\_\allowbreak norm\_\allowbreak le} bounds this field by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ on the \emph{whole} state space, so the cap can be defeated by enlarging the ball after choosing the window, and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak on\_\allowbreak window} produces a solution on every $(t_0 - T, t_0 + T)$. Uniqueness on an interval makes the family coherent --- solutions on nested windows agree where both are defined --- so $\theta(t) := \alpha_{\lceil|t - t_0|\rceil}(t)$ is a solution on all of $\mathbb{R}$ (\texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists}), and \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak cauchy\_\allowbreak problem} states existence and uniqueness together as a single \texttt{ExistsUnique}: the trajectory predicate is inhabited exactly once for each initial state, rather than only by the solutions we write down by hand.

A two-oscillator solution illustrates the dynamics. On two oscillators with unit coupling and zero natural frequency the phase difference obeys $\dot\Delta = -2\sin\Delta$, whose solution $\Delta(t) = 2\arctan(c\,e^{-2t})$ splits symmetrically into an exact trajectory of the full system (\texttt{Examples}~\S15, \texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak trajectory}). At $c=1$ it starts a quarter turn out of phase (\texttt{pairRelax\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak zero}); the cosine of the phase difference tends to $1$, the order parameter tends to $1$ through the two-oscillator identity $r^2 = (1+\cos\Delta)/2$, and the dynamic potential tends to its global minimum. By the uniqueness result it is the only solution through its initial state.

Two general statements describe the long run, both for a zero-frequency system, which by \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak unbounded\_\allowbreak below} is the only case in which the potential is bounded below at all. \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} upgrades the instantaneous descent to monotonicity in $t$ --- \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak deriv\_\allowbreak nonpos} constrains each instant separately and does not by itself say the trajectory settles --- and with the uniform lower bound $-\tfrac12\sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$ monotone convergence gives \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak tendsto}: the potential converges along \emph{every} trajectory.

Integrating the Lyapunov identity gives the quantitative form, \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak eq}: energy dissipated over $[0,T]$ is exactly the potential dropped, so by \texttt{dissipation\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak tendsto} the improper integral $\int_0^\infty \sum_i \dot\theta_i^2\,dt$ converges, to the total drop. The proof uses the derivative identity in \texttt{HasDerivAt} form, which includes differentiability and permits application of the fundamental theorem of calculus. \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak dissipation} transports both to a uniform-frequency system.

Finiteness of that integral is not the same as $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$: an integrable function can spike forever on ever narrower intervals. Barbalat's lemma supplies the implication, proved here as \texttt{tendsto\_\allowbreak zero\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak lipschitz\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak integral\_\allowbreak le}: a non-negative Lipschitz function with $\int_0^T g$ bounded above tends to zero, because a spike of height $\varepsilon$ drags a window of width $\varepsilon/2K$ up with it and only finitely many such windows fit under a finite integral.

Its hypothesis is then checked for the dissipation rate without differentiating it: the velocities are bounded by $\sum_i|\omega_i| + \sum_{ij}|A_{ij}|$, the field is Lipschitz in the configuration, and the configuration is Lipschitz in time because its derivative \emph{is} the bounded field (\texttt{trajectory\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}, by the mean value theorem, with no invariant region needed since the bound holds on the whole state space). The conclusion is \texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak zero}: along \emph{every} trajectory of a zero-frequency system, $\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2 \to 0$; equivalently each $\dot\theta_i \to 0$ and the vector field along the trajectory tends to $0$ in the state space. The statement applies to arbitrary trajectories under these hypotheses, and \texttt{Examples}~\S15 applies it to the relaxing pair, whose velocity starts at $-1$ and tends to zero.

At zero frequencies, the flow is the negative gradient of its dynamic potential. The identity $\dot V = -\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$ is formalized by \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak hasDerivAt}. The minimizers of this potential are characterized by phase-locking. \texttt{lojasiewicz\_\allowbreak estimate} adds the missing ingredient: on the region where every pairwise phase difference lies within a quarter turn, $2a^2W \le \left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)\sum_i\dot\theta_i^2$, where $W$ is the excess of the potential over its minimum and $a > 0$ is a lower bound for the coupling. Its proof uses no spectral gap and no convexity, only two extremal indices: at the site $m$ carrying the largest phase every coupling term has $\theta_j - \theta_m \in [-D,0]$ with $D \le \pi/2$ the spread, so every term is non-positive, and the term from the site carrying the smallest phase is at most $-a\sin D$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak max\_\allowbreak le}); against $W \le \tfrac12\left(\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\right)(1-\cos D)$ the estimate follows from $\sin^2 D = (1-\cos D)(1+\cos D)$ and $\cos D \ge 0$, which is the only place the quarter-turn hypothesis is used.

The initial energy bound keeps the trajectory in this region. Because all terms of the excess are non-negative, a single pair satisfies $\tfrac12 a(1-\cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)) \le W$, so an initial excess below $a/2$ --- which \texttt{dynamic\_\allowbreak potential\_\allowbreak antitone} preserves --- keeps every pairwise cosine strictly positive for all time. A difference escaping $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ would have to cross $\pi/2$ in absolute value by the intermediate value theorem, and there the cosine vanishes; so the quarter-turn region is forward invariant (\texttt{spread\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak small}). Continuity on the connected half-line thus pins the phase differences to that branch.

The differential inequality $\dot W \le -cW$ with $c = 2a^2/\sum_{ij}A_{ij}$ then integrates, in the elementary form ``$W(t)e^{ct}$ has non-positive derivative'', to $W(t) \le W(0)e^{-ct}$ (\texttt{excess\_\allowbreak decay}). The rate transfers to the velocity through $|\sin d| \le \sqrt{2(1-\cos d)}$, giving $|\dot\theta_i(t)| \le Be^{-ct/2}$ (\texttt{velocity\_\allowbreak abs\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak exp}), which is integrable on $(0,\infty)$; since $\theta_i(t) = \theta_i(0) + \int_0^t\dot\theta_i$, the trajectory converges (\texttt{phase\_\allowbreak tendsto}), and $W \to 0$ forces the limit to be phase-locked, hence a global minimiser with $r^2 = 1$ (\texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}). \texttt{rotating\_\allowbreak frame\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum} transports it to uniform frequencies, where what converges is the relative configuration.

The two hypotheses are on the initial data and neither can be removed: splay and twisted configurations are equilibria too, so ``every trajectory reaches the phase-locked state'' is false, and what is proved is the arc condition version --- which the two-oscillator witness satisfies for every $c$, since $2\arctan$ lands in $(-\pi,\pi)$.

\texttt{Examples}~\S17 runs the theorem on three sites, where the method of \S15 is unavailable because the system has no closed-form solution: the trajectory is produced by \texttt{is\_\allowbreak kuramoto\_\allowbreak trajectory\_\allowbreak exists} from the initial data $(0,0,\tfrac12)$, which is not phase-locked, and its limit by the convergence theorem as $\theta(0) + \int_0^\infty\dot\theta$; the numerical content of checking the hypotheses is $\cos\tfrac12 > \tfrac34$, supplied by \texttt{Real.cos\_\allowbreak bound}.

\subsection{Discrete coupling and mesh refinement}

\textit{From continuous to discrete coupling:} The coupling matrix $A_{ij}$ above is not postulated. A triangulation of the substrate assigns to each edge $(u,v)$ a region of the manifold, and the coupling weight is the Lebesgue--Bochner integral of the scalar magnitude of the stress-energy tensor over that region (\texttt{edge\_\allowbreak weight}); symmetry and non-negativity of $A$ are then proved rather than assumed. The bridge back is mesh refinement: as the regions shrink, the discrete energy $\tfrac12\sum_{u,v} \mu(R_{uv})\,f(x_u)$ should converge to the continuous energy $\int_S f$. Stated as a conjecture over the bare triangulation class, that is false: nothing there forces the regions to cover the manifold, empty regions have diameter zero, and the everywhere-empty triangulation refutes it. Stated correctly it is a theorem (\texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement\_\allowbreak convergence}), quantified over a sequence of triangulations of a region $S$ whose regions are measurable, pairwise disjoint across distinct edges, covering, and anchored to the embedded vertices, with mesh size tending to zero. The fixed-mesh error is bounded by $\varepsilon\,\mu(S)$, with $\varepsilon$ a modulus of continuity of the integrand, and the symmetric double sum is proved equal to the midpoint rule over unordered edges.

Two limitations follow. The theorem gives convergence, not the $O(1/N^2)$ rate reported by \texttt{mesh\_\allowbreak refinement.py}. And \texttt{TriangulatedManifold} itself is bare data: it pairs a complex, an embedding and a family of edge regions without relating them, so the geometric conditions live in a separate predicate \texttt{IsRegularTriangulation}. Because those conditions are in tension --- cells disjoint yet covering, every vertex anchored to each of its edges --- \texttt{Examples}~\S6 exhibits an instance: the uniform partition of $[0,1)$ into $N$ half-open cells, on which the convergence theorem is instantiated and the approximations are checked to genuinely change with $N$.

The discretization structure \texttt{DiscreteThermodynamics} \emph{carries} that predicate as a field, which is what makes its edge weights integrals over a geometrically constrained family rather than over arbitrary sets, with three consequences for the coupling weights. \texttt{weight\_\allowbreak self}: a vertex has no edge to itself, so the coupling matrix has zero diagonal. \texttt{face\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak weight\_\allowbreak ne\_\allowbreak zero}: a nonzero weight $w(u,v)$ forces $\{u,v\}$ to be an actual face of the complex, so the discretization cannot invent couplings between vertices the triangulation does not join. And \texttt{total\_\allowbreak weight\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak setIntegral}: $\tfrac12\sum_u\sum_v w(u,v) = \int_S |T|$, i.e.\ the total coupling strength is exactly the scalar stress-energy of the region covered --- nothing counted twice (disjointness), nothing dropped (covering). That last identity is the precise sense in which the coupling matrix is derived from the continuum rather than posited, and it is exactly what the everywhere-empty triangulation violates.

\texttt{Examples}~\S6 witnesses the structure on the same grid: each consecutive edge carries weight $1/N$, and the weights sum to $1$ for every $N$. The witness is one-dimensional and its stress-energy tensor is constant, so it establishes satisfiability, not a physically interesting discretization.

One field of the structure is deliberately left free and is marked \texttt{[MODELLING]}: \texttt{scalar\_\allowbreak magnitude}, the scalar read off the stress-energy tensor, constrained only to be non-negative. A $(0,2)$ tensor has no canonical scalar --- the trace, an observer's energy density and the tensor norm are different functions, and the structure carries neither a metric nor a frame --- so imposing one would pin a choice rather than derive it, and the three statements above hold for every non-negative choice, so it would prove nothing new.

That the choice is nonetheless a physical input is stated as a theorem rather than left as a remark: \S6 exhibits a second instance over the same triangulation, tensor and regions, differing only in that field, for which the partition identity again holds while every edge weight differs (\texttt{gridThermo\_\allowbreak magnitude\_\allowbreak matters}). The remaining constraint, non-negativity, is the discrete shadow of an energy condition and is what makes the induced couplings a legal Kuramoto coupling.

\subsection{The fold-and-cross proof of strict decrease}
\label{sec:fold}
Uniqueness of the coherent branch is the injectivity of $E(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\sin^2\theta]$ on $(0,\infty)$, and what is proved is the stronger statement that $E$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\infty)$ (\texttt{vonMises\-SRatio\_\allowbreak strictAntiOn}). A folding and crossing argument proves the result without differentiation. Differentiating the exponential family in its natural parameter gives $E'(a) = -\mathrm{Cov}_a(\cos^2\theta,\cos\theta)$, which costs differentiation under the integral sign and leaves a covariance that is not sign-definite pointwise: symmetrising with an independent copy gives $\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[(X-X')^2(X+X')]$ with $X = \cos\theta$, an integrand changing sign with $X + X'$. The target is kept as the two-point inequality $C_2(a)Z(b) < C_2(b)Z(a)$ for $a < b$, and two moves settle it.

\emph{Fold first}: the two reflections that proved $I_2 \ge 0$ carry both integrals onto $[0,\pi/2]$, replacing $e^{a\cos\theta}$ by $W(a,\theta) = 2\cosh(a\cos\theta)$ and confining $\cos\theta$ to $[0,1]$, after which $\cos 2x - \cos 2y$ and $W(b,x)W(a,y) - W(a,x)W(b,y)$ have the same sign, both being governed by $\cos x$ against $\cos y$. This is the symmetrisation $X \mapsto -X$ that the covariance form needed the tilt to perform, done once at the start; its analytic content is one identity, $2\cosh X\cosh Y = \cosh(X{+}Y)+\cosh(X{-}Y)$ with $\cosh$ increasing in $|\cdot|$.

\emph{Then cross}: instead of integrating that inequality over a square, let $\alpha$ be the mean of $\cos 2x$ under $W(a,\cdot)$ and $x_0 \in [0,\pi/2]$ a point where $\cos 2x_0 = \alpha$, supplied by the intermediate value theorem; evaluating at $y = x_0$ and integrating in $x$ alone gives the result, since the term carrying $W(b,x_0)$ vanishes by the choice of $\alpha$. No product measure and no Fubini appear.

Run with $\cos\theta$ in place of $\sin^2\!\theta$ the same argument needs no fold --- $R(a) = \mathbb{E}_a[\cos\theta]$ is the mean of the variable the family is tilted by --- and gives \texttt{besselRatio\_\allowbreak strictMono}: $R$ is strictly increasing on all of $\mathbb{R}$, the monotonicity of the Bessel ratio.

\section{Global Sections and the Unity of Consciousness}
Phase synchronization does not imply overlap compatibility. The model represents global consistency by gluing compatible local descriptions; its identification with conscious unity remains a separate stipulation.

\textbf{Theorem 5 (Sheaf-Theoretic Unity):} Given the sheafification of finite spatial measures over a continuous substrate $X$, local measures that agree on all spatial intersections $U \cap V$ uniquely glue into a global spatial measure. The overlap agreement is an independent physical hypothesis; phase-locking does not imply it. This is not a theorem about arbitrary probability marginals over decoded contents, for which compatible overlaps need not determine a unique global joint distribution \cite{abramsky2011}.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak GlobalSection.lean}):} The headline gluing theorem separates two inputs. The class field \texttt{Thermodynamic\-Cover.\allowbreak thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} places the phase field at the potential minimum, while \texttt{LocalSection\-Synchronization.\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak agrees\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak eq} independently requires equal-phase patches to agree on overlaps. Only the second supplies the compatible family consumed by the sheaf theorem; no dynamics in the development derives it.

What the second one says can be stated without the sheaf: on a discrete substrate two patch sections have the same restriction to their overlap exactly when they assign the same mass to every site they share (\texttt{restrict\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak densityOn\_\allowbreak eqOn}), so the hypothesis is that patches at a common phase carry a common local density where they meet. It excludes data the rest of the structure admits: a two-patch cover of the three-site substrate at one phase, with profiles differing at the shared site, satisfies every other requirement and fails this one (\texttt{overlap\_\allowbreak agreement\_\allowbreak fails}). It does not require patches to agree away from their overlaps --- the three-patch witness carries three pairwise-different profiles.

The equilibrium condition can be obtained from a convergence result for a class of initial data: \texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak Equilibrium\-\allowbreak Bridge.lean} supplies \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.of\-\allowbreak Convergent\-\allowbreak Trajectory} from \texttt{kuramoto\_\allowbreak tendsto\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak minimum}, and \texttt{Examples}~\S17.1 is a three-patch cover built over the resulting phase field.

The cover class also has an independent instance (\texttt{Examples}, \S4), so the theorem is not conditional on a structure of unknown realizability. Its three-site substrate has two overlapping patches meeting at one site; local sections are germ families of the phase-indexed measure $\mu_\theta = (1+\cos\theta)^{+}\,\delta_{\mathrm{mid}}$ in the sheafification of the probability presheaf. The family is provably non-constant in $\theta$ (\texttt{phaseMeasure\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak const}), and \texttt{thermodynamic\_\allowbreak equilibrium} follows from \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak locked\_\allowbreak minimizes\_\allowbreak potential}.

Two further results specify the phase and section data. The theorem \texttt{potential\_\allowbreak min\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} (\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak KuramotoDynamics.lean}) characterizes the minimizers of the reduced potential in both directions, from which \texttt{Thermodynamic\-\allowbreak Cover.\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked} follows for every instance. The witness in \texttt{Examples}~\S13 carries a non-constant phase field and a non-uniform invariant measure, with patch-local densities read from the sections through \texttt{densityOn}; agreement at the shared site is an equation between separately computed numbers. The limit is structural: phase-locking equates phases, while agreement of the associated sections remains the independent class field named above.

The gluing construction assumes local overlap agreement and derives both existence and uniqueness of the global section. The lemma \texttt{probability\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique} takes a compatible family to its unique global extension; \texttt{ThermodynamicCover.invariantMeasure} denotes that section. The construction uses local data, not a global state supplied as a premise.

\subsection{Approximate overlap agreement}

Theorem~5 admits no tolerance: the sheaf condition is an equation. Under an
$\varepsilon$-tolerance neither existence nor uniqueness of a global section
survives, and what replaces them is a selection rule with an explicit constant.
The results are stated for a finite substrate whose local states are nonnegative
site masses, with the uniform distance $\max_{x\in U}|f(x)-g(x)|$ on the sites a
pair of patches shares (\texttt{ApproximateGluing.\allowbreak profileDist},
\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak GlobalSection.lean}).

Let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ be a finite cover, let $s_i$ be the local profiles with
$\mathrm{dist}(s_i,s_j)\leq\varepsilon$ on $U_i\cap U_j$ for \emph{every} pair
(\texttt{Compatible}), and let $w_i\geq0$ vanish off $U_i$ and satisfy
$\sum_i w_i(x)=1$ at every site (\texttt{IsPartition}). The selection is
\[
(\mathrm{select}\;w\,s)(x)\;=\;\sum_{i} w_i(x)\,s_i(x).
\]
It depends on the profiles only through their values on their own patches
(\texttt{select\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak eqOn}), so the
bookkeeping values a patch carries elsewhere do not enter. Four estimates follow.
The selection is within $\varepsilon$ of each patch on that patch
(\texttt{select\_\allowbreak close}); two partitions subordinate to the same
cover select states at distance at most $\varepsilon$
(\texttt{select\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}); any two profiles fitting
every patch to tolerance $\delta$ lie within $2\delta$ of each other
(\texttt{approximate\_\allowbreak diameter\_\allowbreak le}); and at
$\varepsilon=0$ the selection is the unique profile fitting all patches
(\texttt{select\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}).

The constant in the second estimate is $C(N)=1$ at every cover multiplicity $N$,
rather than the $N\varepsilon$ a term-by-term count would give: the weights sum
to one rather than to the number of patches active at a site. This is a
statement in the uniform mass metric and not in total variation. A feature
$F$ that is $L$-Lipschitz on profiles therefore satisfies
$\mathrm{dist}\big(F(\mathrm{select}\;w\,s),F(\mathrm{select}\;v\,s)\big)\leq
L\varepsilon$ (\texttt{feature\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}), which is
the resolution condition an interpretation at scale $\delta$ must meet; the
theorem chooses no decoder.

The three-site cortex witnesses both halves (\texttt{Examples}~\S21). Its three
two-site patches have multiplicity two, and the family \texttt{apxW} is
\S17.1's data with one patch reporting $\varepsilon$ more mass everywhere it can
see. Two partitions --- each site assigned to the lowest, respectively the
highest, indexed patch containing it --- select states at distance exactly
$\varepsilon$ (\texttt{apx\_\allowbreak select\_\allowbreak dist}), so the
constant is attained and cannot be improved at this multiplicity. For every
positive $\varepsilon$ that same family has no exact global section at all
(\texttt{apx\_\allowbreak no\_\allowbreak global\_\allowbreak section}): two
patches see the site \texttt{right} and disagree there, so selection stands in
for a gluing that does not exist. The corresponding sections fail the class
field of Theorem~5 exactly as the two-patch fence does
(\texttt{apxSection\_\allowbreak overlap\_\allowbreak fails}), and the tolerance
cannot be dropped to zero
(\texttt{apxW\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak compatible\_\allowbreak zero}).

Checking a spanning subfamily of the overlaps is not checking the family. The
family \texttt{chainW} agrees exactly on the overlaps $(0,1)$ and $(1,2)$ and is
off by $2\varepsilon$ on $(0,2)$; its two selections are $2\varepsilon$ apart
(\texttt{chainW\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak compatible},
\texttt{chainW\_\allowbreak select\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak gt}), so the
hypothesis quantifies over all pairs. On the same witness the total-mass readout
moves by $2\varepsilon$ between the two selections, within the $3\varepsilon$
its Lipschitz constant permits
(\texttt{totalMass\_\allowbreak apx\_\allowbreak dist},
\texttt{totalMass\_\allowbreak apx\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}): the
selection dependence of a readout is a real effect and not an artefact of a
loose bound. At $\varepsilon=0$ the three-patch family of \S17.1 satisfies the
relaxed hypothesis and the selection returns its glued profile
(\texttt{trioW\_\allowbreak compatible},
\texttt{trioW\_\allowbreak glue\_\allowbreak unique}).

\subsection{Contents driven by observation}
\label{sec:content-dynamics}

The agreement above is a hypothesis on a supplied family. What a mechanism
would have to do is change the family, so the contents are carried as fields
$s_i:A\to\mathbb{R}$ on the patches of a declared cover and each patch's field
is mixed with that patch's observation at gain $\eta\in[0,1]$,
$s_i\mapsto(1-\eta)s_i+\eta o_i$. Disagreement across an overlap then obeys
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon'\leq(1-\eta)\varepsilon+\eta\delta,
 \label{eq:content-residual}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon$ the contents' mismatch and $\delta$ the observations' own
(\texttt{update\_\allowbreak residual},
\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak Content\-Dynamics}).
Two consequences follow and no more. Observations that agree on their
overlaps preserve exact agreement, and contract any initial mismatch to
$(1-\eta)^{n}\varepsilon$ after $n$ of them, even when the value they agree
on moves (\texttt{update\_\allowbreak preserves},
\texttt{run\_\allowbreak residual}). Observations that disagree by $\delta$
leave a residual floor at $\delta$, which
Eq.~\eqref{eq:content-residual} charges rather than absorbs.

A readout names which patch to read at each site. Where that choice covers the
site, the readout is within $\varepsilon$ of every patch that sees it
(\texttt{readout\_\allowbreak residual}); at $\varepsilon=0$ it extends every
patch and is the only field that does
(\texttt{readout\_\allowbreak agrees}, \texttt{readout\_\allowbreak unique}).
Coverage is a hypothesis of those theorems rather than a property of a
pre-existing global field, which is the same discipline the selection results
above follow. The mass profiles of this section are such a family read through
the coercion, at the same tolerance
(\texttt{ApproximateGluing.compatible\_\allowbreak pointwise}), so the two
agreement predicates in the development are one predicate.

Observations agreeing only to $\delta$ are covered by the same recursion.
Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:content-residual} gives
$(1-\eta)^{n}\varepsilon+\delta$ exactly, not as an estimate
(\texttt{run\_\allowbreak residual\_\allowbreak floor}), so the initial mismatch
contracts geometrically onto the observations' own disagreement and stops there.
The floor does not decay with $n$. Mixing cannot manufacture agreement that the
observations lack, which is why the next subsection, and not the update rule, is
what carries the mechanism.

The contents remain real-valued fields identified with no neural variable and
with no section of the probability sheaf.

The scope is narrow in three ways. The state space is finite spatial mass
profiles, where restriction is functorial; nothing here bears on matching
marginals of decoded contents \cite{abramsky2011}. Nothing produces the
approximate agreement, selects the cover or selects the weights, so the
interpretive cost of the route is that unity becomes a choice at resolution
$\varepsilon$. Theorem~5, the \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} instances and the
composed chain retain their exact agreement hypothesis.

\subsection{Agreement acquired from coherence}
\label{sec:coherence-agreement}

What makes two patches observe compatibly is the coordination of the
oscillators they read. The order parameter of Sec.~\ref{sec:content-dynamics}'s
population is not merely correlated with agreement; it bounds it.

Write $N$ for the number of oscillators and $r$ for the order parameter. The
identity
\begin{equation}
 N^{2}r^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)
 \label{eq:order-mean-cos}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{order\_\allowbreak parameter\_\allowbreak r\_\allowbreak sq\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak mean\_\allowbreak cos},
\texttt{Phase4\_\allowbreak Kuramoto\-Dynamics.lean}) rewrites $N^2(1-r^2)$ as
$\sum_{i,j}\bigl[1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)\bigr]$, a sum of nonnegative terms.
A single term is therefore at most the whole sum:
\begin{equation}
 1-\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j)\le N^{2}\bigl(1-r^{2}\bigr)
 \label{eq:cos-gap}
\end{equation}
for \emph{every} pair (\texttt{cos\_\allowbreak gap\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherence}).
The factor $N^{2}$ is not slack that better bookkeeping would remove: one
oscillator out of $N$ placed badly moves $r$ by $O(1/N)$, so a pointwise
guarantee drawn from a population average has to pay for it.

A phase is an angle, so a content that is a function of phase must be a
function of the circle point $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$; a model reading
$\theta$ as a real number is not $2\pi$-periodic and is not a function of the
phase at all. In the chord metric on that circle,
$\lVert\cdot\rVert^2=2(1-\cos\Delta)$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:cos-gap} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{chord}(\theta_i,\theta_j)\le\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^{2}}
 \label{eq:chord-bound}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{chord\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherence}). An
encoder declared Lipschitz at constant $L$ in that metric, which is what
\texttt{Lipschitz\-Encoder} states, then gives observations compatible at residual
$L\sqrt{2}\,N\sqrt{1-r^{2}}$
(\texttt{compatible\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherence},
\texttt{Phase5\_\allowbreak Content\-Dynamics.lean}). No compatibility premise
appears anywhere in that statement: the residual is computed from the phases.
At $r^{2}=1$ it is zero and the observations agree exactly
(\texttt{compatible\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak phase\_\allowbreak locked}), so
perfect locking is this scale's endpoint rather than a separate case, and the
readout results of Sec.~\ref{sec:content-dynamics} apply to a family nothing
assumed to be compatible.

The result is fenced on both sides. Two antiphase oscillators have $r^{2}=0$,
computed through Eq.~\eqref{eq:order-mean-cos} rather than asserted, and at that
coherence the two patches demonstrably disagree
(\texttt{Examples/\allowbreak AgencyFoundations.lean}), so the derivation is not
returning a vacuous zero residual. The bound is also not tight there: it gives
$2\sqrt{2}$ where the actual disagreement is $2$, which is the $N^{2}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cos-gap} being paid.

The encoder and its constant are declared hardware, in the same sense as the
actuator's mode profiles. The agreement is not declared: it is derived from the
phases, which is what separates this result from the residual bound above.
Identifying these scalar contents with neural variables, and testing whether
their agreement matters for experience, remain the empirical work of
Sec.~\ref{sec:content-dynamics}'s closing scope.

\section{Reflexive Topology and the Self}
Self-representation is an additional relation between the glued global state and an encoding within it. The model specifies an encoding and readout whose composite fixes that state; contraction gives uniqueness. A dissipation bound imposes neither the encoding nor its accuracy.

\textbf{Theorem 6 (Conditional Auto-Resonance):} On a nonempty complete metric space of global sections, a self-prediction map has a unique fixed point if it is Lipschitz at the independently assumed rate $q=e^{-(K-K_c)\tau/2}<1$. A state with reconstruction residual at most $\varepsilon$ is within $\varepsilon/(1-q)$ of that fixed point. Coherence supplies $K>K_c$; it does not supply the Lipschitz law. To identify this fixed point with the section glued on a given cover, that section must itself be fixed by the map.

\subsection{States and their metric}

The construction in \texttt{Phase6\_\allowbreak ReflexiveTopology.lean} applies Banach's theorem to a complete metric space of global sections. The three-site witness in \texttt{Examples}, \S10, equips these sections with a metric derived from their spatial measures.

On a discrete substrate, every point has a smallest open neighbourhood. Restriction preserves point masses, so a germ at a site determines a mass there. The map \texttt{massEquivOn} identifies sections over an open set $U$ with mass profiles on $U$: injectivity follows because restriction to a point determines the germ, and surjectivity because a finite measure realizes every profile on the finite substrate. The global case is \texttt{massEquiv}. This correspondence also constructs the patch-local sections used by the compatibility witnesses.

The distance between two global sections is the largest difference between their site masses. Completeness follows by constructing the section represented by the limit of a Cauchy sequence of profiles. On this finite substrate, the metric is equivalent to total variation within a factor of the number of sites, but is not equal to it. Distinct sections at distance $1/2$ witness its nontriviality (\texttt{exists\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak one}).

The metric matters to the fixed-point argument. Under the discrete $0/1$ metric, every contraction is constant (\texttt{contracting\_\allowbreak implies\_\allowbreak const}). The witness instead uses uniform distance between densities. It makes no claim of equivalence with the L\'evy--Prokhorov metric: on a discrete substrate, thickening by $\varepsilon<1$ leaves a set unchanged, and averaging a measure with a baseline need not contract that metric.

\subsection{Encoding, readout and information loss}

A \texttt{ReflexiveBoundary} carries an encoding into a local region, called the avatar, and a readout back to global sections. Its predictive map is their composite. This ties reconstruction to the information passing through that region. A map that reads the whole field cannot serve as a witness unless it factors through the stated local encoding.

The separate predicate \texttt{IsRestrictionResonance rb} requires the encoding to be presheaf restriction to the avatar region. Under this condition,
\[
F(s)=\operatorname{readout}(s|_U),\qquad
s_* = \operatorname{readout}(s_*|_U)
\]
at a fixed point. The second equality is \texttt{self\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak readout\_\allowbreak restrict}. The more general \texttt{self\_\allowbreak mem\_\allowbreak range\_\allowbreak readout} places a fixed point in the range of the readout even without restriction resonance. These statements express what the local encoding reconstructs; restriction resonance is an assumption on the boundary, not a consequence of contraction.

Local readings can also determine a global state collectively. If a family of resonant avatar regions covers the substrate, equal readings at every avatar imply equal global states (\texttt{eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak avatar\_\allowbreak eq}). The proof uses the same gluing uniqueness as Theorem 5. A single avatar can discard information away from its region, so collective reconstruction from a cover must be distinguished from reconstruction through one region at a fixed point.

\subsection{The contraction hypothesis}

For $\tau>0$, the assumed factor is
\[
q=\exp\!\left(-\frac{K-K_c}{2}\tau\right),\qquad K_c=2D.
\]
The lemmas \texttt{resonanceRate\_\allowbreak lt\_\allowbreak one} and \texttt{one\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak resonanceRate} split the parameter plane at the threshold. Above it, a map Lipschitz at this factor has a unique fixed point (\texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak supercritical}). The bridge \texttt{self\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak coherent\_\allowbreak order\_\allowbreak parameter} obtains the required inequality from the existence of a coherent order parameter. At or below threshold, the specified factor fails the strict bound required by \texttt{ContractingWith} (\texttt{not\_\allowbreak contractingWith\_\allowbreak resonanceRate}); this argument is unavailable there.

The rate is motivated by the mean-field linearization near the incoherent state, $\dot r=\tfrac12(K-K_c)r+O(r^3)$. Linearizing the cubic normal form about its coherent branch gives relaxation rate $K-K_c$, twice as fast. The assumed factor $q$ is therefore larger than the coherent-branch relaxation factor. Both cross one at $K=K_c$. This comparison motivates a choice of bound; it does not derive a Lipschitz law for a neural encoding or readout from phase dynamics.

Banach's theorem establishes uniqueness for the specified map. E89 additionally requires the section glued on the specified cover to be fixed by that map, making it the unique fixed point. The theorem \texttt{chain} uses this equality and the contraction law but does not require restriction resonance. The interpretation through $s|_U$ needs that additional predicate.

\subsection{Approximate reconstruction}

Let $F(s_*)=s_*$ and $d(s,F(s))\leq\varepsilon$. The triangle inequality and the Lipschitz law give
\[
d(s,s_*)\leq d(s,F(s))+d(F(s),F(s_*))
\leq\varepsilon+q\,d(s,s_*),
\]
hence $d(s,s_*)\leq\varepsilon/(1-q)$. The theorem
\texttt{ReflexiveBoundary.approximate\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak bound}
applies Mathlib's \texttt{ContractingWith.dist\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak fixedPoint}.
An estimate about a supplied fixed point requires neither completeness nor a separate existence proof; those hypotheses enter the existing Banach construction. The approximate states can form a set while the exact fixed point remains unique.

If $d(t,s)\leq\delta$, the same fixed map satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
d(t,F(t))&\leq\varepsilon+(1+q)\delta,\\
d(t,s_*)&\leq\frac{\varepsilon+(1+q)\delta}{1-q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are \texttt{prediction\_\allowbreak residual\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}
and \texttt{approximate\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak dist\_\allowbreak le}.
The errors add before division by the contraction gap, whose inverse amplifies the bound near threshold. The displacement bound is supplied; this result does not derive approximate gluing or weaken Theorem~5's overlap hypothesis.

For $K>2D$, $\tau>0$ and $\varepsilon\geq0$,
\texttt{approximate\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak supercritical}
instantiates the estimate at the prescribed rate, and
\texttt{resonance\_\allowbreak error\_\allowbreak radius\_\allowbreak bounds} proves
\[
\frac{2\varepsilon}{(K-2D)\tau}
\;\leq\;\frac{\varepsilon}{1-e^{-(K-2D)\tau/2}}
\;\leq\;\frac{2\varepsilon}{(K-2D)\tau}+\varepsilon.
\]
The proof uses $1-e^{-x}\leq x$ and $x/(1+x)\leq1-e^{-x}$ for $x=(K-2D)\tau/2>0$, both consequences of $1+x\leq e^x$. Thus the radius has leading behaviour $2\varepsilon/((K-2D)\tau)$ and diverges as $K\downarrow2D$ at fixed positive $\varepsilon$ and $\tau$. This concerns the tolerance bound, not an actual state's error. Zero residual still gives zero distance at every supercritical coupling. The map, metric and Lipschitz law remain specified inputs; no result here models a changing readout or constructs a biological encoding.

\subsection{A nonconstant witness and its controls}

On the three-site cortex, the avatar is one site. The encoding reads its mass (\texttt{avatarRead}), and \texttt{avatarReadout} reconstructs a profile by averaging the reading with a baseline. The resulting map is nonconstant, has a unique fixed point, and contracts by one half of the difference visible at the avatar (\texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak const}, \texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak fixed\_\allowbreak unique}, \texttt{cortexPredict\_\allowbreak dist}). Differences elsewhere are invisible to this map. The whole-field averaging map \texttt{relax} therefore cannot replace it as a witness for a one-site encoding.

The witness takes $K=3$, $D=1$ and $\tau=2\ln2$, so $q=1/2$ (\texttt{cortexResonanceRate}). These are parameters chosen for the example, not calibrated cortical values. The boundary \texttt{cortexReflexive} performs restriction and satisfies resonance. Its avatar distinguishes the silent field from the baseline, as shown by \texttt{cortexReflexive\_\allowbreak restrict\_\allowbreak ne}. One avatar per site gives a covering family and reconstruction from the three readings (\texttt{cortex\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak avatar\_\allowbreak eq}). The same map at $K=1$ exercises the failure of contraction at the prescribed subcritical factor (\texttt{cortexSubcritical\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak contracting}).

Replacing the encoding by a constant makes the composite constant. It still has a unique fixed point, namely the readout of that constant (\texttt{constResonance\_\allowbreak existsUnique\_\allowbreak self}). In the cortical control, this fixed point differs from the original baseline (\texttt{cortexBlind\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak ne}), and distinct states have identical blinded readings (\texttt{cortexBlind\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak determined}). A constant encoding fails restriction resonance whenever two states differ on the avatar region (\texttt{constResonance\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak isRestrictionResonance}). The control thus distinguishes fixed-point uniqueness from sensitivity to the encoded state.

The approximate bound is sharp on the same one-site-avatar witness. The silent profile has $d(s,F(s))=1$ and $d(s,s_*)=2$, attaining $\varepsilon/(1-q)$ at $\varepsilon=1$, $q=1/2$
(\texttt{cortexSilent\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak residual},
\texttt{cortex\_\allowbreak approximate\_\allowbreak self\_\allowbreak sharp}).
At $q=1$, \texttt{cortexIdentity} instead takes the whole substrate as its avatar and uses identity encoding and readout. It is nonexpansive, and the silent and baseline profiles are distinct fixed points
(\texttt{cortex\_\allowbreak nonexpansive\_\allowbreak not\_\allowbreak unique}).
This control establishes the failure of nonexpansive uniqueness on the same metric; it is not a model of compression into a proper local region.

\subsection{What a biological interpretation must add}

The readout, its contraction law and the restriction-resonance condition are choices or hypotheses of a model. No field dynamics here produces them. The squared-drift objective is absent from the fixed-point result, so its minimizer is not identified with the self-model's fixed point.

The article's cup example illustrates the further content assignment: the represented state would include the scene and the perceiver's relation to it, and the encoding would need a demonstrated role in representing that relation. The three-site witness establishes mathematical realizability, not such a role. A nonconstant reconstruction and a unique fixed point alone do not establish a perspective or experience. Interventions on a candidate neural encoding would have to test the functional interpretation separately from the equality defining its fixed point.

\section{Coupling support and continuum observables}
Two mathematical comparisons distinguish a restriction on wiring support from a restriction on the measure used to evaluate a kernel.

\textbf{Theorem 7 (Resource-matched coupling reallocation):} If a fixed wiring support excludes a pair whose phase correlation exceeds the maximum over its wired pairs, a coupling using that pair can achieve greater total correlation at the same resource budget. This is a conditional support comparison, not a general inequality between biological and silicon hardware.

\textit{Coupling reallocation (\texttt{Phase7}):} The lemma \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak better\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak allocation}, wrapped as the theorem \texttt{continuous\_\allowbreak beats\_\allowbreak rigid\_\allowbreak topology}, proves the reallocation step: given a coupling matrix satisfying \texttt{is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} (positive weight on a less phase-correlated pair while a more correlated pair exists), there is another valid coupling with identical total resources and strictly greater total phase correlation.

\textit{Lean 4 Implementation (\texttt{Phase7\_\allowbreak Rigidity.lean}):} Two results specify the scope of the comparison. \emph{(i) Rigidity as a support constraint.} \texttt{RealizableIn S R A} says $A$ is a valid coupling meeting resource budget $R$ and vanishing on every pair the architecture has no wire for --- weights tunable, wiring fixed. \texttt{totalCorrelation\_\allowbreak le\_\allowbreak of\_\allowbreak realizable} proves no realizable coupling exceeds $R\cdot M$ when $M$ bounds phase correlation over the wired pairs, and \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak realizable\_\allowbreak pair} attains it. \texttt{rigid\_\allowbreak is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} then \emph{derives} \texttt{is\_\allowbreak strictly\_\allowbreak suboptimal} for every realizable coupling, whenever some pair $(k,l)$ the wiring misses has correlation exceeding $M$: the hypothesis Theorem 7 would otherwise have to assume is a consequence of the wiring pattern. \texttt{rigid\_\allowbreak gap} gives the quantitative form, a gain of at least $R\,(\cos(\theta_l-\theta_k) - M) > 0$ at equal budget. \texttt{Examples}~\S8 witnesses this on three sites wired only to the maximally anti-correlated pair, where the gap is exactly $2$, and runs \texttt{exists\_\allowbreak better\_\allowbreak coupling\_\allowbreak allocation} on it with no assumed hypothesis. \emph{(ii) Continuity as a measure-theoretic distinction.} \texttt{fieldCorrelation} integrates a kernel against $\mu\otimes\mu$ as in Derivation 4. A finitely-sited architecture is a \texttt{Finset X}; \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_\allowbreak sited\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak zero} proves that on an atomless substrate such a kernel contributes exactly zero, for every phase field and every choice of weights, while \texttt{fieldCorrelation\_\allowbreak patchKernel} gives $c\,\mu(U)^2$ for a patch. \texttt{sited\_\allowbreak architecture\_\allowbreak below\_\allowbreak field\_\allowbreak optimum} combines them; \texttt{Examples}~\S8 instantiates it on $\mathbb{R}$ with a thousand units of weight at the origin against the unit interval.

\textbf{Scope caveat.} Result (i) is resource-matched and not question-begging, but it turns on fixed wiring support, not on continuity: an architecture wired to the best pair is not beaten. Result (ii) is a genuine type-level separation but is \emph{not} resource-matched, since a measure-zero substrate carries zero resource in the $\mu\otimes\mu$ sense as well; what it establishes is that the continuum coupling energy cannot register a finitely-sited architecture at all, which is a claim about the model's observable rather than about computational power. Neither result formalizes manifold structure or lattice geometry. Neither result establishes that digital hardware cannot approximate field dynamics or support consciousness.

\section{Formal soundness: why a global axiom is the wrong shape for a physical postulate}
\label{sec:soundness}

Formalization is only as trustworthy as the axioms it rests on, and an axiom that is inconsistent silently makes every theorem downstream of it vacuous without changing whether the remaining statements type-check. The failure mode below is easy to reproduce: a declaration can constrain a symbol it does not itself bind, making the resulting class inconsistent.

The failure has one shape, and naming it is more useful than cataloguing its instances: \emph{an axiom that constrains a symbol it does not itself bind}. Such a declaration is not a postulate about one system. It is a constraint on every model of the class the symbol belongs to, including models built to violate it, and a class that has such a model is thereby inconsistent. Three concrete cases, each of which proves \texttt{False} on its own:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{spontaneous\_\allowbreak symmetry\_\allowbreak breaking\_\allowbreak pointwise\_\allowbreak min} would take the potential-energy functional as an \emph{implicit} argument occurring only in its hypothesis \texttt{PotentialEnergy phi = V v0}. Instantiating that functional with the constant map $\lambda\,\_\allowbreak .\,V(v_0)$ discharges the hypothesis by \texttt{rfl} and yields the conclusion for an arbitrary field. With $V(v) = v^2$, $v_0 = 0$ and $\phi \equiv 1$ this proves $1 = 0$.
\item \texttt{landauer\_\allowbreak heat\_\allowbreak eq} would equate the free class field \texttt{heat\_\allowbreak dissipation} with a bath-entropy difference \emph{for every instance} of the class. An instance with zero heat dissipation and a bath that gains one bit gives $0 = \log 2$.
\item \texttt{kl\_\allowbreak bound\_\allowbreak axiom} would assert $\sigma \ge D_{KL}(P\|Q)/\Delta t$ for \emph{all} $P$ and $Q$, while $\sigma$ is a single real fixed by a class field. Since $D_{KL}$ is unbounded above, a point mass against a uniform distribution refutes it.
\end{enumerate}

Two further declarations of the same shape are worth recording because their refutation is different in kind. \texttt{phase\_\allowbreak invariant\_\allowbreak periodic} and \texttt{sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq}, as global assertions pinning free fields of \texttt{LocalSectionSynchronization}, are refutable as soon as the probability presheaf admits two distinct global sections; the refutation needs no arithmetic at all, only that the sheaf is not trivial. Neither is a postulate in the current development: the periodicity condition is an obligation of the constructor that builds a cover from an invariant measure, and \texttt{sync\_\allowbreak to\_\allowbreak section\_\allowbreak eq} is a theorem about the global section the sheaf condition produces.

The rule that prevents it is uniform: \emph{a physical postulate that mentions a class field must be a field of that class, never a standalone axiom quantified over all instances.} A postulate is then an obligation each model discharges, not a global claim about every model. Under that rule the development declares no axioms at all.

\subsection{What the witnesses establish, and what they do not}

Explicit witnesses establish that the class assumptions can be satisfied. The \texttt{Examples} witnesses therefore cover every class that carries a physical postulate: a one-bit erasure system with a genuine bath that discharges \texttt{heat\_\allowbreak eq} and reproduces $\Delta Q = k_B T \ln 2$; a perfectly resonant system discharging \texttt{kl\_\allowbreak bound}; and a scalar field on a one-point spacetime discharging the action principles. The gluing structures are the most demanding case and are witnessed by a \texttt{ThermodynamicCover} over a three-site substrate carrying the discrete topology and its Borel $\sigma$-algebra, covered by two overlapping open patches whose intersection is a single shared site.

What the witnesses establish is that the classes are inhabitable, so the theorems about them are not vacuous. What they do not establish is that a cortex is such an instance. In the cover above the phase field is already constant --- every patch locked at $\theta = 0$ --- which is precisely what makes the equilibrium obligation dischargeable; getting a physical system into that configuration is the content of the informal dynamical argument.

\subsection{Joint phase and coupling plasticity control}
\label{sec:supp-plasticity}

This control tests alignment between a coupling kernel and a specified environmental template. It does not measure overlap compatibility. Its role in the article is to show why coherent adaptation under an objective does not itself supply the representational structure that gluing requires.

A finite driven control co-evolves phases and symmetric nonnegative couplings
at fixed total coupling. It uses $N=100$, $D=0.1$, $dt=0.01$, mean row coupling
$4$, and three nearly equal frequency clusters at $0.5$, $1$ and $1.5$ per unit
time. Couplings update every $50$ fast steps with learning rate
$\plasticityLearningRate$ for $400$ time units, across three seeds. The
symmetric-edge derivative of
$\frac12\sum_i v_i^2$ is $(v_i-v_j)\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$; each update clips
negative weights, hollows the diagonal and restores the total resource.
This constrained update does not guarantee monotonic descent during joint noisy dynamics.
Each seed runs four arms on a single phase-noise stream: the gradient step, a
symmetric hollow random step rescaled to the Frobenius norm of that arm's own
gradient, that arm's own gradient step composed with a random node relabelling
drawn afresh at every update, and a frozen kernel. Each direction is computed
from the arm's own phases and couplings, so the controls follow the gradient
arm's recipe and no control receives its contemporaneous step; the step sizes
the recipe produces are comparable rather than identical, since the arms'
kernels diverge. At zero learning rate all four arms reproduce one
trajectory, which is the contract the shared stream is tested against.

The nonzero grand-mean frequency drives the collective coordinate, since
symmetric interactions cancel in its mean drift. That cancellation fixes
$\sum_i v_i$ across phase fields, so the objective cannot fall below
$N\langle\omega\rangle^2/D=\plasticitySigmaFloor$ and the update can only
equalize drift across nodes rather than remove it.

The objective is sampled at the update cadence, at the instant each update has
just acted, so every objective mean in this subsection is a mean of that
immediate post-update readout and not a time mean over the intervals between
updates. On it the frozen arm sits at
$\plasticityFrozenDissipationMin$ to $\plasticityFrozenDissipationMax$; the
gradient arm's second-half mean is $\plasticityDissipationMin$ to
$\plasticityDissipationMax$, removing a fraction $\plasticityDescentMin$ to
$\plasticityDescentMax$ of the headroom between the frozen arm and that floor.
The random arm removes $\plasticityRandomDescentMin$ to
$\plasticityRandomDescentMax$ of it, so the reduction is specific to the gradient
direction and not to displacing a kernel of that step size at fixed resource.
What that readout measures is the objective the update leaves behind it at the
moment it acts. Whether the objective stays below the frozen arm through the
interval that follows is a separate measurement, reported in
Section~\ref{sec:plasticity-intervals}, and it is the one a claim of sustained
minimization would need.
The alignment statistics below are read from the kernel, which is constant
between updates, so sampling them at the update cadence weights them exactly.

A step size set the same way is not a matched deformation. Fresh isotropic
steps cancel across updates, so the random arm ends at a cumulative kernel norm growth of
$\plasticityRandomNormGrowthMin$ to $\plasticityRandomNormGrowthMax$ against the
gradient arm's $\plasticityNormGrowthMin$ to $\plasticityNormGrowthMax$, and
hardly enters the channel by which clipping and rescaling concentrate a fixed
resource onto fewer edges. The permuted arm enters that channel instead.
A node relabelling is an isometry of the matrix and a bijection of the edges, so
it preserves the Frobenius norm of the arm's own gradient and the whole multiset
of its entries and changes only which edges receive them; the arm therefore
takes a step of that gradient's size and of its shape, uncorrelated with the
frequency clusters by construction. It reaches a norm growth of $\plasticityPermutedNormGrowthMin$ to
$\plasticityPermutedNormGrowthMax$, a range bracketing the gradient arm's, and a
second-half mean order of $\plasticityPermutedOrderMin$ to
$\plasticityPermutedOrderMax$, which lies inside the gradient arm's range. Its
descent fraction is
$\plasticityPermutedDescentMin$ to $\plasticityPermutedDescentMax$: a step of
a gradient's own magnitude delivered to the wrong edges raises the post-update
objective. What the two controls jointly support is therefore a comparison
between arms whose step sizes are set by the same rule and whose observed
deformation and coherence ranges overlap, not between arms equated by
construction.

The learning rate is selected by a sweep from $\plasticitySweepLowRate$ to
$\plasticitySweepHighRate$ on one seed, reported in the run's \texttt{REPORT.md}:
the post-update objective's second-half mean falls from
$\plasticitySweepLowDissipation$ at the smallest rate to a minimum at
$\plasticitySweepBestRate$ and rises again to
$\plasticitySweepHighDissipation$ at the largest. Rates past the minimum lose
that reduction while still meeting the plateau criterion, so the criterion does
not by itself select a rate and no convergence in the rate is claimed.

Second-half mean order ranges from
$\plasticityOrderMin$ to $\plasticityOrderMax$, so coherence is maintained
throughout. All seeds meet the declared plateau criterion: the last-quarter mean
differs from the preceding quarter by at most $5\%$.
The environmental template is the covariance of unit-variance latent signals
shared within each frequency cluster, rescaled and hollowed to match the
coupling resource; it is a diagnostic template, not an input to plasticity.
Alignment is reported by two statistics unchanged under rescaling the kernel.
The ratio of mean within-cluster to mean between-cluster coupling starts at
$\plasticityRatioInitialMin$ to $\plasticityRatioInitialMax$ and ends at
$\plasticityRatioMin$ to $\plasticityRatioMax$, against

$\plasticityPermutedPercentileMin$ to $\plasticityPermutedPercentileMax$.

A second partition of the same nodes settles what the loss of within-cluster
coupling is a loss against. The frequency partition is the structure the
environment carries; an interleaved partition of the same three group sizes,
built so that each group draws equally from all three clusters, carries no

The gradient arm's interleaved ratio runs from $\plasticityCrossedRatioMin$ to

a family, so the calibrated form of the same reading is to score the frequency
partition against $2000$ relabellings of itself, each keeping the group sizes

$\plasticityBlindPercentileMax$ of them give a lower ratio than the frequency

$\plasticityFrozenBlindPercentileMax$ for the frozen arm's untouched kernel.
Concentrating a fixed resource onto few edges makes any such ratio fluctuate
widely, which is why the interleaved value alone is uninformative and the
percentile against the family is not.

Two readouts are reported and not relied on. The initial-minus-final Frobenius
distance to the template lies in
$[\plasticityReductionMin,\plasticityReductionMax]$, but the kernel norm grows

of mass onto fewer edges rather than loss of alignment, and the distance to the
shuffled template grows with it. The off-diagonal correlation with the template
ends at $\plasticityCorrelationMin$ to $\plasticityCorrelationMax$, close to its
starting value: at the sparsity the update produces, edge-level variance
dominates a block-mean contrast that the within-over-between ratio resolves.

Minimizing squared drift under a conserved mean drift equalizes drift across
nodes, which rewards coupling between clusters of unlike frequency, whereas the
template rewards coupling within clusters of like frequency. The disagreement is
a property of this pairing of objective and template. The blind-partition
reading is what separates that from the weaker statement it would otherwise be
confused with: the update does not degrade whatever partition a reader scores
it against, but locates the one partition the environment carries and moves
against it. Which structure a different objective would move against, or whether
any objective of this family aligns with an environment, is not settled by one
pairing and is not claimed. The squared-drift
objective includes the drive and fluctuating relative drift; it is not a
thermodynamic entropy-production estimate, which would require
probability-current information.

\section{The cortical field identification}

The framework identifies its posited continuum coupling kernel with the endogenous electromagnetic field of mammalian cortex. This is a claim about biology, not a consequence of any theorem above it.

\subsection{Supporting evidence}

Neurons do not only signal through synapses: transmembrane currents generate extracellular fields that shift membrane potentials of neighbouring cells, a mechanism known as ephaptic coupling. Endogenous fields of $\fermiFieldMin$--$\fermiFieldMax$ mV/mm entrain spike timing \cite{frohlich2010, anastassiou2015}, and stable electric fields carry information that survives representational drift in the underlying spiking population \cite{pinotsis2022, pinotsis2023ephaptic, pinotsis2023cytoelectric}. The field is spatially continuous and superposes contributions from large populations --- a medium in which spatially separated activity is combined without further mechanism. This is the integrative role the CEMI field theory assigns it \cite{mcfadden2002, mcfadden2020, pockett2012}, physically supported by astrocytic syncytia joined by gap junctions \cite{verkhratsky2018}.

These observations support a modulatory interaction, but do not determine its integrated strength relative to phase diffusion. Continuity does not itself establish compatible local states or conscious unity. The model requires independent calibration of the coupling kernel, its spatial aggregation and its rate scale.

\subsection{Why the rate calibration is not derivable}
\label{sec:gamma-identifiability}

The conversion $K_{\mathrm{eff}}=\gamma N s E f$ carries its whole physical content in $\gamma$, and no rearrangement of the remaining factors supplies it. The frustration control above records the dimensional half of this: $s$ is a time shift per unit field, so $sE$ is a time, and multiplying by an oscillation frequency leaves a pure number --- the fractional phase advance a cell receives per cycle from the population field. Multiplying by a population size leaves it a pure number. A coupling in the sense of the main text's stochastic model is an inverse time, and $\gamma$ is the entire gap between the two.

The substantive half is that $\gamma$ is a hypothesis about persistence, not a unit conversion to be fixed by convention. It states how a per-cycle phase advance accumulates into a drift rate, which depends on how long a field-driven timing shift survives. Three candidates are available and they are not interchangeable: $\gamma=f$ takes each cycle's advance to be retained in full and independently; $\gamma=1/\tau$ for a phase relaxation time takes it to decay as the medium relaxes; $\gamma=D$ takes it to accumulate at the rate phase itself diffuses. These need not agree, in cortex or anywhere else, and the field measurement chooses between them only if the persistence of induced timing shifts is measured alongside it.

The third candidate is a physical hypothesis, not an algebraic error. Under $\gamma=D$ the threshold test $K_{\mathrm{eff}}/D>2$ reduces to $NsEf>2$; that cancellation is legitimate if an independent experiment establishes that persistence rate. It is unsafe to choose $\gamma=D$ merely because it cancels the unmeasured diffusion, or to recover any $\gamma$ by requiring the model to cross its own threshold or fit the Bessel relation. Identification requires coupling, diffusion and the persistence rule to be estimated in one cortical regime by routes independent of the field arithmetic, which is item~1 of the main text's protocol.

\subsection{Finite-sheet spatial-decay control}

A finite stochastic control asks whether fixing total coupling while shortening its spatial reach fragments the phase field. On a periodic $128\times128$ sheet spanning 2.0~mm, an exponential kernel was row-normalised to total coupling $K_0=8$ at every decay length; the dynamics used thermodynamic diffusion $0.5$~rad$^2$/s, quenched Gaussian frequency spread $1.5$~rad/s, and 20,000 Euler--Maruyama steps per length. With steady $r>0.2$ as the declared operational coherence criterion, the smallest interpolated decay length above which every larger sampled length remained coherent was \spatialCriticalDecay~mm. On this grid that is \spatialCriticalDecayCells~lattice spacings, and the shortest sampled length from which coherence is sustained is \spatialResolvedDecayCells~spacings, so the sheet places the boundary below its own resolution.

Rerunning the transition band and the lengths above it on a $\spatialRefinedSide\times\spatialRefinedSide$ sheet of the same extent --- half the spacing, so a given physical length spans twice as many cells --- leaves the boundary at \spatialRefinedCriticalDecay~mm, \spatialRefinedBoundaryRatio{} times its coarse value and \spatialRefinedCriticalDecayCells{} spacings on the finer grid. The operational crossing is thus nearly unchanged under this one refinement. Two threshold interpolants agreeing is not convergence of the dynamics they interpolate between: at \spatialSharedCoherentDecay~mm, the shortest sampled length from which both sheets stay coherent, steady order is \spatialSharedCoarseOrder{} on the coarse sheet against \spatialSharedRefinedOrder{} on the finer one, and the refined crossing is still only \spatialRefinedCriticalDecayCells{} cells wide. It neither measures cortex nor proves a transition, and it leaves seed dependence and the near-boundary metastability unresolved.

\subsection{Critics}

The longest-standing objection is that the fields are too weak to matter, pressed from within the field-theoretic camp by Pockett herself \cite{pockett2012, pockett2002}. V\"or\"oslakos and colleagues found that applied current is shunted through scalp, delivering fields at or below $1$ V/m in cortex \cite{voroslakos2018}. That is a bound on \emph{applied} rather than endogenous fields, but it constrains how much causal work a field of that magnitude can do, and is the reason we do not claim the field drives spiking.

\subsection{Falsification conditions}

The physical identification would be challenged if independently calibrated field-mediated coupling were insufficient for the proposed regime, or if a selective perturbation of that interaction left the proposed neural and behavioral endpoints unchanged. Such a perturbation requires controls for direct effects on transmembrane currents and other coupling pathways; selectivity is not assumed experimentally available. Recovery tests must distinguish the coupling-gated account from other slow mechanisms under the same observation model. Coherence alone is not equated with report or experience, so dissociations must be evaluated against independently specified endpoints and the additional compatibility and reflexivity hypotheses.

\section{Relationship to other theories}

Integrated information and global-workspace theories supply different descriptions of integration and access \cite{tononi2016,baars1997,dehaene2014}. The present model specifies a candidate physical coupling and a consistency condition on local states. Gluing does not derive an exclusion principle, and continuity alone does not force compatible local descriptions. A digital architecture is not excluded by the sheaf construction. These mathematical differences do not settle which systems have conscious experience.

\section{The sleep-inertia prediction: assumptions, alternatives, and falsifiability}

\subsection{Assumptions}

The effective coupling must depend on extracellular geometry through a separately calibrated relation. The stationary ansatz assumes identical frequencies in a rotating frame and positive phase diffusion. The square-root temporal onset additionally requires a nonzero crossing speed and adiabatic branch tracking, which critical slowing down limits near threshold. The theorem does not prove dynamical selection. Finite-size fluctuations require an operational escape criterion above the fluctuation floor; neither an exactly flat observable foot nor an infinite initial slope is guaranteed.

\subsection{Finite-ramp numerical check}

A finite-$N$ Euler--Maruyama ensemble quantifies the range over which the
adiabatic reading is accurate.  With identical frequencies, $D=1$, $N=2000$,
$32$ seeded replicas and $\dot K=v\in\{10^{-1},10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-4}\}$,
escape is defined as the first of three consecutive sampled points at $r\geq
0.2$ after $K_c$.  The fastest leg is right-censored: only $\rampFastEscaped/\rampReplicas$ replicas
escape before $K=2.5$.  All replicas escape at the three slower speeds, with
mean delays $\Delta K=\rampDelaySlowOne$, $\rampDelaySlowTwo$ and $\rampDelaySlowThree$.  Their log--log fit gives
$\Delta K\propto v^{\rampDelayExponent}$, near the predicted exponent $1/2$ at this
resolution.  At fixed $v=10^{-2}$ the delay is monotonic in $N$, increasing
from $\rampNDelayOne$ through $\rampNDelayTwo$ to $\rampNDelayThree$ as $N$ increases from $500$ through
$2000$ to $8000$. The corresponding ratios $\Delta K / \sqrt{\ln N}$ are $\rampNScaledDelayOne$, $\rampNScaledDelayTwo$ and $\rampNScaledDelayThree$, which do not collapse to a size-independent constant. At $N=500$, individual replicas reach $r=\rampNPrecriticalMaxOne$ before $K_c$, while at $N=8000$ they reach only $\rampNPrecriticalMaxThree$; the absolute criterion $r \geq 0.2$ therefore fires almost immediately at the smallest size. The control demonstrates the protocol hazard warned of in the main text's measurement protocol: a fixed absolute criterion meeting a size-dependent critical fluctuation floor.

The early-foot fit $r=A(K-K_0)^{\beta_{\rm eff}}$ is declared over the order window $0.1\leq r\leq0.4$. Its exponents are read against the same estimator applied to the exact stationary branch on each leg's own coupling grid, which returns $\beta_{\rm eff}=\rampOnsetReferenceOne,\rampOnsetReferenceTwo,\rampOnsetReferenceThree,\rampOnsetReferenceFour$ from fastest to slowest: the window reaches far enough past threshold that the estimator falls short of $1/2$ even on a branch whose exponent is exactly $1/2$, and that shortfall belongs to the window rather than to the drive. Against that reference the drive still dominates at $v=10^{-2}$, where $\beta_{\rm eff}=\rampOnsetTwo$ stands well above the adiabatic value; the two slower legs return $\rampOnsetThree$ and $\rampOnsetFour$, which fall on either side of their references rather than approaching them from one direction. The legs realise different windows and are comparable only so far as those agree: $K-K_c\in[\rampOnsetExcessMinTwo,\rampOnsetExcessMaxTwo]$, $[\rampOnsetExcessMinThree,\rampOnsetExcessMaxThree]$ and $[\rampOnsetExcessMinFour,\rampOnsetExcessMaxFour]$ over $\rampOnsetSamplesTwo$, $\rampOnsetSamplesThree$ and $\rampOnsetSamplesFour$ samples. The shift $K_0$ is returned at its lower bound on $\rampOnsetPinnedCount$ of the $\rampLegCount$ legs, so pinning describes the estimator on this family of traces and does not single out the fastest fit. estimated $(a,r)$ traces remain close to $I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ even where the
trajectory lags the stationary branch.  Post-critical RMS deviations are

\subsection{Alternative models and failure conditions}

Exponential relaxation is one comparator, not the entire class of electrical models. Slow latent variables, observation filtering and finite-size effects can also generate delayed or sigmoidal traces. Comparisons should use common preprocessing and observation models, with onset criteria specified before inspecting outcomes. A linear apparent onset counts against the adiabatic branch interpretation only in a regime where its assumptions hold.

The concentration--coherence relation tests the stationary-density model. Its failure over an informative concentration range constrains that model; its success does not identify the coupling medium. The ratio $a/r$ is a consequence of the same density ansatz and is unreliable near the fluctuation floor. A coupling--geometry association requires an independently validated geometry proxy and an independently defined recovery endpoint. Stationary absence of a fold does not rule out finite-rate hysteresis.

\subsection{What successive stationary phase distributions cannot separate}
\label{sec:recovery-mechanisms}

The first item of the protocol asks for $K$ and $D$ in the same units because
the stationary density does not carry them separately. In the
identical-frequency rotating frame that density is von Mises with concentration
$a=Kr/D$, so a window of equilibrated phases fixes the ratio $K/D$ and nothing
beyond it. Three recovery mechanisms therefore agree on every stationary
observable, window by window. Write $s\in[0,1]$ for a declared window
coordinate, $q(s)$ for a schedule rising from $1.5$ to an endpoint $b$, and
$r(q)$ for the coherent branch of the stationary self-consistency relation,
which is zero for $q\leq2$. Increasing coupling is $K=q(s)$ at $D=1$;
decreasing diffusion is $K=b$ at $D=b/q(s)$; and a common drive is oscillators
with no coupling to each other, each relaxing towards a shared phase $\psi$
under a prescribed $-h(s)\sin(\theta-\psi)$ at $D=1$ with $h(s)=q(s)\,r(q(s))$.
The first two share $K/D=q(s)$; the third has stationary concentration $h/D$,
which is that same number. Their stationary distributions coincide window for
window, so no estimator reading only those distributions separates them. The
third is one observationally matched drive and not a claim about every drive.

An ideal feasibility check confirms that the coincidence is exact rather than
merely close. Nine equally spaced windows, exact densities on a periodic grid
of $4096$ points, endpoints $b\in\{2.6,3.2,4\}$ and orientations
$\psi\in\{0,0.7\}$ give $\recoveryStudyCaseCount$ cases. Concentration is
estimated by regression of log density on the cosine and sine of phase and
coherence independently by circular integration; each family is then fitted for
$b$ over the same range against the same predicted concentration curve, with the
generating family, $K$, $D$ and true $b$ withheld from the fit. The three
families return the same $b$ on every case, agreeing to within
$\recoveryStudyFitSpread$ at squared errors of at most
$\recoveryStudyMaxSquaredError$, and $\recoveryStudyUniqueCount$ of the
$\recoveryStudyCaseCount$ cases identify a unique generator. No family meets the
declared gate of unique recovery on $80\%$ of its cases, and the study stops
there: observation noise, filtering and pooling applied to distributions that
are already identical cannot separate them.

The observable the protocol is missing is the physical clock. Coupling and
diffusion scaled together leave every stationary density untouched and change
the rate at which phase moves, so resolved increments carry what equilibrated
windows discard: along an ideal continuous path the quadratic variation
accumulates at rate $2D$, and conditional increment means constrain the drift.
Separating an endogenous coupling from a common drive is not settled by the
clock either, since in the mean-field description the two impose the same drift
on each oscillator; that separation needs a measured drive or a controlled
perturbation. Neither measurement is validated by this pilot, and neither would
identify the coupling as electromagnetic. The model comparison of item 6 is
accordingly a comparison of time courses under a declared observation model,
and identifies the mechanism behind a recovery only to the extent that the
observations reach past the stationary density.

\section{Interpretive scope}

The formal endpoint is the section glued on a specified compatible cover, fixed by a specified reflexive map. The interpretation developed in the main article identifies the section with conscious unity and its accurate self-representation with a minimal self. Uniqueness is relative to the cover and map; identifying these with meaningful neural descriptions and a neural self-encoding requires independent evidence. Restriction-resonance supplies the relation between the encoding and the state it represents.

The full commitment proposes that unity and reflexivity occur together in conscious episodes. Gluing by itself does not require reflexivity, so a dissociation could challenge that requirement while leaving the unity clause assessable. The physical recovery tests concern coupling and phase dynamics. Testing the interpretive clauses also requires validated measures of compatibility and self-encoding, together with an independent experience endpoint.

\section{What a measurement of overlap compatibility would require}
\label{sec:compatibility-protocol}

The main article's compatibility requirement needs a neural observable distinct from phase order. This section specifies that measurement: decoded local descriptions, restrictions to shared domains, uncertainty under decoding noise, and validation on examples with known compatibility. The specification is not implemented here.

\subsection{Three components}

\emph{A restriction map.} Two regions with overlapping receptive territories may each support a decoder for content on the shared part. Compatibility is a relation between the resulting objects, so the decoder and its restriction map must be specified before any statistic is computed. The formalization uses finite spatial measures: on its discrete substrate two patch sections have the same restriction exactly when they assign the same mass to every shared site (\texttt{restrict\_\allowbreak eq\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak densityOn\_\allowbreak eqOn}). A content decoder using probability laws needs additional structure: equality of overlap marginals is necessary, but global extension and uniqueness must be tested or assumed separately rather than imported from the spatial-measure theorem.

\emph{A statistic with a null.} A distance between the two restricted distributions is not yet a measurement, because decoding error alone makes that distance positive. The statistic needs the distribution of the distance under compatibility --- what two decoders of the same underlying content produce given their own noise --- so that incompatibility is a departure from a stated null and not a nonzero number. Overlapping territories supply dependent samples, and the uncertainty has to be computed accordingly.

\emph{Validation where the answer is built rather than inferred.} The estimator must be exercised on data in which compatible and incompatible overlaps are constructed by design, so that its sensitivity is known before it is applied where the answer is not. The formalization supplies the configuration to target: local states at a single common phase whose densities disagree where the patches meet, which satisfies every other requirement of the gluing theorem and fails compatibility alone (\texttt{overlap\_\allowbreak agreement\_\allowbreak fails}). An estimator that cannot separate that configuration from its compatible counterpart at realistic decoding quality is not yet a measurement of the property the framework needs.

\subsection{Three ways such a measurement fails silently}

A decoder that shrinks both regional posteriors toward a shared prior reports compatibility whatever the regions encode, having replaced two local descriptions with one global one. A decoder carrying independent per-region bias reports incompatibility whatever the regions encode. These are opposite failures of the same component, neither is visible in the compatibility statistic itself, and both are why the constructed-answer validation above is a requirement rather than a refinement.

The third is circularity. A compatibility statistic computed from phases measures phase agreement, which the counterexample shows can coexist with incompatible local descriptions. E78 connects coherence to a supplied cover's relaxation; the cover's own compatibility field supplies overlap agreement. A statistic for that agreement must use the local descriptions and their restrictions.

\subsection{Scope}

A validated observable would make agreement between the chosen local descriptions measurable. Establishing that coherence maintains it would require a dynamical test in addition, and selecting the content model and cover would remain an independent task. A full test of the consciousness identification would also measure the proposed self-encoding and an experience endpoint: compatibility and supercritical coupling alone leave the reflexive clause unevaluated. Absence of report must be distinguished from evidence against an episode. If compatibility cannot be distinguished from incompatibility at achievable decoding quality, the identification is outside the reach of that measurement.

\section{Exploratory EEG exercise of the concentration--coherence estimators}
\label{sec:empirical-methods}

This section reports an exploratory check of the parameter-free relation $r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ on public propofol-sedation EEG (OpenNeuro ds005620 \cite{bajwa2025}). The saved summary and the generated macros record every numerical result below. This is an estimator exercise, not a test of awakening or of the electromagnetic-field commitment.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

For each recording block, 20 seconds of data and 3 seconds of padding on each side were downloaded from the public S3 bucket. Each channel was detrended, bandpass filtered with a fourth-order Butterworth filter using second-order sections (\texttt{sosfiltfilt}), and converted to instantaneous phase with the Hilbert transform. The padding is discarded after filtering in every montage. The primary analysis uses 61 adjacent bipolar pairs formed from the 62 scalp EEG channels; EOG and EMG channels are excluded.

\subsection{(a, r) estimation}

Each recording was divided into non-overlapping 100 ms bins (500 samples at 5 kHz). The estimand is the pooled spatiotemporal phase distribution over 62 sites and 100~ms, not an instantaneous 62-site spatial distribution. Pooling is required because the independent log-density estimator requires at least 100 phase samples. For each bin $t$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Order parameter $r$:} $r_b = \left|\frac{1}{NM}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{m=1}^{M} e^{i\theta_j(t_{bm})}\right|$, where $N=62$ channels and $M=500$ samples per bin. This is the resultant length of the pooled sample, not an average of instantaneous spatial resultant lengths.
\item \textbf{Concentration $a$:} The slope of the log-density of the same phase distribution regressed on $\cos\theta$, as specified in the main text. Phases are centred on their circular mean, binned into 40 bins on $[-\pi,\pi]$, and $\log$ of the binned density (with a $0.5$ pseudocount) is regressed on $\cos\theta$ by \emph{unweighted} ordinary least squares. Under the von Mises model $\log\rho(\theta) = a\cos\theta - \log(2\pi I_0(a))$, so the slope is $a$.
\end{itemize}

The estimator must not be a maximum-likelihood one. The von Mises family is a one-parameter
exponential family whose sufficient statistic is $\sum_j \cos\theta_j$ --- that is, $r$ itself --- so
any ML estimate of the concentration solves its score equation $I_1(\hat a)/I_0(\hat a) = r$ and
returns $\hat a = A^{-1}(r)$ identically. The pair $(\hat a, r)$ would then lie on
$r = I_1(a)/I_0(a)$ for any input whatsoever, including phases drawn from a distribution that is not
von Mises, and the collapse would be an algebraic identity rather than a test. The implemented regression is unweighted, so it uses the log-density shape rather than defining concentration by inversion of the first trigonometric moment. Other weighting rules are different estimators and are not assumed equivalent to maximum likelihood.

The pseudocount biases the estimator, so its null residual must be calibrated at the sample size and dependence structure of the intended measurement. The exploratory bins contain \eegPooledSamples{} phase values, but the proposed spatial protocol permits only 100 sites. At \eegCalibrationSmallSamples{} independent samples, \eegCalibrationReplicas{} von Mises draws with true $a=\eegCalibrationConcentration$ return mean $\hat a=\eegCalibrationAMean$ and mean residual $\eegCalibrationResidualMean$ with standard deviation $\eegCalibrationResidualSd$. A large residual at that count can therefore be estimator bias rather than evidence against the density relation. Independence in this calibration is itself optimistic for EEG; a spatial experiment must use a dependence-aware null.

The estimator also separates synthetic non-von-Mises alternatives from correctly sampled high-count von Mises data. This confirms that the choice of unweighted log-density regression avoids the maximum-likelihood tautology; it does not calibrate a low-count or dependent neural measurement.

\subsection{Bootstrap confidence intervals}

95\% confidence intervals on the mean (a, r) per block were obtained by bootstrap resampling of the time bins (2000 resamples, percentile method). This procedure does not preserve dependence between neighboring bins; its intervals are exploratory rather than a calibrated time-series uncertainty estimate.

\subsection{Narrowband analysis}

The pipeline was run separately on theta (4--8 Hz), alpha (8--12 Hz), beta (13--30 Hz), gamma (30--40 Hz), and broad (4--40 Hz) bands.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The exploratory bipolar data are compatible with the relation at their observed range and pooling choice. This is a necessary condition for the stationary-density model, not evidence for an electromagnetic coupling medium or a test of the awakening prediction.

The observed range is still too narrow to discriminate the Bessel ratio from its linear approximation at the trace's scatter. The curve departs from that approximation by at most $\collapseLinearDeviation$ over the observed concentration range; a discriminating awakening experiment must span concentrations around $\collapseProbe$, where the separation is $\collapseProbeSeparation$, and must calibrate its estimator at the actual spatial sample count.

The result does not test the empirical commitment that the endogenous electromagnetic field is the coupling medium, nor the stipulation that the global section is experience. The coupling-gated awakening prediction remains untested here; these recordings concern propofol sedation rather than recovery from sleep.
