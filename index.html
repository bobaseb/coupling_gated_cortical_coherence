Coupling-gated cortical coherence

What a field theory of conscious unity must assume, and what it predicts

Abstract

A field account of cortical coordination holds that spatially distributed neural processes are coupled in part through the endogenous extracellular electromagnetic field. We ask what such an account must assume to reach a globally consistent, self-referential state, and what it predicts before it gets there. Its physical core is a noisy mean-field phase model whose coupling drifts through a synchronization threshold. If recovery of coherence after sleep is gated by that crossing, it is not free to take an arbitrary time course. For as long as the phase distribution tracks the stationary branch, it must show a square-root onset and a von Mises concentration–coherence relation with no fitted shape parameter. Simulations bound that constraint rather than confirm it. A Lean 4 formalization composes finite information capacity, Landauer's bound, the noisy Kuramoto transition, sheaf gluing and Banach's theorem into one conditional theorem, isolating the eight hypotheses it consumes. We defend as a philosophical commitment, not a theorem, the identification of the glued section with the unity of a conscious episode and of its reflexive fixed point with a minimal self.

What this is, and what it is not

Nothing here derives consciousness from first principles. The construction is a conditional chain of nine nodes, and the eight arrows between them are assumptions rather than results: one independent physical premise, three formalization gaps, two modelling assumptions and two physical commitments. A common toy model satisfies all eight, which establishes that their conjunction is consistent—not that cortex realizes it. The article is meant to be judged in three separable parts: a prediction that can be tested, a composition that can be checked, and an identification that can be argued about.

1. Capacity & boundaries

A finite phase space has bounded information capacity, and a continuous field reaching two components of a disconnected vacuum manifold must leave that manifold somewhere. Capacity does not imply the boundary: these are independent roots of the chain.

2. Erasure and heat

A non-surjective update on a finite register is non-injective, and under the stated system–bath model erasure carries a Landauer heat cost. Logical many-to-one processing is distinguished throughout from its physical implementation.

3. Predictive dissipation

Dissipation bounds nonpredictive memory. The bound specifies no learning rule and does not require the internal representation to reproduce environmental geometry—a point one of our simulations makes concretely and negatively.

4. Coupling-gated coherence

Above a threshold Kc = 2D the noisy mean-field phase model has a unique coherent stationary branch with square-root onset. This is a statement about the stationary solutions; dynamical selection is not proved.

5. Compatibility, not synchrony

Phases can agree everywhere while the local probabilistic descriptions they carry remain incompatible on their overlaps. The sheaf condition marks that difference and glues compatible local states into a unique global section.

6. The reflexive fixed point

A self-prediction map restricts a global state to a region encoding it and reads that encoding back out. Where the map contracts, Banach's theorem gives a unique fixed point—a state whose self-model is accurate about the state it models.

What the simulations restrict

The numerical controls bound the hypothesis rather than support it, and two of the results transfer well beyond this framework. Ramp speed sets the apparent onset exponent: an exponent fitted to a driven recovery trace reports the drive at least as much as the mechanism, so any analysis reading a critical exponent off a driven transition needs an independently known ramp rate. And squared-drift plasticity does not learn structure: coupling updates that descend the objective preserve coherence while moving away from the generating structure, below the level of a shuffled-label comparison, which is evidence against the common intuition that dissipation minimization implies representational learning.

On hardware

Two formal results bear on the substrate question and are easy to over-read. At matched coupling resource, a fixed wiring support that excludes a better-correlated pair can be suboptimal—a statement about constrained optimization, not about biology. On an atomless substrate, a kernel supported on finitely many points contributes zero to the chosen continuum coupling integral; that is a property of the measure and the observable, and it cuts against this framework's own coarse-graining step as much as against anything else. No conclusion about artificial consciousness follows. Nothing in the sheaf or fixed-point conditions is specific to cortex or to electromagnetic coupling: that substrate claim is empirical and is carried entirely by two named hypotheses.

Formal Verification in Lean 4

Verified with Lean 4

The development declares no axioms of its own: physical postulates are hypotheses or fields of models, each discharged by an explicit witness. What formalization adds is not certainty about the physics but that the assumption list cannot be lost—type-checking forbids reading a theorem about a stationary density as a claim about a trajectory, or agreement of phases as compatibility of representations. It does not make the assumptions true.
