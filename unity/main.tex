\begin{abstract}
Human conscious experience is unified: the brain's many local descriptions of a
shared situation agree. We argue that unity requires this agreement to be
physically enforced, produced and maintained by a coupling through which the
graded physical state of each region continuously pulls the regions it overlaps
toward agreement. Clocked digital hardware computes agreement instead. Its
noise margins make every content readout locally constant in micro state, and
we prove that a readout with a margin, under continuous micro dynamics, depends
on no region's graded state; content read from bits is therefore never
physically enforced, whatever algorithm the bits run. For a coupled sheet of
oscillators we show that the width of the coupling kernel sets how fast
agreement decays with distance, and that only a kernel tail slower than
$r^{-4}$ makes it independent of distance. The premise yields interventional
tests on cortex and on artificial systems, and we state the outcomes that would
refute it on each.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Premise P (physical unity).} A system's contents are unified only if
each region's contents are read from graded content variables that depend
continuously on the graded physical state of the regions it overlaps, through a
coupling that contracts their disagreement.

P is a necessary condition for unity. It rejects functionalism about unity: a
perfect simulation of a coupled field reproduces the field's content-level
dynamics and fails P. The paper makes three claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\claim{sec:enforcement}{A content readout with a noise margin, under continuous micro dynamics, depends on the graded state of no region, so content read from bits is not physically enforced whatever algorithm the bits run}
\claim{sec:reach}{On a noisy coupled sheet, the width of the coupling kernel sets how fast agreement decays with distance, and only a kernel tail slower than $r^{-4}$ makes agreement independent of distance}
\claim{sec:tests}{P predicts that a sub-threshold perturbation of one cortical region shifts the content decoded in an overlapping region toward agreement, and that no perturbation below a digital system's noise margin shifts any of its contents}
\end{enumerate}

Every formal result the paper states is machine-checked in Lean~4, and
Appendix~\ref{app:formal} maps each one to its declaration.

\section{Synchrony is not unity}
\label{sec:synchrony}

Equal phases coexist with incompatible local descriptions, and compatible local
descriptions determine a unique global state. Unity is therefore a property of
what overlapping regions describe, and synchrony is at most a route to it.

\section{Unity as agreement over a declared cover}
\label{sec:cover}

Agreement is measured between regions that decode a shared variable. The cover
of regions is fixed by decodability before agreement is measured, so that the
measurement cannot be emptied by the choice of cover.

\section{Physical enforcement versus routed agreement}
\label{sec:enforcement}

A system is a micro dynamics $f$ on the joint micro state $x=(x_i)_i$ of its
regions, read by a content map $\pi$ that assigns a content to each region.
Its content level is closed when the next content is a function of the current
one, $\pi\circ f = g\circ\pi$ for some content dynamics $g$. A readout has a
noise margin on an operating set when every micro state in that set has a
neighbourhood on which the readout is constant. Content depends gradedly on a
region when no neighbourhood of that region's micro state leaves the next
content unchanged, the others held fixed. The overlap discrepancy, a measure of
disagreement between the contents of overlapping regions, contracts near a
state when one step of $f$ shrinks it by a factor below one throughout a
neighbourhood of that state, so that perturbations below every threshold are
pulled back toward agreement along with the rest. Agreement is physically
enforced at a state when content depends gradedly on each region there and the
overlap discrepancy contracts near it; P requires it at the states the system
occupies while its contents are unified.

If the micro dynamics is continuous and carries a
state into a margin's operating set, no region's micro state enters the next
content gradedly. A thresholded
continuous quantity has a margin off its threshold, and a finite word of such
readouts has one too, so the conclusion holds for any content read from bits.

The theorem places the criterion one level below the content dynamics, and the
placement is forced. Contraction of disagreement at the content level is
computable: a digital system running a consensus algorithm, or integrating a
coupled field, has a content map whose successor contracts the disagreement
between overlapping regions exactly as the field does. A criterion stated on
the content dynamics would classify that system with the field. P is stated on
the dependence of content on graded micro state, and there the two differ: the
field's content moves with every graded change in the regions it overlaps,
while the simulation's content is read from bits held inside their margins and
moves with none of them.

This is the simulation objection, and P answers it by denial. A perfect
simulation of a coupled cortical field reproduces the field's content-level
trajectory and is not unified in the sense P requires, because the agreement
in it is computed from contents rather than enforced by the graded state that
carries them. That is the commitment P makes about unity, stated on the same
footing as a workspace theory's identification of consciousness with broadcast:
a premise whose consequences can be tested, which Section~\ref{sec:tests} does.

Categorical contents have margins in every substrate. The dominant percept in
binocular rivalry, a perceived category, or any class selected by an argmax is
constant on each side of its boundary, so away from the boundary the margin
theorem denies it graded dependence in cortex exactly as in silicon. P is
therefore stated on the graded content variables from which categories are
read, such as a location, an orientation, a time or the relative strength of
competing percepts, and a category meets P through the variable that carries
it. A category read from a carrier depends gradedly on a region only where the
carrier does. The restatement opens no
route for a digital machine: every variable it computes, whether a number, a
vector of numbers or a category, is a function of its bit word, and after any
number of steps that keep its bits off their thresholds such a function depends
gradedly on no region.
The voltages beneath the bits carry none of the machine's contents, at that step
or any later one, so they are not a carrier.

\section{How far a physical coupling enforces agreement}
\label{sec:reach}

At identical natural frequencies a noisy coupled sheet is a gradient system. In
the harmonic approximation the variance of the phase difference between two
sites is the noise strength times their effective resistance, with the coupling
constants as conductances. Under short-range coupling that resistance grows as
the logarithm of distance, with a coefficient inversely proportional to the
kernel's second moment; a kernel tail slower than $r^{-4}$ bounds it. The
logarithm is the Gaussian form of the absence of true long-range order in two
dimensions under short-range coupling \citep{mermin1966}, and the
plane-rotator sheet has a Kosterlitz--Thouless transition \citep{frohlich1981},
the order--disorder crossover that the simulations below locate. Heterogeneous natural frequencies make short-range
coupling weaker still: phases then desynchronize in every dimension up to four
\citep{hong2005}.

The logarithmic growth is a lower bound for every kernel of bounded range. Group
the sites into shells around a site $a$, so that the coupling joins only sites
in the same or adjacent shells. The shells then act as conductors in series,
and the effective conductance from $a$ to any site beyond shell $n$ is at most
$\bigl(\sum_{k<n} 1/C_k\bigr)^{-1}$, where $C_k$ is the coupling crossing from
shell $k$ to shell $k+1$. On a planar sheet
the perimeter of shell $k$ grows linearly in $k$, so $C_k \le c\,(k+1)$ for a
constant $c$ set by the kernel's strength and width, and the effective
resistance is at least $\log(n+1)/c$.
Widening the kernel enlarges its shells and raises $c$, which slows the growth
and leaves it unbounded. Distance-independent agreement on a sheet therefore
needs coupling of unbounded range.

A tail slower than $r^{-4}$ bounds the resistance at every distance. Run a chain
of site sets out from $a$ through discs of radius $2^j$ and back in to $b$, the
two widest discs spanning the separation. The phase difference across $a$ and
$b$ telescopes through the discs' mean phases, and the drop between the means of
two consecutive discs is bounded by the coupling energy between them, shared
among the pairs of their sites. A disc of radius $2^j$ on a planar sheet holds at
least $\alpha\,4^j$ sites, and a kernel $K(r)\gtrsim r^{-(2+\sigma)}$ couples
two sites of consecutive discs by at least $\beta\,2^{-(2+\sigma)j}$. The
number of pairs grows as $16^j$ and their coupling falls as $2^{-(2+\sigma)j}$,
so the drops shrink geometrically with ratio $q=2^{-(2-\sigma)/2}$, which is
below one for $\sigma<2$, and the effective conductance is at least
$(1-q)^2\alpha^2\beta/8$ however far apart $a$ and $b$ lie.

Simulated sheets at identical frequencies, of side \uSideSmall{} and
\uSideLarge{}, with nearest-neighbour, exponential and power-law kernels of
tail exponent $\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=3$ at a common row sum, follow the harmonic
identity in the ordered phase. The measured phase variance is at most
\uHarmonicRatioLow{} times $D\,R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $D=\uNoiseLow$ and at most
\uHarmonicRatioMid{} times at $D=\uNoiseMid$, the excess rising with noise as
the cosine coupling softens, and a logarithm fits its growth with distance
better than a line in \uLogarithmicRuns{} of the \uOrderedRuns{} runs at those
noise levels. At $D=\uNoiseHigh$ the nearest-neighbour sheet disorders: the
mean chord discrepancy $\langle 1-\cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)\rangle$ at the
largest separation is \uChordNearestHigh{}. The exponential and $\sigma=1$
sheets stay ordered at that noise (\uChordExponentialHigh{} and
\uChordSigmaOneHigh{}), and the $\sigma=3$ sheet nearly disorders
(\uChordSigmaThreeHigh{}), so the kernel's width sets the order--disorder
crossover as well as the rate of decay. Exact resistance sums at sheet sides
\uScalingSideA{}, \uScalingSideB{} and \uScalingSideC{} separate width from
tail. The exponential kernel, with \uStiffnessRatio{} times the
nearest-neighbour stiffness, gives resistance at half the side of
\uFarExponentialA{}, \uFarExponentialB{} and \uFarExponentialC{}, still
growing; the $\sigma=1$ tail gives \uFarSigmaOneA{}, \uFarSigmaOneB{} and
\uFarSigmaOneC{}, and the $\sigma=3$ tail, steeper than $r^{-4}$, gives
\uFarSigmaThreeA{}, \uFarSigmaThreeB{} and \uFarSigmaThreeC{}, growing as a
short-range kernel does. Over a finite cortical extent a wide short-range kernel and a
slow tail therefore differ by a slowly growing logarithm, and the measurement
that separates them needs the kernel's second moment as well as its tail
exponent.

Three facts hold without the harmonic approximation. Adding coupling never
raises the effective resistance, so a long-range
tail added to a short-range kernel can only improve agreement, whatever its
shape. A direct coupling between two sites caps their resistance at its
reciprocal, at any distance. A Lipschitz
encoder carries phase agreement to content agreement: the mean-square content
discrepancy is at most the squared Lipschitz constant times the mean-square
phase difference. With the harmonic
identity, an encoder of Lipschitz constant $L$ at noise strength $D$ therefore
has root-mean-square content discrepancy at most $L\sqrt{D\,R_{\mathrm{eff}}}$
between two sites of effective resistance $R_{\mathrm{eff}}$.

Content can agree where phases differ. An encoder that reads the phases of a
stencil around a site relative to the phase at that site reports the same
content at every site of a plane wave, whatever its gradient, and at every site
of a winding on a ring or torus. For a wave
plus a departure, the content discrepancy between two sites of a Lipschitz
relative encoder is at most its Lipschitz constant times the departure's
relative readings at the two sites, with the gradient of the wave and the
separation of the sites absent from the bound. Travelling waves and windings, the
configurations that attain the worst case of a hop-by-hop chain bound, thus
cost a relative encoder nothing; what it pays for is the local roughness of the
field.

\section{The unity window}
\label{sec:window}

Agreement cannot arrive before the causal cone of the coupling allows, and
disagreement decays at the relaxation rate of the coupling. Unity at a given
time scale requires the interval between the two to be nonempty.

The lower edge is causal. A report at one site that tracks a variable at
another to within $\varepsilon$, for two values of the variable more than
$2\varepsilon$ apart, is possible after $T$ rounds of the coupling only if the
second site lies in the first site's causal past for $T$ rounds. The upper edge is relaxation. If the
coupling contracts the overlap discrepancy by a factor $\kappa<1$ at every step
on a set of states it keeps, a discrepancy starting at most $d_0$ is at most
$\varepsilon$ from step $\log(d_0/\varepsilon)/\log(1/\kappa)$ on. The estimate is sharp in form: a
discrepancy that shrinks by at most a factor $\rho$ per step is still above
$\varepsilon$ at every earlier step than $\log(d_0/\varepsilon)/\log(1/\rho)$. The window opens at the later of the
causal deadline and the relaxation time, and unity at the time scale of a
content requires it to open before that content changes.

In cortex the longest cortico-cortical fibres run about \ueFibreCm{}~cm
\citep{nunez2014}, callosal axons conduct at median velocities of
\ueVelocitySlow{} to \ueVelocityFast{}~m/s and the fastest of them at up to
\ueVelocityMax{}~m/s \citep{caminiti2009}, and a chemical synapse adds about
\ueSynapseMs{}~ms \citep{katz1965,sabatini1996}. The synaptic causal deadline
across the cortex is therefore \ueConeMaxMs{}~ms along the fastest axons, and
typical synaptic arrival takes \ueConeFastMs{} to \ueConeSlowMs{}~ms. The unconscious integration
that precedes a conscious percept lasts up to \ueContentMs{}~ms
\citep{herzog2016}. Reading agreement as a \ueReduction{}-fold reduction of
disagreement, decaying exponentially, the window at that time scale is
nonempty when the coupling's relaxation time is at most \ueTauSlowMs{}~ms with
typical arrival along the slower fibres and \ueTauFastMs{}~ms along the faster. The causal deadline
thus takes a small part of the content's time scale, and whether the window
opens turns on the relaxation time.

\section{Current artificial systems}
\label{sec:ai}

A transformer running on clocked digital hardware reads every content from bits
held within their noise margins, so the result of
Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} applies to it. Hardware whose contents are graded
physical quantities coupled without barriers, such as analog, in-memory or
neuromorphic substrates, can satisfy P.

The criterion concerns how a substrate is organised and says nothing of what it
is made of. An analog crossbar whose column currents carry content and settle
through shared conductances depends gradedly on every input voltage, and an
array of coupled electronic or optical oscillators contracts phase
disagreement through a physical coupling exactly as a cortical sheet does. A
neuromorphic chip passes where its contents are read from membrane potentials
coupled continuously, and fails where they are read from spike counts latched
at a clock edge. Biological tissue passes or fails on the same terms: a neural
circuit whose contents were read only from all-or-none events latched far from
threshold would fall under the margin theorem as any digital circuit does.

Spikes are thresholded events, and what makes them graded carriers is their
timing. The time at which a membrane potential rising through threshold crosses
it moves continuously with the sub-threshold input, and postsynaptic potentials
integrate spike times without sampling them, so spike timing carries graded
dependence from one region to the next. A clocked latch compares the time of a
transition with the clock edge, which is a threshold on the transition time, so
the margin theorem applies to it with that time as the thresholded quantity. The clock is a noise margin in time, and it removes from a digital
circuit exactly the graded timing that spikes keep.

Real digital hardware leaks: supply noise, crosstalk and temperature move the
analog quantities behind every bit. The margin theorem locates where such
leakage can reach content. If a content read as a word of thresholded
continuous quantities depends gradedly on any region, some bit of the next state
sits exactly on its threshold,
and correct operation keeps every state off that set. The margin also has a
width. If every bit of the next state is at least $\delta$ from its threshold,
each bit is read from an $L_v$-Lipschitz quantity and the micro dynamics is
$L$-Lipschitz, a change to one region smaller than $\delta/(L L_v)$ changes no
bit. Leakage below that scale carries no
content; leakage above it flips a bit, which is a logical error. Digital
design places ordinary noise well below that scale: a textbook inverter's
static noise margin is \ueMarginPercent{}\% of its supply voltage, against an
expected supply variation of \ueSupplyPercent{}\% \citep{rabaey2003}, so the
expected variation is \ueNoiseToMargin{} of the margin. The
nondeterminism of floating-point reduction order is a choice among logical
trajectories and is outside both theorems: it varies which content is computed,
not how content depends on graded state.

\section{Tests}
\label{sec:tests}

\paragraph{Graded cross-region dependence in cortex.} Weak stimulation of one
region below the behavioural threshold should shift the content decoded in an
overlapping region, gradedly with stimulation strength and toward agreement
with the stimulated region. Transcranial alternating current and weak
intracortical fields supply the perturbation, and the decoded content is a
graded variable, such as a location, an orientation or the strength of evidence
for a percept, read from population activity in the overlapping region; a
decoded category is locally constant away from its boundaries in any substrate
and would stay unchanged whether P holds or not. An unchanged decoded
content in the overlapping region, at stimulation strengths that measurably
move the stimulated one, counts against P, since P then denies unity to the
system whose unity motivates it.

\paragraph{Agreement as an attractor on both substrates.} Perturb one region
and measure whether agreement with its overlap partners is restored, and at
what rate. In cortex the perturbation is stimulation and the measure is the
disagreement between contents decoded from the perturbed region and its
partners; P predicts restoration at the relaxation rate of
Section~\ref{sec:window}, with a restoring pull proportional to the size of the
perturbation below as well as above any threshold. In an artificial system the
perturbation is activation patching of one layer, head or position, applied at
two scales: below the scale at which the logical trajectory changes, and above
it. A digital system can restore agreement above that scale, by computation;
below it the margin theorem predicts that nothing moves at all. The outcome
that counts against the thesis on the artificial side is a graded shift of
decoded content in one region, toward agreement, driven by an analog
perturbation of another that stays inside every bit's margin, such as a supply
or timing perturbation below the error threshold.

\paragraph{The field's causal cone.} At the frequencies of neural activity the
extracellular field is quasi-static \citep{gratiy2017}: it reaches every point
of the tissue it spans with no propagation delay, while synaptic influence
between distant regions arrives only after axonal conduction and synaptic
transmission, no sooner than \ueConeMaxMs{}~ms across the longest
cortico-cortical fibres. Re-agreement between two regions after a local
perturbation, faster than the synaptic route allows, would identify a
non-synaptic contribution to the coupling and measure the reach of the kernel
tail of Section~\ref{sec:reach} directly.

The strength of the field sets the separation at which the test decides
anything. Endogenous fields in cortex peak near \ueFieldPeak{}~mV/mm in vivo
\citep{frohlich2010}, networks of neurons detect fields from
\ueFieldDetection{}~mV/mm \citep{francis2003}, and the potential of a local
cortical source decays as $r^{-2.1}$ across millimetres \citep{rebollo2021}.
With the peak placed at the nearest distance of that measurement, the field of
a local source falls to the detection threshold within \ueFieldReachMm{}~mm and
misses it by \ueFieldDeficitOrders{} orders of magnitude at \ueFibreCm{}~cm.
The field's cone is therefore tested at millimetre separations, where activity
has been observed to synchronize across a cut through cortex by the field alone
\citep{rebollo2021}; at centimetre separations P predicts that re-agreement
arrives through synaptic coupling, which is graded as well. The timing is
measurable with intracortical recording. The stimulation artefact saturates
clinical amplifiers during the first milliseconds, masking the earliest
monosynaptic responses \citep{keller2014}, and early responses at distant
contacts carry a volume-conducted component that scales with distance as the
artefact does \citep{prime2020}. Template subtraction of the artefact
\citep{trebaul2016}, multi-unit activity or current-source density at the
distant site, which the passive spread of the local field potential does not
reach \citep{kajikawa2011}, and a deadline set for each subject from the
tractography length of the connecting fibres \citep{caminiti2013} separate
active re-agreement from the passive field that carries the stimulus.

\paragraph{Separating coupling from information.} In every manipulation of
cortex available so far, graded coupling and information flow move together:
anaesthesia and callosal section remove both. A test that confirms P against
functionalism must move one with the other held fixed. Coherence between
cortical regions arises largely as a consequence of communication between them
\citep{schneider2021}, and transcranial alternating current applied to two
regions drives both from a common source: where in-phase stimulation raised
their synchrony it raised directed information flow with it
\citep{reinhart2019}. The coupling has to be supplied instead. Two regions
whose natural connection is interrupted are bridged by stimulating each in
proportion to the activity recorded at the other, through one of two loops: an
analog loop, whose output moves with every graded change in its input, or a
quantized loop, whose output is read from bits and so has a margin. Noise added
to the analog loop matches the transfer entropy it carries, estimated from the
recorded activity, to the quantized loop's. The two loops then carry the same
information, and only the analog one couples the regions gradedly. Unity
measured as in the next paragraph that follows the analog loop at matched
transfer entropy supports P over functionalism, and unity that is the same
under both loops counts against P. Intracortical stimulation in animals
supports the design with decoded agreement as the measure; reported unity needs
a bridged human preparation.

\paragraph{What counts as evidence about the phenomenal field.} Every test
above measures decoded agreement, which is agreement of the contents available
to readout. If disunity of readout could always be set aside as disunity of
access alone, no observation would bear on P. The observations that count as
evidence about the phenomenal field are therefore fixed in advance: responses
that integrate stimuli across the regions in question when the task requires
it, and reports of a single field of experience spanning them. A dissociation
in which such responses and reports split while graded coupling between the
regions is intact, or stay integrated after graded coupling between them is
removed, is accepted as evidence against P.

\paragraph{Split brain.} Callosal section removes the main route of both
information and graded coupling between the hemispheres. Patients respond to
stimuli in the two visual fields as if perception were divided, yet can report
the presence of stimuli in either field with either hand, which has been read
as divided perception with undivided consciousness \citep{pinto2017}; whether
the phenomenal field divides remains contested \citep{dehaan2020}, and a
switch model, on which consciousness alternates between the hemispheres, is a
live rival \citep{bayne2010}. If the field stays undivided, unity survives on
the residual subcortical routes. Those routes are synaptic and carry
information too, so the case bears on P only if unity is preserved out of
proportion to the information they carry, and it bears on graded coupling of
any kind: synaptic coupling is graded through postsynaptic potentials and
sub-threshold shifts in spike timing, and the extracellular field is one
realization of it among several.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

The closest formal neighbour is the account of computational closure of
\citet{rosas2024}, on which a macroscopic level that is informationally,
causally and computationally closed is the mark of emergent software in a
physical system. The closure is the same property that
Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} places at the content level of a digital system,
and P reads it the other way: a level closed off from the graded state beneath
it is one whose agreement is computed, not enforced. \citet{kleiner2024} reach a
related exclusion from a different premise: if the physical dynamics of a
system is relevant to its consciousness, processors designed and verified to
suppress deviations from their computational dynamics cannot be conscious. The
margin theorem gives the suppression a formal statement, and P confines the
premise to unity and ties it to a coupling that contracts disagreement, which
is what makes it testable on both substrates. \citet{seth2026} argues that
consciousness may depend on biological properties that computation does not
capture; P locates the relevant property in the organisation of the substrate
and admits non-biological hardware that has it (Section~\ref{sec:ai}). Mortal
computation, whose parameters are useful only on the hardware instance that
learned them \citep{hinton2022,ororbia2023}, shares P's refusal to separate the
computation from the medium. Integrated information theory also denies
experience to digital computers running faithful simulations of a brain
\citep{tononi2015,findlay2024}; it does so through the causal structure of the
hardware at its logical grain, while P turns on the dependence of content on
graded physical state and on the contraction of disagreement. The
electromagnetic field theory of \citet{mcfadden2020} identifies consciousness
with the brain's field, supported by evidence that endogenous fields entrain
cortical activity \citep{frohlich2010}; P takes the field as one realization of
graded coupling among several.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

P is a premise and is not derived from the dynamics; the formal results show
what follows from it and not that it holds. It is necessary for unity and not
sufficient for consciousness: coupled pendulums satisfy it. The margin theorem
concerns idealised digital readouts, and graded leakage in real hardware that
carries content-relevant agreement would narrow its reach. The identification of
phase variance with effective resistance rests on the harmonic approximation at
identical natural frequencies. The logarithmic lower bound on resistance is
proved for kernels of bounded range, and the bound at every distance under a tail
slower than $r^{-4}$ is proved for a sheet that supplies the site counts and
couplings of a dyadic chain of discs; that the logarithm is attained, and that a
tail steeper than $r^{-4}$ leaves the resistance growing, are established
numerically on finite sheets. The unity window's edges are proved for a given contraction factor; the
identification of that factor with the coupling's spectral gap is linear
response. The relaxation time of cortical coupling is unmeasured, so the window
estimate states the condition for the window to open at cortical time scales
and leaves open whether cortex meets it; it also reads agreement as a declared
reduction factor of disagreement. The step from phase agreement to content agreement assumes
one oscillator population per content patch, which is a modelling choice about
cortex. A long-range tail improves agreement only in proportion to its
conductance against the short-range part, and the field of a local cortical
source falls below the detection threshold within millimetres, by an estimate
that places the field's peak at a declared distance; across centimetres graded
coupling is carried chiefly by synapses, and the field's contribution there is
a quantity to be measured. The dissociation of coupling from information has,
so far, no human preparation in which reported unity can be measured across a
bridged connection. Above the order--disorder crossover of
Section~\ref{sec:reach} the harmonic identity fails and agreement is lost at
every distance. Human data can falsify P
but cannot by themselves confirm it against functionalism, because graded
coupling and information flow co-vary in every manipulation available so far.
