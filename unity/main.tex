\begin{abstract}
A conscious scene is experienced as one scene, although many brain regions
contribute to it. We argue that this unity requires physically enforced
agreement: the regions must be coupled so that any change in one, however small,
as long as it exceeds the region's own noise, pulls the others toward agreement
within the time a percept takes to form. The condition is functional, but it is
stated at the grain of graded physical dynamics rather than at the grain of an
algorithm, and at that grain cortex and digital chips come apart. It predicts
that when two cortical regions are reconnected by an analog loop and by a
quantized loop carrying at least as much information, in total and about the
task, unity follows the analog loop. An account stated on the information that
regions exchange predicts no difference. We prove that under noise, content
read from bits responds to any change below its margin by at most its error
rate, a bound that is attained, so a digital machine, clocked or clockless,
fails the condition whatever algorithm it runs. Applying one test to both
substrates, a noisy cortical membrane passes near its firing threshold and
reaches such a state within a percept's time scale, while the value a clocked
latch reads passes at none of its states. The condition also bounds how slowly
cortical coupling may relax, and on a noisy sheet of coupled oscillators the
coupling's reach decides how far agreement holds. We specify an animal
preparation for the bridge and the outcomes that would refute the premise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

When you watch a bird fly across the sky, you see one bird in one scene. Yet
many parts of your brain work on that scene at once. A visual area tracks where
the bird is, and a parietal area tracks the same location to plan a reach
toward it. Each part describes a piece of the same situation, and their
descriptions agree. This paper asks what kind of agreement that has to be.

Parts can agree in two ways. Picture first a set of weights joined by springs.
Each weight's position is a graded physical quantity that pulls on its
neighbours all the time, so no weight can move, even slightly, without dragging
the others after it. The weights are coordinates of one physical state, and above
their own noise they cannot vary independently at any scale. We call this
agreement \emph{physically enforced}. Picture next a set of account books kept
consistent by a clerk, who at fixed times compares the books and corrects any
mismatch. Between checks a smudge on one page changes nothing in any other book.
The books agree only because a rule makes them agree, and we call this agreement
\emph{computed}. The springs make one system in the sense physics uses; the books
are many systems kept in step by a protocol.

A digital chip is of the second kind. Each bit is a separate record, built to
ignore small disturbances: a voltage a little above or below the level for 1
still reads as 1. That makes computers reliable, and it also seals each bit off
from small changes in every other bit, so whatever agreement a program produces
among its bits is computed. Our premise is that
the unity of an experience is agreement of the first kind.

\paragraph{Premise P (physical unity).} A system's contents are unified only if
each region's contents are read from graded carriers and, within every window of
the contents' own time scale, some carrier of each region depends on the graded
physical state of every region it overlaps, above the noise floor and through a
coupling that contracts their disagreement.

In short, the regions that contribute to one experience must be held together
by a physical coupling that feels arbitrarily small changes above noise, and not
by an exchange of symbols. P is a necessary condition for unity, and a condition
on how a system's conscious contents are held together: which contents are
conscious, and what they are like, lie outside it. It says nothing
about where in the brain unified contents live: it constrains whichever regions
carry them, whether they span the cortex or are confined to a posterior zone of
it \citep{koch2016}.

\paragraph{A finer grain of function.} P is still a functional condition: it
picks out unity by what physical parts do to one another. What sets it apart is
the level of detail, or \emph{grain}, at which it describes them. Functionalism
leaves the grain open. Described coarsely enough, a nation whose citizens
exchange messages by radio could run a brain's program \citep{block1978}, while
the biology of cognition has been argued to tie mental states to a finer grain
\citep{godfreysmith2016}. P sits at the grain of graded physical dynamics, and
computational functionalism about unity at the grain of the algorithm. For
cortex the two grains agree. They come apart for a perfect digital simulation of
cortex, which is unified at the algorithm's grain and fails P, since its
agreement is kept by a rule acting on separate records. The experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:tests} asks which grain unity tracks, holding fixed the
information two brain regions exchange and changing only whether the link
between them is graded or digital.

\paragraph{The argument.} The case for P rests on two premises, both defended in
Section~\ref{sec:enforcement}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Wholeness}, an account of unity: unified experiences are parts of one
total experience, so they need one physical system to carry them.
\item \emph{Counting}, how physics counts systems: two parts are one system when
each responds to the other at every scale between its own noise and its own
thresholds.
\end{itemize}
Every step between these premises and P is proved. Three results do the work.
Content read from bits responds to a small change by at most its error rate. A
rule that compares records only adds another record, and it joins none of them
into one system. A graded reader does join the regions it reads.

\paragraph{Noise and time.} Two refinements make P fit real brains. Physical
systems are noisy, so P is read on probabilities: a change counts only if it is
larger than the part's own noise, and it must then move what the part does next
by at least as much as the part fluctuates by itself. And a neuron is sensitive
to small inputs mainly when it is close to firing, so asking every neuron to be
sensitive at every instant would count no network of spiking cells as one system.
P asks instead that \emph{some} carrier of each region register the other
regions within each window of the content's time scale.

The paper makes three claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\claim{sec:tests}{When two cortical regions are bridged by an analog and by a quantized loop with the quantized loop carrying at least as much information, in total and about the task, P predicts that unity follows the analog loop; a weak, sub-threshold nudge to one region should also shift the graded content decoded in an overlapping region toward agreement}
\claim{sec:enforcement}{Under noise, content read from bits responds to any change below its margin by at most its error rate, a bound that is attained, so no digital machine meets P, whatever algorithm it runs; by one test applied to both, a cortical membrane passes near its threshold within a percept's time scale, while the value a clocked latch reads passes at none of its states}
\claim{sec:reach}{Enforced agreement must arrive before the content changes, so P predicts that cortical coupling relaxes within \ueTauFastMs{}~ms across the cortex; on a noisy sheet of coupled oscillators, the coupling's reach decides how far agreement in phase holds}
\end{enumerate}

Every formal result is machine-checked in Lean~4 (Appendix~\ref{app:formal}).
The two premises are argued, the proofs run from them, and the bridge tests
them together.

\section{Unity as agreement between overlapping regions}
\label{sec:cover}

Think of an atlas. Each page maps one patch of ground, neighbouring pages
overlap, and where they overlap they show the same roads. Pages that agree on
every overlap fit together into one map of the whole territory. We treat the
brain's regions the same way. Each describes part of a shared situation, and
unity is the property that these local descriptions agree wherever they
overlap. When they do, they fix a single global state.

Synchrony is not unity: two regions can oscillate in phase while encoding a
stimulus at two different locations. Unity is about what overlapping regions
describe, and synchrony is at most one route to it.

\paragraph{Carriers and traces.} A region's \emph{content} is a variable the
system itself reads, not merely one an outside observer can decode: the regions
it drives respond to it, and it determines the quantity the region represents,
such as a location. A variable that meets both conditions is a \emph{carrier};
one that only an observer can decode is a \emph{trace}. The supply current of a
digital circuit reveals enough about its logical state to recover a secret key
\citep{kocher1999}, yet no gate reads it, so it is a trace. In cortex, membrane
potentials and spike times are carriers, because the cells they drive integrate
them. The same test says \emph{when} a variable is a carrier: at the moments its
readers take it in. Feed-forward inhibition
confines a pyramidal cell's integration of its inputs to a window of a few
milliseconds \citep{pouille2001,gabernet2005}, short against the interval
between the spikes it receives \citep{konig1996}, and the timing of first spikes
in somatosensory cortex carries information about a stimulus that the spike
count does not \citep{panzeri2001}. A clocked latch, by contrast, looks at its
input only during a short aperture at the clock edge. The input's value there is
a carrier. The course of the voltage between edges, which nothing reads, is a
trace, like the supply current. So a carrier's \emph{next content} is what its
readers take from it next, at the time they take it: for a membrane, the time of
its next spike; for a node that feeds a latch, its value at the next clock edge.

Agreement is measured between regions whose contents represent a shared
variable. The regions, and each one's content variable, are fixed by this causal
role and by decodability before any agreement is measured. Nobody can make the
measurement trivial by choosing the regions after the fact.

\paragraph{A running example.} A visual area represents the bird's location on
a retinotopic map, and a parietal area represents the same location to plan a
reach. Each area's content is its estimate of the location, carried by the
membrane potentials and spike times of the cells that encode it, and unity, here,
is the two estimates agreeing.

\paragraph{The definition, stated precisely.} Region $i$ carries a content
$s_i$, a description of the variables it represents, and regions $i$ and $j$
overlap in the variables both represent. Restricting a content to an overlap
keeps only the shared variables. The contents are \emph{compatible} when every
two agree on their overlap, $s_i|_{i\cap j}=s_j|_{i\cap j}$. Compatible contents
extend to exactly one global description whose restriction to each region is
that region's content. Unity is compatibility, and its degree is the
\emph{overlap discrepancy}: the largest distance between two contents restricted
to a common overlap.

Approximate agreement still fixes one description: if every overlap discrepancy
of real-valued contents is at most $\varepsilon$, a weighted average of the
contents that cover each variable is within $\varepsilon$ of every region's
content. Agreement is measured by decoding, and two accurate decoders agree
whatever produced their agreement, since $|d_A-d_B|\le|e_A|+|e_B|$ for errors
$e_A$ and $e_B$. The measure is therefore the agreement \emph{in excess of} what
the two errors imply. These results are proved in a companion paper
\citep{bobadillasuarez2026coherence}, and P adds a condition on how that
agreement is produced.

\paragraph{Two stages.} The companion proposes agreement on overlaps as a
correlate of unity, to be tested, and P is conditional on that identification.
Its test asks whether excess decoded agreement is larger on trials the subject
perceives than on trials it does not. A positive result makes the bridge of
Section~\ref{sec:tests} the next question; a negative one removes decoded
agreement as a neural measure of unity and leaves the bridge its behavioural
readout, so the second stage does not wait on the first.

\section{Enforced agreement and computed agreement}
\label{sec:enforcement}

\paragraph{The argument in outline.} Five definitions make ``physically
enforced'' precise. Two theorems show that content read from bits does not
respond to small changes, without noise and with it. Two premises, wholeness and
counting, turn that fact into a claim about unity, and the rest is proved. One
measurement protocol, applied to a cortical membrane and to a logic node, shows
where each substrate holds its carriers.

\paragraph{Definitions.} Each is stated in words first.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Micro state and content.} A system has a physical state, its
\emph{micro state} $x=(x_i)_i$, made up of the states $x_i$ of its regions. One
step of its physics carries $x$ to $f(x)$. A \emph{content map} $\pi$ reads each
region's content off the micro state. The content level is \emph{closed} when
the next content can be computed from the current content alone, without
looking at the micro state: $\pi\circ f = g\circ\pi$ for some content dynamics
$g$. A digital computer's logical level is closed by design. The next content
$\pi(f(x))$ of a carrier is what its readers take from it next.
\item \emph{Noise margin.} A readout has a noise margin on a set of states when
each state in it has a neighbourhood on which the readout is constant, as any
voltage well above a bit's threshold reads as 1.
\item \emph{Graded dependence.} Content depends gradedly on a region when every
change to that region, however small, can move the next content. Formally, no
neighbourhood of the region's micro state leaves the next content unchanged
while the other regions are held fixed.
\item \emph{Contraction.} The overlap discrepancy \emph{contracts} near a state
when one step of $f$ shrinks it by a factor below one throughout a
neighbourhood of that state, so perturbations too small to cross any threshold
are pulled back toward agreement along with larger ones.
\item \emph{Physical enforcement.} Agreement is physically enforced at a state
when content depends gradedly on each region there and the overlap discrepancy
contracts near it. P requires this at the states the system occupies while its
contents are unified.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The margin lemma.} In words: content read through a margin cannot
feel small enough physical changes. Precisely, if the micro dynamics is
continuous and carries a state into a margin's operating set, the next content
depends gradedly on no region's micro state. The proof takes one line:
continuity carries a small enough neighbourhood of each region's micro state
into the neighbourhood on which the readout is constant. A finite word of
thresholded quantities has a margin away from its thresholds, so the lemma
covers any content read from bits. It is elementary by design: a reader who
disputes the separation disputes a definition, and the rest of this section
argues for each.

\paragraph{Noise.} A gate's chance of flipping is a smooth function of its input
voltage and never exactly zero, so the \emph{probability} of a digital machine's
next content depends, very slightly, on every region, and the exact margin lemma
would mark a difference below the noise of both substrates. P is therefore read
on probabilities.

The \emph{response} of a content to a region at scale $\eta$ is the largest
change that moving the region by at most $\eta$ makes to the probability of any
outcome of the next content, a total-variation distance. The yardstick for a
large response is the response that a shift of one standard deviation produces
in a Gaussian carrier,
$2\Phi(1/2)-1=\ueFluctuationTV$, and call it \emph{the fluctuation}: a response
this large moves the content by as much as the content naturally fluctuates.
Graded dependence above the noise floor asks the response to reach the
fluctuation for every change of the region that is larger than its own noise and
smaller than the change that crosses one of its thresholds. When the region
moves a carrier linearly under additive noise whose density has a single peak,
Gaussian or heavy-tailed, a larger change moves the content at least as much as
a smaller one, and the criterion is met exactly when a change of one noise
amplitude reaches the fluctuation (Appendix~\ref{app:noise} treats noise whose
law changes with the state). No constant is fitted to either substrate.

\paragraph{The noisy margin theorem.} The margin lemma has a noisy form, and it
is this form that carries the argument. Let each realization of the noise fix an
$L$-Lipschitz step of the micro dynamics, one that stretches no distance by more
than a factor $L$. Let the content be constant within radius $r$ of the noisy
successor, except on a set of realizations of probability at most
$\varepsilon$. Then a change of one region smaller than $r/L$ moves the
probability of every outcome of the next content by at most $\varepsilon$. For
anything computed from bits, $\varepsilon$ is the probability that some bit of
the noisy successor lands within $\delta$ of its threshold, and the bound is
attained. In plain terms: below its margin, a bit feels a change only through
its error rate.

\paragraph{The simulation objection.} Why state P on the physical state, below
the level of contents? Because at the content level a computer can imitate a
coupled field exactly: a digital system that integrates the field's equations
has a content map whose next step shrinks the disagreement between overlapping
regions just as the field does. P is stated instead on how content depends on
graded physical state, and there the two differ. The field's content moves with
every graded change in the regions it overlaps; the simulation's content is read
from bits held inside their margins and moves with none of them. So a perfect
simulation of a coupled cortical field reproduces its content-level trajectory
and fails P. The next paragraphs give the reason for that verdict.

\paragraph{Why unity tracks enforcement.} Unity settles how many subjects there
are: a unified experience is had by one subject and a divided one by two or
more. So whatever makes contents unified also decides whether they belong to one
system or to several, and agreement at the content level cannot settle that
count. Put the records of one program in different buildings, stretch the
clerk's step to a year, or let one processor visit the records in turn. The
content trajectory stays the same, while the system that realises it goes from
one chip to several machines to one record at a time. Block's nation, whose
citizens run a brain's functional organisation by radio \citep{block1978}, is the
same program spread over a country. A criterion stated on content-level
agreement gives all of these the same number of subjects, with no physical fact
to consult.

\paragraph{Wholeness: unity is not one more content.} Wholeness takes
experiences to be unified when they are parts of a single total experience, the
subject's experience at that time \citep{bayne2010}. Being a part of a whole is
not a further part of it. So the unity of two contents is not a third content
alongside them, and that limits what can realise it.

\paragraph{Records join nothing.} A \emph{record} is a content held by a margin,
sealed off from the graded state of everything else. A rule that compares
records and corrects them reads and writes records, and whatever it produces is
one more record. Once systems are counted as the counting premise below counts
them, by chains of couplings at the noise floor, three facts follow as theorems.
\begin{itemize}
\item A region whose content is held by a margin, with an error rate below the
fluctuation, is coupled to no region that admits a change above its noise inside
the margin, whatever the regions it reads carry. It is one system only with
itself.
\item For any set of records, records that read records included, two regions
are one system exactly when a chain of couplings that avoids every record joins
them. Records drop out of every chain, so rules that check rules add nothing at
any depth.
\item Writing corrections back changes nothing. When one region's next content
depends on another only through a record's symbol, a change of the other inside
the margin moves it by at most the record's error rate, so the rule couples the
two no more than the record does.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Graded readers join.} A graded reader stays in the chain. A region
whose content moves with two regions above their noise, and moves them in turn,
joins them into one system even when they share no coupling of their own. Under
linear dynamics with uniform noise such a reader joins two regions that a record
reading the same two leaves apart (Appendix~\ref{app:formal}). The regress stops
at a reader on the chain, not at a record beside it. A register holding the
bird's location as a number is a record; a cell in a third area that integrates
spike times from the visual and the parietal area, and projects back to both, is
a reader. A workspace that makes every record available to a common reader is
the rule again. Computation adds contents and never produces the whole they are
parts of. What can make
contents parts of one whole, without adding a content, is a physical state whose
coordinates carry them, and whose agreement is a fact about where that state
sits, written nowhere. P asks for exactly this.

\paragraph{Counting: systems at the noise floor.} Physics counts systems by
interaction: parts form one system when their joint state does not split into
independent coordinates. At no particular scale this makes every chip one
system, through wires, supply and crosstalk, which is correct for the voltages.
But unity is a property of contents, a chip reads its contents through margins,
and whether two coordinates split depends on the resolution at which they are
taken. Three steps fix that resolution from the parts themselves.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The lower end is the part's own noise.} A difference smaller than a
part's noise does not survive the part's next step, so it is not a state the
part holds, nor a coordinate along which it can be independent of anything.
\item \emph{The upper end is the part's nearest threshold.} A change that crosses
a threshold changes the part's symbol, and what another part registers of the
crossing is the symbol, the same whatever graded state produced it. That
registration is a message, an exchange of records, and records join nothing.
\item \emph{In between, physics asks for a product.} Two parts are separate
systems at a resolution when the dynamics of each is independent of the other's
state at that resolution. A record meets this outright, since no change inside its
margin moves it beyond its error rate. A graded reader is the opposite case:
every change of the region it reads, between noise and threshold, moves it by at
least its own fluctuation.
\end{enumerate}
The count takes the strict reading of the third step. Two carriers are one
system when \emph{every} change of one between its noise and its nearest
threshold moves the other by at least the other's own fluctuation, and two when
\emph{some} such change leaves the other unmoved. A product decomposition asks
less: it may join a carrier that registers some changes in the range and not
others. The two agree on records and on graded readers, the two cases the
argument turns on, and between them the strict reading is the first two steps
applied inside the range. Where a larger change moves the content at least as
much as a smaller one, as it does under the single-peaked noise treated above, a
carrier can miss a change in the range in two ways. It can miss the small
changes and register the large ones, which is a margin narrower than the range.
Or it can register changes on one side of the state and miss those on the
other, which is a threshold at the state, and then what it registers is which
side was taken, a symbol. Both are the ways a record registers, so the strict
reading adds no condition of its own. Both ends of the range belong to each
part, the size of its noise and the position of its thresholds, so nobody
chooses the scale to separate brains from chips.

\paragraph{A precedent: clusters in a gas.} Physics already counts systems by
comparing an interaction with the parts' own fluctuations. In the theory of
imperfect gases, two molecules belong to one physical cluster when their
attraction outweighs the kinetic energy of their relative thermal motion, and a
cluster is a set of molecules joined by chains of such bonds
\citep{hill1955,sator2003}. Molecules whose attraction is weaker than their
thermal motion still interact, and that interaction changes the pressure of the
gas, yet it joins them into no cluster. The criterion has the three features of
the count: the scale is set by the parts' own noise, systems are joined through
chains, and a real interaction below that scale leaves the parts separate, as
crosstalk below a bit's margin leaves bits separate.

\paragraph{From systems to P.} The count is the noise-floor reading of graded
dependence given above, and the identity is proved: for carriers whose contents
overlap, each depends gradedly above the noise floor on every carrier it
overlaps exactly when every overlapping pair is one system. A region holds many
carriers, and a noisy carrier registers another only while it sits near its own
threshold, as measured below. So two regions are one system when some carrier
of each registers some carrier of the other, and P asks this within every window
of the contents' time scale. Asking it of some carriers is strictly weaker than
asking it of all: a state exists at which the first holds and the second fails.

\paragraph{The chip, counted.} A logic node of \ueNodeCapFf{}~fF at room
temperature has thermal noise of \ueThermalNoiseMv{}~mV
\citep{johnson1928,nyquist1928}, and the textbook inverter's margin is
\ueMarginToNoise{} times that. A change of one noise amplitude flips the bit with
probability below $10^{-\ueErrorOrders}$ \citep{kish2002}, so by the noisy margin
theorem no bit registers any other, and each bit is a system of its own. Between
thresholds, where a working chip spends nearly all of its time, its contents are
as many systems as it has bits, carried on voltages that form one
(Appendix~\ref{app:count}).

\paragraph{Where the line falls.} A criterion built to exclude chips would draw
its line between substrates. This one draws it inside each substrate, at a
carrier's distance from its own threshold, and it discards threshold crossings in
cortex as in silicon: the occurrence of a spike is a message, and a spike count
latched at a clock edge is a record. Cortex passes on what is left, the range
below threshold in which a membrane moves with each input above its noise. An
analog crossbar whose column currents drive the next array directly passes, and
a neural circuit read only from latched counts fails (Section~\ref{sec:ai}).

\paragraph{The membrane, counted.} A spike time passes the test at the moment
the membrane rises through threshold, where one unitary cortical input shifts
the spike by \ueCorticalShiftToJitter{} times its jitter
(Appendix~\ref{app:noise}). To see how often a membrane is in such a state, the
protocol applied to the logic node is applied to a leaky integrate-and-fire
membrane driven by white noise, over every state it occupies, with time
constants of \uOccTauFast{} to \uOccTauSlow{}~ms
\citep{destexhe1999,destexhe2003} and rates of \uOccRateLow{} to
\uOccRateHigh{}~Hz \citep{hromadka2008}. It sits on average \uOccDistanceMin{} to
\uOccDistanceMax{} noise amplitudes below threshold and passes at
\uOccPassMinPercent{} to \uOccPassMaxPercent\% of the states it occupies
(Appendix~\ref{app:occupancy}). A bit restored to a rail passes at none. No single
bit can pass when the noise of the step that reads it includes one amplitude of
the change: asked in both directions, its response under Gaussian noise is at
most $\Phi(1)-1/2=\uYardBinaryCeiling$, the \emph{binary ceiling}, below the
fluctuation.

\paragraph{Rarely at an instant, often within a window.} A stationary membrane
enters a passing state within \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms, the integration time that
precedes a conscious percept, with probability \uOccHitMinLong{} to
\uOccHitMaxLong{}, even when the region it depends on supplies only
\uOccShareMin{} of its noise variance. Some of \uOccCarriersFew{} independent
carriers then links two regions within such a window with probability at least
\uOccLinkFewLong{}, while at a single instant even \uOccCarriersMany{} carriers
link them with probability as low as \uOccInstantLinkMin{}. That is why P is
stated per window. The verdict does not rest on the window's length. No content
changes faster than the \uWinPerceptMs{}~ms at which two events are perceived in
order \citep{hirsh1961}, and some of \uOccCarriersMany{} independent carriers
links two regions more often than not from a window of \uWinLowManyMs{}~ms
upward, and at \uWinPerceptMs{}~ms with probability at least \uWinLinkPercept{}.

\paragraph{Readers, shared input and switching nodes.} Three variations leave the
verdict where it is (Appendix~\ref{app:occupancy}). A reader that registers only
whether the membrane fired within the window finds it passing at
\uOccCountPassMinPercent{} to \uOccCountPassMaxPercent\% of its states; if every
cortical reader pooled spikes only over windows longer than a content's time
scale, P would deny unity to cortex, and that would count against P. Carriers that share input \citep{poulet2008}, up to
\uCorShareTol{} of their noise variance, still link two regions: some of
\uOccCarriersMany{} does so within \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms with probability at
least \uCorLinkManyTol{}. And a chip's nodes, which pass through their thresholds
many times in every window, pass at the voltage as a membrane does, since a
change of one noise amplitude moves a crossing by one jitter. The voltage is not
what the chip reads, though. Timing closure keeps every transition outside the
setup-and-hold window of the latch that reads the node \citep{ginosar2011}, so
the latch reads the restored bit, which passes at no state and in no window. The
separation holds at every yardstick from \uYardLow{} to \uYardHigh{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:yardstick}). What separates the membrane from the restored bit
is the distance from threshold at which each is held when it is read: a few
noise amplitudes, against \ueMarginToNoise{}.

\paragraph{Categories.} Categorical contents, such as the dominant percept in
binocular rivalry, have margins in every substrate, so P is stated on the graded
variables from which categories are read, such as the relative strength of
competing percepts. This opens no route for a digital machine: every variable it
computes is a function of its bits.

\paragraph{Gradual replacement.} Replacing a brain's neurons one at a time by
digital units that compute the same input--output function is the standard
objection to a substrate condition \citep{chalmers1995}. Exact replacement is
unavailable at the grain P concerns: a unit read through margins discards the
graded dependence below them, and whether that changes behaviour is what the
bridge of Section~\ref{sec:tests} tests for one connection. Where behaviour and
report are preserved exactly, P accepts that unity may still change, because
reports are contents and unity is not one more content
(Appendix~\ref{app:replacement}).

\paragraph{What the premise rests on.} Cortex passes the count within a window
of a percept's time scale, which is what P asks, so P rests on wholeness and
counting, and every step between them is proved. Counting has a second support in the effective description physics
already uses at the parts' resolution (Appendix~\ref{app:count}), and a
precedent in how it counts clusters in a gas. The bridge tests the two premises together and decides between wholeness and
its main rival, joint access, on which contents are unified when available
together to the same consumers \citep{bayne2010}. The quantized loop of the bridge carries at least as much
information to the same regions as the analog loop, in total and about the task. Joint
access therefore predicts no advantage for the analog loop, while wholeness,
through P, predicts that unity follows the analog loop.
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what stands if either premise is rejected.

\section{When and how far a coupling enforces agreement}
\label{sec:reach}

P asks two things of the coupling: it must pull the disagreement of unified
contents back before the content changes, and it must reach every pair of
regions whose contents are unified. The first gives the prediction that the
restoration test of Section~\ref{sec:tests} checks; the second says over what
distances that test should find restoration.

\paragraph{Agreement in time.} A correction that arrives after the content has
changed enforces agreement on a content already replaced, so enforced agreement
has a deadline. Two things limit how soon it arrives. It arrives no sooner than
the coupling's causal cone allows: a site can track a variable at another only
after enough rounds of the coupling to place the second site in its causal past.
And it arrives at the coupling's relaxation rate: a coupling that shrinks the
overlap discrepancy by a factor $\kappa<1$ per step brings a discrepancy of
$d_0$ within $\varepsilon$ after $\log(d_0/\varepsilon)/\log(1/\kappa)$ steps,
and one that shrinks it by no more than $\rho$ per step needs at least
$\log(d_0/\varepsilon)/\log(1/\rho)$. In cortex, signal travel typically takes
\ueConeFastMs{} to \ueConeSlowMs{}~ms of the \ueContentMs{}~ms that precede a
conscious percept, so the deadline falls on the relaxation time
(Appendix~\ref{app:window}). P therefore predicts a cortical relaxation time of
at most \ueTauSlowMs{} to \ueTauFastMs{}~ms across the cortex, as signals take
the slower or the faster fibres, and \ueTauLocalMs{}~ms within a posterior zone.
A restoration slower than these counts against P.

\paragraph{Reach.} The regions to be joined lie up to \ueFibreCm{}~cm apart if a
scene's contents span the cortex, and about \ueLocalCm{}~cm apart if they are
confined to a posterior zone. On a sheet of coupled oscillators sharing one
natural frequency, in the harmonic approximation, the variance of the phase
difference between two sites is the noise strength times their \emph{effective
resistance}, the resistance a meter would read if each coupling were a wire of
the coupling's conductance. Under coupling of bounded range the resistance grows
at least logarithmically with distance, the Gaussian form of the absence of true
long-range order in two dimensions \citep{mermin1966}, while a kernel whose tail
falls off more slowly than $r^{-4}$ bounds it at every distance
\citep{chung1951,kunz1976}. A Lipschitz encoder carries phase agreement to
content agreement: at noise strength $D$ the root-mean-square content
discrepancy is at most $L\sqrt{D\,R_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:reach}).
In cortex the long-range coupling is synaptic, since the field of a local source
falls below detection within millimetres (Appendix~\ref{app:field}).

\paragraph{Local unity and switching.} P asks for agreement only among the
regions whose contents are unified, which may form one patch of cortex or
several patches joined through a hub that routes activity between them. A hub
that routes discrete messages, passing thresholded signals, exchanges records,
and records drop out of every chain of couplings. Patches joined only through it
are several unified islands whose agreement with one another is computed, as on
the switch model of the split brain \citep{bayne2010}. A hub that reads the
patches' graded state and drives them gradedly is a graded reader and joins them
into one system, even if it visits them in turn, since P is stated per window.
When the hub is graded, the regress stops at the hub, which is more coupling and
not a reader of records.

The thalamus is the natural candidate hub. Its relay cells fire in two modes
\citep{sherman2001}. In the tonic mode the output follows the input nearly
linearly \citep{lesica2004}. In the burst mode the cell answers with an
all-or-none burst, typically when excitation follows a prolonged inhibition
\citep{lesica2004}, and it can burst again only after a renewed hyperpolarization
\citep{sherman2001}. The protocol of Section~\ref{sec:enforcement}, applied to a
model relay cell with both modes \citep{smith2000} at matched rates, orders
them: within \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms a tonic cell enters a passing state at least
\uRlHitRatioMin{} times as often as a bursting one, in every declared regime
(Appendix~\ref{app:occupancy}). P therefore predicts that unity across patches
joined through the thalamus is greater in the tonic mode, a prediction that
relay cells recorded in each mode test. The same count applies to machines: analog patches joined by a digital router carry several unified
contents and no single one. Bridging two areas within one patch tests P
directly, and bridging two patches across a putative hub tests whether the hub
is graded.

\section{Current artificial systems}
\label{sec:ai}

A clocked digital chip is a collection of separate records. Each bit is held in
place by its own noise margin, and below that margin it varies independently of
every other bit and of the voltages beneath it. The records agree only when the
program, applied at each clock step, makes them agree. A transformer running on
such hardware reads every content from bits held within their margins, so its
agreement is computed, whatever it computes. A chip tracking the bird holds each
area's estimate as a word of bits, and the two words agree when a step of the
program copies or averages one into the other; between steps, neither moves with
the voltages beneath the other. Hardware whose contents are graded physical
quantities coupled without barriers, such as analog, in-memory or neuromorphic
substrates, can satisfy P. Satisfying it means that such a substrate's contents,
the column currents of an analog crossbar for instance, are held together
physically; it does not determine whether any of them is experienced, or whether
any is like a human percept.

\paragraph{Symbols and physical contents.} The line P draws is between symbolic
and physical content, and the line between digital and analog hardware is its
most common instance. A \emph{symbol}, in the sense the margin lemma makes
precise, is a content read through a noise margin: a physical state whose
identity survives every small enough change of the state that carries it, so two
symbols agree only when a rule makes them agree. A \emph{physical content} is a
graded quantity that the system's own dynamics reads, so its agreement with the
contents it overlaps is produced by the coupling between them. A bit and a spike
count latched at a clock edge are symbols; a spike time, a membrane potential
and the phase of a coupled oscillator are physical contents.

\paragraph{Organisation, not material.} The criterion concerns how a substrate is
organised, not what it is made of. An analog crossbar whose column currents
settle through shared conductances, or an array of coupled electronic or optical
oscillators, couples its contents as a cortical sheet does. A neuromorphic chip
passes where its contents are read from membrane potentials coupled continuously,
and fails where they are read from spike counts latched at a clock edge, and a
neural circuit read only from all-or-none events latched far from threshold
would fail as a digital circuit does. In analog in-memory accelerators, a layer's
products are currents summed on the lines of a memory array and then digitized:
in a 64-core phase-change chip each row's current is converted into integers,
and the traffic between cores runs on those integers \citep{legallo2023}. Such a
chip fails P between layers, since its graded stage lies inside each product and
couples no two contents.

\paragraph{Why spikes are not bits.} What makes all-or-none spikes graded
carriers is their timing. When a membrane rises through threshold, the moment it
crosses moves continuously with any small input, and receiving cells integrate
spike times as they arrive. A clock alone does not separate the substrates: a
spike time read through a clock still passes near threshold. What a digital
circuit adds is restoration. Each gate drives its output back to a rail, so every
transition a latch reads starts and ends far from any threshold. Clockless logic
fails in the same way: its transition times move with every voltage, but its
values are read from completed codes held at rails (Appendix~\ref{app:digital}).

\paragraph{Leakage and the margin's width.} Leakage below a bit's margin, from
supply noise, crosstalk or temperature, reaches content only through the error
rate. A gate's restoring gain pulls each bit toward its own rail, and pulls no
two bits toward agreement. Nor is the width of the margin a free design
parameter. A machine whose contents reached the fluctuation at the noise scale
would err at every step, so the line P draws coincides with the line between
digital and analog operation (Appendix~\ref{app:digital}).

\paragraph{Attractor basins.} Cortex has margins of its own. An attractor
network's basin is a dynamical margin: which attractor the network settles in
survives every small enough change. The basin gives the attractor's label a
margin, but it leaves graded the state that carries the label: the position along
a continuous attractor, such as a bump of activity that encodes a direction, and
the membrane potentials and spike times within the basin, which downstream cells
integrate. A cortical circuit whose contents were read only as attractor labels,
far from the basins' boundaries, would fall under the margin lemma as a chip
does, and the weak nudge of Section~\ref{sec:tests} tests which case cortex is
in.

\section{Tests}
\label{sec:tests}

\paragraph{What counts as evidence about experience.} Every test below measures
decoded agreement, the agreement of the contents available to readout. If a
split in readout could always be dismissed as a split in access alone, no
observation would bear on P. So the evidence about experience is fixed in
advance: responses that combine stimuli across the regions in question when the
task requires it, and reports of a single field of experience spanning them. A
dissociation counts against P if such responses and reports split while graded
coupling between the regions is intact, or stay integrated after it is removed.
This standard binds P: in a bridged cortex each region still reads its own
coupled carriers, so behaviour is evidence about unity, and a split of unity
behind unchanged reports is no answer P can give to a null result.

\paragraph{Separating coupling from information.} In every manipulation of
cortex available so far, graded coupling and information move together, and
stimulating the regions from outside does not separate them. Coherence between cortical regions arises
largely from communication between them \citep{schneider2021}, and where
in-phase transcranial stimulation of two regions raised their synchrony, it
raised directed information flow with it \citep{reinhart2019}. The coupling has
to be supplied instead.

\paragraph{The bridge.} Interrupt the natural connection between two regions,
and reconnect them by stimulating each in proportion to the activity recorded at
the other (Figure~\ref{fig:bridge}). The reconnection runs through one of two
loops.
\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{analog loop} is an analog filter and amplifier driving the
stimulator, analog end to end, so its output moves with every graded change in
its input.
\item The \emph{quantized loop} is the same filter and amplifier with a quantizer
between them, so its output is read from bits and has a margin.
\end{itemize}
The two loops share one filter shape. They differ in the quantizer and in one
cutoff frequency, set below.

\paragraph{Matching the information.} The analog loop must carry no more
information than the quantized one, and the two must fall on opposite sides of P. Adding noise to the analog loop to
match them would make it fail P too, so its bandwidth is lowered instead,
keeping the cutoff high enough that the loop settles in time
(Appendix~\ref{app:bridge}). Transfer entropy counts everything one region's
activity tells about the other's, and the loops could match it and still differ
in how much of the task gets through. So the task variable is also decoded from
the activity each loop evokes in the region it drives, and the loops count as
matched when the analog loop carries no more information than the quantized
one, in total or about the task. Both loops were simulated on a model pair of
regions, with a linear and a nearest-neighbour estimator of each quantity. At a step of
\ueBridgeStepToNoise{} noise amplitudes, a cutoff between \uBrCutoffMin{} and
\uBrCutoffMax{}~Hz matches the linear estimate of transfer entropy in every
simulated regime whose task variable spans at least half a step. At that cutoff
the analog loop carries less task information than the quantized one by either
estimator, and less transfer entropy by the nearest-neighbour one. An account
on which unity follows the information regions exchange therefore predicts no
advantage for the analog loop.

\paragraph{The readout.} If unity, with the evidence fixed above, follows the
analog loop, that is evidence that unity tracks the finer grain and not only the
information the regions exchange. If unity is the same under both loops, that
counts against P. In animals, unity is measured by integration across the
bridged connection in behaviour and by decoded agreement in the recordings;
reported unity needs a bridged human preparation. The loops share every stage
but the quantizer and the cutoff, so no property of the electronics separates
them, and the bridge tests P as a criterion of organisation
(Section~\ref{sec:ai}) against accounts on which unity follows the information
regions exchange.

\paragraph{A first preparation.} Closed loops that trigger stimulation at one
cortical site from spikes recorded at another already run for days in behaving
animals \citep{jackson2006,guggenmos2013}; the analog arm replaces the trigger
with a continuous drive. A first preparation places chronic
arrays in two areas that represent a shared variable, such as the visual and
parietal areas that represent the bird's location, interrupts the natural
connection between them, and alternates the two loops across blocks in the same
animal. Transfer entropy \citep{schreiber2000} is estimated from each block's
recordings, and the step and cutoff are set on pilot blocks. The primary
readout is behavioural: a task that requires combining the values the two areas
represent, such as judging whether stimuli in their two fields match. Blocks
with the connection interrupted and neither loop running come first, as a
positive control: the comparison is informative only if the analog loop restores
some of the integration that interruption removes. The companion's excess
decoded agreement is the neural readout. At declared effect sizes the
behavioural comparison is the weaker and needs \uBrTrialsBehaviour{} trials per
loop. The loops record and stimulate in separate frequency bands, so the
recordings stay readable under a continuous drive (Appendix~\ref{app:bridge}).

\paragraph{A weak nudge should spread.} Weak stimulation of one region, below the
level that changes behaviour, should shift the content decoded in an overlapping
region toward agreement with it, in proportion to the stimulation's strength.
Transcranial alternating current and weak intracortical fields supply the nudge.
The decoded content must be a graded variable read from activity the region's
targets integrate, since a decoded category is constant away from its
boundaries in any substrate. A change alone confirms little, since most accounts expect
stimulating one region to alter connected ones. What P predicts is the change's
form: a shift with the sign that agreement requires, in proportion to strength
down to the weakest strength that measurably moves the stimulated region, with
no strength below which it vanishes. No shift, a shift of the wrong sign, or one
that appears only above a threshold strength counts against P.

\paragraph{Agreement should be restored.} Perturb one region and measure how
fast its disagreement with its partners comes back down. P predicts restoration within the relaxation time of
Section~\ref{sec:reach}, with a restoring pull proportional to the perturbation,
below any threshold as well as above. On an artificial system, patching the
activations of one layer, head or position runs the same protocol, and the
margin lemma fixes the outcome: restoration by computation above the scale at
which the logical trajectory changes, and nothing below it. That contrast makes
the cortical outcome informative.

\paragraph{The field's causal cone.} The brain's electric field reaches the
tissue with no propagation delay \citep{gratiy2017}, while synaptic influence
across the cortex takes at least \ueConeMaxMs{}~ms. Re-agreement faster than the
synaptic route allows would reveal a non-synaptic part of the coupling. The
field of a local source falls below detection within millimetres, so the cone is
tested at millimetre distances, and at centimetres P predicts re-agreement
through synaptic coupling, which is graded as well (Appendix~\ref{app:field}).

\paragraph{Split brain.} Callosal section, read as divided perception with
undivided consciousness \citep{pinto2017}, contested \citep{dehaan2020}, or as
consciousness alternating between the hemispheres \citep{bayne2010}, removes
graded coupling and information together, and the remaining subcortical routes
carry both at once: the confound the bridge is built to break.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Appendix~\ref{app:related} relates P to accounts that tie consciousness to
biology, to the analog--digital distinction and to field theories.

\paragraph{Indicator properties.} \citet{butlin2023} assess current AI systems
against indicator properties of consciousness, each stated computationally, so
every indicator sits at the grain of an algorithm. P adds one indicator stated
at the grain of graded dynamics, a condition on how the substrate realises the
others, so a system can have every computational indicator and fail P, as a
faithful simulation of cortex does.

\paragraph{Computational closure.} On the account of \citet{rosas2024}, a
macroscopic level that is informationally, causally and computationally closed
marks emergent software in a physical system. That closure is the property
Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} finds at the content level of a digital system,
and P reads it the other way round: a level closed off from the graded state
beneath it is one whose agreement is computed, not enforced.

\paragraph{Designed to suppress its physics.} \citet{kleiner2024} argue that if a
system's physical dynamics is relevant to its consciousness, processors designed
to suppress deviations from their computational dynamics cannot be conscious.
The margin lemma states that suppression formally, and this paper adds a scale
fixed by the parts' own noise, which counts systems instead of asking whether
physics is relevant at all, a contraction condition, and a test in cortex.

\paragraph{Integrated information.} Integrated information theory also denies
experience to digital simulations of a brain \citep{tononi2015,findlay2024},
through the causal structure of the hardware at the grain of its logic gates. P
turns instead on how content depends on graded physical state, measured by one
protocol on a cortical membrane and on a logic node.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The premise.} P is a premise and is not derived from the dynamics;
the cortical tests bear on whether it holds. It is necessary for unity and not
sufficient for consciousness: coupled pendulums satisfy it. It constrains how a
system's conscious contents are held together and is silent on which contents
are conscious and what they are like. An analog crossbar that passes P holds its
column currents together physically, which makes none of them an experience,
still less one like a human percept.

\item \emph{Wholeness.} An account on which unity is joint access to contents
rejects the argument's first step. Rejecting wholeness leaves the theorems, the
count and the measurements standing, and removes the step from unity to one
physical system; the bridge then tests joint access against wholeness directly.

\item \emph{Counting.} Rejecting counting, in favour of a count at no particular
scale, makes every chip one system. Chips still fail P by the noisy margin
theorem; what is lost is the argument from wholeness to P. The count is stricter
than a product decomposition: a carrier that registers some changes between its
noise and its threshold and not others is two systems by the count and may be
one by a product decomposition. Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} reads such a
carrier as registering through a margin or a threshold, which holds where the
response grows with the change. The formal results start from the count.

\item \emph{Functionalism.} A functionalism that states its roles at the grain
of graded physical state agrees with P on every test here, and it is the
intended reading of P. The tests ask which grain unity tracks.

\item \emph{The hardware counts.} The ceiling on one bit holds for symmetric
single-peaked noise laws that put less than twice the fluctuation of their mass
within one standard deviation of the centre. Thermal noise meets it; a bit under
a Student law with three degrees of freedom can pass. The thermal and switching
counts declare their capacitance, clock, activity, transition time and
synchronizer.

\item \emph{The cortical estimates.} The single-input estimate combines in vitro
measurements of one connection type and one noise recording, through a linear
threshold crossing. The occupancy computation models one integrate-and-fire
membrane under white noise, with a threshold without dynamics of its own, and
its time constants, rates, windows, noise shares and carrier counts are declared
ranges, not fits. Correlation is one shared component at declared shares,
compared with the correlations of neighbouring cells, and omits the unshared
input that drives the single spikes of awake cortex \citep{poulet2008}. The
carrier counts stay far below the anatomical ones. The per-window bound is
proved for independent carriers and, without independence, as the variance of
the number that pass over the square of its mean, a ratio that correlation keeps
above a floor. The sweep of the window's length assumes independent carriers,
and shared input lowers the link most in the shortest windows. The relay cell
is a reduced model with no reticular feedback.

\item \emph{Reach.} Phase variance equals effective resistance in the harmonic
approximation at identical natural frequencies. The logarithmic lower bound is
proved for kernels of bounded range, and the bound under a tail slower than
$r^{-4}$ for a sheet that supplies the site counts and couplings of a dyadic
chain of discs. Unbounded resistance under a kernel of finite second moment is
the classical recurrence criterion on the lattice; logarithmic growth is
established numerically. Above the order--disorder crossover the harmonic
identity fails. One oscillator population per content patch is a modelling
choice about cortex (Appendix~\ref{app:reach}).

\item \emph{Which variables are contents.} Which variables a system's own
dynamics reads is settled system by system from its circuitry, and the margin
lemma takes it as given. A cortex whose unified contents were attractor labels
alone would fail P.

\item \emph{Artificial systems.} Confirming the predictions for artificial
systems checks that hardware runs inside its margins and leaves P untested; the
tests of P are the cortical ones.

\item \emph{Agreement in time.} Its edges are proved for a given contraction
factor, and identifying that factor with the coupling's spectral gap is linear
response. The relaxation time of cortical coupling is unmeasured, and agreement
is read as a declared reduction factor of disagreement.

\item \emph{The field.} A long-range tail improves agreement only in proportion
to its conductance against the short-range part. The field's reach rests on an
estimate that places its peak at a declared distance, and its contribution
across centimetres is a quantity to be measured.

\item \emph{The bridge's reach.} A functional account that counts the
quantizer's nonlinearity, or the cutoff that matches information, as a
difference between the loops can accommodate either outcome; such an account
individuates roles finely enough to see a quantizer, which is the grain P names,
so the bridge leaves it on P's side of the line. The comparison presupposes that
the analog loop restores some integration, which the positive-control blocks
check. That the loops can be matched is shown on a linear model pair of regions.

\item \emph{What a null leaves open.} The bridge routes coupling through
electronics and stimulation, so it tests P as a criterion of organisation. A
null, in which both loops restore integration equally, counts against that
reading and is compatible with a substrate-bound variant on which graded
coupling must run through tissue, a variant no artificial bridge can test. Both
arms also share two auxiliaries: stimulation enters a region unlike synaptic
input, recruiting cells synchronously and antidromically, and the loop realises
P only at the grain of a multi-unit envelope recorded from a few contacts.

\item \emph{Human evidence.} No human preparation yet measures reported unity
across a bridged connection. Human data can falsify P but cannot by themselves
tell the graded grain from the grain of information exchanged, because the two
co-vary in every manipulation available so far.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reach: resistance, encoders and simulations}
\label{app:reach}

The logarithmic growth is a lower bound for every kernel of bounded range. Group
the sites into shells around a site $a$, so that the coupling joins only sites
in the same or adjacent shells. The shells then act as conductors in series,
and the effective conductance from $a$ to any site beyond shell $n$ is at most
$\bigl(\sum_{k<n} 1/C_k\bigr)^{-1}$, where $C_k$ is the coupling crossing from
shell $k$ to shell $k+1$. On a planar sheet
the perimeter of shell $k$ grows linearly in $k$, so $C_k \le c\,(k+1)$ for a
constant $c$ set by the kernel's strength and width, and the effective
resistance is at least $\log(n+1)/c$.
Widening the kernel enlarges its shells and raises $c$, which slows the growth
and leaves it unbounded. Distance-independent agreement on a sheet therefore
needs coupling of unbounded range.

A tail slower than $r^{-4}$ bounds the resistance at every distance. Run a chain
of site sets out from $a$ through discs of radius $2^j$ and back in to $b$, the
two widest discs spanning the separation. The phase difference across $a$ and
$b$ telescopes through the discs' mean phases, and the drop between the means of
two consecutive discs is bounded by the coupling energy between them, shared
among the pairs of their sites. A disc of radius $2^j$ on a planar sheet holds at
least $\alpha\,4^j$ sites, and a kernel $K(r)\gtrsim r^{-(2+\sigma)}$ couples
two sites of consecutive discs by at least $\beta\,2^{-(2+\sigma)j}$. The
number of pairs grows as $16^j$ and their coupling falls as $2^{-(2+\sigma)j}$,
so the drops shrink geometrically with ratio $q=2^{-(2-\sigma)/2}$, which is
below one for $\sigma<2$, and the effective conductance is at least
$(1-q)^2\alpha^2\beta/8$ however far apart $a$ and $b$ lie.

Both results match classical potential theory. Imagine a random walker on the
lattice that jumps between sites with probabilities proportional to the kernel,
which is the same at every site. The resistance from a site to infinity is
infinite exactly when that walker is sure to return to its start, that is, when
the walk is recurrent \citep{doyle1984}. Shorting every site outside a disc
around $a$ can only lower the resistance from $a$ to a site $b$ beyond the disc,
so under recurrence that resistance grows without bound as $b$ recedes. A
symmetric kernel of finite second moment, which includes every tail steeper
than $r^{-4}$, gives a recurrent walk in two dimensions \citep{chung1951}. The
bound above is the matching statement for tails slower than $r^{-4}$, whose
walks escape. The same threshold holds beyond the harmonic approximation: the
plane rotator in two dimensions with coupling falling as $r^{-\alpha}$,
$2<\alpha<4$, has true long-range order at low noise \citep{kunz1976}, which
short-range coupling forbids. The plane-rotator sheet also has a
Kosterlitz--Thouless transition \citep{frohlich1981}, an order--disorder
crossover that the simulations below locate. When natural frequencies differ
from site to site, short-range coupling fares worse still: phases then
desynchronize in every dimension up to four \citep{hong2005}.

Two further properties bound the resistance from above. Adding coupling never
raises it, and a direct coupling caps it at its reciprocal. Across a finite
cortex a wide short-range kernel and a slow tail differ by a slowly growing
logarithm, so telling them apart takes a measurement of the kernel's second
moment as well as of its tail.

An encoder that reads the phases of a small neighbourhood relative to the phase
at its centre does better than the Lipschitz bound of Section~\ref{sec:reach}.
It reports one content at every site of a travelling plane wave or a winding,
whatever the gradient, and for a wave plus a departure from it its discrepancy
between two sites is at most its Lipschitz constant times the departure's
relative readings there, so it pays for the local roughness of the field and
for nothing else. Both bounds read one oscillator population per content patch.
Across centimetres, where two territories describing one quantity need not be
neighbours on the sheet, that reading is an assumption, the
\emph{phase-to-content assumption}: each territory's content is read, by
encoders of Lipschitz constant $L$ that are equal where territories overlap,
from the phase of one oscillator population assigned to it, so that the phase
agreement of the two populations bounds the agreement of the two contents. It
is a modelling choice about cortex, since the coupling of a field is spatial
while two territories describing one quantity need not be neighbours. Under it
the coupling's reach bounds phase disagreement between distant territories
directly and content disagreement through it.

Simulated sheets at identical frequencies, of side \uSideSmall{} and
\uSideLarge{}, with nearest-neighbour, exponential and power-law kernels of
tail exponent $\sigma=1$ and $\sigma=3$ at a common row sum, follow the harmonic
identity in the ordered phase. The measured phase variance is at most
\uHarmonicRatioLow{} times $D\,R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at $D=\uNoiseLow$ and at most
\uHarmonicRatioMid{} times at $D=\uNoiseMid$, the excess rising with noise as
the cosine coupling softens, and a logarithm fits its growth with distance
better than a line in \uLogarithmicRuns{} of the \uOrderedRuns{} runs at those
noise levels. At $D=\uNoiseHigh$ the nearest-neighbour sheet disorders: the
mean chord discrepancy $\langle 1-\cos(\theta_a-\theta_b)\rangle$ at the
largest separation is \uChordNearestHigh{}. The exponential and $\sigma=1$
sheets stay ordered at that noise (\uChordExponentialHigh{} and
\uChordSigmaOneHigh{}), and the $\sigma=3$ sheet nearly disorders
(\uChordSigmaThreeHigh{}), so the kernel's width sets the order--disorder
crossover as well as the rate of decay. Exact resistance sums at sheet sides
\uScalingSideA{}, \uScalingSideB{} and \uScalingSideC{} separate width from
tail. The exponential kernel, with \uStiffnessRatio{} times the
nearest-neighbour stiffness, gives resistance at half the side of
\uFarExponentialA{}, \uFarExponentialB{} and \uFarExponentialC{}, still
growing; the $\sigma=1$ tail gives \uFarSigmaOneA{}, \uFarSigmaOneB{} and
\uFarSigmaOneC{}, and the $\sigma=3$ tail, steeper than $r^{-4}$, gives
\uFarSigmaThreeA{}, \uFarSigmaThreeB{} and \uFarSigmaThreeC{}, growing as a
short-range kernel does.

\section{Agreement in time for cortex}
\label{app:window}

The causal cone is stated precisely as follows: a report at one site that tracks
a variable at another to within $\varepsilon$, for two values more than
$2\varepsilon$ apart, is possible after $T$ rounds of the coupling only if the
second site lies in the first site's causal past for $T$ rounds.

The longest cortico-cortical fibres run
about \ueFibreCm{}~cm \citep{nunez2014}. Callosal axons conduct at median
velocities of \ueVelocitySlow{} to \ueVelocityFast{}~m/s, and the fastest of
them at up to \ueVelocityMax{}~m/s \citep{caminiti2009}. A chemical synapse
adds about \ueSynapseMs{}~ms \citep{katz1965,sabatini1996}. The earliest a
synaptic signal can cross the cortex is therefore \ueConeMaxMs{}~ms, along the
fastest axons, and typical arrival takes \ueConeFastMs{} to \ueConeSlowMs{}~ms.
For the time scale of a content, take the unconscious integration that precedes
a conscious percept, which lasts up to \ueContentMs{}~ms \citep{herzog2016}.
Count agreement as reached when disagreement has fallen \ueReduction{}-fold,
decaying exponentially. Agreement then arrives in time when the coupling's relaxation
time is at most \ueTauSlowMs{}~ms with typical arrival along the slower fibres,
and \ueTauFastMs{}~ms along the faster. Over a localized extent of
\ueLocalCm{}~cm the earliest arrival is \ueConeLocalMaxMs{}~ms, typical arrival
along the faster fibres is \ueConeLocalFastMs{}~ms, and the relaxation time may
reach \ueTauLocalMs{}~ms. Signal travel thus uses only a small
part of the time available, and whether agreement arrives in time depends on how fast
the coupling settles.

\section{A single input and noise that changes with the state}
\label{app:noise}

\paragraph{A neuron in numbers.} Suppose an
input $\Delta V$, too small to fire the cell on its own, arrives while the
membrane potential is rising through threshold at rate $\dot V$. It moves the
spike by $\Delta V/\dot V$. Voltage noise $\sigma_V$ jitters the spike by
$\sigma_V/\dot V$. The shift therefore exceeds the jitter exactly when
$\Delta V$ exceeds $\sigma_V$, whatever the slope, and spike generation adds
little noise of its own \citep{mainen1995}. A unitary excitatory potential
between cortical pyramidal cells averages \ueEpspMv{}~mV, with a coefficient of
variation of \ueEpspCv{} \citep{markram1997}, and the membrane noise near
threshold is \ueMembraneNoiseMv{}~mV \citep{jacobson2005}. Counting the input's
own variability as noise, one such input shifts the spike by
\ueCorticalShiftToJitter{} times its jitter, a response of \ueCorticalTV{}.
Every input larger than \ueEpspPassMv{}~mV passes, up to the input that fires
the cell.

\paragraph{Noise whose law changes with the state.} When the law of the noise
changes with the state, as the variance of a spike count does, a larger change
still moves the content at least as much as a smaller one on each side of the
state, provided the laws have a monotone likelihood ratio, as exponential
families do: such laws cross once along the parameter the region moves. The
criterion of Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} is then met exactly when a change of
one noise amplitude up and one down both reach the fluctuation.

\section{Counting in detail}
\label{app:count}

\paragraph{The logic node.} A change of one amplitude toward the threshold moves
a restored bit by the fluctuation only from half an amplitude away,
\ueGradedWindowPercent\% of the margin's width, where the bit is in the middle
of switching, and a change in both directions moves it by less at every
distance, by the binary ceiling. No other bit registers a change anywhere in the
margin except through the error rate. By the noisy margin theorem, once that
rate is under the fluctuation and each carrier has a change above its noise
inside the margin, no carrier of any region registers any other, and the
per-window form of P fails exactly as the per-instant form does.

The chip's wires pass on only changes that cross a threshold, which is the rule
applied at each clock step. Crosstalk and supply noise below the margin are real
interactions, and they move the voltages. By the noisy margin theorem they move
the contents by at most the error rate, the quantity a working chip is designed
to keep small. The count looks strange only because ordinary usage counts by
program, and one program uses all of the bits. Counted by physics, each bit is
restored to its own rail by its own gates, and what keeps the bits in step is
the rule the program applies, a protocol among separate records. The citizens of
Block's nation likewise register each other only through the messages they
exchange.

\paragraph{The same count from effective descriptions.} Physics reaches the same
count a second way, through the effective description it uses at the parts' own
resolution. Such a description keeps the variables that survive coarse-graining
between the parts' noise and their thresholds, and it treats as separate degrees
of freedom exactly those whose dynamics it can write without reference to one
another's state. A chip has one such description, its logic. Each bit evolves,
at each clock step, by a Boolean function of the bits wired to it, and the
voltages beneath are integrated out at the cost of the error rate. This is the
closure of the content level defined above, the property that marks a
macroscopic level as computationally closed \citep{rosas2024}, and in it the
bits interact only through the rule. A coupled field has no description of that
kind at the same resolution. Averaging a sheet of coupled oscillators or
membranes over blocks leaves a sheet whose blocks pull on their neighbours, and
each block's dynamics is written with its neighbours' state in it. So the
effective description makes a chip's contents many systems and a coupled
field's contents one, as the noise-floor count does. It is the description an
engineer uses to design the chip and a physicist uses to model the field, not
one built to separate them.

\section{Gradual replacement}
\label{app:replacement}

The standard objection to a substrate condition
replaces a brain's neurons one at a time by digital units that compute the same
input--output function \citep{chalmers1995}. If behaviour and report are
preserved throughout while unity fades or divides, then at the intermediate
stages the subject is wrong about its own experience, which the argument takes
to be absurd. P answers in two parts.

First, exact replacement is unavailable at the grain P concerns. By the margin
lemma, a unit that reads its inputs through margins reproduces a neuron's
function to the resolution of its margins, and discards the graded dependence
below it. Whether discarding it changes behaviour is an empirical question. The
bridge of Section~\ref{sec:tests} asks it for one connection, so the bridge is a
local, testable instance of replacement.

Second, where replacement preserves behaviour and report exactly, P accepts the
conclusion the argument calls absurd, and gives its reason. Reports are contents
computed from contents, and unity is not one more content, so reports can stay
the same while unity changes. Behaviour is evidence about unity in a system
whose contents are carried by its own coupling, and a system built to reproduce
that evidence by computation is the simulation objection again. A bridged cortex
is of the first kind, so at the bridge P stakes itself on behaviour
(Section~\ref{sec:tests}).

\section{The occupancy computation}
\label{app:occupancy}

\paragraph{What the membrane is asked.} The membrane's next content is the time
of its first spike within \uOccNextShort{} or \uOccNextLong{}~ms, and every
change of one to \uOccShiftMax{} noise amplitudes, in either direction, must move
that content by the fluctuation. Its time constants span the in vivo
high-conductance state and its rates the sparse rates of awake cortex. The
passing fractions of Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} are for a region that
supplies all of the membrane's noise. A bit restored to a rail at the logic
node's margin passes at none of its states, and its largest response over them
is below $10^{-\uOccBitOrders}$. The binary ceiling stays below the fluctuation
under every symmetric noise law with one peak that puts less than twice the
fluctuation of its mass within one standard deviation of its centre, logistic
and Laplace noise included, as shown at the end of this appendix. A graded
content clears the fluctuation by a wide margin: at its best state the membrane
responds by at least \uYardBestMin{} in every regime and at every noise share.

\paragraph{Windows.} For independent carriers the chance that none passes within
a window falls geometrically with their number: with \uOccCarriersMid{} carriers
some carrier passes within \uOccContentShortMs{}~ms with probability at least
\uOccLinkMidShort{}. Reading the spike time through a clock of
\uOccLatchShortMs{} or \uOccLatchLongMs{}~ms leaves the passing fraction
unchanged. The value a latch reads links in no window of any length.

\paragraph{Readers that ignore spike times.} A reader that registers only whether
the membrane fired within the window, and discards every spike time, passes with
a next content of \uOccNextShort{}~ms at the same share of states in every
regime as a reader of spike times. At a membrane a few amplitudes below
threshold, what a change of one noise amplitude moves is the chance that it
fires within the window, and a spike count registers that; a count responds at
least as much as this reader, since whether the membrane fired is a function of
the count. So P classes a cortical membrane as a graded carrier whether its
readers integrate spike times at the grain of milliseconds, as the evidence of
Section~\ref{sec:cover} indicates, or pool spikes over the content's window.

\paragraph{Shared input.} In awake cortex neighbouring membrane potentials are
highly correlated during quiet wakefulness and less so during active behaviour
\citep{poulet2008}. The model gives every membrane a shared component carrying a
fraction $c$ of its noise variance, so that two membranes correlate by $c$ and
each keeps its own law. At least one of \uOccCarriersMid{} such carriers enters a
passing state within \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms with probability at least
\uCorLinkMidTol{} for every share up to $c=\uCorShareTol$, in every regime.

\paragraph{Switching nodes.} Take a \uSwClockGHz{}~GHz clock and nodes that
switch in \uSwActivityLow{} to \uSwActivityHigh{} of its cycles, each transition
lasting \uSwTransitionPs{}~ps. If a node's next content were the time of its
crossing, the node would pass on the rising half of each transition, at
\uSwNodePassMinPercent{} to \uSwNodePassMaxPercent\% of its states, within the
membrane's range, and within every window of \uOccContentShortMs{}~ms except with
probability below $10^{-\uSwNoTransitionOrders}$. By the carrier condition of
Section~\ref{sec:cover}, though, a node's next content is what its latch reads
at the clock edge, the node at its rail. The crossing a latch discards is
exactly the content a membrane delivers when its readers integrate spike times
as they arrive. The separation holds for count readers too: a reader that
registered only whether a node switched within a cycle would take a quantity
fixed by the node's logical inputs, which are restored bits, moved by its noise
only through the error rate.

\paragraph{A thalamic relay cell.} The integrate-and-fire-or-burst model
\citep{smith2000}, with its published parameters, adds to the membrane a
low-threshold calcium current that flows above $-60$~mV, inactivates there and
recovers below it. Noise gives the passive membrane a standard deviation of
\uRlSigmaLow{} or \uRlSigmaHigh{}~mV, the unit of every change. In burst mode a
constant current holds the membrane below the calcium threshold, where the
current recovers and noise that carries the membrane past the threshold sets
off a burst; in tonic mode it holds the membrane above, the current stays
inactivated, and the cell is an integrate-and-fire membrane. In each mode the
current is calibrated to a rate of \uRlRateLow{} or \uRlRateHigh{}~Hz, and the
next content is the first spike time within \uOccNextShort{} or
\uOccNextLong{}~ms, as for the cortical membrane, with the calcium current's
state kept under each change. At an instant the two modes pass at similar
shares of their states, \uRlTonicPassMinPercent{} to
\uRlTonicPassMaxPercent\% in tonic mode and \uRlBurstPassMinPercent{} to
\uRlBurstPassMaxPercent\% in burst mode. A bursting cell's passing states lie in
and around its bursts, which are rare, while a tonic cell's lie below its spike
threshold, where it spends the time between spikes. Within
\uOccContentLongMs{}~ms a tonic cell enters a passing state with probability
\uRlTonicHitMin{} to \uRlTonicHitMax{}, and a bursting cell with probability
\uRlBurstHitMin{} to \uRlBurstHitMax{}. At matched noise, rate and window the
tonic cell's chance is at least \uRlHitRatioMin{} times the bursting cell's,
within \uOccContentShortMs{}~ms as well. Probability is propagated exactly on a
grid in membrane potential and inactivation, and a run at half every grid step
moves no visit probability by more than \uRlResolutionPercent\%.

\paragraph{The yardstick.} The separation of the membrane from the value a latch
reads holds at every yardstick from \uYardLow{} to \uYardHigh{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:yardstick}), not only at the fluctuation. Across that range the
latched logic node passes at none of its states, and the membrane enters a
passing state within \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms with probability at least
\uYardHitMinHigh{} at the highest yardstick, and at least as often at every lower
one. Below $\Phi(1)-1/2$ one bit can reach the yardstick, so there a
synchronizer's first stage can pass, at no more than \uSwSyncPassPercent\% of its
samples. The formal results take the yardstick as a parameter and hold at every
value of it. The fluctuation is the natural choice because it makes
\emph{large} mean what it means for the carrier itself: a change that moves the
content by as much as the content moves of its own accord.

\paragraph{Method.} The membrane is an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process in units of its own stationary
standard deviation, with the threshold at zero. After a spike it is refractory
and then reset to its mean, and the mean is calibrated so that the stationary
rate equals the declared rate. Probability is propagated on a voltage grid
through the exact Ornstein--Uhlenbeck step, and a crossing between two sampled
times is counted with the Brownian-bridge probability
$\exp(-2xy/s^2)$ for sub-threshold samples $x$ and $y$ and step deviation $s$.
Nothing is sampled. From each grid state, the law of the first spike time in
the window, with its absence as one more outcome, is propagated in one pass, and
the response to a change is the total-variation distance between two such laws.
A state passes when every admissible change moves the law by at least the
fluctuation, where a change is admissible if it is at least one noise amplitude
of the carrier and does not carry the membrane across threshold. The fraction of
occupied states that pass is the stationary mass of the passing states. The
probability of entering a passing state within a window is computed on the same
chain with the passing states made absorbing. A carrier that supplies a share
$\varphi$ of the membrane's noise variance has noise amplitude $\sqrt{\varphi}$
in the membrane's units, and its changes are scaled accordingly. The bit is a
carrier restored each step to a rail at a declared margin, read after one step of
unit Gaussian noise, with occupancy given by that noise around the rail. The
same computation is repeated at yardsticks from \uYardLow{} to \uYardHigh{} in
place of the fluctuation, at every regime and noise share. For correlated
carriers, each membrane is the sum of a shared Ornstein--Uhlenbeck component of
variance $c$ and a private one of variance $1-c$, both with the membrane's time
constant. Given the shared path the carriers are independent, so the
probability that none of $K$ passes within a window is the average of
$(1-h)^K$ over shared paths, where $h$ is one carrier's chance of passing given
the path. For each of \uCorPaths{} shared paths from a fixed seed, the
membrane's probability is propagated through the exact step with the private
noise, its centre moved by the shared path's increment, after a burn-in of five
time constants. Halving
both the grid spacing and the time step changes the passing fraction by
\uOccResolutionPercent\%, and the correlation of two carriers' passing by at
most \uCorResolutionRho{}.

Correlation lowers the per-window link. The chance that \uOccCarriersMid{}
carriers leave a window of \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms without a link is at most
\uCorMissA{} at $c=\uCorShareA$, \uCorMissB{} at $c=\uCorShareB$ and
\uCorMissC{} at $c=\uCorShareTol$. These bounds, and the links of at least
\uCorLinkMidTol{} and \uCorLinkManyTol{} in Section~\ref{sec:enforcement}, lie
two Monte Carlo standard errors beyond the estimate in every regime. The shorter window is more sensitive: within
\uOccContentShortMs{}~ms the link falls to about \uCorLinkShortTol{} at
$c=\uCorShareTol$, so P's per-window condition is met at the time scale of a
percept rather than of its fastest components.

The window is also swept on its own, from one time step to \uWinLongestMs{}~ms,
for independent carriers. The chance that one carrier occupies a passing state
at some step of a window is propagated once on the same chain, so every window
comes from one pass, and the link of $K$ carriers within it is
$1-(1-h)^K$. The link reaches one half in every regime and at every noise share
from \uWinLowManyMs{}~ms with \uOccCarriersMany{} carriers and from
\uWinLowMidMs{}~ms with \uOccCarriersMid{}, and since $h$ grows with the
window it stays above one half at every longer one. Halving the grid spacing and
the time step moves both ends earlier, so these are the conservative values.

The switching chip is counted in closed form. On the rising half of a
transition the node's voltage crosses its threshold at a slew $\dot V$, a change
$\Delta V$ moves the crossing by $\Delta V/\dot V$ and the node's noise jitters
it by $\sigma/\dot V$, so the response is $2\Phi(\Delta V/2\sigma)-1$ whatever
the slew. Every admissible change of at least one noise amplitude then reaches
the fluctuation, exactly at one amplitude, so the node passes at the
fluctuation and at no higher yardstick. A node that switches in a fraction
$\alpha$ of the cycles of a clock of frequency $f$, with transitions of
duration $t$, spends $\alpha f t/2$ of its time on the rising half of one, and a
window of $N$ cycles holds no transition with probability $(1-\alpha)^N$. A
synchronizer with aperture $T_W$, clock $f_C$ and data changing at rate $f_D$
samples inside its aperture at the rate $T_W f_C f_D$, so at most a share $T_W
f_D$ of its samples do. Each sample is read as one bit, so it passes at no
yardstick above $\Phi(1)-1/2$, and at lower yardsticks that share bounds its
passing fraction. A second stage allowed a time $S$ to resolve fails
once in $e^{S/\tau}/(T_W f_C f_D)$ on average, with $\tau$ the stage's
resolution time constant \citep{ginosar2011}; the declared values are that
tutorial's 28~nm example, $\tau=\uSwTauPs$~ps and $f_D=\uSwDataMHz$~MHz, with
$S$ one cycle.

The one place a working chip latches a transition is a synchronizer between
clock domains. With a \uSwAperturePs{}~ps aperture and data changing at
\uSwDataMHz{}~MHz \citep{ginosar2011}, its first stage samples inside the
aperture at most \uSwSyncPassPercent\% of the time, $\uSwSyncEventsLong$ times
in a window of \uOccContentLongMs{}~ms, and which way it resolves moves with the
timing of the transition; a second stage given one cycle to settle leaves it
unresolved once in more than $10^{\uSwMtbfOrders}$ years. What that second
stage takes is the bit the first resolves to. The noise in what the first stage
samples includes the transition's own jitter, one amplitude of the change, and
it is thermal, so Gaussian, and one bit under Gaussian noise does not reach the
fluctuation: at the centre of the aperture, where its two-sided response is
largest, a change of one noise amplitude in the transition's timing moves the
probability of either resolution by at most $\Phi(1)-1/2=\uYardBinaryCeiling$,
and the stage's own noise only lowers that. The synchronizer therefore fails
graded dependence at every sample, those inside its aperture included,
whichever protocol carries the data across the boundary, and so does a random
source that reads its bits from how a metastable stage resolves.

The ceiling depends on the noise law only through its mass near the centre.
A bit read as 1 when a quantity plus noise exceeds a threshold responds to a
change $a$ by the noise law's mass on one of the two intervals of width $a$ that
meet at the threshold, so its two-sided response is the smaller of the two, at
most half the mass of an interval of width $2a$. For a symmetric law with one
peak the smaller mass is largest with the threshold at the state, where it is
the mass between the centre and one amplitude. One bit therefore stays below
the fluctuation at every threshold exactly when the law puts less than twice the
fluctuation of its mass within one amplitude of its centre. Scaled to unit
standard deviation, Gaussian, logistic and Laplace noise meet this, and a
Student law with three degrees of freedom does not.

Several bits held near their thresholds together can read one graded quantity
$q$, as the stages of a time-to-digital converter read one transition time.
The word is a function of $q$ plus the quantity's own noise and the stages'
noise, and the stages' noise is independent of $q$. The response of the word
to a change of $q$ is therefore at most the response of $q$ itself,
$2\Phi(a/2\sigma)-1$ under Gaussian noise of amplitude $\sigma$, by the
data-processing inequality for total variation. With two thresholds half an
amplitude on either side of the state and no stage noise, the word's three
outcomes split the line where the two laws cross, and a change of one amplitude
in either direction moves it by exactly the fluctuation. A word whose
thresholds straddle the quantity within its noise thus passes exactly where the
quantity passes.

\section{Digital hardware in detail}
\label{app:digital}

\paragraph{Clockless logic.} Asynchronous circuits have no clock. In a
quasi-delay-insensitive design, each stage signals completion to the next
through a handshake, and correctness holds under every assignment of gate and
wire delays apart from a few forks assumed isochronic \citep{martin2006}. Their
transition times vary gradedly with every voltage, as a spike time does. But
the design makes the computed values independent of those times: a value is
accepted once its code is complete, whatever delays produced it. The timing of
such a circuit is therefore a trace in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:cover}. It
moves with the physics, while the circuit's own contents are read from completed
codes, each held at a rail by restoring gates, so they meet the margin lemma as
a clocked circuit's contents do.

\paragraph{Leakage.} Real digital hardware leaks: supply noise, crosstalk and
temperature move the analog quantities behind every bit. The margin lemma says
where such leakage can reach content. If a content read as a word of
thresholded continuous quantities depends gradedly on any region, some bit of
the next state sits exactly on its threshold, and correct operation keeps every
state off that set. Digital logic does pull perturbations back, but each gate
does so alone: its gain pulls a disturbed voltage back toward its own rail. That
restores each bit toward its own logical value, pulls no region toward agreement
with another, and is what keeps the bits inside their margins. The chip's
records stay separate even in the one place where its physics contracts.

The margin also has a width. If every bit of the next state is at least
$\delta$ from its threshold, each bit is read from an $L_v$-Lipschitz quantity
and the micro dynamics is $L$-Lipschitz, then a change to one region smaller
than $\delta/(L L_v)$ changes no bit. Leakage below that scale reaches content
only through the error rate (Section~\ref{sec:enforcement}), and leakage above
it flips a bit, which is a logical error. Digital design keeps ordinary noise
well below that scale: a textbook inverter's static noise margin is
\ueMarginPercent{}\% of its supply voltage, against an expected supply
variation of \ueSupplyPercent{}\% \citep{rabaey2003}, so the expected variation
is \ueNoiseToMargin{} of the margin. The nondeterminism of floating-point
reduction order is a choice among logical trajectories and lies outside both
results: it changes which content is computed, not how content depends on
graded state.

\paragraph{The margin's width.} If P turned on how wide an engineer happened to
make a noise margin, that would be a reductio of P: two machines running one
program would differ in unity by a design parameter. The width is not free in
that way. By the noisy margin theorem a content's response to a region is at
most its error rate, so a machine whose contents reached the fluctuation at the
noise scale would err with probability at least \ueFluctuationTV{} at every
step. Its bits would fail to hold their values from one step to the next, its
program's trajectory would stop being reproducible, and whatever agreement
survived would come from the physics beneath the bits and not from the program.
The line P draws therefore coincides with the physical line between digital and
analog operation, and working systems sit far from it on either side: a logic
node's margin is \ueMarginToNoise{} noise amplitudes wide, while a cortical
membrane sits on average \uOccDistanceMin{} to \uOccDistanceMax{} noise
amplitudes below its threshold, and some membrane of a region reaches a passing
state within a window of a percept's time scale with high probability.

\section{The bridge in detail}
\label{app:bridge}

The analog loop must carry no more information than the quantized one, and the
two must fall on opposite sides of P.
Section~\ref{sec:enforcement} fixes what the second condition means: a change
of the recorded region by its own noise amplitude must move the analog loop's
output by at least the fluctuation, and the quantized loop's output by less.
The analog loop passes exactly when its noise, in the region's units, is at
most the region's own. The quantized loop fails at every state farther from a
quantization edge than the region's noise plus \ueEdgeQuantile{} times the
recording noise, so its step must be many times both. Matching information by
adding noise to the analog loop defeats this. At matched information the added
noise is \ueMatchedNoiseToStep{} of the quantizer's step, so an analog loop
that still passes caps the step at \ueNoiseMatchMaxStep{} noise amplitudes of
the region, and the quantized loop then fails at no more than
\ueNoiseMatchFailPercent\% of its states, taking states as uniform within a
step. The analog loop's bandwidth is lowered instead, with no noise added,
until the information it carries, estimated from the recorded activity, no
longer exceeds the quantized loop's. Lowering the bandwidth slows the loop, and a loop
that settles after the content has changed fails P's timing condition
(Section~\ref{sec:reach}) however gradedly it couples. A first-order filter
with cutoff $f_c$ settles with time constant $1/(2\pi f_c)$, and the bridged
coupling relaxes no faster than its loop, so the analog loop's cutoff is kept
at or above \ueBridgeMinCutoffHz{}~Hz, whose time constant is at most the shortest
relaxation time that brings agreement in time in Appendix~\ref{app:window}. If
matching would take the cutoff lower, the quantizer's step is coarsened
instead, which lowers the quantized loop's transfer entropy and raises the
share of its states that fail. A null result then cannot come from an analog
loop too slow for the content's window. Above that floor the step is free: at a step of
\ueBridgeStepToNoise{} noise amplitudes, with recording noise no larger than
the region's own, the quantized loop fails at \ueBandMatchFailPercent\% of its
states or more. Stepping the quantized loop's resolution checks that its
transfer entropy rises with the resolution, so that the resolution, and not
recording noise, sets the matched value.

\paragraph{Whether the loops can be matched.} Two settings need not meet two
conditions, so both loops were simulated on a model pair of regions, at a 1~ms
step and in units of region A's noise amplitude. A task variable, a process of
standard deviation \uBrTaskSdLow{} to \uBrTaskSdHigh{} and time constant
\uBrTaskTauShort{} or \uBrTaskTauLong{}~ms, drives region A on top of A's own
noise. A is recorded with noise of its own amplitude and passed through the
shared filter, then through the analog loop's cutoff or the quantizer, and the
shared output stage drives region B, a leaky integrator with noise of its own.
Both quantities are estimated twice. The linear estimator takes transfer
entropy from A's recording to B's by the linear-Gaussian formula, and task
decodability as the variance of the task variable that a linear decoder of B's
recording explains on held-out trials. It misses what the quantizer's
nonlinearity passes on, so the nearest-neighbour estimator takes transfer
entropy as a conditional mutual information \citep{kraskov2004,frenzel2007} and
decodability as the variance a decoder would explain if the task information
in B's recording, estimated the same way, were Gaussian; on the analog loop the
two estimators agree. For each step and estimator, two cutoffs are read off a
grid from the floor above to 1~kHz: the one that matches the quantized loop's
transfer entropy and the one that matches its decodability. Both measures rise
with the cutoff, so at the lower of the two the analog loop carries no more of
either.

At the step of \ueBridgeStepToNoise{} noise amplitudes, in every regime whose task
variable spans at least half a step, the linear transfer entropy is matched
between \uBrCutoffMin{} and \uBrCutoffMax{}~Hz, well above the floor, and the
matched analog loop explains \uBrGapMin{} to \uBrGapMax{} less of the task
variable's variance than the quantized loop. The same holds at every simulated
step no larger than twice the task variable's standard deviation. Lowering the
bandwidth removes fast activity that carries transfer entropy and little of the
task, while the quantizer removes small changes at every frequency, so at
matched linear transfer entropy the quantized loop keeps more of the task. When
the task variable spans only a fifth of a step, the matched analog loop can
carry more task information, by up to \uBrGapOtherMax{}, and the step has to be
made finer before the loops are compared. The task is therefore chosen so that
its variable spans several noise amplitudes of the recorded region.

By the nearest-neighbour estimator the quantized loop carries more transfer
entropy than the analog loop does at any cutoff up to 1~kHz, because its jumps
tell the driven region when the recorded one crossed a step, so at the declared
step no cutoff matches it. At the steps where one does, the matched analog loop
carries more task information in \uBrNnGapPositive{} of \uBrNnGapCases{} cases,
by up to \uBrNnGapMax{}: which side of a transfer-entropy match the task
information falls on depends on the estimator. The lower match does not. The
nearest-neighbour decodability is matched between \uBrNnCutoffMin{} and
\uBrNnCutoffMax{}~Hz, above the linear transfer-entropy match in every regime,
so the lower match is the linear one, and a preparation sets the cutoff there.
At that cutoff, by the nearest-neighbour estimator, the analog loop carries
\uBrCommonTeMinPercent{} to \uBrCommonTeMaxPercent\% less transfer entropy and
explains \uBrCommonGapMin{} to \uBrCommonGapMax{} less of the task variable's
variance than the quantized loop, and it carries less of both at every simulated
step no larger than twice the task variable's standard deviation.

On the preparation, the decoder is fitted on other blocks of the same loop, the
step and the cutoff are set together on pilot blocks, and the check is repeated
on the blocks that are compared.

With blocks of \uBrBlockTrials{} trials repeated on independent noise, the matched transfer
entropy and the gap vary from block to block, and their confidence intervals
narrow with the square root of the trials. The behavioural comparison, accuracy
\uBrAccHigh{} against \uBrAccLow{} at a two-sided level of 0.05 and power 0.8,
needs \uBrTrialsBehaviour{} trials per loop, and the neural comparison, at a
standardized difference of \uBrNeuralEffect{}, needs \uBrTrialsNeural{}. At the
behavioural count, by either estimator, the transfer entropies are known to
within \uBrTeTolPercent\% and the difference in task variance to within
\uBrGapTol{}, smaller than every margin above, so the pilot blocks can confirm
that the analog loop carries no more of either. The model
has no spikes, no stimulation artefact and no plasticity.

The analog loop has to be analog end to end, because a loop that digitized the
recording before driving the stimulator would run the quantized condition in
both arms.

\paragraph{Stimulation artefact.} Event-triggered loops blank the recording
around each pulse, and a continuous drive leaves no interval to blank. The loops
therefore record and stimulate in separate frequency bands. Each loop reads
multi-unit activity, above a few hundred hertz, takes its envelope, and drives
the other region with a current that follows the envelope and so lies in the
band below it. Rectifying and smoothing are analog operations, so the analog
loop stays analog end to end. What leaks across the bands is removed by
subtracting the loop's own output, which the loop knows exactly, passed through
the transfer from stimulator to recording contact measured on open-loop
calibration blocks. Both loops end in the same output stage, which smooths the
quantizer's steps into the stimulation band, and share the stimulator, the
bands and the subtraction, so a residual artefact reaches both arms by one
path. Open-loop blocks in which each stimulator follows activity recorded in
another block measure that residual in each arm, and the comparison of the
loops is made after it is removed.

\section{Timing the field's cone}
\label{app:field}

Brain tissue carries an electric field as
well as synapses. At the frequencies of neural activity that field is
quasi-static \citep{gratiy2017}: it reaches every point of the tissue it spans
with no propagation delay. Synaptic influence between distant regions, by
contrast, arrives only after axonal conduction and synaptic transmission, no
sooner than \ueConeMaxMs{}~ms across the longest cortico-cortical fibres. If two
regions re-agree after a local perturbation faster than the synaptic route
allows, the coupling has a non-synaptic part, and the timing measures the reach
of the kernel tail of Section~\ref{sec:reach} directly.

The field's strength sets the distance at which the test of the field's causal
cone in Section~\ref{sec:tests} can decide anything.
Endogenous fields in cortex peak near \ueFieldPeak{}~mV/mm in vivo
\citep{frohlich2010}, networks of neurons detect fields from
\ueFieldDetection{}~mV/mm \citep{francis2003}, and the potential of a local
cortical source decays as $r^{-2.1}$ across millimetres \citep{rebollo2021}.
With the peak placed at the nearest distance of that measurement, the field of a
local source falls to the detection threshold within \ueFieldReachMm{}~mm, and
misses it by \ueFieldDeficitOrders{} orders of magnitude at \ueFibreCm{}~cm and
by \ueFieldDeficitLocalOrders{} at \ueLocalCm{}~cm.
The field's cone is therefore tested at millimetre distances, where activity
has been observed to synchronize across a cut through cortex by the field alone
\citep{rebollo2021}. At centimetre distances P predicts that re-agreement
arrives through synaptic coupling, which is graded as well.

The timing can be measured with intracortical recording, with care. The
stimulation artefact saturates clinical amplifiers during the first
milliseconds, masking the earliest monosynaptic responses \citep{keller2014},
and early responses at distant contacts carry a volume-conducted component that
scales with distance as the artefact does \citep{prime2020}. Three measures
separate active re-agreement from the passive field that carries the stimulus:
template subtraction of the artefact \citep{trebaul2016}; multi-unit activity or
current-source density at the distant site, which the passive spread of the
local field potential does not reach \citep{kajikawa2011}; and a deadline set
for each subject from the tractography length of the connecting fibres
\citep{caminiti2013}.

\section{Further related work}
\label{app:related}

\paragraph{Biology and the medium.} \citet{seth2026} argues that consciousness
may depend on biological properties that computation does not capture. P
locates the relevant property in how the substrate is organised, and admits
non-biological hardware that has it (Section~\ref{sec:ai}). Mortal computation,
whose parameters are useful only on the hardware instance that learned them
\citep{hinton2022,ororbia2023}, shares P's refusal to separate the computation
from the medium.

\paragraph{Analog and digital.} On the mechanistic account of
\citet{piccinini2008}, a digital computer manipulates strings of digits, states
its components distinguish reliably, and which class a system belongs to is
fixed by its mechanism. P's symbol, a content read through a noise margin, is
that reliability stated as a property of the readout, with the margin measured
in the parts' own noise; P then asks what the reliability costs, namely graded
dependence below the margin. \citet{maley2011} separates analog from continuous
and digital from discrete representation, and P's line follows the second
pair, not the first: a spike time is a physical content whether or not it
represents by analogy, and a continuous quantity latched through a margin is a
symbol. \citet{maudlin1989} argues that a computation's counterfactual
structure can be carried by machinery that is idle on the actual run, which
sits badly with consciousness supervening on present physical activity. P
states its counterfactual condition on present activity: graded dependence
asks how the actual carriers respond to changes of the actual state, above
their noise, and idle machinery responds to none.

\paragraph{Field theories.} The electromagnetic field theory of
\citet{mcfadden2020} identifies consciousness with the brain's field, supported
by evidence that endogenous fields entrain cortical activity
\citep{frohlich2010}. \citet{miller2026} place cognition and consciousness in
travelling waves that synaptic and ephaptic coupling sustain together, and take
consciousness to arise when those waves bring cortex into a globally integrated
state. Their analog computation is set apart from digital computation by
running in parallel. P treats the field, and the waves it carries, as ways
among several to realize graded coupling, and draws the line between analog
and digital at the noise margin, which a parallel digital machine keeps.
